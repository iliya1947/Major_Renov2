## 2. Corrections and rechecks

R1 — Distinguish perceived progress, intention and actual progress, even under experimental feedback. IND-02.1 E3 reports a reinforcing cycle but does not identify exactly which outcome sustained the indirect effect. Original Study2 results: manipulated social-comparison feedback changed reported importance (eta squared .02), expectancy (.02), satisfaction (.04), perceived progress manipulation check (.10), not stress (p=.561) or new importance/intention index (p=.304). Same-day reported progress total effect .25 CI [-.07,.57], p=.126; indirect via satisfaction .13 CI [.05,.29], p=.026. Neither direct nor indirect effect on step count significant; no significant indirect effects at week end on subjective or objective outcomes. Discussion has wording implying similar objective and subjective findings, but detailed results distinguish them. Satisfaction associated with same-day step count B=.18 CI [.01,.36], not evidence of an indirect intervention effect. No total-effect significance required for mediation mathematically, but randomizing feedback does not randomize mediator or exclude mediator-outcome confounding. Feedback changes more than perceived progress alone, including social comparison; exclusion of comprehension failures after manipulation threatens original randomized contrast, not proven direction/magnitude of bias. Arithmetic flow 442-167-2-38=235. Actual progress criterion independent of perceived success; no demonstrated durable behavior change. Root cause: overly compressed summary of outcomes and causal chain, already guarded by general method but specific result needed. Retest against direct results and IND-02.3 step1/5/6: target outcome, appraisal, satisfaction, subsequent observed behavior remain separate; no contradiction of bounded feedback model.

R2 — Distinguish framing effect, action initiation and intensity, and within/between person. Woolley Study4 933 invited back, 803 completed, 40 excluded (zero scans/outlier). Primary analysis conditions on post-assignment scanning; randomized assignment does not automatically guarantee unbiased inference in this selected subset. Including zeros gives similar small effect on scan count, not full intention-to-treat because missing follow-up remains. Relative scan difference (11.55-9.18)/9.18=25.82% among primary selected sample; not 25.82% higher probability of starting or goal completion. Binary initiation analysis p=.525 (not equivalence), all-zero-inclusive result d=.20. Study1 association mainly between persons. Study3 self-reported motivation and earlier steps cannot support prospective causation. Correction: separate willingness/entry, starting, intensity, maintenance and completion; causal claim limited to tested framing conditions and analyzed outcome, not unique latent intrinsic motivation or general enduring individual change. Retest: compare observed results, preserve distinction from SDT regulation quality and original IND-02.1 goal hierarchy. No inflation to clinical or relationship intervention claim.

R3 — Reverse inference: complete relevant model scope. Wilms Table3 reports four positive significant distraction-goal coefficients: feeling better .19, avoiding conflict .12, maintaining appearances .11, making someone else feel better .10. Also negative association with influencing others -.07 (table), with inconsistent wording in results about robustness for this variable; do not interpret its direction without further inspection. Original integration listed three selected correlates, not exhaustive; extend to four and retain multiple selection. Model explains 8% of within-person variance for distraction and 22% for suppression; not prediction accuracy or probability of a specific motive. Even exact P(action|motive) would not equal P(motive|action), which additionally depends on base rates and alternatives. Regression coefficients are not either probability. For facial movements use reliability, specificity, context/generalization and independent emotional criterion; no unique hidden motive from outward behavior. Root cause: incomplete set of model correlates and insufficiently explicit inverse-inference requirements. Retest: table matches values, existing general conclusion strengthened; original suppression coefficients and interaction null confirmed.

R4 — Distinguish flexibility, strategy selection, efficacy, and goal adjustment. New independent source https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42241085/ (indexed original abstract): coordinated analysis 12 daily-life datasets N1511, 161513 occasions, challenges generalized short-term strategy effectiveness; no exact coefficients reaudited and possible dataset overlap with IND-02.2 E4 unverified. Treat as separate estimand, not automatically independent sample evidence. New Riddell et al. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-025-02312-4 (direct abstract and definitions): 235 studies, 1421 effects, low-to-moderate accumulated evidence due to cross-sectional designs, heterogeneity and possible publication bias. Disengagement, reengagement and general flexibility distinct; cessation of one action may be adjustment, pausing, termination, alternative means or new goal. Explicitly identify referent and timescale; never classify absence of action as concealed commitment or loss of all value. Persistence is not always desirable and withdrawal is not necessarily failure. No universal beneficial strategy or prediction from trait flexibility. Root cause: existing procedure mentions goal adjustment but gives less explicit differentiation of cessation/reengagement and implementation versus efficacy. Retest against original IND-02.1/.2/.3 distinctions; additions narrow inference, no contradiction with goal hierarchy. Transfer to IND-03 and SYS-02/03 is conditional; no audit of those units claimed yet.

## 3. Revised scientific rule and result
A proposed explanation must identify the actor, target and hierarchy, observed opportunities, goal value, expectation, endorsed/controlled regulation, possible approach/avoidance orientation, affective appraisal and temporal window, evidence of regulatory attempt, perceived and observed outcomes, goal adjustment and alternatives. Separate initiation, amount, persistence and completion; clarify objective versus subjective success; do not treat immediate relief as attainment of a different goal. When extrapolating experiments distinguish assignment from mediator, post-assignment selection, outcome and time horizon. When inferring latent causes from signals, require evidence that differentiates rival accounts, base-rate sensitivity if quantified, and matched opportunity/context. Absence of direct evidence is not confirmation of a concealed cause. These are generic methodological rules, not an individual diagnosis, validated detector or independent test of assistant competence. No personal records used.
