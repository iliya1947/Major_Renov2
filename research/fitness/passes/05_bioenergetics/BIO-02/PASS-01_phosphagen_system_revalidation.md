- treating stored ATP as a large independent energy source;

## 2. Stored ATP inside the phosphagen framework

BIO-01 already established that the ATP pool is small relative to exercise demand.

BIO-02 retains stored ATP as the immediately available ATP pool at exercise onset.

Important boundary:

**stored ATP and PCr are related components of the immediate high-energy-phosphate system, but they are not the same molecule or the same pool.**

Stored ATP:

- is used directly by ATPases;
- is quantitatively small;
- is defended by rapid resynthesis.

PCr:

- transfers phosphate through CK to regenerate ATP from ADP;
- therefore buffers ATP availability rather than replacing ATP as the direct ATPase substrate.

Canonical rule:

**PCr does not “power contraction instead of ATP”; PCr supports contraction by rapidly regenerating ATP.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 3. Phosphocreatine

Phosphocreatine (PCr; creatine phosphate) is a phosphorylated creatine pool present at higher concentration than ATP in skeletal muscle and serves as a temporal/spatial high-energy-phosphate buffer.

Current exercise-metabolism review evidence describes:

- a large fraction of total muscle creatine in phosphorylated form at rest;
- rapid PCr use at contraction onset;
- functional coupling of CK near ATP-consuming and ATP-producing sites.

The project does not retain one universal resting PCr concentration because values depend on:

- dry vs wet mass convention;
- muscle/fiber type;
- training status;
- creatine status;
- measurement method.

Canonical rule:

**PCr is a rapidly mobilizable phosphate buffer/reservoir for ATP regeneration, not a direct ATP substitute.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 4. Creatine kinase reaction

The core reversible reaction is:

`PCr + ADP + H+ ↔ Cr + ATP`

catalyzed by creatine kinase (CK).

During rapid ATP hydrolysis in contracting muscle:

- ADP rises locally;
- the CK reaction is driven toward ATP regeneration;
- PCr falls and free creatine rises.

During recovery:

- ATP supplied mainly by oxidative metabolism drives the reverse direction;
- creatine is re-phosphorylated to PCr.

Important boundary:

**CK direction is state-/compartment-dependent; “PCr breakdown” and “PCr resynthesis” are opposite net directions of the same reversible reaction network.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 5. CK as temporal and spatial energy buffer

The CK/PCr system should not be reduced to a single bulk reaction.

CK isoenzymes are positioned near:

- ATP-consuming sites;
- glycolytic machinery;
- mitochondria.

This supports:

- local ATP/ADP buffering;
- high-rate phosphate transfer;
- intracellular energy transport/buffering.

The exact strength and necessity of a literal “PCr shuttle” model varies by tissue and model, so the project retains the broader, safer statement:

**the CK/PCr system provides temporal and spatial buffering of cellular energy transfer.**

Confidence: **HIGH for energy-buffering role / MODERATE-HIGH for detailed shuttle topology as a universal model**.

---

## 6. Adenylate kinase / myokinase

Adenylate kinase (AK; myokinase) catalyzes:

`2 ADP ↔ ATP + AMP`.

During high ATP turnover:

- ADP accumulation can drive ATP regeneration through AK;
- AMP rises;
- AMP can be deaminated to IMP during intense exercise;
- AMP/ADP also participate in cellular energy sensing.

Current skeletal-muscle reviews and human exercise evidence support this reaction as part of adenine-nucleotide buffering.

Important boundary:

**AK is a secondary ATP-buffering reaction, not evidence that AMP is a large independent exercise fuel.**

The reaction also amplifies changes in energetic state into larger relative changes in AMP.

Exact downstream purine loss / AMPD physiology belongs BIO-11/PHY where relevant.

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 7. Phosphagen ATP-production rate

The CK/PCr system is characterized by very high ATP-regeneration rate because:

- PCr and CK are already present intracellularly;
- the reaction is near equilibrium and rapidly responds to ADP/ATP changes;
- no long multistep pathway is required before phosphate transfer.

This makes phosphagen flux especially important when ATP demand rises abruptly.

However:

**“fastest ATP system” is a rate statement, not a statement that it is the only active system or that it supplies all ATP in the first seconds.**

Glycolytic and oxidative pathways can contribute from exercise onset.

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 8. Phosphagen capacity

High phosphagen power is paired with limited finite capacity.

The limiting substrate pool is primarily:

- intramuscular PCr;
- plus the small stored ATP pool;
- with a smaller adenylate-kinase contribution.

As PCr falls:

- the instantaneous ability to buffer ATP through CK declines;
- other ATP-resynthesis pathways become relatively more important.

Capacity is not one universal number because it depends on:

- starting PCr/total creatine;
- recruited muscle mass;
- muscle/fiber characteristics;
- exercise intensity and duration;
- recovery state;
- prior bouts;
- supplementation status.

Canonical rule:

**high rate ≠ high capacity.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 9. Contribution during very short maximal efforts

Direct human studies show large PCr use during brief maximal exercise.

PMID **9124295**:

- 8 healthy subjects;
- 9 s maximal-rate exercise depleted PCr by about 61%;
- 30 s maximal exercise depleted PCr by about 92%.

PMID **8964751**:

- after a 30 s cycling sprint PCr fell to about 17% of resting;
- during a subsequent sprint, available PCr was almost completely utilized within the first 10 s.

This supports:

**PCr/CK provides a major high-rate ATP-buffering contribution during very brief maximal work.**

It does not support:

- phosphagen = 100% of ATP during the first 10 s;
- glycolysis “starts after” phosphagen;
- oxidative ATP “starts later”;
- one universal crossover second.

Confidence: **HIGH for major early contribution / HIGH against sequential-system model**.

---

## 10. PCr depletion

PCr decreases when CK-mediated phosphate transfer to ADP exceeds PCr resynthesis.

Depletion magnitude varies widely with:

- work rate;
- contraction duration;
- muscle recruitment;
- exercise mode;
- training status;
- starting PCr;
- prior exercise;
- pH/metabolic state.

PCr depletion can be large without equivalent depletion of ATP because PCr buffers ATP.

Canonical boundary:

**PCr depletion is evidence of phosphagen-system use, not identical to ATP depletion.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 11. PCr depletion ≠ fatigue

PCr depletion often accompanies reduced ability to sustain high power, but it is not a complete causal definition of fatigue.

Fatigue can also involve:

- Pi accumulation;
- H+/pH-related effects depending on context;
- excitation-contraction factors;
- neural factors;
- substrate/metabolic limitations;
- task-specific constraints.

Thus:

**low PCr can constrain rapid ATP buffering, but fatigue must not be reduced to “PCr ran out.”**

Exact metabolic fatigue causation belongs BIO-11/PHY.

Confidence: **HIGH construct boundary**.

---

## 12. PCr resynthesis

After exercise, PCr begins to recover as creatine is re-phosphorylated.

In intact human muscle, post-exercise PCr recovery is strongly linked to oxidative ATP production and is widely used as an in-vivo index of muscle oxidative capacity.

The 2025 systematic review/meta-analysis PMID **40189235** included 128 studies and found substantial methodological heterogeneity in PCr recovery estimates.

Therefore:

**PCr recovery is physiologically informative but not one universal time constant.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 13. PCr recovery is predominantly oxidative

Classic human experiments support strong oxidative dependence:

- vascular occlusion can abolish PCr resynthesis until blood flow returns;
- hypoxia slows PCr recovery;
- hyperoxia can accelerate it in some protocols;
- PCr recovery rate correlates with oxidative capacity.

PMID **10368368** directly manipulated inspired O2 in trained humans:

- recovery was slower in hypoxia;
- faster in hyperoxia;
- intermediate in normoxia.

The 2024 systematic review PMID **38245757** concluded that PCr dynamics are tightly coupled with muscle O2 dynamics.

Canonical rule:

**post-exercise PCr resynthesis is predominantly supported by oxidative metabolism and therefore depends on adequate oxidative capacity and O2 delivery/utilization.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 14. O2 dependence ≠ universal O2-limited recovery

Important correction:

**oxidative dependence does not mean oxygen delivery is always the rate-limiting step in healthy normoxic muscle.**

PCr recovery kinetics can also depend on:

- intrinsic mitochondrial oxidative capacity;
- end-exercise PCr depletion;
- intracellular pH;
- muscle recruitment/localization;
- measurement protocol;
- training/health status.

The 2025 meta-analysis PMID **40189235** found:

- significant heterogeneity;
- longer PCr recovery with more acidic end-exercise pH in healthy muscle;
- demographic/disease effects.

The 31P-MRS expert consensus PMID **32037688** likewise warns that exercise standardization, recruitment heterogeneity and physiological confounders affect interpretation.

Project rule:

**PCr recovery is an oxidative-capacity marker only under conditions where O2 delivery and other confounders are adequately controlled/considered.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 15. Recovery kinetics are not a universal stopwatch

PCr recovery is commonly approximated with exponential kinetics, but actual recovery behavior and fitted time constants depend on protocol and physiological state.

Direct human examples:

- after one 6 s sprint, PCr was ~55% of baseline at 10 s, ~69% at 30 s and ~90% at 3 min in one trained sample (PMID **9241025**);
- after repeated 5 × 6 s sprints, PCr was ~27%, ~45% and ~84% at the same recovery times;
- after a 30 s maximal sprint, PCr reached ~65% after 1.5 min and ~86% after 6 min in another study (PMID **7714837**);
- after 3.8 min recovery following a 30 s sprint, PCr was ~79% of baseline in PMID **8964751**.

Therefore:

**there is no universal “PCr is fully restored after X minutes” rule.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 16. Rest interval

Longer recovery intervals generally allow greater PCr restoration before the next high-intensity bout.

PMID **24068048** directly showed:

- greater PCr restoration with 48 s than 30 s than 18 s recovery;
- greater intermittent high-intensity exercise tolerance with longer recovery;
- performance-restoration relationships with PCr reconstitution.

PMID **18481078** likewise manipulated 3, 6 and 15 min recovery and showed the preceding metabolic state influenced the next bout.

Canonical rule:

**rest duration changes the starting phosphagen state of the next bout, but the mapping from “rest time” to “% PCr restored” is protocol/person dependent.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 17. Rest interval and resistance training boundary

For resistance training, phosphagen recovery is one mechanistic reason longer rest can preserve high-force/high-power performance across sets.

But BIO-02 does not prescribe:

- one optimal rest interval;
- one rest interval for hypertrophy;
- one rest interval for strength;
- one minimum PCr recovery percentage before the next set.

Those are downstream PRO/BIO-12 decisions integrating:

- training goal;
- load;
- set duration;
- proximity to failure;
- volume;
- cardiorespiratory demands;
- performance loss.

Status: **HIGH mechanistic boundary / prescription deferred**.

---

## 18. PCr resynthesis and repeated-bout performance

Available PCr before a subsequent high-intensity bout can influence early-bout power/ATP buffering.

Classic sprint studies show correlations between:

- PCr restoration;
- restoration of peak/early sprint power.

But repeated-bout performance is not determined by PCr alone.

Project rule:

**PCr restoration is an important contributor to repeated high-intensity performance, not a complete performance model.**

Confidence: **MODERATE-HIGH**.

---

## 19. Creatine supplementation boundary

Creatine supplementation can increase intramuscular total creatine and, in many contexts, expand the creatine/PCr pool.

This is mechanistically relevant to BIO-02.

Direct human evidence (PMID **12043832**) showed after short-term creatine ingestion:

- higher PCr during recovery;
- increased PCr resynthesis during longer recovery;
- modestly higher repeated-bout power in that protocol.

Recent 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis PMID **42027564** also reports performance/body-composition effects across creatine trials, but supplementation decisions are not BIO-02 scope.

BIO-02 retains only:

**creatine availability can modify the size/behavior of the creatine-PCr system.**

Detailed questions of:

- loading vs no-loading;
- daily dose;
- responders/nonresponders;
- efficacy by outcome;
- safety;
- body-mass effects;
- timing;
- population-specific use

belong NUT.

Confidence: **HIGH mechanism / downstream efficacy prescription deferred**.

## 21. Measurement boundaries

### Stored ATP
Immediate ATP pool directly available to ATPases.

### PCr
Phosphorylated creatine pool used by CK for rapid ATP regeneration.

### CK flux
Rate of phosphate transfer through the reversible CK reaction.

### PCr depletion
Change from resting/pre-bout PCr caused by net CK flux toward ATP regeneration.

### PCr resynthesis
Post-exercise re-phosphorylation of creatine, predominantly supported by oxidative ATP supply in intact human muscle.

### PCr recovery time constant / half-time
Model-derived kinetic descriptors; protocol- and physiology-dependent rather than universal constants.

### Phosphagen capacity
Finite high-rate ATP-buffering potential tied primarily to stored ATP + PCr + adenylate-kinase buffering; exact operational definition varies by method.

## 23. Established content-level findings

### HIGH

1. Stored ATP and PCr are distinct pools; PCr rapidly regenerates ATP through CK.
2. CK reaction is reversible and shifts toward ATP regeneration during high demand and toward PCr resynthesis during recovery.
3. Adenylate kinase catalyzes `2 ADP ↔ ATP + AMP` and contributes to adenine-nucleotide buffering.
4. CK/PCr provides very high-rate but limited-capacity ATP buffering.
5. PCr makes a major contribution during very brief maximal work but is not the exclusive ATP source.
6. PCr can be heavily depleted during short maximal exercise.
7. PCr depletion is not identical to ATP depletion or fatigue.
8. Post-exercise PCr resynthesis in intact human muscle is predominantly oxidative.
9. O2 availability can materially alter PCr recovery.
10. PCr recovery kinetics are strongly protocol/physiology dependent and cannot be reduced to one universal recovery time.
11. Longer rest generally allows more PCr reconstitution before the next high-intensity bout.
12. Creatine availability can modify the creatine-PCr pool/system; supplement prescription belongs NUT.
13. Phosphagen, glycolytic and oxidative pathways contribute concurrently rather than as sequential isolated systems.

### MODERATE-HIGH

14. PCr restoration materially contributes to restoration of repeated-bout high-power performance.
15. CK/PCr serves both temporal and spatial energy-buffering functions.
16. PCr recovery can index mitochondrial oxidative capacity when O2 delivery, pH, depletion and measurement conditions are controlled.

### LOW / NOT RETAINED AS UNIVERSAL RULE

17. One exact “phosphagen lasts N seconds” threshold.
18. One exact PCr concentration for all skeletal muscle.
19. One exact percentage of ATP from phosphagen during a specific second of exercise.
20. One universal full-PCr-recovery time.
21. One rest interval that guarantees a given % PCr recovery.
22. One PCr threshold defining fatigue or readiness for the next set.

## 25. Evidence-ledger candidates

1. PCr rapidly buffers ATP through the reversible CK reaction; PCr is not a direct substitute for ATP at ATPases.
2. Adenylate kinase provides secondary adenine-nucleotide buffering through `2 ADP ↔ ATP + AMP`.
3. The phosphagen system has very high ATP-regeneration rate but limited capacity set largely by finite PCr/high-energy-phosphate stores.
4. PCr contributes strongly during very brief maximal exercise, but glycolytic and oxidative pathways are concurrently active; no universal “phosphagen-only” duration exists.
5. PCr depletion reflects phosphagen use but is not equivalent to ATP depletion or fatigue.
6. Post-exercise PCr resynthesis is predominantly oxidative and depends on mitochondrial/O2-related conditions, but O2 delivery is not universally the sole rate-limiter.
7. PCr recovery is protocol- and physiology-dependent; no universal full-recovery time or fixed rest→%PCr mapping is established.
8. Longer rest generally allows greater PCr reconstitution and can support repeated high-intensity performance, without defining one universally optimal rest interval.
9. Creatine availability can modify the creatine-PCr pool; dosing/safety/performance prescription belongs NUT.
10. CK/PCr, glycolytic and oxidative ATP-resynthesis pathways act concurrently rather than sequentially.
