## Established findings

### F1 — Question framing precedes the main review process
**Confidence: HIGH**

Cochrane states that determining the review focus and clearly framing the questions is the first major decision. Well-formulated questions guide:
- eligibility criteria;
- searching;
- data collection;
- synthesis structure;
- presentation.

Project consequence:
the search cannot define the question retroactively.

### F2 — Relevance to the intended decision/use matters
**Confidence: HIGH**

Cochrane explicitly frames review questions around questions important to decision makers and notes opportunity cost of undertaking a review.

Project consequence:
before deep research we should state what the answer is expected to inform:
- a practical decision;
- a safety boundary;
- a measurement choice;
- or a foundational causal/modeling question.

The project's “Decision before evidence” rule is therefore a **project operationalization** of an established relevance principle, not a direct Cochrane wording.

### F3 — Review PICO, synthesis PICO and study PICO are distinct
**Confidence: HIGH**

Cochrane distinguishes:
1. review PICO — scope/eligibility;
2. PICO for each synthesis — exact comparison/grouping being synthesized;
3. PICO of included studies — what each study actually investigated.

Project consequence:
a broad topic does not authorize pooling every included study into one estimate.

### F4 — Broad and narrow questions are both legitimate
**Confidence: HIGH**

Cochrane does not prescribe “narrower is always better”.

Breadth depends on:
- relevance/impact;
- theoretical/biological context;
- expected generalizability/validity;
- available resources.

Project consequence:
scope must be justified by the decision/problem, not by a fixed preference for broad or narrow questions.

### F5 — Broad review scope and pooling heterogeneity are separate decisions
**Confidence: HIGH**

A broad review can include diverse studies while still using multiple narrower syntheses.

Project consequence:
do not equate “broad topic” with “one pooled effect”.

### F6 — Question refinement can be legitimate, but must be documented
**Confidence: HIGH**

Cochrane acknowledges that fuller understanding of evidence can require modifying/refining questions.

Risk:
data-driven refinement can introduce bias.

Required checks include:
- why was refinement made?
- could observed results have influenced it?
- does search/eligibility need revision?
- do collection methods still fit?

Project consequence:
scope changes are allowed, but must be logged and re-run through affected search/eligibility steps.

### F7 — Pre-specification is not rigidity
**Confidence: HIGH**

The correct principle is:
- predefine enough to reduce result-driven decisions;
- allow justified, transparent refinement when evidence architecture reveals a real issue.

Project consequence:
“never change the question” is too rigid;
“change it whenever convenient” is biased.

## Practical rules produced by PASS-01

1. Every topic starts with an explicit **scope statement** before full searching.
2. Scope includes the intended use/decision or states that the topic is foundational.
3. Separate:
   - review/topic scope;
   - each synthesis question;
   - actual study question.
4. Broad topic may contain several narrow syntheses.
5. Any post-search scope refinement must record:
   - reason;
   - whether results could have influenced it;
   - search/eligibility consequences.
6. Do not call a topic closed merely because its broad question has been described.
