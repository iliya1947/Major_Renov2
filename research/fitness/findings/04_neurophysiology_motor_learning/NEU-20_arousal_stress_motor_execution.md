# NEU-20 — Arousal, stress and motor execution

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-19**  
Область: 04 — Neurophysiology / Motor Learning

## 0. Core decision

Arousal, stress, anxiety and pressure are related but distinct.

A defensible project model is:

`task demands`
×
`pressure/appraisal`
×
`attentional allocation`
×
`expertise/automaticity`
×
`current activation state`
→
`acute motor performance`.

Durable learning is assessed separately.

Project rule:

**do not prescribe one universal arousal level; assess the response required by the actual task and retest learning outside transient pressure states.**

---

## 1. Physiological arousal

Physiological arousal is an activation state.

It can include changes in:

- heart rate;
- autonomic balance;
- muscle activation/tension;
- pupil-linked arousal;
- cortical preparation.

No single physiological marker defines “readiness.”

Project rule:

**arousal-performance effects are task dependent.**

---

## 2. Stress, anxiety and pressure

These constructs should not be collapsed.

### Stress
Demand/resource/appraisal process.

### Anxiety
Affective/cognitive/somatic response.

### Pressure
Context in which performing well is perceived as especially important.

Project rule:

**name the construct being manipulated or measured before interpreting its effect.**

---

## 3. No universal inverted-U

Inverted-U relations are supported in some perceptual/precision tasks.

They are not established as a universal motor-performance law.

Strength tasks can benefit from deliberate activation/psyching-up.

Project rule:

**do not use one generic “optimal arousal” target across tasks.**

---

## 4. Pressure can help or hurt

Pressure can produce:

- facilitative/clutch performance;
- preserved performance;
- choking/underperformance.

Project rule:

**pressure is a context, not a guaranteed impairment.**

---

## 5. Attentional narrowing

Narrow attention can be useful when it preserves task-relevant information.

It can be harmful when it removes information needed for execution.

Project rule:

**the issue is attentional-information fit, not narrowness itself.**

---

## 6. Attentional mechanisms under pressure

Candidate mechanisms include:

- self-focus / explicit monitoring / reinvestment;
- distraction / worry;
- overarousal;
- altered internal-model/predictive processing.

No single mechanism explains every pressure failure.

Project rule:

**do not infer the mechanism from poor performance alone.**

---

## 7. Coordination under stress

Pressure can alter:

- movement timing;
- kinematics;
- muscle activity;
- accuracy;
- force control;
- gaze/visual sampling.

No universal stress-specific coordination pattern exists.

Project rule:

**measure the task variable that actually changes.**

---

## 8. Choking

Choking is not any poor attempt.

A defensible current definition requires:

- meaningful pressure/perceived importance;
- motivation to perform well;
- a consequential performance decrease;
- deterioration outside expected performance variability.

Project rule:

**baseline/context/magnitude matter before labeling choking.**

---

## 9. Familiarity and automaticity

Proceduralized skills often require less conscious control.

Excessive conscious monitoring can disrupt expert execution in some tasks.

But automaticity does not create immunity to pressure.

Project rule:

**expertise changes control demands; it does not eliminate pressure susceptibility.**

---

## 10. Pressure familiarity

Task familiarity and pressure familiarity are different.

A skilled athlete can remain vulnerable in an unfamiliar pressure context.

Project rule:

**pressure robustness should be demonstrated under relevant pressure conditions.**

---

## 11. Pressure training

Manageable pressure training can improve later pressure robustness in some tasks.

Useful principles:

- purposeful;
- progressive;
- relevant to the target context;
- not indiscriminate stress exposure.

Project rule:

**pressure training is conditional, not a universal motor-learning intervention.**

Exact implementation belongs PSY/COA.

---

## 12. Counter-choking interventions

Intervention meta-analysis shows average benefit for:

- motor accuracy;
- executional success;
- target deviation.

Effects are more robust in closed-skill contexts than open-skill contexts.

No single intervention is universally superior.

Project rule:

**strategy selection must remain task/context specific.**

---

## 13. Strength-specific psyching-up

Direct strength evidence shows self-selected psyching-up can acutely increase deadlift bar velocity.

This supports activation for some high-force tasks.

It does not prove:

- better technique;
- better retention;
- better learning;
- one optimal arousal level.

Project rule:

**acute psyching-up benefit = performance evidence, not learning evidence.**

---

## 14. Pressure decrement ≠ learning loss

A learned skill can be temporarily expressed worse under pressure.

If performance returns after pressure removal, the evidence supports state-dependent execution loss rather than erased learning.

Project rule:

**retest outside pressure before inferring skill loss.**

---

## 15. Pressure robustness ≠ retention

These are different properties.

A skill can be:

- well retained but pressure fragile;
- pressure robust in one context but not another.

Project rule:

**test ordinary retention and pressure-specific transfer separately.**

---

## 16. Resistance-exercise boundary

Direct compound-lift evidence for pressure-induced technique degradation remains sparse.

Current direct strength evidence mainly concerns:

- psyching-up;
- velocity;
- acute performance.

Therefore no universal rule is justified for:

- squat/deadlift/bench arousal target;
- technical choking threshold;
- pressure-training dose.

These remain downstream NEU-22 / PSY / COA / TST / IND questions.

---

## 17. Sports-psychology boundary

NEU-20 does not prescribe individualized:

- mindfulness;
- cognitive restructuring;
- self-talk;
- anxiety treatment;
- competition routines.

Those belong Area 12 / PSY-COA.

NEU-20 provides the motor-performance boundaries those interventions must respect.

---

## 18. Practical synthesis

For later resistance-training work:

1. identify the task as force/power, precision or mixed;
2. distinguish arousal from anxiety/pressure;
3. establish a viable low-pressure skill first when learning a new movement;
4. test performance under realistic pressure/load;
5. retain activation if it helps force output without degrading task criteria;
6. reduce/restructure activation if coordination/precision deteriorates;
7. protect task-relevant attention;
8. avoid unnecessary conscious micromanagement of proceduralized skill;
9. use progressive pressure training only when pressure robustness is an actual goal;
10. after pressure-related failure, retest at baseline before diagnosing lost learning.

Status:

**PRACTICAL_HEURISTIC**, not a universal arousal prescription.

---

## 19. Canonical non-equivalences

1. Arousal ≠ stress.
2. Stress ≠ anxiety.
3. Anxiety ≠ pressure.
4. Pressure ≠ impairment.
5. High arousal ≠ automatically harmful.
6. Low arousal ≠ automatically beneficial.
7. Inverted-U ≠ universal motor law.
8. Attentional narrowing ≠ automatically harmful.
9. More anxiety ≠ necessarily worse motor performance.
10. Choking ≠ any poor attempt.
11. Self-focus ≠ universal choking mechanism.
12. Distraction ≠ universal choking mechanism.
13. Automaticity ≠ pressure immunity.
14. Expertise ≠ pressure familiarity.
15. Pressure training ≠ indiscriminate stress exposure.
16. Psyching-up effect ≠ motor learning.
17. Acute pressure decrement ≠ loss of learning.
18. Pressure robustness ≠ retention.
19. Physiological marker ≠ causal pressure mechanism.
20. One psychological intervention ≠ universal solution.

---

## 20. Measurement boundaries

### Arousal
Physiological activation state.

### Anxiety
Subjective/cognitive/somatic response.

### Pressure
Performance-importance context.

### Stress appraisal
Demand-resource interpretation.

### Choking
Consequential pressure-related performance decrement outside expected variability.

### Clutch/facilitative response
Successful goal-directed performance under pressure.

### Pressure robustness
Ability to maintain relevant performance under target pressure.

### Retention
Delayed skill expression outside transient state effects.

### Pressure-specific transfer
Performance under a different but relevant pressure context.

---

## 21. Confidence

### HIGH

- pressure can facilitate or impair;
- arousal/stress/anxiety/pressure are distinct;
- no universal optimal arousal level;
- choking ≠ any poor attempt;
- acute pressure decrement ≠ loss of learning;
- pressure robustness ≠ retention.

### MODERATE-HIGH

- attentional mismatch/self-focus/distraction can contribute to choking;
- pressure training can improve robustness;
- automaticity changes control demands;
- anti-choking interventions show average benefit;
- psyching-up can improve acute strength performance.

### MODERATE

- task-specific nonlinear arousal-performance relations;
- attentional narrowing as adaptive vs maladaptive;
- pressure familiarity as trainable.

### LOW / CONTEXT-DEPENDENT

- compound-lift optimal arousal;
- technical-choking threshold;
- pressure-training dose for lifting;
- exact individual choking mechanism;
- one physiological marker of “optimal state.”
