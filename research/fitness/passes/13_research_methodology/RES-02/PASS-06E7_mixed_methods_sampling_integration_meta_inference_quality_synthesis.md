# 3. Central quality architecture

Mixed-methods quality is represented as four distinct but related layers.

## QL1 — Strand quality

- quantitative component quality;
- qualitative component quality.

## QL2 — Integration quality

- whether and how strands are genuinely integrated;
- whether integration is coherent with design;
- whether integration operations are defensible.

## QL3 — Meta-inference quality

- whether integrated conclusions are warranted by component evidence and their relationship.

## QL4 — Reporting transparency

- whether readers can reconstruct:
  - rationale;
  - design;
  - samples;
  - integration;
  - divergence;
  - added value.

### Critical rule

Good reporting ≠ good methodology.

Good strand quality ≠ good integration.

Integration ≠ defensible meta-inference automatically.

---

# 4. Mixed-method sampling is its own design problem

Mixed methods does not merely require:

- one QUAN sample;
- one QUAL sample.

It requires explicit understanding of:

> how the two sampling structures relate.

Canonical relationship types retained:

1. **IDENTICAL**
2. **NESTED**
3. **PARALLEL / SEPARATE**
4. **MULTILEVEL**

---

# 5. Identical samples

Same participants contribute:

- QUAN;
- QUAL.

### Strength

Allows:

- person-level integration;
- direct case comparison;
- score ↔ account linkage.

### Limitation

Same participants do not guarantee:

- integration;
- adequate QUAL depth;
- adequate QUAN representativeness.

---

# 6. Nested samples

One sample is a subset of another.

Common route:

large QUAN sample  
→ smaller purposive QUAL subsample.

Typical in:

- explanatory sequential;
- embedded process evaluation.

### Strength

Supports:

- case-level linkage;
- result-driven sampling.

### Risk

Selection into the nested sample can be:

- outcome-dependent;
- willingness-dependent;
- nonrepresentative.

---

# 7. Parallel / separate samples

QUAL and QUAN samples are different but related to:

- same population;
- same setting;
- same program;
- same phenomenon.

### Appropriate integration

Usually:

- domain-level;
- group-level;
- program-level.

### Critical rule

No person-level concordance without person-level linkage.

---

# 8. Multilevel samples

Different strands may operate at different levels.

Examples:

QUAN:

- athletes.

QUAL:

- coaches/managers.

or:

QUAN:

- patients.

QUAL:

- organizations/sites.

### Critical rule

Integration must specify the cross-level bridge.

---

# 9. Sample relationship and timing are separate axes

A nested sample can occur:

- sequentially;
- approximately concurrently.

An identical sample can occur:

- convergently;
- longitudinally.

### Project rule

Store independently:

- sample relationship;
- timing;
- strand dependency.

---

# 10. Probability + purposive sampling

Mixed-method studies often combine:

## QUAN

- probability;
- census;
- randomized/trial samples;
- convenience clinical cohorts.

## QUAL

- purposive;
- maximum variation;
- criterion;
- theoretical;
- extreme/deviant case.

### Critical rule

One strand’s sampling logic does not validate the other.

---

# 11. Probability sampling does not transfer representativeness to QUAL

Example:

representative survey  
→ 20 purposively selected interviews.

### Valid

Survey prevalence inference may be population-representative.

### Invalid

QUAL themes are automatically representative because interviewees came from the survey.

---

# 12. Purposeful sampling does not invalidate the QUAN strand

The reverse error also occurs.

QUAL may be purposive.

QUAN may be:

- randomized;
- probability sampled.

### Rule

Appraise each strand according to its own inferential logic.

---

# 13. Sample integration quality

Onwuegbuzie/Johnson legitimation lineage includes:

> sample integration legitimation.

For RES-02 this means:

> Does the relationship between QUAN and QUAL samples support the integrated claim being made?

### Example

Person-level explanation requires:

- linked participants.

Population/domain-level complementarity may not.

---

# 14. Sample imbalance

Large asymmetry is common:

- QUAN n=500;
- QUAL n=20.

This is not intrinsically poor mixed methods.

### Appraise

- why sizes differ;
- what claim each sample supports;
- integration granularity;
- whether small QUAL sample is adequate for its methodology.

---

# 15. Sample non-overlap can be productive

Different samples may deliberately represent:

- different stakeholders;
- different system levels;
- different perspectives.

### Example

patient outcomes  
+ clinician implementation accounts.

### Critical rule

Difference must be analytically intended, not hidden.

---

# 16. Sampling relationship must be visible in joint interpretation

For every meta-inference, ask:

> Which participants/units produced each side of this integrated claim?

If this cannot be reconstructed:

- sample-integration quality is weak.

---

# 17. Integration has multiple possible levels

Current integration literature supports integration at:

1. research question;
2. design;
3. sampling;
4. data collection;
5. data analysis;
6. interpretation;
7. reporting.

### Critical rule

Integration does not have to occur at every level.

But it must occur sufficiently to create:

- purposeful interdependence;
- added value.

---

# 18. Design-level integration

Visible when:

- sequence/dependency is planned;
- mixed-method question is explicit;
- strands are intentionally related.

Examples:

- QUAN result drives QUAL sampling;
- QUAL themes build survey;
- parallel strands target same implementation domains.

---

# 19. Sampling-level integration

Primary mechanism:

**connecting**.

Examples:

- survey score → interview sample;
- trial outcome → responder/nonresponder interviews;
- qualitative category → quantitative stratum.

---

# 20. Data-collection integration

Primary mechanism:

**building**.

Examples:

- QUAL themes → QUAN questionnaire;
- QUAN results → interview guide;
- one data form modifies another collection procedure.

---

# 21. Analysis-level integration

Possible operations:

- merging;
- case linkage;
- joint matrices;
- crossover analysis;
- data transformation;
- following a thread.

---

# 22. Interpretation-level integration

Researchers explicitly determine:

- convergence;
- complementarity;
- expansion;
- discordance;

and generate a:

- meta-inference.

---

# 23. Reporting-level integration

Readers should be able to see:

- where integration happened;
- how;
- what it produced.

### Critical rule

Transparent reporting is necessary to appraise integration.

But transparency alone does not make integration valid.

---

# 24. Four canonical integration mechanisms

E1–E6 remain stable.

## Connecting

one strand informs sampling of another.

## Building

one strand shapes later data collection/measurement/intervention content.

## Merging

results/data are brought together for direct comparison/synthesis.

## Embedding

one component is structurally nested within a larger parent design/program.

### E5 correction retained

Embedding as structural relation does not itself guarantee mixed-method integration.

---

# 25. Joint displays

MMIQF 2024 treats joint displays as:

- an important optional integration tool;
- not a mandatory criterion.

### Quality questions

A strong joint display should:

- align commensurable evidence;
- identify data/result sources;
- show comparable aggregation level;
- include integrated interpretation/meta-inference where appropriate;
- remain interpretable with accompanying narrative.

---

# 26. Joint display coherence

Do not compare:

- individual raw quote

directly against:

- population-level adjusted effect

without explaining the level mismatch.

### Better

Match:

- inference ↔ inference;
- case ↔ case;
- domain ↔ domain.

---

# 27. Joint display as analysis, not decoration

A joint display can be used:

- during analysis;
- iteratively;
- to identify missing comparisons;
- to find discrepant cases;
- to generate meta-inferences.

### Critical rule

Post-hoc formatting of already-separated results is weaker than analytic use.

---

# 28. Data transformation

## Quantitizing

QUAL representation → numerical/categorical representation.

## Qualitizing

QUAN representation → narrative/profile/category.

### Required provenance

Store:

- original evidence;
- transformation rule;
- person/unit linkage;
- lost information;
- uncertainty.

---

# 29. Conversion legitimation

Onwuegbuzie/Johnson lineage identifies:

> conversion legitimation.

For RES-02:

> Did the transformation preserve enough meaning/structure to support the later meta-inference?

### Risk

A binary theme variable can erase:

- intensity;
- ambivalence;
- context;
- absence ambiguity.

---

# 30. Crossover analysis

Crossover analysis deliberately uses:

- one analytic tradition to examine data/output from the other strand.

Examples:

- theme categories cross-tabulated against ratings;
- quantitative profiles used to structure qualitative case interpretation.

### Current 2026 development

Contingency-table crossover procedures continue to be developed for convergent designs.

### Critical rule

Crossover analysis is an integration technique, not a new study design.

---

# 31. Following a thread

One result is pursued across:

- datasets;
- phases;
- analyses.

Example:

low exercise adherence  
→ subgroup statistics  
→ interview cases  
→ contextual process data  
→ revised explanation.

### Strength

Maintains a coherent analytic question across strands.

---

# 32. Integration granularity

E2/E3 field retained branch-wide.

Possible levels:

1. PERSON / CASE
2. SUBGROUP
3. DOMAIN / CONSTRUCT
4. SITE / ORGANIZATION
5. PROGRAM
6. SYSTEM
7. WHOLE-STUDY
8. PROGRAM-OF-RESEARCH

### Critical rule

Meta-inference granularity cannot exceed the linkage actually established.

---

# 33. Granularity mismatch

Example:

QUAN:

- population prevalence.

QUAL:

- 12 purposively sampled cases.

### Valid integration

Interpretation of:

- what a construct means;
- why patterns may occur.

### Invalid integration

Use 12 interviews to estimate:

- population prevalence of themes.

---

# 34. Fit is not the same as quality

Strands may:

- agree;
- complement;
- expand;
- contradict.

### Critical rule

Agreement does not prove validity.

Discordance does not prove failure.

---

# 35. Productive discordance vs methodological incoherence

Current 2026 coherence literature makes a useful distinction.

## Productive discordance

Both strands are methodologically defensible but produce genuinely different insights.

## Correctable incoherence

Difference arises from:

- construct mismatch;
- poor sampling;
- invalid transformation;
- inappropriate integration level;
- analytic error.

### Project rule

Before preserving discordance as substantive:

test for methodological incoherence.

---

# 36. Divergence audit

For every major discordance, check:

1. same construct?;
2. same sample?;
3. same time window?;
4. same unit?;
5. adequate power?;
6. adequate QUAL coverage?;
7. missingness?;
8. scale validity?;
9. social desirability?;
10. transformation loss?;
11. subgroup heterogeneity?;
12. context changed?;
13. implementation changed?;
14. analysis error?;
15. true substantive difference?

# 37. Meta-inference definition

Canonical RES-02 definition:

> **Meta-inference = an integrated higher-order conclusion generated from the relationship between qualitative and quantitative findings/inferences, producing insight that is not simply a repetition of either strand alone.**

---

# 38. Meta-inference is a product of integration

It can be produced through:

- convergent comparison;
- explanatory follow-up;
- exploratory building;
- multistage synthesis;
- process/outcome integration;
- case-study integration.

---

# 39. Meta-inference can have different functions

Current literature supports at least:

- confirmation;
- explanation;
- expansion;
- complementarity;
- contradiction/refinement;
- development;
- program recommendation/decision.

### Project rule

Store:

**META_INFERENCE_FUNCTION**

separately from:

**FIT_CATEGORY**.

---

# 40. Fit category vs meta-inference function

Example:

fit:

- discordant.

meta-inference function:

- theory refinement.

Another:

fit:

- complementary.

function:

- program recommendation.

### Why

“Agreement/disagreement” does not fully describe what integrated inference accomplishes.

---

# 41. Good meta-inference criteria

A defensible meta-inference should be:

1. traceable to strand findings;
2. aligned with the MMR question;
3. coherent with component inferences;
4. explicit about sample/unit linkage;
5. compatible with uncertainty;
6. open to alternative explanation;
7. calibrated to design;
8. transparent about divergence;
9. not more causal/generalizable than component designs allow;
10. useful beyond merely restating both strands.

---

# 42. Alternative explanations

MMIQF explicitly emphasizes considering:

- alternative explanations.

### Example

QUAN:

- lower adherence.

QUAL:

- transportation problems.

Before meta-inference:

> transport explains low adherence

consider:

- illness severity;
- access;
- scheduling;
- motivation;
- sampling bias.

---

# 43. Oversimplification risk

Meta-inference can compress complexity too aggressively.

### Example

Three qualitative pathways  
+ one mean score  
→ one universal “mechanism”.

### Project rule

Preserve:

- heterogeneity;
- subgroup differences;
- minority cases;
- uncertainty.

---

# 44. Transferability/applicability of meta-inference

Mixed-method inference may be transferable at:

- conceptual;
- contextual;
- programmatic

levels.

### Critical rule

Transferability is not the same as:

- statistical generalizability.

# 45. Component validity is necessary

MMAT 2018 mixed-method criteria explicitly require that:

> each component adheres to the quality criteria of its own methodological tradition.

### Consequence

Poor QUAL + excellent QUAN + excellent integration is still limited.

Poor QUAN + excellent QUAL + excellent integration is still limited.

---

# 46. Integration cannot rescue invalid measurement

Example:

invalid scale  
+ rich interviews  
→ beautifully integrated joint display.

### Result

Interpretation remains constrained by invalid QUAN measurement.

---

# 47. Integration cannot rescue incoherent QUAL

Example:

thin open-ended comments  
+ sophisticated trial data.

Calling the comments:

- “qualitative strand”

does not create methodological depth.

# 48. MMAT role

MMAT 2018 is a **critical appraisal tool** for empirical studies in mixed-study reviews.

Its mixed-method criteria ask whether:

1. rationale for mixed methods is adequate;
2. components are effectively integrated;
3. integration outputs are adequately interpreted;
4. divergences/inconsistencies are addressed;
5. components meet their own quality criteria.

### Important

MMAT is not:

- a reporting guideline;
- a design taxonomy.

---

# 49. MMAT overall-score caution

Current MMAT guidance discourages reducing appraisal to one overall score because:

- a single metric hides which criteria failed.

### Project rule

Do not create one numeric “mixed-method quality score” from MMAT.

Store criterion-level judgments.

# 63. Mixed-method-specific biases

E7 introduces explicit branch-wide bias fields.

## Integration omission

Strands never meaningfully meet.

## Integration mismatch

Noncommensurable constructs/levels are forced together.

## Sample-linkage bias

Meta-inference exceeds sample relationship.

## Transformation bias

Quantitizing/qualitizing distorts original meaning.

## Integration-selection bias

Only convergent findings are highlighted.

## Discordance suppression

Contradictions are minimized or removed.

## Dominance bias

One strand is systematically privileged without rationale.

## Sequence-induced bias

Earlier phase changes later responses/behavior.

## Outcome-aware interpretation bias

Process/QUAL findings are re-read after outcomes are known.

## Publication fragmentation bias

Linked phases are treated as independent evidence.

---

# 64. Strand dominance

Unequal weighting is legitimate.

Problem arises when:

- priority is not stated;
- weak dominant strand overrides strong contradictory evidence;
- subordinate strand is used only decoratively.

### Project field

`STRAND_PRIORITY`

and:

`ACTUAL_INFERENTIAL_DOMINANCE`.

# 65. Integration selection bias

Researchers may only integrate:

- statistically significant QUAN findings;
- attractive QUAL themes;
- convergent evidence.

# 66. Discordance suppression

A meta-inference should not be constructed by:

- dropping inconvenient cases;
- redefining constructs post hoc;
- ignoring nonsignificant results.

### Current PCORI/MMAT support

Both emphasize explicit treatment of divergence/inconsistency.

# 79. Quality decision hierarchy

A useful branch-wide decision order is:

1. **Are there valid QUAL and QUAN components?**
2. **Is there a real rationale for mixing?**
3. **Are samples/units linkable at the claimed level?**
4. **Does integration actually occur?**
5. **Is the integration operation appropriate?**
6. **Are divergence and alternative explanations handled?**
7. **Does a new meta-inference emerge?**
8. **Is that meta-inference warranted by both strands and their relationship?**
9. **Is the process transparently reported?**
10. **Are claims calibrated to the parent designs?**

# 80. Mixed methods added value test

A study should ideally answer:

> What did mixing allow us to conclude/do that neither strand alone could support?

Possible answers:

- explain heterogeneity;
- develop a measure;
- identify context;
- refine intervention;
- expose mismatch;
- produce program recommendation;
- triangulate/qualify inference.

### Failure mode

If no added value can be identified:

mixed-method classification may be nominal rather than substantive.
