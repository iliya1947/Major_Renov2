## Synthesis and practical analysis
Evidence supports separating exposure, response and recovery; cognitive ability may correlate with each differently (E02). Selected high-ability cohort E03 supports prospective association of a specific sensitivity measure with later symptoms, not a universal giftedness trait or resilience mechanism. E04 tests moderation in general-range cognition, E05 reports main/indirect associations but not interactions for different resources, E06 shows prospective but not significant lagged links, E08 shows outcome-dependent interaction evidence. No universal cognitive buffer. E01/07 demonstrate how exposure-adjusted trajectories can improve interpretation, but residual not causal effect and not a person's stable trait. No direct adult IQ150–160+ conclusion.
Operational rules (PRACTICAL_HEURISTIC):
1. Define stressor (type, dose, duration, controllability, actual exposure), appraisal (reported threat/challenge), response (affective, bodily, cognitive, behavioral, each measured distinctly), recovery (time course), outcome (well-being, symptoms, function, costs). Same outward function may coexist with high distress; low distress without adversity is not demonstrated resilience. Avoid inferring neuroendocrine state from self-report.
2. Compare within-person changes relative to ordinary baseline and relevant context; external support/resources may lower exposure rather than change reaction. Both can be valuable, do not rank moral worth. Distinguish resistance (little change), recovery (return), adaptation (new functioning), delayed deterioration, persistent disturbance; these are descriptive trajectories, not fixed types. Multiple outcome dimensions may diverge. Do not demand growth after adversity.
3. Separate candidate resource association (Y~R), statistical moderation (Y~E+R+E*R with contrast and CI on appropriate scale), mediation (hypothesized E→M→Y with temporal ordering and causal assumptions), and differential exposure (R associated with E). Main effect favorable at all stress levels may be promotive without buffering; significance in one subgroup not another is not interaction. Interaction scale matters; a logistic coefficient does not imply constant risk-difference modification. Observational conditioning on exposure may introduce selection if exposure is affected by resource/prior symptoms; define estimand and DAG, no automatic adjustment. Repeated diary association not intervention effect. 
4. Candidate alternatives: workload, health/sleep, socioeconomic access, prior distress, sensory sensitivity, social support, task fit, 2e and compensation. Separate predisposition, context, consequence and maintenance; no evidence of IQ as single explanation. Compare simpler exposure/context model to added response mechanism and preserve counterexamples. If actual observations do not discriminate, remain provisional; do not engineer stress, withdraw support or elicit unnecessary history.
5. Application: assess stress and recovery in context, keep useful supports, distinguish output from effort and cost. Persistent/severe symptoms, impairment or safety concerns warrant appropriate qualified assessment regardless of ability. No treatment or diagnosis based on a resilience score. GFT-14.3 and integration are relevant to sustainable compensation; final GFT-15 integration must inspect actual GFT-14 closure. INT-02/03/GFT-19 govern updating and provenance.
## Internal check — wholly fictional new tasks
A. A fictional ferry dispatcher reports more stressful events than colleagues but shorter mood disruption. Analyst attributes resilience to high cognitive test score. Distinguish load, severity, mood recovery and work assignment; no individual causal trait inference. New: colleagues handle different routes, and shorter reactions occur only when another dispatcher takes over paperwork. Revise to context/support and differential exposure; no general resilience claim, preserve reported mood pattern. Comparable ordinary shifts and outcome measures only if useful. No induced stress.
B. A fictional costume workshop member maintains output after a major deadline change. Analyst equates output with resistance and absence of distress. New: the record shows reduced discretionary activities and prolonged fatigue after completion, with output restored by assistance. Separate functioning, symptoms, cost and recovery; revise narrow success to assisted short-term output, sustainability unknown. No hidden diagnosis; appropriate evaluation if persistent distress/functional deterioration. Preserve real achievement and support, avoid withdrawal experiment.
C. Fictional adult education cohort: support associated with lower distress both at low and high workload; analyst calls it a specific buffer. Need interaction contrast/CI, common measurement and time. New: no clear interaction and supported participants experience fewer disruptions; mediation or differential exposure hypotheses, not proof of either. Preserve main association; null interaction not equivalence, separate causal mechanisms and individual transfer. No claim high IQ moderates.
