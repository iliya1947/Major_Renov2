# BIO-05 — Acid–base physiology and buffering

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-19**  
Область: 05 — Bioenergetics / Exercise Metabolism

## 0. Core decision

Exercise-induced acidification is real, local, compartment-specific and physiologically relevant.

Project rule:

**lactate is not “the acid,” and exercise acidosis is not a one-metabolite or one-reaction phenomenon.**

The working model integrates:

- ATP turnover;
- strong-ion / weak-acid / PCO2 effects;
- intracellular buffering;
- extracellular buffering;
- membrane transport;
- oxidative and respiratory handling.

---

## 1. Compartments are distinct

Keep separate:

- muscle intracellular pHi;
- interstitial pH;
- venous blood pH;
- arterial/systemic blood pH.

Project rule:

**blood pH or blood lactate cannot be substituted numerically for local muscle pHi.**

---

## 2. H+ accumulation during intense exercise

High-intensity exercise can substantially lower muscle pHi.

Magnitude depends on:

- intensity;
- duration;
- recruited fibres;
- muscle group;
- substrate/energetic state;
- perfusion;
- transport;
- starting acid–base state.

Project rule:

**there is no one universal pH response or failure threshold.**

---

## 3. Sources of exercise acidification

Reject:

`glycolysis → lactic acid dissociation → H+`

as a complete mechanism.

Biochemical proton bookkeeping includes:

- ATP hydrolysis;
- PCr/CK;
- glycolytic reactions;
- LDH;
- oxidative metabolism;
- CO2/HCO3− chemistry.

Physicochemical analysis additionally considers:

- PCO2;
- strong-ion difference;
- non-volatile weak acids.

Project rule:

**exercise acidification is an integrated system state; no single biochemical reaction is retained as the universal sole cause.**

---

## 4. Lactate is not H+

The LDH reaction in the lactate-forming direction consumes H+ in stoichiometric notation.

Lactate concentration can rise alongside acidosis without being the proton concentration itself.

Lactate can still matter through:

- H+-coupled transport;
- strong-ion effects;
- metabolite signaling;
- correlation with glycolytic state.

Project rule:

**lactate ≠ H+, but lactate is not physiologically irrelevant to acid–base state.**

---

## 5. Intracellular buffering

Major contributors include:

- proteins/histidyl groups;
- phosphate species;
- carnosine;
- bicarbonate/CO2 chemistry;
- phosphagen/creatine-linked proton handling;
- other weak acid/base groups.

Project rule:

**buffering limits pH change; it is not the same as proton efflux.**

Carnosine is one contributor, not “the buffer.”

---

## 6. Extracellular buffering

Relevant extracellular mechanisms include:

- bicarbonate/CO2;
- hemoglobin/plasma proteins;
- phosphate/weak buffers;
- perfusion/distribution;
- ventilation and CO2 elimination.

Project rule:

**extracellular buffering supports systemic pH control and transmembrane gradients but is not a passive waste dump.**

---

## 7. Bicarbonate system

Core system:

`CO2 + H2O ↔ H2CO3 ↔ H+ + HCO3−`.

Bicarbonate is a major extracellular acid–base mechanism.

But:

**bicarbonate ≠ the whole acid–base system.**

Human alkalosis experiments show it can attenuate muscle acidification and improve some high-intensity tasks, but not universally.

Supplement dosing/timing belongs NUT.

---

## 8. H+ equivalent transport

Skeletal-muscle pH regulation uses multiple pathways:

- MCT lactate/H+ cotransport;
- Na+/H+ exchange (NHE1);
- Na+/HCO3− cotransport;
- carbonic-anhydrase-assisted CO2/HCO3− handling.

Project rule:

**no single transporter is the universal H+ clearance pathway or rate limiter.**

---

## 9. pH recovery

Recovery pH kinetics depend on:

- end-exercise pH;
- PCr resynthesis;
- oxidative metabolism;
- perfusion;
- transport;
- lactate/H+ gradients;
- ventilation;
- task/muscle.

Project rule:

**pH recovery ≠ lactate disappearance and does not define one universal rest interval.**

---

## 10. Acid-base state and fatigue

Reject both extremes:

- “H+ causes all fatigue”;
- “acidosis is irrelevant.”

Current synthesis supports:

- moderate acidosis may have limited direct force/power effect;
- more severe intracellular acidosis can reduce force, shortening velocity and power;
- fatigue also reflects Pi/H2PO4−, K+, Ca2+ handling, glycogen, ROS and other factors.

Project rule:

**severe acidosis can contribute to fatigue but is neither necessary nor sufficient to explain all task failure.**

---

## 11. H+ and phosphate interaction

H+ changes phosphate speciation.

Acid–base disturbance and phosphate-related fatigue chemistry interact at:

- cross-bridges;
- Ca2+ sensitivity;
- myosin ATPase;
- excitation–contraction coupling.

Detailed integrated fatigue belongs BIO-11.

---

## 12. Exercise “burn”

Direct human metabolite-infusion evidence supports combined sensory effects of:

- H+;
- lactate;
- ATP;

rather than lactate alone.

Group III/IV afferents contribute to sensory and cardiorespiratory responses.

Project rule:

**exercise burn/discomfort is not a lactate sensor.**

---

## 13. Blood measures vs muscle state

Blood lactate, pH and bicarbonate are useful contextual measures.

They do not directly specify:

- local pHi;
- fibre-specific pH;
- H+ production;
- muscle buffer occupancy;
- local transporter flux.

Project rule:

**systemic measurements ≠ direct local muscle chemistry.**

---

## 14. Resistance-training interpretation

For resistance exercise, use:

`high ATP turnover + PCr/glycolytic flux`
→
`Pi / ion / acid-base disturbance`
+
`buffering / transport / perfusion`
→
`task-specific force/power/perception changes`.

Do not infer:

- exact rest from lactate;
- exact rest from one pH curve;
- complete recovery from normalized pH;
- muscle pHi from blood lactate.

Exact work:rest prescription remains BIO-12/PRO.

---

## 15. Canonical non-equivalences

1. pH ≠ linear H+ quantity.
2. muscle pHi ≠ interstitial pH.
3. muscle pHi ≠ blood pH.
4. lactate ≠ H+.
5. lactate formation ≠ direct lactic-acid dissociation.
6. lactate production ≠ proton-production rate.
7. ATP-hydrolysis equation ≠ complete net cellular proton balance.
8. intracellular buffering ≠ proton efflux.
9. proton efflux ≠ lactate disappearance.
10. bicarbonate ≠ total acid–base regulation.
11. blood HCO3− ≠ muscle buffer capacity.
12. MCT ≠ total H+ transport.
13. lower pH ≠ sole cause of fatigue.
14. normal blood pH ≠ normal pHi in all active fibres.
15. exercise burn ≠ lactate concentration.
16. pH recovery ≠ complete metabolic recovery.
17. one pH value ≠ universal task-failure threshold.
18. extracellular alkalosis ≠ guaranteed performance improvement.

## 17. Confidence

### HIGH

- local exercise acidification is real;
- compartments are non-equivalent;
- lactate ≠ H+;
- lactate formation is not direct lactic-acid dissociation;
- buffering ≠ efflux;
- bicarbonate is a major extracellular mechanism;
- MCT/NHE/bicarbonate transport contribute to pH regulation;
- pH recovery ≠ lactate disappearance;
- severe acidosis can contribute to fatigue;
- fatigue is multifactorial;
- exercise burn is not caused by lactate alone;
- no universal critical pH or pH-recovery time is retained.

### MODERATE-HIGH

- buffer capacity can adapt;
- carnosine materially contributes to intracellular buffering;
- fibre-type differences create pH heterogeneity;
- extracellular alkalosis can improve some high-intensity tasks.

### MODERATE / CONTEXT DEPENDENT

- exact fractional contribution of individual biochemical vs physicochemical causes;
- exact transporter shares in vivo;
- exact H+ vs Pi/K+/Ca2+ causal partition in task failure;
- exact individual carnosine contribution to total buffering.

### NOT RETAINED AS UNIVERSAL

- lactate = acid;
- lactate production = proton-production rate;
- blood lactate = muscle pH;
- H+ = sole fatigue cause;
- acidosis = irrelevant;
- bicarbonate = universal ergogenic solution;
- MCT = only proton pathway;
- burn = lactate;
- pH recovery = lactate clearance;
- one pH threshold = universal failure point.
