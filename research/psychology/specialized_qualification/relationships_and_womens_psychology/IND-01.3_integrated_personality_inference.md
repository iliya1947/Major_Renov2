## 1. Purpose and evidence boundaries

Integrate the prior context-expression and longitudinal-change notes into a reusable method; challenge the method with published empirical findings not generated as practice exercises. The target is construct separation, conditional description and disciplined inference about an adult individual's development. No private records, personal case material or individualized hypotheses used. Not a systematic literature review or a validated assessment instrument. No claim of source access beyond the portions actually inspected.

The critical question: can apparently stable behavior, changing behavior and changing circumstances be separated without presuming that a broad personality trait, state, motive or life event is its cause? No fixed number of observations or time interval establishes the answer.

### A. Momentary variability and durable development

Beckmann, N., Birney, D. P., Minbashian, A., & Beckmann, J. F. (2021). Personality dynamics at work: The effects of form, time, and context of variability. European Journal of Personality. https://doi.org/10.1177/08902070211017341
Access: publisher HTML abstract, introduction, design passages, Table 11, selected results, discussion, limitations and conclusion directly inspected. Not full article end-to-end. Exploratory (not preregistered) study of 346 Australian professionals with intensive field and structured-task state reports, conventional trait assessments at baseline and after two years. Indices of situational contingencies and relative state variability showed some stability, but links to longer-term trait change were limited. Authors reported only one small association after controlling for their selected predictors. Trait measurements at two time points, scale differences, selected facets, varying available observations and multiple exploratory comparisons limit inference.

External-data exercise A1 (published aggregate Table 11, not personal records): For situation-contingent conscientiousness, wave 1-to-5 r=.230 and wave 4-to-5 r=-.140; mean cross-wave r=.17. For situation-contingent neuroticism mean cross-wave r=.29. The relevant claim is modest between-person stability of estimated contingencies, not invariant behavior or permanence of an individual slope. Variation across wave pairs does not by itself establish individual developmental change. Table Ns vary (50–341 for contingencies).
A2: For relative variability of neurotic states, the article reports a small negative association with conventional trait change (standardized beta=-.23, p<.001). This is not a universal predictive mechanism and does not establish that deliberately increasing state fluctuation would produce durable change. Conscientiousness and other variability operationalizations did not uniformly reproduce it. Reject the stronger state-variability-to-trait-change shortcut. The integrated rule should separate level, fluctuation, conditional response, temporal persistence and mechanism.

### B. Convergence of self and informant reports

Oltmanns, J. R., Jackson, J. J., & Oltmanns, T. F. (2020 issue; online 2019). Personality Change: Longitudinal Self–Other Agreement and Convergence With Retrospective-Reports. JPSP 118, 1065–1079. https://doi.org/10.1037/pspp0000238 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30843725/
Access: indexed abstract and institutional publication summary; direct PubMed/PMC opening blocked by browser check. Abstract reports substantial longitudinal self–informant agreement in five domains in older adults, weaker cross-method links involving retrospective ratings. Treat as an abstract-level finding, not independently verified detailed estimates.

Nguyen, P. L. L., Syed, M., & DeYoung, C. G. (2023 issue; online 2022). Four types of change and self-other agreement on change in personality traits during college years: A multi-informant longitudinal study. Journal of Personality 91, 441–463. https://doi.org/10.1111/jopy.12740 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35686939/
Access: directly inspected PubMed abstract and figure captions, indexed full-text excerpts; not full text end-to-end. Baseline N=259 college attendees, mean age 18.79. Limited group mean change despite individual variation; no significant convergence of self- and informant-rated change reported. Different populations, timeframes, instruments and change estimators may matter; no direct causal explanation of the cross-study discrepancy established.
External-data exercise B: Agreement on baseline levels can coexist with nonconvergence of change scores, and disagreement does not identify which rater is wrong. Conversely agreement is not automatic proof of a causal account. Retain reporting channel and observation opportunities separately; compare the same constructs over comparable times. Do not discard discordant evidence or mechanically average distinct perspectives.

## 3. Operational protocol: person × situation × time

Use the following sequence, not a personality checklist applied indiscriminately:

1. Purpose: specify the actual question, scope and intended use; separate descriptive pattern from explanation, prediction and desired outcome.
2. Observation: record actor, action, timing, relevant context, opportunity, channel and gaps. Distinguish behavior, reported experience, informant report, context fact and analyst hypothesis. Absence of a recorded action is not evidence of absence without adequate observation and opportunity.
3. Construct: decide whether the claim concerns a broad trait, narrower tendency, state, goal, appraisal, conditional pattern, or developmental change. A trait label cannot serve as an identified mechanism.
4. Compare at two scales: (i) comparable contexts at different times; (ii) different contexts within comparable time periods. Describe both central tendencies and variability; distinguish opportunity, situation contact and construal. A generalization outside sampled contexts is a hypothesis.
5. Model alternatives: measurement/selection artifact; state fluctuation; changed opportunities/context composition; stable context-conditioned pattern; durable change; pre-existing trajectory; combinations. Rank alternatives by actual differential evidence, not merely narrative plausibility. Do not force exclusivity.
6. Development: separate change in observed behavior from change in a stable conditional pattern, broad trait, self-concept or inferred mechanism. Retain pre-event trend, other transitions, duration, persistence, and comparison quality. Group-level estimates provide a qualified prior only. No deterministic age or life-event inference.
7. Contradictions: check same-person and cross-context data, reporting method, missingness and evidence dependence. If a direct contradiction changes a premise, revise dependent conclusions; do not defend a preferred narrative.
8. Prediction: use conditional and prospectively testable predictions, with uncertainty, rival forecasts and disconfirming observations. Do not manufacture numeric probabilities. Evaluate predictions when new observations become available; retrospective fitting is not prospective validation. No coercion, hidden tests or manufactured distress.
9. Output: narrowest supported conclusion, observed scope, confidence limits, alternatives, and revision condition. Distinguish what is plausible from what is supported. No formal diagnosis or clinical authorization.

Conceptual model (not fitted or mechanistic): observed behavior is a time-dependent outcome of person, situation, state, appraisal, goals, affordance and prior history, with measurement error. Some components are latent and interacting. State distributions can be stable while individual observations vary; conditional response patterns can change while aggregate trait scores remain similar; the same measured shift may arise through different pathways. Model observables and conditions rather than inferring one latent explanation by default.
