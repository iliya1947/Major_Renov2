Question: how to integrate stable personality tendencies, context-conditioned states, motivation, appraisal and affect with typed individual hypotheses, observable forecasts and evidence revision? ## 2. External empirical challenges

Not a systematic review. Three new independent publications examined to the indicated depth. Clinical research below is used only as methodological evidence about modeling and validation, not as evidence about any private individual or clinical authority.

E1. Fechtelpeter et al. Computational network models for forecasting and control of mental health trajectories in digital applications. npj Digital Medicine 9, 70 (2026 issue; published 2025-12-30). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-025-02252-3
Access: publisher HTML abstract, selected introduction, results, figure captions, discussion and methods directly inspected; not complete end-to-end. Three 40-day studies included 145 participants (46/48/51). First sample used for tuning; subsequent two for evaluation. Models retrained chronologically. Nonlinear PLRNN forecasts had MAE .831/.795 in the two evaluation samples, outperforming tested VAR models. Simple persistence and mean baselines sometimes beat linear dynamic models. Error increased with horizon. Effective network edges and simulated perturbations are model-dependent, not automatically identified causal relations. Training data were dense structured reports, unlike sparse natural narratives; selection, missing data, intervention context and particular model classes limit transfer.

E2. Schreynemackers et al. Individual-Level Modeling of Depressive Symptom Severity Using Smartphone and Wearable Data: Time-Aware 1-Year Study With Feature-Group Contributions. JMIR Formative Research (2026-08-25). https://doi.org/10.2196/99204 ; https://formative.jmir.org/2026/1/e99204/
Access: indexed primary abstract, discussion and limitations, PubMed bibliographic record; direct page opening failed. Time-separated evaluations of 11 participants with sufficient data; linear and nonlinear models each improved over personal mean baseline in 8 of 11, but not the same sets (reported Wilson CI 43–90%). No universally superior model or feature group. Small selected sample, many predictors, changing contexts, missingness and overfitting risk constrain interpretation. Feature importance does not identify causation. No passive tracking or individualized mental-state inference is authorized by this finding.

E3. Frumkin, Piccirillo, Beck, Grossman & Rodebaugh. Feasibility and utility of idiographic models in the clinic: a pilot study. Psychotherapy Research 31(4), 520–534 (2021 issue; online 2020-08-24). https://doi.org/10.1080/10503307.2020.1805133 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32838671/
Access: indexed primary abstract, article excerpts and institutional publication summary; direct PubMed/PMC/publisher access blocked. Twelve clients completed repeated assessments five times daily over three weeks; fitted individual network structures differed from clients' own predicted networks. This challenges treating intuitive formulations as already verified, while neither fitted networks nor self-reports establish a unique generating mechanism. Small pilot, model assumptions, clinical setting and potential misunderstanding of prediction instructions limit transfer. Not independent evidence of clinical efficacy or predictive superiority.

External-data challenge: E1 rejects a universal preference for simple models (some nonlinear models performed better), while E2 rejects automatic preference for complexity. E3 distinguishes subjective explanatory expectations from fitted associations. The findings support matching model class and validation to the actual question, not a choice of one universally correct framework. Results were visible before analysis; this is retrospective comparison, not a blinded or independent assessment. No raw data reanalysis or full-text audit. Prior IND-03.1/.2 numerical and source limitations remain open.

## 3. Integrated operational procedure

Use the minimal relevant depth, escalating on consequential, contested or weakly supported claims. Do not apply a long checklist mechanically to every observation.

1. PURPOSE: identify the actual question and target outcome, actors, temporal horizon, stakes and appropriate use. Separate understanding, prediction, decision and desired outcome. Do not substitute an intermediate indicator for a target goal.
2. EVIDENCE: preserve direct observations and reports, context, time, opportunity, missingness, provenance and episode/source/method dependencies. Never count the same underlying event as independent evidence merely because it has been retold. Distinguish observed facts, reported experience and analyst interpretation.
3. CLAIM LEVEL: separate stable trait, current state, conditional pattern, developmental change, goal value, relative priority, regulatory purpose, affect, intention, behavior, functional association, prediction and causal mechanism. Use atomic propositions rather than a fused personality narrative.
4. INTEGRATION: relate person, situation and time (IND-01) to goals, expectancy, constraints, appraisals, emotion and regulation (IND-02). Track reciprocal or delayed changes and competing goals where relevant. This is a conceptual hypothesis map, not an identified universal causal sequence. Emotional relief is not necessarily goal completion; actions and feelings do not by themselves establish motives or consent.
5. ALTERNATIVES: retain simple/context-only and measurement explanations alongside richer hypotheses. A theory-supported mechanism is plausible, not individually established. Compare actual discriminating evidence, including contrary observations and contextual boundary conditions. Avoid forcing compatible processes into mutually exclusive alternatives. Never infer hidden desire from lack of action or interpret every contradictory outcome as confirmation.
6. FORECAST: specify a measurable observation, horizon, context/opportunity requirements, rival predictions, outcome adjudication and comparator when possible. Freeze the version before new evidence, not after. If rival predictions coincide, that observation does not discriminate them. Prefer ordinary consensual observations and voluntary clarification; no covert tests, engineered distress or manipulation.
7. VALIDATION: where sufficient data exist, compare simple, shared, partially pooled and person-specific models on the relevant level and held-out time window. Report individual performance, uncertainty, exclusions and missing outcomes. Do not equate in-sample fit, group-level accuracy or random-split classification with same-person future prediction. With sparse qualitative evidence, use calibrated verbal uncertainty and bounded conditional forecasts; no invented probabilities.
8. REVISION: update the smallest affected claim, retain historical predictions, and propagate changes only along real dependency paths. Distinguish correction of source evidence, mismatch, out-of-scope and unresolvable outcome. Do not defend a preferred theory by moving its criterion after observation. Allow NO UPDATE, RETAIN, STRENGTHEN, WEAKEN, SPLIT, MERGE, REVISE RELATION, RETIRE or ADD NEW HYPOTHESIS under INT-02.
9. OUTPUT: provide the strongest supported conclusion in its actual scope, leading alternatives where material, uncertainty, and revision condition. Keep confidence distinct from action permission. Respect explicit decisions and boundaries regardless of hypothetical internal motives. No formal diagnosis or professional authorization.

Compact route: purpose -> observation/provenance -> typed proposition -> alternatives -> context/time -> conditional prediction -> review -> dependent revision -> bounded conclusion.

## 4. Integration exercise and regression review

Original fictional workshop setting, unrelated to any private record. No completed real-person forms. A participant consistently performs an optional spatial puzzle when materials are accessible. Later participation falls during sessions with unfamiliar tools, and the participant reports both curiosity and frustration. A facilitator restores familiar tools, after which participation resumes.

Analysis: a context-conditioned descriptive pattern is supported within the fictional observations. Curiosity and frustration are self-reported experience, not a trait, commitment or proof of internal causal conflict. Competing explanations include changing task affordance, skill demands, expectancy, transient state, goal priority and changing interest. Resumption after restored tools is compatible with opportunity/expectancy effects, but does not identify either as causal; other contemporaneous changes and sequence effects are possible. A broad trait or durable change claim is unsupported.

Prospective discrimination (illustrative only): define comparable later sessions, accessible tools and an observable participation criterion before the outcome. A persistent decline across accessible comparable sessions would weaken a simple unfamiliar-tools-only account; resumption alone cannot distinguish that account from several rivals. Unknown access or missing recording is unresolvable, not a failure. Preserve prior hypotheses; revise only supported propositions and dependent claims when the source or outcome changes. No claim of actual follow-up, experiment, forecast accuracy or independent scoring.

Earlier IND-03.2 executable synthetic check tested duplicate exclusion, invalidation and independent later evidence. It is inherited as a bookkeeping regression check, not psychological validation. IND-03.1 synthetic pooled-score example illustrates a level-of-analysis error, not predictive success. No new numerical pass count assigned.
