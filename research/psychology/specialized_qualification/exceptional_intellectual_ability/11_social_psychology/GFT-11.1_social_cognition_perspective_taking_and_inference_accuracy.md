## Operational gate
Identify observable behavior and source, candidate state/trait/role or environmental explanations, available cues and actual uncertainty. Separate inference of belief, feeling, intention, factual knowledge and stable preference; behavior may follow constraints rather than desire. Compare at least two plausible explanations when evidence underdetermines, specify discriminating observations (including contradictory evidence). Direct voluntary clarification, target report and later observed action can improve information but each has limitations; respect refusal and privacy, no intrusive testing or assumption of deception. Never equate coherence, confidence, similarity or group consensus with accuracy. Record predicted event/outcome before feedback and assess repeated observable predictions, bias and calibration separately from verbosity; no invented numerical probability or individual IQ from behavior. Separate perceiver, target, relationship, task, cue availability and occasion. Social-cognitive task scores, knowledge, motivation to use it, actual interaction and relationship outcomes are distinct. Do not apply clinical tests as self-diagnosis or use score cutoffs absent appropriate validation. High intelligence can facilitate processing available information but cannot substitute for missing information; not a universal deficit or superiority. 

## Internal assessment (wholly invented cases)
A. Fictional community pottery instructor notices a member repeatedly declines group demonstrations, infers disdain based on accurate past prediction of technical preferences. Rival models: prior skill, schedule, hearing conditions, embarrassment or dislike of group activity. Predict differing choices if timing and format change; voluntary direct clarification can test reported reason. New: member attends quiet evening demonstrations, reports daytime work conflict and difficulty hearing. Withdraw disdain inference, retain observation of declines, distinguish contextual explanation from proven causal mechanism. No personality/IQ estimate.
B. Fictional museum volunteers rate their perspective-taking highly and accurately choose emotion words from photographs but disagree on a visitor's intent. Compare ambiguous stimuli, verbal/contextual information, criterion uncertainty and confidence; forecast observable next step without claiming private mental access. New: visitor explains request was for accessibility information; prior facial interpretation consistent with several feelings. Revise intent inference, not erase emotion test performance; self-rating and actual accuracy distinct. No global ability classification.
C. Fictional educational study finds a selected high-score group excels on a verbal story mentalizing task but not on a video task; instructor attributes null to ceiling and asserts universal hidden advantage. Compare item distributions, language, task demands, selection, precision and validity; ceiling is a measurement hypothesis, not proof of superiority. New: challenging alternate stories retain group contrast, nonverbal interactive measure has broad distribution but overlapping scores and target-dependent effects. Preserve verbal-task association; withdraw universal advantage, no equivalence or causal conclusion. Require independent representative sampling and meaningful criterion.
