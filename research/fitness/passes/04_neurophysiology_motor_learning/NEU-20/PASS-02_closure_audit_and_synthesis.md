## 3. Arousal / stress / anxiety / pressure taxonomy audit

PASS.

The 2026 ESSA position statement operationalizes performance under pressure as a context in which performing well becomes especially important and explicitly distinguishes pressure responses from one another.

For project purposes:

- arousal = activation state;
- stress = demand/resource/appraisal process;
- anxiety = affective/cognitive/somatic response;
- pressure = performance-importance context.

These can covary but are not interchangeable.

Canonical rule:

**name the construct manipulated/measured before interpreting a performance effect.**

No blocker.

---

## 4. Universal inverted-U audit

PASS — UNIVERSAL LAW REJECTED.

PMID 38277436 provides strong evidence for an inverted-U relation between pupil-indexed spontaneous arousal and performance across several perceptual-decision tasks.

But the paper itself concerns sensory/perceptual decision processing, not all motor performance.

Strength-specific evidence points in another direction:

PMID 41628393:

- 200 competitive strength athletes;
- psyching-up increased deadlift velocity from ~0.34 to ~0.39 m/s;
- reported improvement ≈ 18.58%;
- predicted 1RM increase ≈ 4.3%.

Therefore:

**task class matters.**

Arousal that degrades precision/decision performance can support high-force execution.

Canonical rule:

**do not prescribe a universal “moderate arousal” target; estimate performance response in the actual task.**

No blocker.

---

## 5. Pressure response audit

PASS — PRESSURE CAN FACILITATE, PRESERVE OR IMPAIR.

ESSA 2026 explicitly distinguishes:

- choking;
- clutch/facilitative performance.

Pressure is therefore a context, not a guaranteed impairment.

This is consistent with expert-sport studies in which pressure increased anxiety/effort without necessarily degrading output.

Canonical rule:

**pressure response must be observed, not assumed.**

No blocker.

---

## 6. Attentional narrowing audit

PASS WITH REFRAMING.

The phrase “attentional narrowing” is too crude if used as a universal deficit.

PMID 40566819:

- six studies;
- total n = 1,590;
- experimentally narrowing visual attention increased effort and running pace in several conditions.

Thus narrower attentional scope can be adaptive.

Pressure-performance literature instead supports a broader model:

- self-focused overcontrol;
- task-irrelevant distraction/worry;
- reduced relevant environmental sampling;
- task-focused selective attention.

Canonical rule:

**the important variable is whether attention covers the information required for the task, not whether scope is globally narrow or broad.**

No blocker.

## 7. Coordination-under-stress audit

PASS — REAL EFFECT, NO UNIVERSAL MOTOR SIGNATURE.

PMID 20409012 showed pressure-associated changes in:

- anxiety/effort;
- HR/HRV;
- muscle activity;
- clubhead acceleration;
- putting performance.

PMID 42151265:

- pressure and sensory uncertainty increased anxiety/failure appraisal;
- altered shooting performance;
- changed kinematics;
- changed prefrontal oxygenation.

PMID 39547475 linked pressure-related failures in a reaching task to cerebellar/internal-model-related activity.

Together these show:

**pressure can reorganize motor execution.**

They do not support a single stress-specific kinematic pattern.

No blocker.

## 8. Choking definition audit

PASS — ORDINARY UNDERPERFORMANCE MUST BE SEPARATED.

The 2026 Delphi study:

- 23 choking experts;
- 91% consensus;
- choking requires a consequential performance decrease under a situation of perceived importance;
- the decrease should fall outside expected performance variability;
- motivation to perform well is part of the construct.

Therefore:

**one miss, slow rep or technical deviation is not automatically choking.**

Magnitude/context/baseline matter.

No blocker.

---

## 9. Choking mechanism audit

PASS — HETEROGENEITY CONFIRMED.

Current literature supports several candidate mechanisms:

- overarousal;
- self-focus / explicit monitoring / reinvestment;
- distraction / attentional-control failure;
- predictive/internal-model modulation.

The 2026 review explicitly organizes these as competing/complementary accounts.

The ESSA statement likewise treats self-focus and distraction as broad attentional families.

Canonical rule:

**poor performance under pressure does not identify the mechanism.**

No blocker.

---

## 10. Automaticity / expertise audit

PASS — MODIFIER, NOT IMMUNITY.

PMID 15260208:

- novice golfers benefited from skill-focused/accuracy conditions;
- experts performed better when conditions reduced explicit step-by-step control.

This supports proceduralization/automaticity effects.

But current pressure literature includes choking among skilled performers, and facilitative pressure responses among experts.

Therefore:

**automaticity changes the cost of conscious control but does not eliminate pressure susceptibility.**

No blocker.

---

## 11. Pressure-training audit

PASS WITH TARGET-SPECIFIC LIMIT.

PMID 38376935:

- n = 84;
- 300 golf putts across two training days;
- mild-pressure training preserved performance across later low/mild/high-pressure tests;
- control deteriorated under pressure;
- measured mental effort/reinvestment changed, but neither clearly mediated the performance effect.

The earlier pressure-training meta-analysis:

- 14 studies;
- n = 394;
- pooled effect after outlier removal g ≈ 0.67;
- low heterogeneity;
- studies spanned sport and law-enforcement domains.

ESSA 2026 recommends pressure training as a context-specific tool, not indiscriminate stress exposure.

Canonical rule:

**pressure robustness can be trained, but pressure exposure should be purposeful, progressive and linked to a real performance requirement.**

No blocker.

---

## 12. Counter-choking intervention audit

PASS — AVERAGE BENEFIT, NO UNIVERSAL TREATMENT.

2026 meta-analysis PMID 41951087:

- 21 studies;
- random-effects synthesis;
- motor accuracy g ≈ 0.660;
- executional success g ≈ 0.444;
- target deviation g ≈ 0.301;
- closed-skill effect ≈ 0.455;
- open-skill effect smaller/less robust after publication-bias adjustment.

Interventions included multiple distinct strategies.

Therefore:

**interventions can improve pressure performance on average, but no single method is universally superior and open-skill generalizability is weaker.**

Individualized intervention design remains PSY/COA scope.

No blocker.

---

## 13. Strength-specific psyching-up audit

PASS WITH ACUTE-PERFORMANCE BOUNDARY.

PMID 41628393 directly supports acute benefit of self-selected psyching-up for deadlift bar velocity in experienced strength athletes.

However:

- free-choice strategy vs passive control;
- no isolated causal arousal dose;
- no long-term learning outcome;
- no technical-retention test;
- no evidence of one superior psyching-up category.

Canonical rule:

**psyching-up can support acute high-force performance; it is not evidence that higher arousal improves motor learning or technique.**

No blocker.

---

## 14. Performance decrement vs learning-loss audit

PASS — CORE PROJECT BOUNDARY.

A pressured athlete can:

1. possess a learned skill;
2. show a state-dependent performance decrement;
3. recover performance when pressure is removed.

That is not loss of learning.

Learning loss would require evidence such as:

- impaired delayed retention outside pressure;
- persistent deterioration across baseline contexts;
- altered relearning not attributable to current state.

Canonical rule:

**retest outside the transient pressure state before inferring that a skill was lost.**

No blocker.

---

## 15. Pressure robustness ≠ retention

PASS.

Pressure robustness and skill retention are separate dimensions.

A skill can be:

- well retained but pressure fragile;
- less retained but temporarily expressed well under activation;
- robust under one pressure context but not another.

Therefore:

**pressure-specific transfer must be tested separately from ordinary retention.**

No blocker.

## 18. Measurement non-equivalence audit

PASS.

Separate:

### Arousal
Physiological activation.

### Anxiety
Subjective/cognitive/somatic state.

### Pressure
Performance-importance context.

### Stress appraisal
Demand-resource interpretation.

### Choking
Consequential pressure-related performance decrement outside expected variation.

### Clutch/facilitative response
Successful goal-related performance under pressure.

### Pressure robustness
Ability to preserve relevant performance under target pressure.

### Skill retention
Delayed capability outside transient state effects.

Project rule:

**do not infer one construct from another marker alone.**
