## 3. VO2max audit

PASS.

BIO-09 remains authoritative.

VO2max is the maximal attainable whole-body oxygen uptake under defined test conditions and integrates:

- pulmonary function;
- cardiac output;
- arterial O2 content;
- perfusion/distribution;
- extraction;
- mitochondrial use.

Canonical rule:

**VO2max is an integrated oxygen-cascade capacity, not one-organ or one-organelle function.**

No blocker.

---

## 4. VO2peak audit

PASS.

VO2peak remains:

**highest VO2 observed in a specific test.**

It is not automatically confirmed VO2max because:

- mode;
- protocol;
- local fatigue;
- effort;
- test termination;
- maximality interpretation

can alter the observed peak.

Detailed plateau/verification/maximality criteria remain TST.

No blocker.

## 5. VO2max vs endurance-performance audit

PASS AFTER PERFORMANCE-WORDING TIGHTENING.

PMID **42071093**:

- 888 individuals;
- runners and cyclists;
- heterogeneous from recreational to world class;
- speed/power at LT/LTP correlated strongly with VO2max and inversely with economy;
- fractional-utilization variables contributed much less in that heterogeneous dataset.

This supports:

**VO2max is important but not sufficient for endurance performance.**

It does not prove:

- VO2max + economy explain 95% of actual race performance in every athlete;
- the regression partition is causal;
- durability/technical/environmental factors are irrelevant.

Canonical rule:

**VO2max is an important upper-capacity determinant, not a complete performance score.**

No blocker.

## 6. Fractional utilization audit

PASS AFTER DOWNGRADING.

PMID **42686133** reviewed 35 studies and found only a minority supported a substantial independent relationship between fractional utilization and endurance performance.

Limitations included:

- heterogeneous VO2max methods;
- heterogeneous LT definitions;
- heterogeneous performance endpoints;
- limited trainability evidence in trained athletes.

Canonical wording:

**fractional utilization remains a useful descriptive concept, but current evidence does not support treating it as a universally strong independent determinant or a standalone training target.**

Confidence retained as **MODERATE-HIGH boundary**, not universal mechanism.

No blocker.

- descriptive/predictive evidence;

## 9. LT vs VT audit

PASS WITH AGREEMENT BOUNDARY.

PMID **41699863**:

- 21 trained male adults;
- 4-min stepwise cycling;
- substantial disagreement for lower-threshold surrogates;
- stronger but imperfect agreement at higher thresholds;
- wide limits of agreement remained.

This does not imply LT and VT never agree.

Canonical rule:

**LT and VT can approximate related transitions, but individual interchangeability must be demonstrated rather than assumed.**

No blocker.

---

## 10. CP vs MLSS audit

PASS WITH NO CORRECTION FACTOR.

PMID **40124604**:

- 10 studies;
- CP power averaged 12.42 W above MLSS;
- pooled limits of agreement roughly -19 to +44 W;
- substantial interindividual variation;
- moderators influenced differences.

Therefore reject:

- “MLSS = CP - 12 W”;
- “CP and MLSS are the same threshold.”

Canonical rule:

**CP and MLSS are related heavy–severe-boundary constructs but are not interchangeable at the individual level.**

No blocker.

## 11. LT vs critical speed audit

PMID **42120287**:

- 31 trained to world-class runners;
- critical speed was faster than two lactate-derived threshold estimates;
- wide individual limits of agreement;
- differences did not disappear with athlete caliber.

Canonical rule:

**laboratory LT and field CS cannot be substituted numerically without validation.**

No blocker.

## 13. Economy definition audit

PASS.

Exercise economy is retained as:

**submaximal oxygen/energy cost for a specified external task.**

It must specify:

- mode;
- speed/power;
- grade/surface;
- environmental/equipment context;
- physiological state.

Canonical rule:

**economy is a task-context cost, not a context-free athlete trait.**

No blocker.

---

## 14. Oxygen cost vs energy cost audit

PASS AFTER WORDING TIGHTENING.

Oxygen cost is commonly used as a practical economy measure.

Energy cost can differ because:

- caloric equivalent of O2 varies with substrate mix/RER;
- steady-state assumptions matter;
- measurement and normalization matter.

Canonical rule:

**VO2-based economy and energetic cost are closely related but not numerically identical constructs under all conditions.**

No blocker.

---

## 15. Economy vs efficiency audit

PASS.

Economy asks:

- how much physiological/metabolic cost is required for a task.

Mechanical efficiency asks:

- mechanical output divided by metabolic input.

Canonical rule:

**economy ≠ mechanical efficiency.**

No blocker.

## 17. Treadmill vs overground audit

## 18. Strength-training economy audit

PASS WITH HETEROGENEITY BOUNDARY.

PMID **40153564**:

- umbrella review of 17 systematic reviews;
- generally supports improved running economy with strength training in trained endurance athletes;
- confidence in many included reviews was low/critically low;
- VO2max generally did not increase.

PMID **40016936**:

- well-trained male runners;
- improved economy durability after 90-min run and high-intensity TTE.

PMID **42413900**:

- 25 recreational runners;
- 8-week resistance training;
- no significant level/uphill economy improvement.

Canonical rule:

**strength training can improve economy in some endurance contexts, but the effect is not universal and can occur independently of VO2max change.**

No blocker.

## 33. Adaptation vs limitation audit

PASS.

“Adaptation” asks:

- what changed with training.

“Limitation” asks:

- what currently constrains maximal/performance output.

The same component can adapt without being the sole rate limiter.

Canonical rule:

**central/peripheral adaptation ≠ central/peripheral rate limitation.**

No blocker.

## 43. Closure decision

**BIO-15 = CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0.**

Meaning:

1. VO2max is integrated maximal whole-body oxygen uptake, not a complete endurance-performance score;
2. VO2peak is the highest observed value in a test and is not automatically confirmed VO2max;
3. endurance performance depends on more than VO2max, including economy, task specificity and durability;
4. fractional utilization is not retained as a universally strong independent determinant;
5. LT/VT/GET/RCP/CP/CS/MLSS are method-defined related constructs rather than one interchangeable threshold;
6. CP and MLSS are not interchangeable at individual level and no fixed correction is retained;
7. economy is submaximal task cost and is distinct from VO2max and mechanical efficiency;
8. economy depends on speed/workload, mode, environment, equipment and fatigue state;
9. the 1% treadmill rule is not universally valid;
10. strength training can improve running economy in some endurance contexts but not universally;
11. mitochondrial content/function are distinct constructs;
12. ET/HIT/SIT can all increase mitochondrial content;
13. mitochondrial adaptation is important but not sufficient to explain VO2max/performance;
14. capillarization is trainable but metric dependent;
15. capillary density alone does not define total microvascular adaptation;
16. central and peripheral adaptations can coexist;
17. adaptation and current rate limitation are different questions;
18. the dominant VO2max limiter depends on population, mode, environment and training state;
19. elite-athlete oxygen-delivery limitation is not generalized to all populations;
20. oxidative metabolism is mechanistically relevant to resistance-set recovery;
21. whole-body VO2max is not retained as a strong universal predictor of set-to-set resistance recovery;
22. local oxidative/PCr-recovery physiology and whole-body VO2max are non-equivalent;
23. exact testing remains TST;
24. exact programming remains PRO.
