## 2. Corrections and substantive extensions

R1 — Formation conditional probability, denominator and measurement. Direct Stinson Table3 sample Ns [60,92,191,243,298,336,677] sum1897; percentages [40,65.2,70.7,72.8,70.5,62.2,70.6] imply rounded counts [24,60,135,177,210,209,478], total1293/1897=.6816 consistent with weighted mean68.2%. Model-based median .66 is different estimand: logistic(.66)=.6593; transformed CI logits .38/.95 give .5939/.7211. I2=86%, substantial sample heterogeneity. Not probability of romance among friendships; denominator conditional on reported prior/current romance. For forward transition probability require prospective at-risk friendship cohort and observation horizon; selection, relationship definitions, both actors' preferences, competing endings and censoring. Friendship not explicitly defined, authors acknowledge retrospective reconstruction. Study4 N298,210 friends-first respondents, 209 valid intention responses; 147/209=.7033 reported neither initially became friends for romantic interest (single respondent's retrospective report about both, not independently verified partner intention);25/209 and37/209 reported own/partner romantic motives. Among reported friends-first histories median12 months, mean21.90 SD28.23, range1–180 months: no estimated waiting time for friendship to turn romantic. Table6 counts sum293 rather than stated298; 139/293=47.44%, consistent with reported47.4%; missing five responses reason not stated in inspected passage. Table5 and6 separate outcomes and denominators. Study4 sample N298 and friends-first count210 exactly match Study3 Sample5; do not count as independent replication without confirming lineage. No data-sharing consent as article states. Root cause: original denominator warning sound but incomplete distinctions among pooled estimands, motivation and waiting time. Retest direct HTML and arithmetic.

R2 — Persistence, felt constraints and shared study infrastructure. Rhoades N1184,873/1184=.7373 continued,311/1184=.2627 ended over8 months; naive majority continuation accuracy73.7% in sample is not validated forecast for future people. Distinguish event rate, conditional odds associations, discrimination and calibration. More material/perceived constraints and less felt constraint independently associated with continuation in model; does not imply increasing barriers is beneficial or identify safety/quality/motives. Kelmer N870 long-distance vs close-proximity, same grant R01HD047564 as Rhoades confirmed from indexed primary records; likely shared project, exact respondent overlap unknown. Do not add N or count as independent sample replication. Null observed breakup contrast is not equivalence; baseline anticipated probability not actual event. Tran correlations are dependent constructs, no additive independent causal formula; earlier Le/Agnew, Tran and dissolution meta-analysis may share studies, no exact matching. Maintenance correlations and duration are separate outcomes, potential reverse direction and selection. Root cause: sample-level baseline and dependency omitted. Retest indexed source counts, grant metadata and construct map; no causal advice to increase constraints.

R3 — Quality levels, change, and dissolution predictions. Joel baseline actor relationship-variable R2 45% vs partner-report15%; 18% at later level is not percent correct or ability to predict improvement. Authors analyze 21 models per dataset per outcome group (7 per baseline/follow-up/change); sample sizes differ by analysis. One dataset, Supporting Healthy Marriages, contributed6298/11196=.5625 of total couples; a single large cohort cannot be treated as independent replications per couple or as 43 equally representative populations. Full weighting, out-of-bag implementation, person/dyad grouping and independent transport not checked, so do not claim leakage or externally calibrated prediction. Additional primary evidence Uhlich et al. (2026-05-30) https://doi.org/10.1177/08902070261455278: indexed publisher abstract (direct timeout), Study1 N1281 US/Canada dating sample, logistic regression and random forest balanced accuracy64%; Study2 N6947 German sample tracked10 years, random forest71%. This qualifies universal impossibility of breakup prediction, not contradiction of weak quality-change prediction. Balanced accuracy=(sensitivity+specificity)/2 for binary outcome, not observed success fraction, calibrated personal probability, or superiority against all meaningful baselines. Exact holdout procedure, predictor timestamps, calibration, class prevalence, missingness, comparison intervals and cross-sample transport not verified. Further preregistered Bühler & Orth 2025 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40111896/ original abstract and institution record, four longitudinal studies: model-estimated terminal decline onset .58–2.30 years before separation. Alignment to known future separation is retrospective and conditions on outcome; not prospective detection of irreversible tipping point, deadline, or proof decline causes breakup. Need prospective risk-set sampling and held-out event prediction. Root cause: broaden prior forecasting limits with recent endpoint-specific evidence and selection. Retest indexed source results; route predictive validation details to SYS-03. No private forecast.

R4 — Love measurement scope and poor model fit. Sorokowski final N7332 from >11000 respondents; no exact initial denominator, attrition reason counts or representative sampling established. Scalar invariance as reported concerns included countries and instrument/constraints, not general validity for all relationship forms, proof of three causal modules or developmental sequence. Braida direct CFA results CFI=.76,TLI=.75,RMSEA=.10,SRMR=.08; poor fit against authors' thresholds .90/.90/.08/.06. Removing exclusivity wording, splitting passion into erotic/romantic factors and other alternative models did not provide adequate fit; reduced model only somewhat better; EFA no clearly interpretable factor structure. Thus not simply a few exclusivity items; no verified replacement scale. Partner X vs Y differ in duration, daily contact and primary labels, so cannot assign partner-specific scores to relationship structure alone; selective recruitment and reports about multiple partners dependent. Sample includes ages16–17, not adult-only evidence. Distinguish scale validity, latent structure and theoretical construct. Root cause: original note acknowledged counterexample but omitted concrete fit and failed alternative models. Retest direct Results and methods; no universal love classification or exclusive definition.

R5 — Responsiveness and appraisal are distinct, effects not interchangeable across context. Laurenceau 1998 event-contingent lead now located at original abstract level; 2005 dyadic daily model 96 couples/42 days, up to8064 person-days before missingness (not actual count). Neither design establishes that disclosure causes intimacy through responsiveness. Projection study supports perceiver-side contribution; partner's own report not objective benchmark. Birnbaum initial-acquaintance vs established-partner studies use different protocols, measured outcomes and participants; cannot claim a directly tested causal stage interaction from their juxtaposition. Gender effects need direct interaction and equivalent measurements; absence of significance in women not proof opposite or zero effect. Impett and Shoikhedbrod distinguish responsiveness motivation (trait/day measures and specific models not reverified) from self-neglect and controlled reasons, not an obligation to meet another's needs. Prekatsounaki assigned interactions do not validate observed actor/partner associations as causal intervention contrasts; exact assignment effects remain unverified, do not imply responsiveness always generates attraction. Root cause: strengthen measurement/mediation and cross-study comparability; earlier notes already retain many constraints, not a new causal theory. Retest source abstracts and evidence-channel separation. Respect explicit preferences and consent independently.

R6 — Discrepancy computation, outcome and shared lineage. Jodouin absolute difference D=|A-B| confirmed, 35-day and12-month datasets of same research participants are not independent replication across 229+229 couples. Subgroup nulls not equivalence; absolute difference hides direction and common level. Fischer perceived discrepancy defined by absolute difference between respondent's own and respondent-estimated partner interest (two five-point items), not a direct distress rating. Separately computed discrepancy uses own reports of both partners, not objective verified desire. Source indexed methods wording for 'actual' score is ambiguous (absolute values of each partner's score subtracted); do not assert exact absolute-difference transform without full code/method verification. Pattern of significance unchanged after adjustment for perceived emotional intimacy; do not imply computed discrepancy became nonsignificant because of adjustment. Perceived discrepancy relates to satisfaction, not necessarily distress or causal mechanism. Country counts218+207+135+117=677. Additional review https://academic.oup.com/smr/article/12/4/611/7686406 explicitly identifies earlier Fischer 2695-person study and 677-dyad analysis as same study; not independent evidence. Response-surface method: A,B and their polynomial terms, agreement line A=B and disagreement line A=-B on common centered scale can assess level/direction; a squared/absolute difference alone imposes restrictive symmetric dependence. Algebra check: when A=B=x, quadratic outcome f=b0+(b1+b2)x+(b3+b4+b5)x²; when A=-B=x, f=b0+(b1-b2)x+(b3-b4+b5)x². This is mathematical specification, not empirical effect or practical test. ESSM statements are opinion-based due to limited evidence; discrepancy not diagnosis or duty to conform. Root cause: add operational measurement distinctions and algebra, qualify adjustment and same-study dependence. Retest indexed primary text and mathematical identity.

R7 — Temporal effects, conditioning and simultaneous changes. Park, already reviewed WOM-03, cannot count as independent replication. Four annual waves, model-specific within-person residual prediction; prior direct Table3 comparison of directions p=.046 retained at original verified scope, not rechecked table today. Change of significance on partner path when frequency included is not evidence of mediation; no causal arrow. McNulty 207 marriages/8 waves and Zhao 287 individuals/12-day reports demonstrate different lagged estimands; compare within/between separation, relationship stage, measurement, common causes, sample selection. Byers specifically reports concurrent changes in both satisfaction variables despite limited evidence for directional hypotheses; do not describe as evidence of no relationship between them. Park indexed discussion uses causal-sounding wording, design remains observational; not a causal conclusion. Time-scale explanation remains plausible hypothesis, not identified. Add design requirement for distinguishing temporal accounts: comparable outcome definitions, multiscale repeated observations, prespecified lags, actor/partner separation, measurement validity, competing directions and sensitivity to time-varying common causes; predictive comparison to baseline only where possible. Root cause: precise contemporaneous-vs-directional distinction and source causal language. Retest primary indexed abstracts and preserve WOM-03 restrictions.

## 4. Revised integrated method
Define precise actor, recipient, relationship form, time, outcome, and channel. Distinguish general desire, target-specific attraction/affection, intimacy, provider response behavior, recipient perception, sexual motivation/desire, satisfaction, dedication, alternatives, constraints, consent and mutual choice. Never reverse conditional probabilities, infer motives from persistence alone, or interpret aggregate romantic pathways as a plan for inducing someone's feelings. Actual mutual agreement and stated choices remain authoritative for action, not theories about hidden feelings. Use appropriate measurement, dependent data handling and conditional transfer. For predicted outcome distinguish future level, change, actual event and timing, model information available at prediction time, comparator, calibration, coverage and uncertainty. Avoid declaring retrospective alignment to later outcome prospective forecast. For dyadic discrepancies retain each actor's level and appraisal, direction, time, actual experience and distress; no diagnosis or prescribed obligation. Keep candidate mechanisms and disconfirming evidence; no pressure, covert testing, manipulation or interference with freedom to leave. No clinical qualification.
