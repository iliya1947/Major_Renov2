# PHY-05 — Acute response to resistance exercise

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-18**  
Область: 03 — Physiology

## 0. Decision

Создать модель acute resistance-exercise response, достаточную для ответа на вопрос:

> **какие acute responses полезны для понимания, но не являются surrogate for gains?**

и для downstream chronic adaptation, hypertrophy, strength, fatigue, programming, monitoring and recovery без:

- pump = growth;
- lactate = growth;
- hormone spike = anabolic score;
- cytokine spike = chronic inflammation/growth;
- EMG = adaptation;
- soreness/CK = session quality;
- one-marker recovery;
- acute response = chronic adaptation.

## 1. Force decline

Force-producing capacity can decline during and after a fatiguing resistance-training set/session.

Major determinants include:

- set duration;
- proximity to failure;
- total volume;
- density/rest;
- relative load.

Project rule:

**force decline is an acute fatigue/performance signal, not a hypertrophy score.**

## 2. Velocity and power decline

As fatigue accumulates under maximal intended velocity:

- repetition velocity usually falls;
- power falls;
- force capacity can also fall.

Project rule:

**velocity loss can operationalize within-set fatigue under standardized conditions, but does not by itself quantify future gain.**

Detailed VBT prescription → PRO/TST.

## 3. Local metabolic disturbance

Resistance exercise can acutely alter:

- phosphocreatine availability;
- glycolytic flux;
- lactate;
- acid-base state;
- Pi/ADP-related state;
- substrate turnover.

Project rule:

**metabolic disturbance describes energetic demand/fatigue context; more metabolic stress is not automatically more hypertrophy.**

## 4. Blood flow / hyperemia

Contracting skeletal muscle shows dynamic perfusion responses involving:

- mechanical vascular compression;
- rapid vasodilation;
- post-contraction hyperemia;
- metabolite/endothelial/neural control;
- rest-interval effects.

Project rule:

**blood flow is a physiological state variable, not a direct chronic hypertrophy predictor.**

## 5. Acute swelling / pump

Muscle size/thickness can increase immediately after exercise due to:

- fluid shifts;
- edema/swelling;
- vascular/interstitial changes.

Project rule:

**acute swelling is not immediate tissue accretion.**

Post-workout morphology can therefore be contaminated by swelling.

## 6. Cell-swelling hypertrophy boundary

A correlation between early swelling and later hypertrophy does not establish:

- causality;
- necessity;
- sufficiency;
- surrogate validity.

Current evidence does not support using the pump/cell swelling as an established hypertrophy-driving mechanism.

Project rule:

**pump ≠ validated growth mechanism.**

## 7. Acute systemic hormones

Resistance exercise can transiently change:

- testosterone;
- GH;
- cortisol;
- catecholamine-related responses;
- other endocrine signals depending on context.

Project rule:

**acute endocrine response describes the bout; it is not an acute “anabolic score.”**

## 8. Hormone-spike surrogate boundary

The magnitude of transient postexercise systemic GH/testosterone response is not a validated predictor of later hypertrophy or strength gains.

Project rule:

**do not select training programs to maximize post-workout hormone spikes.**

Chronic endocrine status is a separate physiological question.

## 9. Acute cytokine / myokine response

Resistance exercise can transiently change circulating signaling molecules including:

- IL-6;
- IL-1ra;
- IL-15;
- TNF-α.

Project rule:

**acute cytokine increase is not equivalent to chronic pathological inflammation.**

It is also not a validated hypertrophy score.

## 10. Neuromuscular-function change

Fatiguing resistance exercise can acutely change:

- contractile function;
- excitation–contraction behavior;
- afferent/perceptual fatigue;
- voluntary activation;
- motor-unit behavior;
- force/velocity/power output.

Project rule:

**neuromuscular fatigue is multidimensional; surface EMG amplitude alone is insufficient.**

Detailed central/peripheral mechanisms → NEU / PHY-10.

## 11. Protocol dependence

Acute response is shaped by the entire session dose:

- load;
- repetitions;
- proximity to failure;
- sets;
- volume;
- density;
- rest intervals;
- velocity-loss threshold.

Project rule:

**the exercise name alone does not define the acute physiological response.**

## 12. Postexercise recovery

Recovery means restoration of the relevant target function/marker over time.

Possible domains:

- maximal force;
- submaximal velocity;
- power;
- soreness;
- CK;
- perceived readiness;
- metabolic/endocrine state.

Project rule:

**recovery is outcome-specific.**

## 13. One-marker recovery error

No single variable proves complete recovery.

Examples:

- low soreness;
- normalized lactate;
- low CK;
- normalized hormones;
- normal bar velocity;
- good subjective readiness.

Project rule:

**different systems recover asynchronously; one normalized marker ≠ fully recovered organism.**

## 14. Acute response vs chronic adaptation

### Acute
Minutes to days:

- fatigue;
- metabolites;
- blood flow;
- swelling;
- hormones;
- cytokines;
- soreness;
- temporary performance changes.

### Chronic
Retained changes after repeated exposure:

- hypertrophy;
- strength;
- power;
- architecture;
- metabolic remodeling;
- connective-tissue adaptation.

Project rule:

**acute response magnitude ≠ chronic adaptation magnitude unless predictive validity is independently established.**

## 15. Why acute markers are still useful

Acute markers can be useful for their valid domains.

### Velocity / power
Useful for:
- within-set fatigue;
- effort/density comparison;
- monitoring.

### Metabolites
Useful for:
- energetic/glycolytic demand.

### Blood flow / oxygenation
Useful for:
- perfusion state.

### Swelling
Useful for:
- acute fluid response;
- detecting contaminated morphology measurement.

### Hormones
Useful for:
- endocrine physiology.

### Cytokines
Useful for:
- immune/signaling physiology.

### Force / neuromuscular tests
Useful for:
- fatigue/recovery state.

Project rule:

**use a marker for the construct it validly measures, not for an unvalidated downstream outcome.**

## 16. Surrogate-failure examples

### Lactate
Higher lactate can accompany similar chronic hypertrophy/strength.

### GH
Higher acute GH can occur without superior chronic strength/CSA.

### Swelling
Acute pump can occur without evidence that swelling itself causes hypertrophy.

### Soreness / CK
Repeated-bout protection reduces damage markers without proving reduced training efficacy.

Project rule:

**bigger disturbance ≠ bigger gain.**

## 17. Integrated acute-response model

`mechanical work + neural drive → force/velocity/power decline + metabolic disturbance + perfusion changes + transient swelling/endocrine/immune responses + neuromuscular fatigue → multidimensional recovery`

Repeated exposure can contribute to chronic adaptation.

But the valid inference is not:

`bigger acute response → bigger chronic adaptation`.

## 18. Training-relevant implications

1. **Velocity loss can quantify fatigue, not hypertrophy.**
2. **More lactate is not automatically more growth.**
3. **More blood flow is not automatically more growth.**
4. **Pump is not immediate hypertrophy.**
5. **Post-workout GH/testosterone spikes should not drive program design.**
6. **Acute cytokine changes are not automatically harmful or anabolic.**
7. **EMG alone is not a complete fatigue/recruitment metric.**
8. **Closer-to-failure/high-volume/high-density work generally increases acute fatigue.**
9. **Recovery must be defined by the outcome being monitored.**
10. **One marker cannot certify global recovery.**
11. **Acute physiology informs session management but does not replace longitudinal outcome evidence.**

## 19. What PHY-05 can establish

PHY-05 establishes:

1. force/velocity/power acute decline;

3. blood-flow/hyperemia response;

5. endocrine response;

## 21. Current non-blocking uncertainties

1. Exact causal role of individual metabolites.
2. Exact causal role of cell swelling.
3. Exact predictive value of individual cytokine patterns.
4. Exact chronic relevance of specific acute molecular signals.
5. Exact recovery time for a specific person/session.
6. Exact chronic outcome associated with a specific velocity-loss threshold.

Project rule:

**absence of a universal acute gain biomarker is retained as an evidence-based constraint, not filled with heuristics.**

## 22. Canonical project rules

1. Acute response ≠ chronic adaptation.
2. Force decline = fatigue/performance information.
3. Velocity loss = fatigue information, not gain score.
4. Metabolic stress ≠ automatic hypertrophy.
5. Blood flow ≠ automatic hypertrophy.
6. Pump/swelling ≠ immediate growth.
7. Acute GH/testosterone ≠ chronic-gain predictor.
8. Cytokine response ≠ chronic pathological inflammation.
9. EMG alone ≠ complete neuromuscular state.
10. Recovery is multidimensional.
11. One normalized marker ≠ complete recovery.
12. Biological plausibility ≠ surrogate validation.
13. Bigger acute disturbance ≠ bigger gain.

## 23. Evidence / confidence

### HIGH
- acute force/velocity/power impairment;
- protocol dependence of fatigue;
- contraction-related hyperemia;
- transient endocrine responses;
- transient myokine/cytokine responses;
- swelling as an acute phenomenon;
- multidimensional recovery;
- acute ≠ chronic distinction.

### MODERATE-HIGH
- rejection of pump/systemic-hormone/metabolic-stress magnitude as stand-alone hypertrophy surrogates.

### MODERATE / CONTEXT-DEPENDENT
- exact causal role of specific metabolites/signals;
- exact individual recovery time;
- exact chronic implications of a given velocity-loss threshold.
