## 2. Bony pelvis / pelvic ring

Bony pelvis comprises:

- right os coxae;
- left os coxae;
- sacrum;
- coccyx.

Each os coxae is formed by fusion of:

1. ilium;
2. ischium;
3. pubis.

These converge at the acetabulum.

Pelvic ring closure:

- anterior → pubic symphysis;
- posterior → paired sacroiliac joints.

Useful load-transfer map:

`spine → sacrum → SI interfaces / ilia → acetabula → femora`

Project rule:

**pelvis is a load-transmitting ring, not two independent hip sockets floating under the trunk.**

Exact load sharing → MEC.

## 4. Sacroiliac joints

SI joints connect sacrum and ilia and are major axial→pelvic load-transfer interfaces.

Anatomically they combine:

- anterior synovial/cartilaginous articulation;
- strong posterior/interosseous ligamentous complex.

Project rule:

**normal SIJ is highly constrained but not fused and not motionless.**

### Current 2026 dynamic evidence

A 2026 biplane-radiography study in 10 healthy young adults quantified small but measurable multi-planar SIJ motion during gait.

Reported average ranges in that sample were on the order of:

- ~1–3 mm translations;
- ~1.5–2° rotations.

These values are **sample/task-specific**, not universal normative cutoffs.

Project conclusion:

**“small motion” is accurate; “zero motion” is not.**

Exact SIJ mechanics → MEC.

---

## 5. SIJ normal variation

Current 2024–2025 reviews emphasize substantial:

- interindividual variation;
- right-left variation;
- accessory facets / dysmorphic shapes;
- imaging appearances that can mimic pathology.

Project rule:

**SIJ asymmetry or variant morphology alone does not diagnose dysfunction.**

This is especially important before later posture/pelvic-assessment work.

---

## 6. Pubic symphysis

Pubic symphysis:

- secondary cartilaginous joint;
- fibrocartilaginous interpubic disc;
- completes anterior pelvic ring;
- allows small deformation/movement.

It is not a freely mobile synovial joint.

## 7. Hip joint

Femoral head ↔ acetabulum.

Type:
- synovial ball-and-socket;
- multiaxial;
- relatively deep/congruent compared with GH joint.

Movements:

1. flexion/extension;
2. abduction/adduction;
3. internal/external rotation;
4. circumduction.

Project rule:

**hip combines substantial mobility with strong osseous/capsulolabral stability.**

“Stable joint” does not mean low ROM.

---

## 8. Acetabulum

Formed by:
- ilium;
- ischium;
- pubis.

Core structures:

- lunate articular surface;
- acetabular fossa;
- acetabular notch;
- transverse acetabular ligament;
- labrum.

The acetabulum covers a substantial portion of femoral head and contributes major osseous stability.

---

## 9. Acetabular labrum

Labrum = fibrocartilaginous/fibrocartilage-rich rim attached around acetabular margin.

Broad structural roles:

1. deepens effective socket;
2. expands contact region;
3. contributes to fluid/suction-seal behavior;
4. contributes to stability/load distribution.

Project rule:

**labrum is part of integrated hip stability, not merely a passive rim.**

Pathology/tear significance → MED.

## 10. Femoral head / neck / proximal femur

Relevant structures:

- femoral head;
- fovea capitis;
- neck;
- greater trochanter;
- lesser trochanter;
- intertrochanteric line;
- intertrochanteric crest;
- gluteal tuberosity.

Femoral neck geometry creates space and lever-arm relationships between pelvis and shaft.

Exact neck-shaft angle/version mechanics → MEC/IND.

## 11. Hip capsule

Hip capsule = dense fibrous capsular structure surrounding femoral head-neck/acetabular articulation.

Current 2025–2026 reviews emphasize an integrated capsuloligamentous system including:

- iliofemoral ligament;
- pubofemoral ligament;
- ischiofemoral ligament;
- circumferential **zona orbicularis** fibers.

Project rule:

**hip capsule ≠ three isolated ligament straps.**

Its restraint is position-dependent and integrated.

---

## 12. Major capsular ligaments

### Iliofemoral

Strong anterior capsular structure.

### Pubofemoral

Anteroinferior capsular structure.

### Ischiofemoral

Posterior capsular structure.

These spiral around the joint and become tensioned differently across combined hip positions.

Project rule:

**do not assign each ligament one single “stops X movement” action as if tension were position-invariant.**

Detailed strain maps → MEC.

---

## 13. Zona orbicularis

Zona orbicularis = circumferential capsular fibers around femoral neck.

Current capsule reviews include it as part of the integrated stabilizing anatomy.

Project relevance:

- helps avoid a simplistic three-ligament-only capsule model.

Exact mechanical contribution → MEC.

---

## 14. Ligament of head of femur

Ligamentum teres connects femoral fovea to acetabular/transverse-ligament region.

It has:
- vascular relevance, especially earlier in life;
- sensory/stability roles depending context.

Project rule:

**it is not the primary adult static stabilizer compared with osseous + capsulolabral systems.**

## 15. Hip stability model

### Static contributors

- bony morphology/congruence;
- labrum;
- capsule;
- iliofemoral/pubofemoral/ischiofemoral ligaments;
- zona orbicularis;
- ligamentum teres as a secondary contributor.

### Dynamic contributors

- periarticular muscles.

Current 2026 consensus/reviews on instability likewise preserve the broad triad:

**bone + capsulolabral soft tissue + muscle.**

Project uses this only as a structural model, not a diagnostic framework.

## 16. Femoral version — normal variability

Current 2025 scoping review shows that reported “normal” femoral version varies materially with:

- measurement method;
- reference-axis definition;
- population.

Different methods generated materially different mean/reference values.

Project rule:

**there is no single method-independent femoral-version number that defines normal anatomy for every person.**

Consequences for:
- comfortable rotation;
- stance;
- apparent foot/knee orientation

must be evaluated individually and biomechanically.

---

## 17. Acetabular orientation — normal variability

Current large 3D datasets of asymptomatic individuals show meaningful variability in:

- sagittal orientation;
- version;
- coverage;
- sex-related morphology.

Project rule:

**acetabular shape/orientation is continuous and variable, not a binary atlas-normal vs abnormal distinction.**

This strengthens the legacy rule:

**one universal squat stance cannot be derived from generic pelvis anatomy.**

Personal morphology cannot be inferred reliably from appearance alone.

---

## 18. Gluteus maximus

Broad origin includes:

- posterior ilium;
- sacral/coccygeal region;
- sacrotuberous/connective structures;
- gluteal fascia-related attachments.

Broad role:

- powerful hip extension;
- external-rotation contribution;
- task-dependent pelvic/trunk control.

Innervation:
- inferior gluteal nerve.

### 2025 structural re-evaluation

A cadaveric study of 25 Japanese donors challenged the common simplified insertion description that the larger superior portion primarily inserts into the IT tract.

It found:

- superior ~three-quarters formed a strong plate-like distal tendon;
- that tendon attached predominantly to the femoral gluteal-tuberosity region;
- IT-tract connections were present but described as partial adhesions/accessory connections;
- inferior portion had a more complex relationship with lateral intermuscular septum / distal tendon / vastus-lateralis proximal tendon.

Project conclusion:

**do not encode “most gluteus maximus inserts into IT band” as settled universal anatomy.**

Because this is one cadaveric population, exact generalization remains cautious.

---

## 19. Gluteus medius

Origin:
- lateral/external ilium between gluteal lines.

Insertion:
- greater trochanter via regionally distributed tendon attachments.

Innervation:
- superior gluteal nerve.

Broad roles:

- hip abduction;
- frontal-plane pelvis–femur control;
- angle/fascicle-dependent rotational and flexion/extension contributions.

Project rule:

**gluteus medius ≠ one simple “side-glute abductor”.**

---

## 20. Gluteus minimus

Deep to medius.

Origin:
- external ilium.

Insertion:
- anterior/lateral greater-trochanter region.

Innervation:
- superior gluteal nerve.

Broad roles:

- abduction;
- pelvic stabilization;
- angle/fascicle-dependent internal-rotation/flexion contributions.

Gluteus medius/minimus are fan-shaped systems with regional lines of action.

---

## 21. Gluteal / ITB continuity nuance

IT tract remains a thickened lateral specialization of fascia lata with muscular/fascial connections including:

- TFL;
- gluteal tissues;
- deep fascia.

Current 2025–2026 anatomy shows that exact gluteal→ITB connections are more nuanced than a single “muscle inserts into band” cartoon.

A 2026 study specifically investigated a gluteus-medius connection to the midportion ITB.

Project rule:

**use the ITB as a regional fascial transmission structure; do not reduce gluteal insertion anatomy to one simplistic line.**

---

## 22. Iliopsoas complex

Current 2026 NCBI source describes the iliopsoas musculotendinous unit as including:

- psoas major;
- iliacus;
- psoas minor in the broader complex description.

Important distinction:

- psoas major and iliacus converge toward lesser-trochanter/femoral insertion;
- psoas minor, when present, does not share the same femoral insertion and is anatomically variable.

Innervation differs:

- psoas major → direct lumbar-plexus branches;
- iliacus → femoral nerve.

---

## 23. Iliopsoas functions / boundary

Primary hip function:
- flexion.

Also:
- external-rotation contribution;
- lumbopelvic/hip stabilization depending fixed segment.

Psoas major links lumbar spine and femur.

Project rule:

**“tight psoas” is not an anatomy-based diagnosis for arbitrary posture or pain.**

Exact position-specific function → MEC.

---

## 24. Rectus femoris / sartorius / pectineus boundary

### Rectus femoris

- crosses hip + knee;
- proximal AIIS/supra-acetabular attachment;
- hip flexion + knee extension roles.

Detailed quadriceps → ANA-09.

### Sartorius

- ASIS → pes anserinus;
- crosses hip + knee;
- hip flexion/abduction/external-rotation tendency;
- knee flexion/rotation contribution.

### Pectineus

- pubic/pectineal region → proximal femur;
- hip adduction/flexion with position-dependent rotational contribution;
- innervation can vary.

These are retained because multiarticular geometry matters later.

## 25. Adductor group

Project-relevant adductor system:

1. adductor longus;
2. adductor brevis;
3. adductor magnus;
4. gracilis;
5. pectineus functionally;
6. obturator externus as an adduction/rotation contributor.

Primary group label:
- hip adduction.

But group functions are broader and angle-dependent.

Project rule:

**“adductors = inner-thigh adduction only” is incomplete.**

---

## 26. Adductor magnus — current anatomical update

Traditional useful division:

- adductor portion;
- hamstring portion.

This remains operationally useful because:
- origins/insertions differ;
- innervation differs;
- hamstring portion has major hip-extension role.

### 2026 cadaveric refinement

A study of 9 formalin-embalmed Japanese donors consistently distinguished four structural components:

1. adductor minimus;
2. superior adductor part;
3. inferior adductor part;
4. hamstring part.

It also described more complex insertion/tendinous architecture and interindividual variation than classic simplified maps.

Project conclusion:

**two-part functional model remains useful, but exact adductor-magnus internal anatomy is more complex.**

Because sample size was small/cadaveric:
- do not convert the four-part description into a universal exercise-program taxonomy.

---

## 27. Gracilis

Origin:
- pubic body/inferior ramus.

Insertion:
- pes anserinus.

Crosses:
- hip;
- knee.

Broad roles:
- hip adduction;
- knee flexion;
- medial leg rotation when knee flexed.

Innervation:
- obturator nerve.

---

## 28. Hamstring complex

Current 2026 reference:

1. biceps femoris long head;
2. biceps femoris short head;
3. semitendinosus;
4. semimembranosus.

True hip-crossing hamstrings:

- biceps femoris long head;
- semitendinosus;
- semimembranosus.

Biceps femoris short head:
- originates on femur;
- does **not** cross hip.

Project rule:

**“hamstrings are all biarticular” is false.**

---

## 29. Hamstring proximal/distal anatomy

### Proximally

- BF long head + semitendinosus have close/conjoint proximal tendon-aponeurotic relationships;
- semimembranosus has distinct proximal architecture;
- BF short head originates from femur.

### Distally

- biceps femoris → fibular/lateral-knee system;
- semitendinosus → pes anserinus;
- semimembranosus → posteromedial proximal tibia with expansions.

### Current variation note

2025 cadaveric work on semimembranosus demonstrates meaningful proximal-tendon morphological variation.

Project rule:

**hamstring tendon maps are modal, not identical in every individual.**

---

## 30. Hamstring innervation / function

Most hip-crossing hamstrings:
- tibial division of sciatic nerve.

BF short head:
- common fibular division.

Broad functions of biarticular hamstrings:

- hip extension;
- knee flexion;
- knee-rotation contribution when flexed.

Hip flexion + knee extension lengthens them across both joints.

This is structural groundwork for later hinge/leg-curl mechanics.

## 31. Deep external rotators

Classical six:

1. piriformis;
2. obturator internus;
3. superior gemellus;
4. inferior gemellus;
5. obturator externus;
6. quadratus femoris.

Broad roles:

- external rotation, especially near neutral/extension;
- femoral-head / posterior hip stabilization;
- some abduction/adduction contributions depending hip position.

Project rule:

**deep rotators are a group, but not six identical actuators.**

---

## 32. Gemelli / obturator-internus system — current update

Current 2026 NCBI anatomy:

- superior gemellus and inferior gemellus are distinct muscles;
- they converge with obturator internus into a shared insertional/conjoined tendon system;
- origins and innervation differ.

Current 2026 cadaveric work also documents substantial morphology variants/classifications.

Project rule:

**shared insertion/functional synergy does not erase distinct muscle identity.**

Variation alone ≠ pathology.

---

## 33. Piriformis

Origin:
- anterior sacral/pelvic region.

Insertion:
- greater-trochanter region.

Broad action changes with hip position:

- external-rotation contribution in extension/neutral;
- abduction contribution in flexion.

Project implication:

**muscle action labels are joint-angle dependent.**

No “piriformis syndrome” diagnosis belongs here.

---

## 34. Obturator externus / quadratus femoris

### Obturator externus

- external obturator region → trochanteric fossa;
- obturator nerve;
- external-rotation / adduction / stability contribution.

### Quadratus femoris

- ischial region → intertrochanteric crest/quadrate-tubercle region;
- nerve to quadratus femoris;
- external-rotation / adduction / stabilization contribution.

Exact force role → MEC.

---

## 35. TFL / fascia lata / IT tract

### TFL

Origin:
- ASIS/anterior iliac-crest region.

Blends into:
- ITB/fascia lata.

Innervation:
- superior gluteal nerve.

Broad roles:
- hip flexion;
- abduction;
- internal rotation;
- tensioning lateral fascial system;
- pelvic control.

### IT tract

- longitudinal thickened lateral fascia lata;
- connective tissue, not muscle;
- distal attachment prominently includes Gerdy’s tubercle, with additional lateral-knee fascial connections.

Project rule:

**ITB does not independently contract.**

Claims about “stretching the IT band” require mechanics evidence.

---

## 36. Current ITB nuance

Recent 2025–2026 work emphasizes:

- ITB as complex connective-tissue structure;
- variable tension across functional tasks;
- proximal relationships more complex than only “TFL + glute max insert into one band”.

Project conclusion:

**retain ITB as a force-transmitting fascial structure without pretending its exact in-vivo tension/function is fully described by static anatomy.**

Detailed mechanics → MEC.

---

## 37. Multiarticular muscles

Relevant hip-crossing multiarticular muscles:

- rectus femoris;
- sartorius;
- gracilis;
- biceps femoris long head;
- semitendinosus;
- semimembranosus.

Their length depends on combinations of:
- hip angle;
- knee angle.

Project consequence:

exercise positions can create materially different muscle-tendon geometry.

Anatomy alone does not establish hypertrophy superiority.

---

## 38. Hip flexors / extensors are distributed systems

### Hip flexion contributors

- iliopsoas;
- rectus femoris;
- sartorius;
- TFL;
- pectineus;
- adductors depending angle.

### Hip extension contributors

- gluteus maximus;
- hamstrings;
- adductor magnus hamstring/posterior portions;
- other muscles depending position.

Project rule:

**do not reduce hip flexion to “the psoas” or extension to “the glutes”.**

Relative contribution → MEC.

---

## 39. Hip abduction / adduction / rotation are angle-dependent

Abduction contributors can include:

- gluteus medius;
- gluteus minimus;
- TFL;
- regionally gluteus maximus;
- deep rotators in flexed positions.

Adduction contributors include:

- adductor group;
- gracilis;
- pectineus;
- position-dependent deep structures.

Rotation action can change with joint angle.

Project rule:

**one muscle action label is a summary, not a constant line-of-action law across ROM.**

---

## 40. Single-leg pelvis–femur control

During single-leg support, stance-side hip abductors contribute to frontal-plane pelvis–femur control.

This is a broad functional anatomy statement.

Project rule:

**visible pelvic drop does not by itself diagnose “weak glute med”.**

Potential contributors include:
- anatomy;
- load;
- pain;
- motor strategy;
- strength/endurance;
- measurement method.

Diagnosis/testing → MED/TST.

---

## 41. Pelvic tilt terminology

Pelvis can move relative to trunk/femur via:

- anterior/posterior tilt;
- lateral tilt;
- axial rotation.

These are segment motions, not identical to hip-joint motion.

Example:

posterior pelvic tilt during deep hip flexion can coexist with ongoing femoroacetabular flexion.

Technique relevance → MEC/TEC.

---

## 42. Lumbopelvic coordination

Trunk bending / hinging distributes motion between:

- lumbar spine;
- pelvis relative to femora;
- hip joints.

Project rule:

**there is no universal fixed lumbopelvic gear ratio.**

This parallels scapulohumeral coordination:
dynamic strategy, not constant ratio.

Exact movement coordination → MEC/TST.

---

## 43. Femoral / acetabular morphology and individual stance

Current evidence confirms meaningful variation in:

- femoral version;
- acetabular version/orientation;
- acetabular coverage/depth;
- pelvic morphology.

Measurement method itself can materially change reported “normal” femoral-version values.

Therefore:

**one universal squat stance, toe angle or hip-rotation ROM cannot be derived from atlas anatomy.**

But also:

**personal bone shape cannot be reliably inferred from external appearance alone.**

Imaging-dependent morphology → MED/TST/IND when actually necessary.

---

## 44. Hip bursae

Important regional bursae include:

- trochanteric;
- iliopsoas;
- ischial.

They permit tissue gliding.

Project rule:

**bursa presence ≠ bursitis.**

Pathology → MED.

## 45. Minimum neural map

1. inferior gluteal nerve → gluteus maximus;
2. superior gluteal nerve → gluteus medius/minimus/TFL;
3. femoral nerve → iliacus, sartorius, rectus femoris;
4. direct lumbar-plexus branches → psoas major;
5. obturator nerve → most adductor system;
6. sciatic/tibial division → most hip-crossing hamstrings;
7. common fibular division → biceps femoris short head;
8. named sacral-plexus branches → obturator internus/gemelli/quadratus femoris/piriformis systems.

Detailed plexus course → ANA-12.

---

## 46. Normal anatomical variation

Relevant variation includes:

- SIJ morphology/asymmetry;
- sacral/lumbosacral morphology;
- femoral version;
- acetabular orientation/coverage;
- gluteus-maximus distal architecture;
- adductor-magnus internal architecture;
- hamstring tendon morphology;
- gemelli/deep-rotator morphology;
- TFL/ITB relationships.

Project rule:

**atlas anatomy is modal, not identical in every person.**

Variation alone does not diagnose dysfunction.

## 47. What changed versus legacy ANA-08

### Confirmed

Legacy core remains valid:

- pelvic ring = paired hip bones + sacrum/coccyx with SIJ/pubis closure;
- hip = deep multiaxial ball-and-socket;
- labrum/capsule/ligaments contribute stability;
- major gluteal, iliopsoas, adductor, hamstring and deep-rotator groups are correctly identified;
- TFL/ITB are structurally linked but ITB is connective tissue;
- multiarticular hip/knee muscles matter for later exercise mechanics;
- morphological variation prevents one universal stance rule.

### Strengthened / updated

1. **SIJ:** 2026 dynamic biplane radiography directly supports small but measurable healthy gait motion; “zero motion” is rejected.
2. **SIJ variation:** 2024–2025 reviews emphasize frequent normal morphological/asymmetric variants and risk of imaging overinterpretation.
3. **Hip capsule:** 2025–2026 reviews support integrated iliofemoral/pubofemoral/ischiofemoral + zona-orbicularis capsular architecture.
4. **Femoral version:** 2025 scoping review shows reported normal values depend strongly on measurement method.
5. **Acetabular morphology:** current large 3D asymptomatic datasets reinforce continuous, sex-/individual-dependent orientation/coverage variation.
6. **Gluteus maximus:** 2025 cadaveric re-evaluation challenges the simplified claim that the majority of the muscle primarily inserts into ITB; major femoral distal-tendon attachment was found in that sample.
7. **Adductor magnus:** 2026 cadaveric work refines the classic two-part model into four structural components while retaining substantial variation.
8. **Gemelli:** 2026 anatomy retains distinct superior/inferior muscles despite shared obturator-internus insertional complex and documents morphological variants.
9. **Hamstrings:** current 2025–2026 anatomy reinforces tendon-level variation, especially semimembranosus.

### Tightened

1. No diagnosis from pelvic/SIJ asymmetry.
2. No universal squat stance/toe angle from anatomy.
3. No “most glute max inserts into ITB” universal claim.
4. No four-part adductor-magnus exercise taxonomy from one small cadaveric study.
5. No pathology inference from version/coverage variation alone.

## 48. Established findings

### HIGH

1. Pelvis is a load-transmitting ring connecting spine and femora.
2. SIJ is highly constrained but not fused/motionless in normal adults.
3. Hip stability is integrated across bone + labrum/capsule/ligaments + muscle.
4. Hip capsule includes iliofemoral, pubofemoral, ischiofemoral and circumferential zona-orbicularis components.
5. Gluteus maximus, medius and minimus have distinct architectures/functions rather than one generic “glute” role.
6. Iliopsoas links lumbar/pelvic/femoral structures and is a major hip-flexion system.
7. Adductor magnus includes functionally/anatomically distinct regions; classic adductor vs hamstring division remains useful.
8. Most hamstrings cross hip + knee, but biceps-femoris short head does not cross hip.
9. Deep external rotators form a differentiated posterior/deep hip system.
10. ITB is connective tissue/fascia rather than a contracting muscle.
11. Hip muscle actions are joint-position dependent.
12. Pelvic/femoral/acetabular anatomy varies materially among individuals.

### MODERATE / MODEL- OR SAMPLE-DEPENDENT

1. Exact SIJ healthy ROM values are task/sample dependent.
2. Exact gluteus-maximus distal insertion proportions need replication beyond the 2025 cadaveric sample.
3. Four-component adductor-magnus architecture is a useful current refinement but based on a small cadaveric sample.
4. Exact deep-rotator/gemelli variant frequencies and functional consequences vary by study.
5. Exact ITB tension/function during movement belongs to biomechanics rather than static anatomy.
