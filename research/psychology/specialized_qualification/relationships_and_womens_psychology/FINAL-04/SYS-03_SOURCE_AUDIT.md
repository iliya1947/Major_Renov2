## 3. Clarifications and methodological corrections

R1 — Calibration is not ranking, classification accuracy or correctness of one forecast. Van Calster direct calibration-hierarchy passages distinguish mean, weak, moderate and strong calibration. Intercept target 0 and slope target 1 do not ensure a correct flexible calibration curve; slope below 1 may indicate excessively extreme probabilities in the specified regression setting. The suggested sample of 200 events and 200 nonevents for precise curves is clinical-methodological guidance, not a universal sample requirement for any psychological question. Do not infer calibration from sparse examples or qualitative confidence. Proper scores evaluate probability forecasts against actual outcomes under specified scoring conventions. In binary Brier loss, E[(p-Y)^2]=q(1-q)+(p-q)^2, minimized at p=q under a true Bernoulli probability q. Scoring a single event is possible but cannot establish population calibration. A constant forecast may match marginal prevalence while having no discriminatory value. Metric choice, matching benchmark, target, time horizon, outcome resolution and missingness rules must precede evaluation. Avoid probabilities without defensible grounding.

R2 — Tournament performance versus mechanism of improvement. The Mellers studies are evidence that forecast accuracy can be evaluated with resolved questions, and report favorable effects of training/team structures and selection of strong performers. New indexed original reanalysis explicitly reports changed interpretations when accounting for extraneous method variance and latent response strategies/propensities, complicating attribution of superior scores to latent forecasting ability or the training mechanism. This is a material counterweight, not proof that training or teams never improve observed accuracy. Original tournament and reanalysis are dependent evidence, not independent replication. Separate randomized assignment, performance-based selection, aggregation, response strategy, realized score and latent ability. No transfer of geopolitical outcomes or training effect to individual relationships or assistant performance.

R3 — Prediction targets and transfer. Joel 2020 distinguishes levels of relationship quality from change across studied longitudinal datasets. Joel 2017 original abstract reports actor variance prediction 4–18% and partner 7–27%, but no unique dyadic desire prediction from predate traits/preferences in its speed-dating design. Actor variance, partner variance and unique relationship variance are different estimands, not percentages of correct decisions or universal ceilings. Initial encounters do not establish prediction limits for familiar dyads. Salganik original abstract reports limited improvement over simple baseline across its selected life outcomes; predictive error is more associated with family than technique within outcome. Beck/Jackson indexed abstract supports person, situation and temporal predictors in intensive longitudinal individual forecasting; direct training-split detail unavailable, so do not claim independent participant or prospective validation beyond the abstract. Together these studies justify comparing outcome-specific predictions to simple appropriate benchmarks, not the claim that complex models are always unnecessary or always superior. Prior FINAL-02/03 evaluations remain historically documented and are not rerun.

R4 — Cross-level inference and source dependence. Fleeson/Gallagher indexed original text explicitly distinguishes variance in a person's average manifestations across people from variance in momentary behavior. The cognitive-affective system theory supplies hypotheses about situation construal and conditional patterns, not direct estimates for an individual. Fisher et al.'s comparison of group and within-person estimates is contested by Hamaker/Ryan: a squared standard error does not measure true individual differences. The authors' reply addresses temporal instability of cross-sectional estimates; this exchange is not an empirical replication and does not establish a personal uncertainty multiplier. Adolf/Fried identify other conditional routes to generalization; strict ergodicity is not necessary in every design. Yarkoni and Bear/Phillips distinguish ambitious verbal claims, actual sampling/design and statistical models. Adding random effects cannot compensate for missing variation or unjustified target scope. Preserve both true heterogeneity and estimation uncertainty as competing possibilities; require matching constructs, level, time, context and independent person-level evidence. A population association can motivate a candidate explanation without establishing it. Copied records or repeated syntheses are not automatically independent evidence.

R5 — Reliability is not validity. Flinn's indexed abstract flags potential inflation of agreement by pooling raters and limited language/publication scope. Sørbye direct Results reports ICC=.82 [.75,.87] for a single rater's similarity rating across 30 matched and 30 mismatched whole-formulation pairs; inferred-mechanism similarity ICC=.67 [.61,.73], using four experienced judges rating 100 matched and 100 mismatched pairs. Overall agreement does not establish agreement with psychological ground truth; mixing matched and mismatched pairs creates variation relevant to this reliability estimate. Authors note matched-case mean similarity only 4.6 on a 7-point scale and possible inflation by descriptive information. Training of raters, experienced writers and selected cases limit generalization. Do not equate the ICC with reliability of a single psychological explanation, validity, or a quantified individual probability. Grove's prior aggregate formal-versus-clinical prediction estimates not reverified beyond bibliographic access; no numerical claim upgraded. A formal model may outperform unaided judgment in some tasks but requires appropriate validation and can fail. No clinical expertise inferred.

R6 — Revision and eligibility discipline. Original typed architecture P/S/G/D/O/H/F/A is a proposed record schema, not an empirically discovered latent model. The distinction between literal duplication and conditionally independent evidence is mathematically sound only under the specified hypotheses/likelihood model. A correction reopens affected descendants, not every claim or automatically its opposite. Separate a model's newly revised forecast from the previously issued frozen forecast. Define conditional eligibility before observing results. A canceled opportunity can be ineligible under a conditional contract; unobserved outcome is unresolved, not a confirmed negative. Confirmed failures must remain in the scoring record. A conditional forecast is not a loophole to exclude unexpected outcomes retrospectively. Forecast error may weaken an explanatory model when the prediction discriminates its alternatives; predictive success alone does not identify motive. Respect the actual choice of another person and restrict to legitimately available observations, without engineered tests, surveillance or pressure.
