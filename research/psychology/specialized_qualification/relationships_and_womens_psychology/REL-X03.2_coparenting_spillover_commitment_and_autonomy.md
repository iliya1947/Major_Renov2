## 1. Question and dependency gate

E3. Counterexample to treating all family spillover as parent-to-child or necessarily bidirectional. Different ages, outcomes and family samples from E3. E7. Rhoades, Stanley & Markman (2010), Should I stay or should I go? Predicting dating relationship stability from four aspects of commitment. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0021008 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20954764/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2958669/ . ## 3. Integrated conceptual framework
Separate subsystems: romantic partnership (closeness, attraction, satisfaction, commitment); coparenting (coordination of child-related tasks, mutual support, undermining, disagreement); parent-child relationships (care, attachment, adjustment); and wider work, household and family environments. Coparenting can continue after romantic dissolution. Good coordination around shared duties does not prove romantic interest or a desire for reunion; relationship dissatisfaction does not automatically imply deficient parenting.

Spillover is a hypothesized within-person cross-domain process; crossover is a hypothesized cross-person process. Shared external exposure may generate both actors' changes without either effect. Bidirectional feedback is a candidate, not a default causal conclusion. Same-day covariation, lagged prediction and a sustained change are distinct. Distinguish perceived support, observable coordination, actual workload, role valuation and available opportunities.

Interdependence and autonomy: each person's available choices and outcomes can depend on shared arrangements, resources, actions and expectations. Separate desire to maintain a bond, actual continuation, material and perceived costs of leaving, felt constraint, influence capacity, satisfaction with influence, actual decision practices, and free choice. A person's ability to choose does not require isolation or equal choices in every domain; voluntary delegation differs from inability to refuse. Equal resource scores do not establish equal autonomy, and asymmetry in resources alone does not establish abusive intent. Coercive conduct and safety must be evaluated independently of average quality or a theory of mutual influence.

## 4. Operational reasoning
1. Specify subsystem, actor, outcome, temporal scale and relationship definition. Keep reported stress, objective demand, behavior, perceptions and underlying preferences separate.
2. Compare viable mechanisms: opportunity restrictions with stable valuation, changed priorities, stress spillover, partner influence, shared external events, reverse direction and measurement effects. Do not treat family role or parenthood as a sufficient causal explanation.
3. Use naturally occurring comparable observations and voluntary reports to distinguish across-domain changes, cross-person associations, temporary recovery and sustained patterns. Check actual prior baseline. If competing accounts predict the same observation, do not claim a discriminating update.
4. Examine within-person versus between-person estimands, autocorrelation, shared causes, selection, attrition, actor/partner measurement and lag. APIM and cross-lagged paths alone cannot establish causality. Compare child age, family configuration and time interval before reconciling contradictory results.
5. Distinguish voluntary dedication from observed persistence, practical barriers and feeling trapped. Record separately each actor's stated preference, feasible alternatives and autonomy; no assumptions that continued contact or shared tasks establish mutual romantic intent.
6. Examine power domain, perceived influence and actual ability to refuse or leave, without interpreting a single resource indicator as proof of relational dominance. No tactics that increase dependency, manipulate children, exploit vulnerability or manufacture distress. System interdependence does not imply equal responsibility for harm.
7. Update only the supported proposition; preserve unresolved alternatives and no numerical individual probabilities without calibration. Clinical and safety questions remain separately scoped.

## 5. Internal transfer checks
Rubric: construct separation, competing explanation, discriminating evidence, inference boundary. Critical errors include unsupported diagnosis or motives, causal inflation, group-to-individual determinism, disregard of counterevidence, coercion or private-data leakage. Six original neutral cases, self-authored and not held-out.

A. Former spouses coordinate school transport efficiently but choose separate personal lives. Response: coparenting quality differs from romance; duty and child-focused cooperation may explain coordination. Voluntarily expressed relationship preferences and actual mutual choices distinguish outcomes. No inference of reconciliation.

B. Two adults become irritable after a building-wide utility disruption. Response: shared stress and crossover differ; common discomfort and mutual responses may coexist. Compare timing and ordinary contextual variation. Synchronous changes do not identify partner causation.

C. Early childhood and adolescent cohorts report different directions of association with coparenting. Response: child developmental stage, endpoints, reporters and models differ. Compare compatible estimands, lag and uncertainty; no universal arrow or child blame.

D. Someone maintains a shared lease while reporting uncertainty about continuing the partnership. Response: persistence, dedication and barriers are distinct; practical arrangements or genuine ambivalence compete. Voluntary future preferences and feasible choices clarify. No proposal to increase barriers.

E. Partners have unequal incomes yet each reports effective participation in decisions. Response: resource distribution, perceived influence and actual autonomy differ. Examine decision domains and ability to disagree or decline; no inference of equal or unequal global control from income alone.

F. A conventional cross-lagged model reports a relation which weakens after stable person differences are separated. Response: between-person association and within-person dynamics differ; stable confounding, measurement and model specification remain plausible. Compare matched models, temporal design and precision; no causal conclusion.
