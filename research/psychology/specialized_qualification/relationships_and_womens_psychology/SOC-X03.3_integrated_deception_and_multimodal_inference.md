## 3. Integrated model
Track separately: observable communication, literal proposition, pragmatic implication, relevant verified facts, speaker's contemporary knowledge, possible intention, reported experience, facial/vocal/temporal signal, observer interpretation, perceived credibility, actual choice and consequences. An inaccurate claim can be established without establishing deliberate deception. A change in preference or a failed plan need not imply earlier dishonesty. Privacy and nondisclosure differ from deception; alleged violation requires evidence of a relevant legitimate obligation. A material violation or documented threat can justify a protective decision without a definitive motive attribution.

Nonverbal signals may inform context-specific judgments of affect or conversational interaction, but do not automatically distinguish the truth of a statement or its speaker's motive. Experience, display, regulation, communication and perception are different constructs. Consider physical conditions, culture, opportunity, language, context and observer expectations. Synchronous speech timing differs from asynchronous message latency. Do not use one-direction rules for pauses or a body-language dictionary. Multiple cues sharing a cause or observation are dependent; their accumulation does not constitute independent confirmation.

Define the binary reference criterion before discussing accuracy. Overall accuracy depends on base rate and conditional correct-classification rates. A tendency to accept messages can increase total accuracy when truths predominate without improving discrimination. Confidence, sensitivity, specificity, predictive values, response tendency and actual discrimination are separate. No individual probabilities from aggregate experimental results, no claim of calibrated inference without prospective predictions and independent outcome checks.

## 4. Operational procedure
1. Define the exact claim, actor, time, source, channel and decision relevance. Preserve evidence and provenance; distinguish direct observation from reconstruction.
2. Check verifiable facts and actual agreements. Separate demonstrated falsity from inferred belief and intention. Consider honest error, ambiguity, ordinary privacy, changed conditions and genuine deception as appropriate; do not invent missing evidence.
3. Define what the cue could measure and a relevant criterion. Compare plausible alternatives, including observer expectations and situational factors. Explicit choice and consent cannot be overridden by a speculative interpretation of expression.
4. Seek naturally available corroboration and voluntarily offered clarification where appropriate; respect privacy and refusal. No manufactured tests, interrogation, surveillance or tactics for deception or exploitation.
5. Assess base rates, false positives, false negatives, measurement quality, dependencies, task transfer and uncertainty. Treat confidence and consistency of a narrative as insufficient validation.
6. Revise the narrowest claim supported; specify contradictory evidence and unresolved alternatives. Do not conflate prediction of an action with explanation of motive. Preserve safety and autonomy without requiring a diagnosis or certainty about intent.
