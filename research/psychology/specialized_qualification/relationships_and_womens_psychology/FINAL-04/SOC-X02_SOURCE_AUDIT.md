## 3. Corrections and clarifications

R1 — Public versus private conformity is an operational contrast, not identification of motives. Sowden Methods retain 21 participants after exclusions; Results give t(21)=3.10 and 3.56 for private effect and additional public effect, despite N=21, where conventional paired tests would have df=20. Relative contrast t(20)=1.06, p=.303. Published means 1.53 and 2.80, difference 1.27; published public congruency effect 4.33 agrees with their sum. Difference does not establish greater normative effect; paired covariance unavailable, do not reconstruct uncertainty from marginal SEMs. Discrepancy in reported df remains unresolved; not silently corrected. Private responding does not guarantee removal of social expectations, independent belief or lasting internalization. Bond's wider synthesis supports context-dependent patterns, not inference of one hidden motive.

R2 — Distinguish threat manipulation, measured reactance, refusal and downstream persuasion. Rains K=20 supports best fit among tested latent representations, not proof of causal or diagnostic specificity. Li & Shi pooled effects of higher versus lower freedom-threatening language on anger r=.21 (k=28, CI [.17,.26], PI [.02,.40]) and negative cognitions r=.17 (k=25, CI [.14,.21], PI [.05,.29]); combined reactance r=.20 (k=53). Difference reported Δr=.04, z=2.49, p=.006. Tail conventions for z-test not independently verified; do not silently substitute one. Gain/loss framing effects -.03, -.01, -.02 are nonsignificant, not equivalent. Associations of anger and negative cognitions with persuasion outcomes -.23 and -.18 are different estimands from randomized message contrasts. Study/effect counts differ; multiple outcomes are not independent experiments. No transfer of these pooled effects to a person's refusal or motivation. Avoid treating autonomy-preserving communication as a technique for overcoming refusal.

R3 — Autonomous motivation, received support, positional resources, subjective influence and actual meaningful choice are not interchangeable. Don & Hammond abstract supports motivational and support-seeking associations with enacted support and subjective experience; observational pathway not identified causally. Holding prospective study supports temporal association with own goal progress but does not establish experimental effects. Körner & Schütz's Table 6 uses bootstrapped 99% intervals for balance-of-power contrasts; positional balance coefficient .01, CI [-.04,.07], p=.532; other listed balance effects also not statistically significant. This is distinct from associations of levels of experienced power with quality. Non-significance does not prove equivalence or establish that resource imbalance is harmless. Reported influence does not verify available alternatives or safety. No new partner-effect or gender rule inferred.

R4 — Exposure/outcome correlations and heterogeneous measurements. Lohmann Table 2: 45 studies/107 effects overall, coercive-control PTSD r=.32 [.28,.37], 30 studies/31 effects; depression r=.27 [.22,.31], 35 studies/38 effects. Table gives I²=71.77% and 87.20%; PTSD Results prose instead gives I²=79.51%, Q(20)=97.62. Conventional Q calculation (97.62-20)/97.62=79.51%; table/prose sample or model difference unexplained, do not select one as verified overall heterogeneity. Depression Q(37)=289.02 gives 87.20% consistent with prose/table. PTSD association higher in support-service/shelter studies than community studies as reported; selection and context may explain differences. No individual exposure/diagnosis inference from symptoms, reverse causal inference, or equivalence of psychological IPV and coercive control.

R5 — Screening accuracy, benefit of screening alone, multicomponent support and guidance are distinct. Feltner: 3 screening RCTs N=3759, no detected outcome benefit over 3–18 months; nine accuracy studies N=9800, sensitivity 26–87% and specificity 80–97%, not paired operating points. Of 13 intervention RCTs N=7425, selected home visit and multi-risk counseling trials reported SMD -.34 [-.59,-.08] and -.40 [-.68,-.12]. The other home-visit trial figure -.04 [-.23,.14] and two brief IPV-only counseling trial estimates .19 [-.03,.41] and .22 [-.37,.80] illustrate mixed results; no direct comparison between trial effects without design/outcome adjustment. Different follow-up and targeted perinatal populations, imprecision, co-interventions; no guarantee of efficacy. USPSTF Grade B recommendation applies to reproductive-age women including pregnant/postpartum, with referral/provision of appropriate multicomponent ongoing support; not proof that detection alone works. No unsupported transfer to men, older women, nonclinical remote assessments or caregiver abuse. WHO guidance is not proof of intervention efficacy. Rivas is older and may share trials; no independent pooling of overlapping reviews.

R6 — Measurement validity is not diagnosis. GREI study 1 full scalar gender invariance rejected Δχ²(10)=53.18,p<.001; study 2 metric model Δχ²(10)=14.91,p=.135, scalar Δχ²(10)=33.71,p<.001. Study 2 partial scalar model after freeing three intercepts Δχ²(7)=12.05,p=.099. Cross-cultural model also required freeing six intercepts, partial scalar Δχ²(4)=8.77,p=.067. Authors report latent mean results under partial models; raw mean comparisons may be biased, and data-driven modifications, language and sample selection matter. No universal gender/cultural prevalence claim. Study 2 alpha=.96, correlations with psychological IPV .72, physical IPV .46, economic IPV .44, depression .56 and relationship quality -.43. Internal consistency and convergent validity are not sensitivity/specificity or proof of intentional manipulation. Cross-sectional measures, construct overlap and same-reporter effects restrict causal interpretation; no clinical cutoff. CASR-SF measures multiple experienced forms; taxonomy content coverage is distinct from predictive validity. A discrepancy in memory, an argument or an undesired outcome cannot alone identify a pattern or motive. Concrete serious harmful conduct can justify safety-oriented action independently of a psychological label.

R7 — Recognition of rhetorical form versus factual accuracy and response bias. Roozenbeek et al. d=.37 for studied material and .10 for cross-protection (difference .27 on standardized scales, not a verified between-effect test); task reliability/susceptibility outcomes do not establish general discrimination. Pennycook's five studies N=7286 found improved recognition of emotional manipulation without improved truth discrimination when used alone; combined intervention increased belief in true content. Disclosed platform-related funding/employment; does not invalidate results but requires scrutiny. Counterevidence: Modirrousta-Galian & Higham 2023 (https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0001395 ; institutional abstract https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/475360/) ROC reanalysis of five earlier studies found increased false judgments for both classes without improved discrimination on comparable items. Simchon et al. later hierarchical Bayesian SDT synthesis of 33 experiments instead reports improved discrimination without systematic response-bias increase; one coauthor co-developed interventions. Different selection, materials, estimands, dependence and analytical approach are plausible reasons for divergence, not established resolution. Additional original indexed abstract https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41398488/ reports no improved discrimination in an Indian headline task; full details not rechecked. Higham et al. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.copsyc.2023.101785 explains mean rating differences may depend on response criterion. JMIR review indexed extracts report significant Egger test for credibility assessment, t(29)=-3.27,p=.003, and mostly immediate measurement (k=35); exact model details and possible overlapping experiments remain unverified. Do not count reviews and shared primary studies as independent replications. Neither the positive nor negative news-task findings establish accuracy about hidden motives in intimate relationships.

## 4. Revised analytical rules
1. Separate a change in behavior, private endorsement, factual belief, free choice, available options, constraints, subjective impact, harm and hypothesized intent. Ordinary persuasion and voluntary cooperation do not imply coercion; agreement does not guarantee free choice.
2. Recognize concrete threats and serious harmful conduct without requiring certainty about motive. Distinguish actual consequences of refusal, repeated patterns and isolated events; respect refusal and safety independently of theory.
3. Verify material factual assertions where possible. Persuasive or suspicious form is not a criterion of factual truth or intent. Avoid diagnosis or a generalized suspicion score based on selected conduct.
4. Assess false positives and false negatives separately. Illustrative neutral arithmetic, not empirical data: in a balanced set of 100 verified positive and 100 verified negative items, an evaluator with 70 true positives and 20 false positives has 75% accuracy; a changed response criterion with 90 true positives and 40 false positives still has 75% accuracy. More detected positives alone do not establish greater discrimination; full ROC or suitable calibrated measures require adequate data and assumptions.
5. Distinguish clinical screening accuracy, effect of screening, effect of subsequent support and actual safety. Do not estimate individual risk from unmatched sensitivity/specificity ranges or sample associations. Preserve autonomy, privacy and voluntary choice; seek appropriate professional support for credible safety concerns.
6. Do not infer causal mechanism from best-fitting observational model, scale reliability, association or successful prediction. No unsupported population-to-individual transfer, techniques to overcome refusal, personalized influence strategy, surveillance or engineered tests.
