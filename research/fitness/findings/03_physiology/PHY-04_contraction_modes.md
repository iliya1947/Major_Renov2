# PHY-04 — Contraction modes

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-18**  
Область: 03 — Physiology

## 0. Decision

Создать физиологическую модель contraction modes, достаточную для ответа на вопрос:

> **чем modes различаются по force, cost, fatigue и adaptation?**

и для downstream acute response, strength, hypertrophy, power, fatigue, biomechanics and programming без:

- mode-label simplification;
- eccentric-superiority overclaim;
- DOMS-as-quality logic;
- acute→chronic substitution;
- ignoring task specificity;
- collapsing SSC into contraction mode alone.

## 1. Concentric action

Concentric action = **active shortening while producing force**.

Mechanical role:

**positive mechanical work.**

Key physiological property:

as shortening velocity increases, maximal force capability generally decreases.

Project rule:

**concentric ≠ universally “lifting phase”; classification depends on the active target muscle.**

## 2. Eccentric action

Eccentric action = **active lengthening while producing force**.

Mechanical role:

**negative work / energy absorption.**

Broad properties:

- high force capacity;
- low intrinsic metabolic cost per unit force/work;
- greater novelty-dependent EIMD potential;
- history-dependent/passive structural contributions alongside cross-bridge mechanics.

Project rule:

**eccentric ≠ passive stretch.**

## 3. Isometric action

Isometric action = **force production with no intended gross external change in joint/muscle-tendon-unit length**.

Important nuance:

internal fascicle/tendon/aponeurosis lengths can still redistribute.

Project rule:

**externally isometric does not mean every internal structure is perfectly static.**

## 4. Isokinetic condition

Isokinetic exercise = **apparatus-controlled approximately constant joint angular velocity**.

Resistance changes to match applied torque.

It can contain:

- concentric action;
- eccentric action.

Project rule:

**isokinetic is a movement-control/testing category, not a fourth sarcomeric contraction mechanism.**

## 5. Force–velocity relationship

### Concentric
Faster shortening → lower maximal force capacity.

### Isometric
Zero gross shortening/lengthening velocity → isometric point.

### Eccentric
Active lengthening can support force above concentric and often above isometric levels.

The eccentric branch is influenced by:

- starting length;
- temperature;
- activation;
- cross-bridge state;
- passive/history-dependent mechanics.

Project rule:

**eccentric force–velocity behavior is not a mirror image of concentric behavior.**

## 6. Force–length relationship

Force depends on muscle/sarcomere length through:

- actin–myosin geometry;
- passive elastic structures;
- titin-related effects;
- architecture;
- tendon compliance.

Project rule:

**contraction mode and muscle length must be interpreted together.**

Detailed joint torque/exercise geometry → MEC.

## 7. Mode-specific force capacity

Broad laboratory ordering under many matched maximal conditions:

`eccentric ≥ isometric > concentric`

But no universal fixed ratio exists.

Magnitude depends on:

- velocity;
- length;
- activation;
- familiarization;
- apparatus;
- task;
- training status.

Project rule:

**higher eccentric force capacity does not prove universally superior chronic training results.**

## 8. Metabolic cost

Eccentric action generally requires less:

- oxygen;
- ATP turnover;
- activation

per unit force/mechanical output than concentric action.

Project rule:

**lower intrinsic eccentric energy cost ≠ lower whole-session recovery cost in every protocol.**

Volume, load, tempo, novelty and damage still matter.

## 9. Fatigue

Fatigue must be separated into:

1. metabolic/peripheral fatigue;
2. neural/neuromuscular fatigue;
3. immediate mechanical performance loss;
4. delayed damage-related loss.

Concentric work can be metabolically more demanding for matched output.

Eccentric work can be metabolically economical but still produce prolonged performance loss when high-strain/novel.

Project rule:

**contraction mode changes the fatigue profile rather than assigning one universal fatigue ranking.**

Detailed fatigue mechanisms → PHY-10.

## 10. Eccentric EIMD / DOMS

Novel or high-strain eccentric exercise can increase:

- DOMS;
- strength loss;
- swelling;
- CK;
- structural stress.

Magnitude is modified by:

- novelty;
- dose;
- strain/muscle length;
- volume;
- velocity/tempo;
- training status;
- prior exposure.

Project rule:

**eccentric action does not inevitably produce severe damage.**

## 11. Repeated-bout effect

Prior exposure to eccentric/damaging exercise produces protective adaptation.

Current evidence supports reduced later:

- strength loss;
- DOMS;
- CK response.

Project rule:

**DOMS decreases with familiarization without implying the training stimulus stopped working.**

## 12. Strength adaptation specificity

Strength adaptation is mode/task specific.

Examples:

- eccentric training preferentially improves eccentric strength;
- isometric training preferentially improves isometric strength;
- dynamic training transfers to isometric strength, but less than to dynamic strength.

Project rule:

**strength is not one context-free construct; the test mode is part of the outcome definition.**

## 13. Eccentric-only vs concentric-only training

Current meta-analytic evidence supports:

- stronger eccentric-specific gains after eccentric-only training;
- meaningful cross-mode transfer;
- no universal superiority across all measured strength modes.

Project rule:

**mode specificity > universal eccentric superiority.**

## 14. Isometric training

Isometric training can substantially improve strength.

Current evidence supports:

- particularly strong isometric gains;
- partial transfer to isokinetic/dynamic outcomes;
- meaningful hypertrophy potential.

Project rule:

**isometric training is a valid tool, not merely a testing method.**

## 15. Joint-angle / task specificity

Isometric adaptation depends on:

- task;
- muscle length;
- joint angle;
- testing mode.

Transfer beyond the trained angle/task exists but is incomplete.

Project rule:

**isometric training is neither “one exact angle only” nor universally transferable across the full ROM.**

Exact angle prescription → TEC/PRO after MEC.

## 16. Hypertrophy by mode

Current evidence does not establish:

`eccentric > concentric > isometric`

as a universal hierarchy.

Eccentric and concentric training can produce similar overall hypertrophy.

Isometric training can also produce hypertrophy when dose/effort/muscle length are appropriate.

Project rule:

**all major resistance modes can contribute to hypertrophy; mode alone does not determine outcome.**

## 17. Accentuated eccentric loading

AEL increases eccentric-phase load/stimulus.

Current 2026 synthesis found:

- some larger acute responses;
- similar chronic maximal strength outcomes;
- similar muscle CSA;
- no consistent architecture superiority

versus constant-load resistance training.

Project rule:

**larger eccentric stimulus does not automatically mean larger chronic adaptation.**

## 18. Architectural adaptation

Mode can influence:

- fascicle length;
- pennation;
- regional hypertrophy.

But findings depend on protocol and are not uniform.

Project rule:

**do not use a fixed “eccentric = fascicle length / concentric = pennation” rule.**

## 19. Stretch-shortening cycle

SSC combines:

1. preactivation;
2. active lengthening;
3. rapid transition;
4. shortening.

Performance can reflect:

- elastic energy storage/recovery;
- muscle-tendon interaction;
- reflex/neural contributions;
- history-dependent contractile properties.

Project rule:

**SSC is an integrated muscle–tendon–neural phenomenon.**

Detailed mechanics → MEC.  
Neural control → NEU.  
Exercise/programming use → TEC/PRO.

## 20. Acute response ≠ chronic adaptation

The following do not independently prove superior long-term adaptation:

- higher eccentric force;
- lower oxygen cost;
- greater soreness;
- higher CK;
- higher lactate;
- greater EMG;
- higher RPE;
- slower eccentric tempo.

Project rule:

**acute mode response is mechanistic/context evidence, not a chronic-gain surrogate.**

## 21. Integrated contraction-mode model

### Concentric
`active shortening → positive work → force decreases with faster shortening → relatively higher metabolic cost`

### Isometric
`active force without intended gross length change → strong task/angle specificity`

### Eccentric
`active lengthening → negative work → high force capacity → low intrinsic metabolic cost → greater novelty-dependent EIMD potential`

### Isokinetic
`apparatus-controlled constant joint angular velocity` layered on dynamic actions.

## 22. Training-relevant implications

1. **Eccentric force capacity is high, but high force alone does not prove better adaptation.**
2. **Eccentric work is metabolically economical but can still create large recovery demands.**
3. **Concentric performance is constrained strongly by force–velocity.**
4. **Muscle length modifies force in every mode.**
5. **Strength adaptation is mode/task specific.**
6. **DOMS is not a quality metric.**
7. **Repeated exposure reduces eccentric damage responses.**
8. **No universal contraction-mode hypertrophy winner is established.**
9. **Isometric training is a legitimate strength/hypertrophy tool.**
10. **Isokinetic results should not be equated with free-weight mechanics.**
11. **SSC requires MEC/NEU integration.**
12. **Acute responses cannot substitute for longitudinal evidence.**

## 26. Canonical project rules

1. Concentric = active shortening.
2. Eccentric = active lengthening.
3. Isometric = no intended gross external length change.
4. Isokinetic = apparatus-controlled velocity condition.
5. External joint motion ≠ exact fascicle behavior.
6. Force depends on velocity and length.
7. Eccentric force capacity is high.
8. Eccentric intrinsic metabolic cost is low relative to force.
9. Low metabolic cost ≠ low total recovery cost.
10. Eccentric damage is novelty/dose dependent.
11. Repeated-bout protection is real.
12. Strength adaptation is mode/task specific.
13. All major modes can support hypertrophy.
14. No universal hypertrophy ranking by mode is established.
15. Isometric training is useful but specific.
16. SSC is integrated muscle–tendon–neural physiology.
17. Acute response ≠ chronic adaptation.

## 27. Evidence / confidence

### HIGH
- mode definitions;
- force–velocity/force–length foundations;
- high eccentric force capacity;
- lower eccentric intrinsic metabolic cost;
- mode/task specificity of strength;
- eccentric EIMD with novel/high-strain exposure;
- repeated-bout protection;
- isometric strength efficacy.

### MODERATE-HIGH
- broad eccentric-vs-concentric chronic strength comparisons;
- lack of universal hypertrophy superiority;
- partial cross-mode transfer.

### MODERATE / CONTEXT-DEPENDENT

- exact fatigue ranking across protocols;
- exact isometric angle transfer;
- exact SSC mechanism weighting;
