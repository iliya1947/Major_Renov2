# PHY-09 — Power / RFD

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-18**  
Область: 03 — Physiology

## 0. Core decision

Power, RFD and maximal strength are related but distinct outcomes.

### Maximal strength
High-force capacity in a defined task.

### RFD
How rapidly force rises:

`RFD = ΔF / Δt`.

### Mechanical power
Force expressed at velocity:

`P = F × v`

or rotationally:

`P = torque × angular velocity`.

Project rule:

**maximal strength, RFD and power must not be used interchangeably.**

## 1. Power definition

Power requires both force and velocity.

Therefore:

- maximal force can occur near zero velocity;
- maximal velocity can occur with low external force;
- maximal power typically occurs where meaningful force and velocity coexist.

Project rule:

**power is neither force alone nor velocity alone.**

## 2. Measurement boundary

Reported power depends on:

- exercise;
- measured system;
- force/velocity measurement method;
- body/load mass inclusion;
- movement phase;
- device/software;
- filtering/sampling.

Project rule:

**watts are not universally comparable across exercises/devices without measurement definition.**

## 3. RFD

RFD is a rapid-force outcome, not a power metric.

Early RFD is especially sensitive to rapid neural activation.

Later RFD is more influenced by:

- maximal force capacity;
- muscle contractile properties;
- MTU mechanics.

Project rule:

**RFD requires its own test and time window.**

## 4. Peak vs mean power

### Peak power
Highest short-window power value.

### Mean power
Average power across a defined phase/interval.

Project rule:

**peak power ≠ mean power.**

## 5. Velocity boundary

External-load/bar velocity, joint angular velocity and fascicle shortening velocity are different.

Project rule:

**external movement velocity is a task metric, not direct fascicle velocity.**

## 6. Load–power relation

Higher loads increase force demand but reduce movement velocity.

Lower loads permit higher velocity but lower external force.

Many tasks show high power in an intermediate/broad moderate region.

Current chronic synthesis supports:

- moderate loads;
- fast concentric intent;
- power RT;
- Olympic-style weightlifting

for power development.

Project rule:

**there is no universal %1RM that maximizes power across all exercises.**

## 7. Acute optimal-power-load boundary

The load that acutely maximizes measured power is not automatically the load that maximizes chronic adaptation.

Current power-clean meta-analysis shows a broad acute peak-power plateau above ~40% 1RM rather than one clearly superior higher-load zone.

Project rule:

**acute watt maximum ≠ universal chronic prescription.**

## 8. Neuromuscular determinants

Power depends on:

- maximal force capacity;
- rapid motor-unit activation;
- high intended acceleration;
- movement velocity capacity;
- intermuscular coordination;
- task skill.

Detailed neural control → NEU.

## 9. Muscular / MTU determinants

Relevant contributors include:

- muscle size/PCSA;
- fiber/contractile phenotype;
- architecture;
- fascicle behavior;
- tendon/elastic behavior;
- force–velocity properties.

Detailed mechanics → MEC.

## 10. Maximal strength as foundation

Higher maximal strength expands force-side potential for power.

But maximal strength alone does not guarantee high power.

Project rule:

**strength provides capacity; rapid/high-velocity practice develops expression.**

## 11. Power vs maximal-strength training

### Maximal-strength training
Emphasizes:
- high force;
- heavier loads;
- maximal-strength specificity.

### Power training
Emphasizes:
- rapid force production;
- high intended acceleration;
- useful movement velocity;
- task-specific force–velocity exposure.

Project rule:

**power training is not merely lighter strength training, and heavy strength work alone is not a complete power program.**

## 12. Ballistic / high-velocity practice

Ballistic tasks can allow continued acceleration and greater high-velocity specificity.

Examples include:

- jumps;
- throws;
- Olympic-weightlifting derivatives.

Project rule:

**ballistic exposure can complement strength development, but no single ballistic method is universally superior.**

## 13. Task specificity

A person can improve differently across:

- jump power;
- barbell power;
- sprint acceleration;
- RFD;
- maximal force.

Project rule:

**power is task-specific rather than one universally transferable scalar.**

## 14. Force–velocity profiling

F–V profiling can describe:

- force-oriented deficit;
- velocity-oriented deficit;
- F0;
- V0;
- Pmax;
- profile imbalance.

Current evidence shows possible benefit for some profile variables and jump performance but not consistent superiority for Pmax, sprint or functional performance.

Project rule:

**F–V profiling is descriptive/hypothesis-guiding, not a universally validated personalization algorithm.**

## 15. Chronic-training implications

1. **Develop maximal strength when force capacity is limiting.**
2. **Include high-velocity/ballistic intent when rapid expression matters.**
3. **Judge power programs against the exact target task.**
4. **Use moderate-load power work as evidence-supported general guidance, not a universal optimum.**
5. **Do not choose chronic load solely from the acute peak-watt load.**
6. **Measure RFD separately if rapid force is a target.**
7. **Specify peak vs mean power.**
8. **Specify the measurement system/device before comparing watt values.**
9. **Treat F–V profile interventions as hypotheses requiring outcome confirmation.**

## 16. Canonical project rules

1. Power = force × velocity.
2. RFD = rate of force rise, not power.
3. Maximal strength ≠ power.
4. Peak power ≠ mean power.
5. External velocity ≠ fascicle velocity.
6. Power values are test/device specific.
7. Load–power relationship is task specific.
8. No universal optimal power load exists across exercises.
9. Acute watt-maximizing load ≠ proven best chronic prescription.
10. Maximal strength is a major foundation for power.
11. Rapid/high-velocity specificity is also required.
12. Power training and maximal-strength training overlap but are not identical.
13. F–V profiling is not yet a universally superior prescription system.
14. Acute mechanical description ≠ chronic efficacy evidence.

## 17. What PHY-09 does not prescribe

These remain NEU, MEC, TEC, PRO, TST and IND.
