## 3. Training age audit

PASS — USEFUL DESCRIPTOR, POOR STANDALONE LEARNING VARIABLE.

The 2026 expertise-stratified attentional-focus NMA (PMID 42419609):

- included 87 studies;
- reclassified instruction in 78 studies;
- classified expertise using training/performance caliber tiers;
- focus rankings differed across tiers;
- conclusions remained sensitive to small-study effects and reporting bias.

This supports expertise as a moderator candidate.

It does not establish:

- years trained as the causal moderator;
- one cue for all novices;
- one cue for all experts.

The RT review PMID 40551899 further shows training-status differences but also major methodological inconsistency and limited elite-athlete data.

Canonical rule:

**use exact-task exposure, current proficiency and performance caliber before generic years trained.**

No blocker.

## 4. Training age ≠ expertise audit

PASS.

A person can have:

- long general gym exposure but little experience with a specific lift;
- high strength with low technical familiarity;
- many repetitions with poor-quality feedback;
- expert-level performance in one movement family and novice status in another.

Therefore:

`years trained`
≠
`task-specific expertise`.

For motor-learning decisions, stronger variables are:

- exact-task practice history;
- current performance stability;
- error-detection ability;
- cue dependence;
- retention/transfer behavior.

No blocker.

## 5. Age audit

PASS — GLOBAL AGE-DEFICIT MODEL REJECTED.

PMID 42157004 combined meta-analysis and well-powered experiments and found:

- older adults had poorer discovery/caching of explicit strategies;
- implicit recalibration was enhanced in the studied paradigms.

PMID 39792202:

- young n=28, older n=20;
- implicit adaptation and spontaneous recovery were similar.

PMID 42886636:

- young n=18, older n=19;
- both groups learned and retained a procedural sequence;
- older adults showed stronger next-day retention in that protocol.

Therefore:

**age effects are learning-process specific rather than one-directional global loss.**

No blocker.

## 6. Biological age terminology audit

PASS — SOURCE-MAP TERM REQUIRES SPECIFIC MEASURE.

The Area 04 map says “biological age,” but studies operationalize different constructs.

PMID 42043085:

- youth n=71;
- biological age indexed by years from peak height velocity;
- pilot results underpowered/inconclusive;
- practice/exposure may explain meaningful variance.

PMID 42147461:

- 40,579 individuals;
- MRI-derived biological brain-age measures associated with cognitive/motor performance;
- observational association, not motor-learning prescription.

Thus:

**biological age is not one universal variable.**

Canonical rule:

**name the biological-age construct and measurement; do not substitute calendar age or infer learning capacity from the label.**

No blocker.

## 7. Previous motor experience audit

PASS — TRANSFER DEPENDS ON STRUCTURAL OVERLAP.

Current evidence supports that prior motor memory can:

- facilitate transfer;
- interfere during switching;
- have no meaningful effect.

PMID 40811438 shows learning transfer across hands with re-expression of learning-related large-scale activity.

PMID 41213806 shows transient errors during switching due to perseveration of the previous motor policy.

Therefore:

**prior experience is not intrinsically positive; compatibility with the new task matters.**

For lifting, direct squat↔hinge↔press transfer remains downstream NEU-22/TEC work.

No blocker.

---

## 8. Handedness / dominance audit

PASS — RECENT EVIDENCE STRONGLY REJECTS GLOBAL DOMINANT-LIMB SUPERIORITY.

PMID 42378273:

- tested reaching/tool use and novel-effector control;
- found no general dominant-limb motor-control advantage;
- large dominance emerged where lifelong task-specific practice mattered;
- dominance disappeared with a novel effector;
- practice could create symmetric improvement.

The strongest defensible interpretation is:

**skilled dominance is predominantly task/practice specific rather than a general superior controller.**

This does not deny that hand preference may have biological/developmental contributors.

No blocker.

---

## 9. Interlimb transfer audit

PASS — ASYMMETRY CAN BE TASK/EXPRESSION DEPENDENT.

PMID 41192342:

- right-handed pistol-shooting learners;
- initial dominant→nondominant transfer occurred;
- nondominant→dominant transfer did not;
- task-related exposure could eliminate the asymmetry;
- authors interpret the result as expression/access limitation rather than unequal acquisition/storage.

Therefore:

**asymmetric transfer does not establish one universal side-first training law.**

No blocker.

---

## 10. Cognitive load / working-memory audit

PASS — MODERATOR, NOT TYPOLOGY.

PMID 41196177:

- effector-independent motor working memory correlated with explicit visuomotor learning;
- inferred effector-specific motor working memory correlated with implicit learning.

PMID 41996616:

- n=40 novice hockey learners;
- different cognitive abilities predicted different learning phases;
- working memory predicted post-coaching improvement;
- psychomotor ability predicted gains through repetition.

These are useful moderator signals.

But they are:

- correlational/task specific;
- not evidence for a single general “cognitive load tolerance” trait;
- not evidence for one permanent coaching style.

Canonical rule:

**adapt instruction when overload is observed; do not assign a learner identity from a cognitive score.**

No blocker.

## 11. Attention-profile audit

PASS — REAL INDIVIDUAL DIFFERENCES, NO DIRECT MOTOR CUE MAP.

PMID 38769271:

- 90 independent samples;
- >23,000 participants;
- evidence for a broad attention-control factor plus subcomponents.

PMID 41114956:

- attention control, intensity and consistency were separable;
- attention control related directly to learning and indirectly through strategy/self-efficacy;
- other attention dimensions had different indirect relationships.

Thus:

**attention is multidimensional.**

No evidence establishes a deterministic mapping:

`attention profile X → external/internal/analogy cue Y`.

No blocker.

## 13. Fatigue-sensitivity audit

PASS — STATE/PROTOCOL DEPENDENCE DOMINATES CURRENT EVIDENCE.

The 2026 systematic review PMID 42480681:

- 38 articles / 43 experiments;
- acquisition effects:
  - 65.1% detrimental;
  - 9.3% facilitatory;
  - 16.3% neutral;
  - 9.3% mixed/inconclusive;
- retention varied by fatigue modality, task and test context;
- participant-level moderators were rarely tested.

This strongly rejects one universal fatigue-learning rule.

No blocker.

---

## 14. Fatigue trait audit

PASS — STABLE “FATIGUE-SENSITIVE LEARNER” PHENOTYPE NOT ESTABLISHED.

PMID 42811472:

- n=60;
- cardiovascular-fatigue vs control before visuomotor tracking;
- both groups learned and retained;
- no reliable group-level impairment or fatigue-resilience benefit;
- exploratory individual-difference patterns appeared;
- confirmatory moderation models gave limited evidence for systematic moderators.

Therefore:

**one-session individual variation is insufficient to infer a stable fatigue-learning trait.**

Canonical rule:

**fatigue sensitivity requires repeated task-specific measurement across comparable exposures.**

No blocker.

---

## 15. Resistance-training fatigability audit

PASS — PROTOCOL DEPENDENT.

PMID 40112869:

- 34 studies in resistance-trained participants;
- most studies found minor-to-no sex differences;
- some male-greater fatigability appeared in specific protocol conditions;
- heterogeneity was substantial.

PMID 41527566:

- 21 men + 21 women;
- bench press 75% 1RM, sets of 5, 90 s rest to failure;
- women completed more repetitions;
- estimated-1RM recovery did not differ;
- difference appeared linked partly to between-set recovery.

Thus:

**fatigability is protocol-specific and multidimensional, not one stable person score.**

No blocker.

## 16. Preferred / efficient cueing audit

PASS — EMPIRICAL RESPONSE, NOT LEARNER TYPE.

PMID 29617214:

- n=25 children;
- both internal and external focus improved short-term putting;
- no overall condition difference;
- task-specific preference related to differential immediate performance.

This is preference-performance evidence, not durable learning superiority.

PMID 42911075:

- n=60 young adults;
- choice increased perceived autonomy;
- did not improve jump height;
- cue formulations differed in performance;
- participants frequently altered/interpreted wording;
- authors label findings exploratory/hypothesis generating.

Combined with PMID 42419609:

- cue classification/expertise affect estimates;
- reporting/small-study limitations remain.

Canonical rule:

**test cue effectiveness in the task and update it over time; preference is information, not identity.**

No blocker.

## 18. Group moderator ≠ personal treatment effect audit

PASS.

A group-level interaction/moderator does not prove:

- stable individual responder status;
- causal treatment-effect heterogeneity in one person;
- repeatability across tasks.

This is especially important for:

- cue effects;
- fatigue effects;
- cognitive moderators.

Project rule:

**group moderators generate individual hypotheses; repeated task-specific response validates individual rules.**

No blocker.
