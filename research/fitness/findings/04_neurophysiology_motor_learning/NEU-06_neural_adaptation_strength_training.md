# NEU-06 — Neural adaptation to strength training

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-19**  
Область: 04 — Neurophysiology / Motor Learning

## 0. Core decision

Neural adaptation to strength training is distributed and task specific.

Canonical model:

`repeated task-specific resistance exposure`
→
`motor-unit recruitment/discharge changes`
+
`descending-drive / inhibition changes`
+
`intermuscular coordination`
+
`task learning`
interacts with
`muscle + tendon + architecture + mechanics`
→
`task-specific strength expression`.

Project rule:

**there is no single neural-adaptation variable or one anatomical site that explains strength gain.**

## 1. Early strength gain

Strength can increase before large measurable hypertrophy develops.

Contributors can include:

- neural drive;
- motor-unit recruitment/rate coding;
- coordination;
- task familiarity;
- technique/movement strategy;
- improved maximal-effort expression.

Project rule:

**early strength gain can be strongly neural/skill-mediated, but “early gain = purely neural” is not retained.**

## 2. Motor-unit recruitment

Strength training can alter recruitment thresholds and population recruitment behavior.

Project rule:

**the direction and magnitude of recruitment adaptation are task-, muscle-, force-level- and protocol-specific.**

Do not assume all training lowers recruitment thresholds or simply recruits “more units” at every force level.

## 3. Firing rates

Motor-unit discharge rates can increase with strength training in some protocols.

However pooled evidence is heterogeneous.

Project rule:

**firing-rate adaptation is real but not universally upward.**

## 4. Neural drive

Neural drive is ensemble motor-neuron output.

Project rule:

**neural drive ≠ surface EMG amplitude.**

Absolute multichannel EMG can sometimes approximate within-person early changes, whereas MVC normalization can conceal them.

## 5. Antagonist coactivation

Training can alter agonist-antagonist coordination.

Project rule:

**lower absolute antagonist activation is not a universal neural adaptation or universal goal.**

Relative coordination and absolute antagonist activation can change differently.

## 6. Intermuscular coordination

Training can change:

- muscle weighting;
- activation timing;
- intermuscular coherence;
- synergy organization.

Project rule:

**coordination adaptation is task/load specific; there is no single universal “better coordination” signature.**

## 7. Task-specific skill

Strength is partly a learned motor performance.

Dynamic resistance training improves trained dynamic strength substantially more than it transfers to non-trained isometric strength.

Project rule:

**trained-task strength and generalized force capacity are related but non-identical outcomes.**

## 8. Cortical / corticospinal adaptation

Resistance training can change:

- corticospinal excitability;
- cortical inhibition;
- motor-evoked potentials;
- cortical silent period;
- SICI.

Project rule:

**one TMS marker cannot quantify the cortical share of strength gain.**

Marker direction depends on task, state, training type and protocol.

## 9. Spinal / motoneuron adaptation

Adaptation can occur at or below the spinal motor-neuron level.

Relevant measures include:

- H-reflex;
- V-wave;
- motor-unit recruitment/discharge;
- motoneuron intrinsic properties / synaptic input estimates.

Project rule:

**H-reflex, V-wave and motor-unit measures are not interchangeable “spinal adaptation” scores.**

Precise site attribution requires converging evidence.

## 10. Retention

Different neural adaptations can persist for different durations.

Project rule:

**preserved strength does not prove every neural marker is preserved.**

Likewise:

**loss of one neural marker does not prove the trained motor solution has been erased.**

## 11. Neural detraining

Neural detraining is differential regression.

Possible changes include:

- corticospinal excitability/inhibition;
- motor-unit output;
- agonist-antagonist coordination;
- task familiarity;
- rapid-force expression.

Project rule:

**there is no universal neural detraining rate or percent-per-week rule.**

## 12. Relearning / retraining

Previously trained people can regain recent strength rapidly after a break.

Potential retained contributors include:

- task familiarity;
- motor skill;
- neural coordination;
- residual strength/size/architecture;
- cellular and molecular history.

Project rule:

**rapid regain does not prove one unique neural “muscle-memory” mechanism.**

## 13. Canonical project rules

1. Neural adaptation to strength training is distributed, not unitary.
2. Early strength gain can precede large hypertrophy.
3. Early gain is not automatically purely neural.
4. Motor-unit recruitment and firing adapt in task/protocol-specific ways.
5. Neural drive is not surface EMG.
6. MVC-normalized EMG can conceal training-related neural changes.
7. Antagonist coactivation does not have a universal downward adaptation rule.
8. Intermuscular coordination changes are task/load specific.
9. Strength adaptation contains a real task-specific skill component.
10. Cortical/corticospinal adaptation is supported, but one TMS marker is not a cortical-adaptation score.
11. Spinal/motoneuron adaptation is supported, but H-reflex, V-wave and motor-unit measures are not interchangeable.
12. Neural retention and detraining occur on multiple clocks.
13. Rapid retraining is possible.
14. Rapid retraining does not establish a unique neural-memory mechanism.
15. Neural changes must be interpreted together with muscular, tendon, mechanical and technical adaptations.

## 14. Training-relevant implications

1. **Do not infer “neural efficiency” from one EMG or TMS value.**
2. **Do not assume a beginner's early strength gain is all hypertrophy or all neural.**
3. **Treat practiced movement skill as part of strength development.**
4. **Expect transfer to different strength tests to be smaller than gains in the trained task.**
5. **Do not program for lower antagonist activity as a universal goal.**
6. **Do not infer absence of neural adaptation from unchanged H-reflex or normalized EMG.**
7. **Do not infer anatomical site from one evoked-response measure.**
8. **After a break, assess current performance directly rather than estimating loss from time alone.**
9. **Expect possible rapid regain without assigning a fixed retraining multiplier.**
10. **Keep exact load/volume/velocity/frequency prescription in PRO/GPP.**

## 15. What NEU-06 does not prescribe

Not owned here:

- exact optimal load for neural adaptation;
- exact velocity prescription;
- exact volume/frequency for neural adaptation;
- individualized neural bottleneck diagnosis;
- EMG/TMS/H-reflex diagnostic thresholds;
- retraining progression after injury/illness;
- bilateral transfer;
- fatigue-specific neural adaptation.

These remain NEU-07/08/09, TST, TEC, PRO, GPP, MED and IND.

## 16. Confidence

### HIGH
- early strength gain can substantially precede large measurable hypertrophy;
- task/test specificity is a major part of strength adaptation;
- neural drive is not equivalent to surface EMG;
- neural adaptation spans more than one nervous-system level;
- rapid functional reacquisition after a break can occur.

### MODERATE-HIGH
- motor-unit recruitment/discharge behavior can adapt;
- intermuscular coordination can adapt;
- cortical/corticospinal measures change with RT;
- spinal/motoneuron output can adapt;
- neural variables can be retained/lost at different rates.

### MODERATE / CONTEXT-DEPENDENT
- exact direction/magnitude of firing-rate change;
- exact recruitment-threshold shift;
- exact antagonist coactivation change;
- exact causal role of one coherence/synergy change;
- exact cortical vs spinal contribution;
- exact retention duration for one neural adaptation.

### LOW / NOT ESTABLISHED AS UNIVERSAL
- one neural mechanism explaining early strength gain;
- one universal cortical/spinal biomarker;
- one neural detraining half-life;
- unique neural muscle-memory mechanism;
- universal retraining advantage magnitude.
