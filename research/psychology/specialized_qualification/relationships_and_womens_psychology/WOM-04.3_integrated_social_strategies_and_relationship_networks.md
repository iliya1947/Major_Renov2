E1. Demographic Variation in Social Support and Intimate Friend Across 22 Countries: A Cross-National Analysis (2025). https://doi.org/10.1007/s41042-025-00226-0 . Publisher HTML study population, measures, demographic results, country comparisons and discussion excerpts inspected. Global Flourishing Study first wave, 202,898 participants, nationally representative within 22 selected countries, mostly 2023 data. Random-effects pooled social-support mean for women 7.50 (95% CI 7.14–7.87) versus men 7.29 (6.90–7.69) on 0–10 scale; having a confidant .85 (.83–.87) versus .82 (.80–.85). Social support item concerns expected availability of help, not network size; confidant item does not specify friendship rather than other relationship. Direction reversed in some countries (Brazil p=.057, Hong Kong p=.022, Kenya p=.023; nominal tests). Selected countries, complex survey weights, single items and cross-sectional comparisons limit interpretation. Overlapping intervals of separate means do not decide significance of their contrast. Cross-national differences cannot identify causes or predict an individual woman's network. No global population prevalence inference.

E2. Gender differences in social networks and physical and mental health: are social relationships more health protective in women than in men? (2023 volume, 2024 publication). https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1216032 . Publisher HTML participants, selected methods, Tables 1/2, results and discussion/limitations inspected. South Korean age/sex-stratified online survey, N=620 ages 40–69, 305 women. Authors selected four latent profiles separately by gender based on fit and interpretability; female LMR-LRT did not favor four over three (p=.75), alternative model criteria differed. Female sample profile proportions: diversified 41.97%, family-unsupported 23.28%, friend-based 18.36%, restricted 16.39%. Friend-based profile combined engagement and support with conflict; family-centered ties did not guarantee high perceived support. Women reported more contact with family/friends (d=.24/.18), men more received spousal support (d=.23). These are sample estimates and model-dependent profiles, not natural types; entropy does not establish external classification accuracy. Cross-sectional measures, profile composition differing by gender, selection and possible common-method effects preclude causal or individual conclusions. Separate significance by gender does not test moderation. Within-women variation is informative independently of comparisons with men.

E3. Choi & Carr (2026), Partnership Trajectories and Older Adults' Supportive and Strained Friendship Ties: Do Patterns Differ for Men and Women? https://doi.org/10.1177/01640275261459000 . Complete final publisher abstract inspected; article restricted. US Health and Retirement Study, 2006–2022, N=13,495. Married/cohabiting respondents reported less friend support and strain than other partnership-status groups. Partnered men reported less support than partnered women. Newly partnered respondents reported more support and strain, with attenuation after baseline friendship measures were included. This qualifies automatic substitution of friendships by romantic partnerships. Status and transitions differ, baseline selection matters. Exact effects, age range, model, attrition and sex-specific transition contrasts not verified. No causal identification or claim about younger adults.

E4. Gender Differences in the Longitudinal Relationship Between Psychological Distress and Perceived Social Support (2026). https://doi.org/10.1111/aphw.70148 ; PMID42056709. Indexed publisher and primary article excerpts inspected; Wiley 403, PMC browser verification, PubMed cache miss. New Zealand panel, 14 annual waves, N=75,404, 62.1% women. Authors report reciprocal within-person associations of distress and perceived support; direction strength varies by gender and subgroup. Indexed results report a small stronger distress-to-support association among New Zealand European women compared with reverse direction. Do not infer causal loops or global female sensitivity. Exact coefficients, measurement, missingness and ethnic subgroup analyses not fully verified; supporting abstract/excerpt-level evidence only.

E5. Hall (2011), Sex differences in friendship expectations: A meta-analysis. https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407510386192 . Complete final publisher abstract inspected, full text restricted. 37 manuscripts, 36 samples, N=8,825. Overall expectation difference d=.17 favoring females; communion .39, reciprocal loyalty .17, solidarity .03, agency -.34. These measure desired characteristics and normative expectations in same-sex friendship, not actual conduct or received support. Includes developmental samples; no verified adult-only estimate or updated synthesis. Evolutionary interpretation does not identify causal mechanisms.

## 4. Integrated framework and conditional transfer
Separate (1) agency and communion self-concepts/values, (2) actual goals, autonomous endorsement and relative priorities, (3) roles, audience, norms and anticipated versus actual sanctions, (4) available resources, competence, uncertainty and opportunities, (5) communication and actual cooperation/competition, (6) social network structure, relationship-specific perceived and received support, reciprocity, obligations, strain and quality, (7) emotional and practical outcomes over time. These can interact; a conceptual model is not an identified causal system.
Procedure: define actor, target, beneficiary, time, situation and exact behavior. Compare self-report, observed interaction, recipient account and available opportunities without privileging one channel automatically. Distinguish contact amount from emotional closeness, cooperative behavior from motive, other-orientation from submission, assertiveness from aggression, and anticipated rejection from actual rejection. Consider role adaptation, personal preferences, relative desire for change, information, incentives, resource cost, support/strain and measurement. Preserve competing explanations and conditional revisions; distinguish within-person, group and dyadic estimates. No universal female communication or competition rule. Respect explicit choices; no covert tests, exploitation of vulnerabilities or pressure. Individual inference follows IND-03 and INT-02.
Retrospective checks: E1 country reversals qualify universal support advantage; E2 support and strain coexist; E3 baseline adjustment weakens transition interpretation; E4 reciprocal temporal association qualifies one-way support mechanism; E5 expectation is not behavior. WOM-04.1 cooperation null and WOM-04.2 replication failure remain central counterexamples.
Original invented exercise: an adult woman takes an active organizing role in a neighborhood arts association, reports helpful colleagues but tiring commitments, and reduces meeting attendance during a busy season. Agency, communal values, support and strain may coexist. Compare changes in time, opportunity, expected evaluation, goal priority and satisfaction before inferring withdrawal or stable motive. Reduced attendance alone does not establish decreased closeness or a hidden intention. Natural comparable observations and voluntary reports may discriminate some hypotheses. No actual individual assessment.
Checks use known results, internal reasoning only. No independent examiner, blinded test, calibrated individual prediction or causal validation.
