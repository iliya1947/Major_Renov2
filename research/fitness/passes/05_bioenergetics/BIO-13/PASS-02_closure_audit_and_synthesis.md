## 4. Energy-system isolation audit

PASS.

BIO-08 remains authoritative:

- contribution changes with time and state.

BIO-13 therefore rejects:

- “ATP-PC session” as PCr-only;
- “glycolytic session” as glycolysis-only;
- “aerobic session” as oxidative-only.

Canonical wording:

**“focused” means relatively biased toward a demand profile, never biochemical isolation.**

## 5. Intensity audit

PASS.

PMID **41804294**:

- 115 RCTs;
- 3196 participants;
- broad age/training spectrum;
- HIIT showed modestly larger average VO2max and maximal aerobic power/speed improvements vs moderate-to-vigorous continuous training;
- no clear difference for several thresholds, economy or overall physical-performance indices;
- training status, sex, interval type and mode moderated some comparisons.

Canonical rule:

**higher intensity changes the training stimulus and can improve selected outcomes, but does not establish universal superiority across outcomes/populations.**

No blocker.

## 6. Absolute vs relative intensity audit

PASS.

External speed/power can correspond to different internal relative intensity depending on:

- MAP/MAV;
- critical power/speed;
- VO2max;
- threshold profile;
- fatigue;
- environment.

Canonical rule:

**absolute work rate ≠ relative physiological intensity.**

Detailed threshold setting remains TST/PRO.

No blocker.

## 7. Intensity ≠ energy-system ownership

PASS.

Reject:

- “very high intensity = anaerobic only”;
- “moderate intensity = oxidative only.”

Canonical rule:

**intensity changes relative flux and sustainable duration; it does not assign exclusive pathway ownership.**

No blocker.

## 8. Work-duration audit

PASS WITH PROTOCOL LIMITATION.

PMID **38451737**:

- n=14 healthy men;
- 4×10 s / 80 s vs 2×20 s / 160 s;
- matched total sprint time and sprint:rest ratio;
- 20-s condition produced greater average local deoxygenation in VL/RF;
- more short-sprint repetitions did not necessarily increase oxidative response.

This supports:

**absolute work-bout duration can alter acute physiology despite matched total sprint time and ratio.**

It does not establish a universal chronic adaptation hierarchy.

No blocker.

---

## 9. tVO2max audit

PASS AFTER STRONG SURROGATE BOUNDARY.

PMID **42237396**:

- 86 articles;
- 239 HIIT protocols;
- long intervals (≥2 min) produced greater tVO2max than short/moderate intervals on average;
- variable-intensity work intervals also increased tVO2max;
- the authors explicitly state that the dose-response relationship between tVO2max and long-term adaptation requires further investigation.

Canonical rule:

**tVO2max is an acute exposure metric; it is not a validated standalone chronic adaptation dose.**

No blocker.

---

## 10. Long-interval HIIT audit

PASS.

Retain:

- longer intervals often increase acute high-VO2 exposure.

Do not retain:

- longer intervals are universally superior;
- maximizing tVO2max maximizes VO2max;
- ≥2 min is a universal training threshold.

Canonical rule:

**long intervals are one tool for high oxidative exposure, not a universal optimal prescription.**

No blocker.

## 11. Work:rest-ratio audit

PASS.

Rest changes:

- PCr restoration;
- residual metabolite/ion state;
- VO2 during recovery;
- power maintenance;
- density.

Canonical rule:

**work:rest influences the starting state of subsequent bouts.**

No blocker.

---

## 12. Ratio vs absolute durations audit

PASS.

A ratio alone is insufficient.

PMID **38451737** shows different responses under matched sprint:rest ratio and total sprint duration.

PMID **41507409** further supports different acute responses with different absolute work/rest intervals despite matched nominal ratio in circuit exercise.

Canonical rule:

**always specify absolute work and rest durations in addition to the ratio.**

No blocker.

---

## 13. Shorter-rest acute audit

PASS.

PMID **42712884**:

- n=21 men;
- 10×6-s maximal cycle sprints;
- 24-, 18-, or 12-s active recovery;
- shorter rest increased VO2 during work/recovery;
- shorter rest also reduced sprint performance;
- replication reproduced the direction of the prior claim with a smaller effect.

Canonical rule:

**shorter recovery can raise oxidative/cardiorespiratory demand while impairing repeated-bout output.**

No blocker.

---

## 14. Shorter-rest chronic inference audit

PASS.

Do not infer:

- higher acute VO2 = greater chronic VO2max;
- greater fatigue = better conditioning;
- lower sprint output = stronger training stimulus.

PMID **42477971** found no clear universal work:rest moderation of SIT-induced VO2max improvements.

Canonical rule:

**acute internal load and chronic adaptation are distinct evidence questions.**

No blocker.

---

## 15. Hypoxic work:rest evidence audit

PASS WITH STRONG RESTRICTION.

PMID **40833321**:

- n=19 elite track endurance cyclists;
- FiO2 14.5%;
- 6-s/24-s vs 10-s/20-s vs mixed repeated sprints;
- shorter sprints preserved higher peak/mean power, torque and reoxygenation;
- longer sprints produced greater work per sprint, sprint decrement, blood lactate accumulation and muscle desaturation.

Use only as:

**protocol-specific acute evidence under hypoxia in elite cyclists.**

Do not transfer exact ratios/magnitudes to normoxia, other sports or general populations.

PMID **40874288** is a publisher’s correction associated with this publication. The PubMed abstract continues to report the study’s main results, but BIO-13 does not depend on exact corrected detail for closure.

No blocker.

## 17. Mode moderation audit

PASS WITH CAUSAL CAUTION.

PMID **41804294** found exercise-mode moderation in subgroup/meta-regression analysis.

This is not equivalent to randomized evidence that one mode is universally superior.

Canonical rule:

**study-level mode moderation can generate hypotheses; it does not establish a universal mode hierarchy.**

No blocker.

## 18. Running/cycling transfer audit

PASS WITH “NO EQUIVALENCE” BOUNDARY.

PMID **42267259**:

- only 7 eligible studies;
- no statistically significant run-only vs cycle-only differences for treadmill VO2max, cycle VO2max or running performance;
- confidence intervals were wide;
- protocols were heterogeneous and often older;
- some studies had methodological concerns/high risk of bias.

The review explicitly cautions that the evidence does not establish interchangeability.

Canonical rule:

**absence of a detected between-mode difference ≠ proof of equivalence or full transfer.**

No blocker.

## 19. Frequency audit

PASS.

Frequency distributes training exposure across time.

It interacts with:

- per-session dose;
- total weekly load;
- recovery;
- fatigue;
- adherence;
- target outcome.

Canonical rule:

**frequency is a dose-distribution variable, not an independent energy-system switch.**

No blocker.

## 20. SIT frequency / sprint-count audit

PASS WITH POPULATION BOUNDARY.

PMID **42477971**:

- 93 SIT trials across 78 studies;
- n=1188;
- all-out cycling;
- focused on untrained populations;
- mean VO2max improvement ~8.3%;
- each additional sprint had a trivial estimated association with VO2max change;
- intervention duration had a clearer positive association.

The meta-analysis does not authorize:

- more sprints = more VO2max;
- fewer sprints are universally optimal for athletes;
- training frequency is irrelevant to all outcomes.

Canonical rule:

**within untrained cycling SIT evidence, sprint count does not show a meaningful linear VO2max dose-response.**

No blocker.

---

## 21. Intervention-duration audit

PASS AFTER CAUSAL WORDING NARROWING.

PMID **42477971** found larger VO2max improvements with longer intervention duration at study level.

PMID **42961026** also found longer duration associated with greater VO2max effects in team-sport HIIT.

These are study-level associations.

Canonical rule:

**longer intervention duration may be associated with larger adaptation, but meta-regression does not by itself establish an individual causal dose-response.**

No blocker.

---

## 22. Phosphagen-focused training audit

PASS WITH ACUTE-LOGIC BOUNDARY.

Phosphagen focus is defined by:

- brief very-high-power efforts;
- preserving high output;
- recovery sufficient to avoid deliberately turning every repetition into a low-output metabolically accumulated effort.

Direct chronic “phosphagen system isolation” trials do not exist because the system cannot be isolated.

Canonical rule:

**phosphagen-focused = high-power/brief-work bias, not PCr-only training.**

No blocker.

## 23. Power maintenance vs PCr audit

PASS AFTER PROXY BOUNDARY.

Preserved sprint power is useful for task quality.

It is not a direct measurement of PCr concentration.

Canonical rule:

**power maintenance is a performance criterion for the intended task, not a biochemical PCr assay.**

No blocker.

- blood lactate ≠ glycolytic ATP flux;

- oxidative contribution remains active.

Canonical rule:

**glycolytic focus is a task-demand bias, not a lactate target.**

No blocker.

## 27. SIT aerobic-adaptation audit

PASS WITH POPULATION/COMPARATOR BOUNDARY.

PMID **42477971** confirms VO2max improvement after SIT in untrained populations.

A 2026 umbrella review (PMID **40991003**) found SIT improves cardiorespiratory fitness vs control, while superiority to moderate-intensity continuous training is not supported consistently and review quality is often limited.

Canonical rule:

**brief all-out training can produce aerobic adaptation; it is not “aerobically irrelevant.”**

No universal superiority claim is retained.

No blocker.

---

## 28. Mixed-demand audit

PASS.

PMID **42961026**:

- 46 studies;
- 1204 team-sport athletes;
- adding HIIT improved VO2max, maximal aerobic power, ventilatory threshold, Yo-Yo IR performance, and Wingate peak/mean power on average.

Canonical interpretation:

**one training family can improve multiple energetic/performance qualities.**

Do not infer:

- one HIIT protocol maximizes every outcome;
- the same effects apply to every sport/population.

No blocker.

---

## 29. Repeated-sprint sport audit

PASS WITH SPORT-SPECIFIC BOUNDARY.

PMID **41983005**:

- 17 RCTs;
- 386 soccer players;
- field-based RST improved short sprint, repeated-sprint ability, change of direction and high-intensity running;
- no clear overall effect on aerobic capacity.

Canonical rule:

**repeated-sprint training can strongly transfer to intermittent high-intensity sport qualities without guaranteeing a large VO2max/aerobic-capacity effect.**

No blocker.

---

## 30. Concurrent-training interference audit

PASS AFTER ABSOLUTISM REMOVAL.

PMID **41734815**:

- 9 studies;
- 177 healthy adults;
- SIT + resistance training vs resistance training alone;
- no significant pooled differences in lower/upper-body strength, jump or sprint;
- VO2max favored concurrent SIT+RT.

However:

- evidence base is small;
- “no significant difference” does not prove zero interference in every context;
- a published correction exists (PMID **41927039**);
- exact sequencing, volume, recovery and population can modify outcomes.

Canonical rule:

**available SIT+RT evidence does not show a clear pooled interference signal for strength/power outcomes, but this does not establish absence of interference under all concurrent-training designs.**

No blocker.
