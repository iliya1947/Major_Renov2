## 3. Bilateral-deficit audit

PASS.

The 2026 review (PMID 42171503) confirms that BLD:

- is common but not universal;
- appears more often in dynamic than isometric tasks;
- can differ with angle, side, population and movement;
- is affected by biomechanics, stabilization, synergist contribution and shortening velocity;
- changes with training specificity.

Therefore BLD cannot be treated as:

- a stable individual neural trait;
- a global index of nervous-system efficiency;
- a purely cortical phenomenon.

Canonical rule:

**bilateral deficit is a property of a defined task/test context, produced by interacting neural and mechanical factors.**

No blocker.

---

## 4. Bilateral-facilitation audit

PASS.

The opposite sign of the bilateral index can occur.

Available studies demonstrate that:

- bilateral training can shift performance toward facilitation in some tasks;
- bilateral testing can exceed unilateral-sum expectations in some apparatus/tasks;
- force, velocity and power can show different bilateral-index signs in the same exercise.

Therefore:

**bilateral facilitation does not represent a universally superior neural state.**

Canonical rule:

**deficit and facilitation are context-dependent expressions of the same bilateral-performance relation.**

No blocker.

---

## 5. Unilateral-vs-bilateral specificity audit

PASS — HIGH confidence.

The 2026 multilevel meta-analysis (PMID 42458613; 24 RCTs) found:

- unilateral training advantage for unilateral strength;
- bilateral training advantage for bilateral strength;
- no significant hypertrophy difference.

The 2025 Sports Medicine meta-analysis (PMID 39794667) independently found:

- bilateral training superior for bilateral dynamic strength;
- unilateral training superior for unilateral dynamic strength;
- no significant hypertrophy difference.

Canonical rule:

**unilateral vs bilateral strength adaptation follows specificity; neither mode is globally superior.**

No blocker.

---

## 6. Cross-education existence / magnitude audit

PASS.

The 2026 upper-limb meta-analysis (PMID 42323824):

- included 31 studies / 1,214 participants;
- found small significant gains in contralateral MVIC;
- found small significant gains in contralateral 1RM;
- rated certainty low to very low;
- found no significant contralateral muscle-thickness effect.

Lower-limb and earlier broader syntheses also support cross-education of strength.

Therefore:

**contralateral strength transfer is real at group level, but exact magnitude is variable and should not be guaranteed for an individual.**

No blocker.

## 7. Neural-mechanism audit

PASS WITH SITE-ATTRIBUTION LIMIT.

The 2025 J Physiol study (PMID 40500979) demonstrated in the untrained limb:

- increased strength;
- improved force steadiness;
- greater relative shared synaptic input;
- lower common-input variability;
- lower motor-unit recruitment thresholds.

This is direct evidence of contralateral neural adaptation without direct mechanical overload.

However historical neurophysiology syntheses did not find a single consistent TMS/EMG mechanism that tracked cross-education magnitude.

NEU-06 therefore remains binding:

- MEP;
- SICI/cSP;
- H-reflex;
- V-wave;
- EMG;
- motor-unit measures

are not interchangeable mechanism scores.

Canonical rule:

**ordinary cross-education is predominantly neural, but there is no single established cortical, spinal or motor-unit master mechanism.**

No blocker.

---

## 8. Dominance/asymmetry audit

PASS WITH UNCERTAINTY BOUNDARY.

The 2024 meta-analysis (PMID 38568258):

- found only three eligible studies / five effect sizes;
- provided minimal support for dominant→nondominant superiority;
- judged the estimate unstable due to sparse evidence.

Therefore:

**dominance is not a sufficient reason to choose which side to train for cross-education.**

This does not prove exact symmetry; small true directional differences remain possible.

No blocker.

---

## 9. Strength-transfer vs hypertrophy audit

PASS — CRITICAL CONSTRUCT BOUNDARY.

The 2026 upper-limb meta-analysis (PMID 42323824) found:

- contralateral strength gains;
- no significant contralateral muscle-thickness gain.

The 2024 high-load unilateral-arm RCT cited in PASS-01 likewise found contralateral strength transfer while muscle-thickness gains were confined to directly trained arms.

Canonical rule:

**contralateral strength gain does not imply contralateral hypertrophy.**

This distinction is closure-critical and passes.

No blocker.

---

## 10. Immobilization-preservation audit

PASS WITH CONTEXT BOUNDARY.

The 2026 immobilization meta-analysis (PMID 42141765) found that training the available limb:

- attenuated strength loss in the immobilized limb;
- produced a small muscle-size preservation effect overall, stronger in some proximal-immobilization conditions.

This is not equivalent to ordinary hypertrophy in a healthy untrained limb.

Canonical rule:

**preservation during disuse ≠ growth above baseline in an ordinarily active contralateral limb.**

No blocker.

---

## 11. Limited-equipment applicability audit

PASS AS PRACTICAL HEURISTIC.

Two distinct mechanisms remain separated.

### Direct unilateral loading

With fixed limited external load, unilateral execution can increase the relative challenge per active limb.

This is a mechanical/load-distribution consequence.

### Cross-education

Training one limb can transfer some strength to the other limb.

This is a contralateral neural/performance effect.

Canonical rule:

**when both limbs are healthy and trainable, unilateral variants can be useful primarily because they increase per-limb loading and develop unilateral strength; cross-education is secondary, not the main hypertrophy strategy.**

No blocker.

---

## 12. Testing-boundary audit

PASS.

Unilateral and bilateral tests are not interchangeable because:

- bilateral deficit/facilitation can change the relation between conditions;
- stabilization and apparatus alter the result;
- force, velocity and power can show different bilateral-index signs;
- dominance and familiarization can influence interpretation.

Canonical rule:

**bilateral index must always be attached to a defined test protocol and outcome.**

No blocker.
