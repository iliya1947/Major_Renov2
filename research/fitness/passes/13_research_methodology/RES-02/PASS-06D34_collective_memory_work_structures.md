# 3. Lineage

Collective Memory Work developed in West Germany in the 1970s–1980s through Frigga Haug and the Frauenformen collective at the intersection of:

- feminism;
- Marxist theory;
- social science;
- political practice;
- critical psychology.

The 1987 English book **Female Sexualization** presented the collective's work on how women actively participate in the construction of gendered identities and the sexualisation of the body.

### Core historical move

Instead of treating:

> socialisation = norms imposed on a passive person,

CMW asks how people actively participate in constructing themselves within historically available social relations and meanings.

### Project lineage representation

**feminist/Marxist collective inquiry  
→ written autobiographical memory-scenes  
→ collective textual analysis  
→ self/socialisation/power reconstruction  
→ expanded capacity for action.**

---

# 4. What “Collective Memory Work” means

The name is easy to misread.

CMW means:

> a group collectively working with **individual memories**.

It does **not** mean:

> research into a society’s collective/cultural memory.

This distinction is explicit in current CMW guidance and the 2022 methodology paper.

### Critical rule

Do not route every study using the phrase:

> collective memory

to CMW.

History/memory-studies work on monuments, public commemoration, national narratives or shared cultural memory is a different research domain unless CMW's actual procedure is used.

---

# 5. Canonical scientific target

CMW asks how participants’ remembered everyday experiences reveal and reproduce:

- taken-for-granted meanings;
- socialisation;
- power relations;
- gendered/classed/racialised norms;
- language/discourse;
- identity constructions;
- body norms;
- available possibilities for action.

### Canonical architecture

shared problematic/topic  
+  
group of participant-researchers  
+  
self-generated memory-scenes  
+  
scene-level textual scrutiny  
+  
collective discussion  
+  
contradictions / absences / language / power made visible  
+  
recursive rewriting/reanalysis  
+  
connection to theory/social structure  
+  
reconstruction of meaning  
+  
possible increase in capacity for action.

# 6. Researcher and researched are deliberately blurred

A foundational CMW move is weakening the conventional:

researcher  
→ studies  
participant.

Members of the collective are positioned as active:

- memory producers;
- textual analysts;
- interpreters;
- critics;
- theorists;
- learners;
- co-researchers.

The 2022 article explicitly describes CMW as moving beyond the subject/object distinction.

### Critical rule

Participants are not merely:

> people from whom memories are extracted.

They participate in **working the memories**.

---

# 7. Group ownership begins before memory writing

A fuller CMW process starts before individual stories are written.

The group may collectively develop:

- the problematic;
- scope;
- presuppositions;
- research question;
- trigger word/phrase;
- practical rules;
- how writing will occur;
- how workshops will be run.

### Project implication

Extract:

1. who set the topic?;
2. who selected the trigger?;
3. who defined the research question?;
4. who controlled analysis?;
5. who controlled publication?

Participation can be strong during workshops yet collapse later during publication.

---

# 8. Trigger construction

The group normally develops a **trigger** that evokes a specific remembered scene.

Possible forms:

- word;
- phrase;
- sentence;
- prompt;
- concrete situation.

A strong trigger should avoid forcing an already polished ideological answer.

### Example in sport/PA

Instead of:

> Explain gender oppression in gyms.

a more CMW-compatible prompt could be:

> A time when I felt watched in the gym.

The latter invites a concrete scene rather than a rehearsed abstract position.

---

# 9. Memory-scene, not life history

Foundational CMW does not ask participants to write a full autobiography.

The preferred empirical object is a:

- particular episode;
- action;
- event;
- brief scene;
- concrete remembered moment.

The 2024 sport/PA chapter recommends focusing on a memory lasting only:

- seconds;
- minutes;

rather than an entire life period.

### Critical rule

CMW intentionally **fragments biography**.

The purpose is to make small, ordinary moments available for detailed interrogation rather than to impose a smooth retrospective life narrative.

---

# 10. Historical Haug writing rules

The classic Haug/Crawford-derived procedure commonly asks participants to:

1. write a short memory of one concrete event/scene;
2. write in **third person**;
3. use pseudonyms;
4. include abundant concrete/trivial/sensory detail;
5. avoid retrospective explanation/justification/interpretation;
6. avoid writing a biographical summary.

### Why third person?

Historically, third-person writing was designed as a distancing/alienation device.

It helps the writer treat their earlier self as:

- not self-evident;
- observable;
- strange enough to inspect.

### Why detail?

Apparently trivial details may expose:

- norms;
- assumptions;
- bodily discipline;
- contradiction;
- power.

---

# 11. Third person is not a universal modern requirement

This is an important taxonomy correction.

The 2024 sport/PA methodology chapter explicitly allows groups to choose:

- first person;
- third person.

It states advantages/disadvantages for each.

## Third person

May create analytic distance and expose taken-for-granted assumptions.

## First person

May bring the participant closer to the scene and produce a more evocative account.

### Project rule

Store:

- Haugian third-person rule followed?;
- first-person adaptation?;
- group rationale?;

but **do not reject a study as non-CMW solely because first person was used**.

Confidence: **HIGH** for contemporary sport/PA routing.

---

# 12. Anti-explanation rule

Classic CMW asks writers to stay close to:

- what happened;
- what was seen;
- what was heard;
- what was felt;
- what was said;
- bodily sensations;
- concrete environmental detail.

The initial memory-scene should avoid immediately explaining:

> “I acted this way because society had taught me…”

because that can overwrite the contradictions the later group analysis is designed to investigate.

### Critical rule

Initial description and later interpretation are deliberately separated in classic CMW.

Modern variants can adapt this, but should explain the adaptation.

---

# 13. Memory is not treated as a factual recording

CMW does not require the assumption that a memory is:

> a perfectly accurate replay of the historical event.

The 2024 sport chapter explicitly frames memory as a way of **working with experience**, with meaning neither singular nor fixed.

### Scientific target

The important question is often not:

> Did every remembered detail happen exactly this way?

but:

> How is the experience remembered, constructed, narrated, revised and socially interpreted now?

### Claim boundary

CMW memory should not automatically be used as independent proof of:

- historical chronology;
- prevalence;
- another person's intentions;
- objective causal events.

---

# 14. Memory itself is constructed

The remembered scene is shaped by:

- current identity;
- present language;
- later knowledge;
- cultural narratives;
- forgetting;
- emotional salience;
- social expectations.

CMW treats this constructedness as analytically useful rather than as a defect to eliminate.

### Critical rule

A contradiction or gap in a memory text can be **data for analysis**, not merely recall error.

---

# 15. Collective textual scrutiny is the methodological core

After memory-scenes are written, the group works on them together.

Members may ask:

- Who is present?
- Who is absent?
- What does each person do?
- What language is used?
- Which details are unexplained?
- What is taken for granted?
- What contradictions appear?
- Which identities are invoked?
- What bodily sensations appear?
- What objects/materials matter?
- Where is power operating?
- What alternative meanings are possible?

### Critical rule

CMW is not complete when the memory is written.

The **collective working of the text** is constitutive.

---

# 16. The text is scrutinised, not the author psychologically diagnosed

The initial analytic object is the written memory-scene.

Group questioning should avoid collapsing into:

> Why are you psychologically like this?

Instead, participants work through:

- words;
- actions;
- absences;
- scene construction;
- relationships;
- social meanings;
- contradictions.

### Project rule

CMW ≠ group psychoanalysis.

---

# 17. Recursive analysis

CMW analysis is often iterative:

memory writing  
→ group analysis  
→ rewriting/reconstruction  
→ further discussion  
→ comparison to prior assumptions/theory  
→ renewed analysis.

The 2022 methodology article explicitly describes parallel/recursive data generation and analysis.

### Project implication

Do not force CMW into a linear:

> collect → lock dataset → analyze

model.

---

# 18. Rewriting is analytic work

Rewriting can:

- remove adult hindsight;
- recover sensory detail;
- clarify action;
- expose contradiction;
- remove clichés;
- situate bodies/objects;
- change perspective;
- open alternative interpretations.

### Critical rule

The revised memory should not automatically be treated as:

> more historically factual.

It is an analytically reworked representation.

---

# 19. Analysis of language

Haugian CMW treats language as socially saturated.

Language supplies:

- ready-made meanings;
- normative categories;
- accepted explanations;
- moral framings.

Therefore analysis may examine:

- clichés;
- pronouns;
- labels;
- passive/active constructions;
- moral terms;
- body words;
- gendered descriptions;
- evaluative language.

### Sport example

Terms such as:

- “fit”;
- “strong”;
- “fat”;
- “girly”;
- “aggressive”;
- “disciplined”;
- “lazy”;
- “athletic”;

can encode power and normative bodily expectations.

---

# 20. Contradiction is not noise

CMW assumes people often smooth contradictions in everyday self-understanding.

Memory writing and collective scrutiny aim to make visible inconsistencies such as:

- wanting to participate while hiding ability;
- feeling empowered while simultaneously self-surveilling;
- enjoying sport while fearing bodily judgement;
- valuing strength while fearing muscularity;
- describing a coach as supportive while narrating coercive practices.

### Scientific value

Contradiction may expose the interaction of:

- agency;
- social norm;
- adaptation;
- resistance.

---

# 21. Agency and structure

A central CMW contribution is refusing both extremes:

## Pure determinism

> society simply writes norms onto passive people.

## Pure individualism

> people freely construct themselves without structural limits.

CMW examines how people actively construct themselves within historically available:

- meanings;
- institutions;
- gender regimes;
- class relations;
- racialised structures;
- bodily norms;
- family/school/sport systems.

---

# 22. Societalisation / subjectification

Haugian work is interested in how people become certain kinds of subjects through everyday practices.

In sport/PA this can include becoming:

- “sporty girl”;
- “non-athletic child”;
- “tough athlete”;
- “good patient”;
- “disciplined exerciser”;
- “acceptable feminine athlete”;
- “real man” in a gym context.

### Critical rule

The claim is not that identity is simply imposed.

CMW studies the person's **active participation** in adopting, negotiating, resisting and reproducing these positions.

---

# 23. Feminist and political roots are not optional historical trivia

CMW emerged as an explicitly political knowledge practice.

Original goals included:

- challenging male-dominated science;
- making women’s everyday experience scientifically meaningful;
- understanding gendered socialisation;
- linking personal experience to social structures;
- increasing capacity for action.

### Project classification

A contemporary CMW study can be adapted beyond the exact politics of the original collective.

But stripping away:

- power;
- social structure;
- participatory analysis;
- praxis;

risks turning CMW into generic memory elicitation.

---

# 24. Praxis / capacity for action

CMW historically aims beyond:

> knowing for knowing’s sake.

The 2022 methodology article emphasizes **generalised action potence**:

new insight  
→ changed perception/positioning  
→ potentially expanded collective capacity to act.

### Boundary

This does not mean every CMW study must produce a political campaign or measurable behavioral intervention.

Praxis may involve:

- changed self-understanding;
- reinterpreting prior norms;
- altered professional practice;
- collective awareness;
- future action possibilities.

---

# 25. CMW is not therapy

Haug explicitly distinguished memory-work from therapy.

The objective is not clinical treatment of distress.

Participants may nevertheless experience the process as:

- validating;
- connecting;
- supportive;
- “therapeutic.”

### Critical rule

A participant reporting therapeutic benefit does not turn CMW into psychotherapy.

Nor should a researcher promise therapeutic outcomes from a research workshop.

---

# 26. CMW vs consciousness-raising alone

CMW shares roots with feminist consciousness-raising.

But it adds structured scientific work:

experience  
+  
written memory text  
+  
collective textual analysis  
+  
theory  
+  
systematic interrogation.

### Critical rule

Sharing stories of oppression in a group is not sufficient for CMW.

The memories must be **worked**.

---

# 27. Data-generation and analysis are interwoven

Current CMW scholarship describes multiple empirical layers:

1. written memory-scenes;
2. group discussion/analysis;
3. rewritten memories;
4. workshop transcripts;
5. group-process observations;
6. optional photographs/artifacts;
7. theoretical/literature material entering discussion.

### Project rule

Store each layer separately.

Do not pretend the “data” are only the original written stories.

---

# 28. Theory enters during inquiry

CMW is not purely inductive.

Existing theory may be used to:

- sharpen the problematic;
- challenge assumptions;
- frame social relations;
- interpret language/power;
- revise theoretical concepts.

The 2022 article describes a process in which findings can be compared with existing theory and potentially contribute to theory reformulation.

### Critical rule

Theory should not be used merely to overwrite what participants say.

The collective analytic process remains central.

---

# 29. Flexible process, stable core

Haug intentionally resisted reducing CMW to one rigid step-by-step recipe.

Contemporary CMW allows variation in:

- group size;
- number of sessions;
- trigger selection;
- first vs third person;
- creative methods;
- photographs;
- acting scenes;
- online delivery;
- rewriting style;
- representation.

### Stable core for taxonomy

A credible CMW study should still retain:

- participant-researcher group;
- individual memory-scenes;
- collective working of memories;
- recursive interpretation;
- social/power inquiry;
- participatory ownership/analysis.

---

# 30. Group size is guidance, not a design law

The 2022 online article suggests, as a rule of thumb:

- minimum around 5;
- maximum around 15;

for productive plenary work.

The 2024 sport chapter suggests approximately:

- no fewer than 6 total;
- no more than 12, unless breakout groups are used.

### Project rule

These are **practical heuristics**, not universal validity thresholds.

A study should not be rejected purely because N falls outside them.

Extract actual group structure and justify feasibility.

# 31. Online / digital CMW is methodologically viable

The 2022 COVID-era paper demonstrated a complete online CMW process across five online workshops.

It retained:

- shared topic development;
- memory scenes;
- collective textual analysis;
- recursive discussion;
- participant-researcher parity;
- group reflection.

### Taxonomy implication

Physical co-presence is **not constitutive**.

Online CMW is valid if the collective analytic architecture survives.

# 32. Digital delivery changes group dynamics

Online CMW may change:

- hierarchy cues;
- embodied presence;
- turn-taking;
- privacy;
- disclosure;
- fatigue;
- access;
- group cohesion.

The 2022 study reported surprisingly strong egalitarian group dynamics, but explicitly treated this as contextual rather than universal.

### Critical rule

Do not infer:

> Zoom automatically equalizes power.

That is an empirical/contextual observation, not a method law.

# 33. Ethics and vulnerability

CMW can evoke memories involving:

- body shame;
- sexuality;
- exclusion;
- abuse;
- disability;
- humiliation;
- authority;
- injury;
- family;
- coaches/teachers;
- peers.

Because memories are analyzed collectively, ethical risk includes:

- emotional distress;
- involuntary disclosure;
- group confidentiality failure;
- third-party identification;
- power pressure to share;
- publication risk.

### Project extraction

Record:

1. trigger sensitivity;
2. voluntary disclosure;
3. ability to decline;
4. group confidentiality agreement;
5. stopping/withdrawal process;
6. support resources;
7. publication consent;
8. third-party protection.

---

# 34. Safety and trust are methodological, not merely administrative

The 2024 sport chapter emphasizes:

- rapport;
- empathy;
- trust;
- vulnerability;
- safe/private space;
- collective awareness of roles.

These are required because CMW asks participants to expose memories to detailed group scrutiny.

### Critical rule

A consent form cannot substitute for a group culture in which participants can safely disagree, withhold and challenge.

---

# 35. Power is never fully equal

The 2024 sport chapter explicitly states that power among group members is never perfectly balanced.

Possible inequalities:

- academic ↔ student;
- supervisor ↔ trainee;
- coach ↔ athlete;
- clinician ↔ patient;
- experienced researcher ↔ novice;
- adult ↔ child;
- majority ↔ marginalised identity.

### Project rule

Do not accept claims of:

> “we were all equals”

without examining:

- role hierarchy;
- recruitment;
- facilitation;
- speaking time;
- analytic authority;
- publication control.

---

# 36. Participatory workshop equality can disappear at publication

A particularly important current finding:

during workshops, participants may share:

- topic ownership;
- memory generation;
- analysis.

But academic researchers may later regain control of:

- manuscript selection;
- framing;
- authorship;
- journal decisions.

The 2024 chapter explicitly identifies this as a power challenge.

### Project extraction

Store participation by stage:

1. question;
2. trigger;
3. memory writing;
4. analysis;
5. rewriting;
6. theory;
7. manuscript;
8. authorship;
9. dissemination.

---

# 37. Authorship is a methodological/power issue

If participants are treated as co-researchers during analysis but disappear from the publication process, the published paper may reproduce the researcher/researched hierarchy CMW intended to weaken.

### Critical rule

Co-authorship is not automatically mandatory for every participant.

But the study should transparently explain:

- who wrote the paper;
- how memories were selected;
- whether participants reviewed representation;
- why authorship differed from workshop participation.

---

# 38. CMW vs narrative inquiry

Both can study remembered experience.

## Narrative inquiry

May examine:

- plot;
- identity;
- temporality;
- storytelling;
- master narratives;
- narrative performance.

It does not require participant-researchers collectively analyzing one another’s written memories.

## CMW

Requires a group that:

- writes memory-scenes;
- collectively scrutinizes them;
- rewrites/reinterprets them;
- interrogates power/socialization.

### Critical rule

A narrative interview about childhood sport ≠ CMW.

---

# 39. CMW vs focus group

A focus group typically involves:

moderator  
→ group discussion  
→ transcript analyzed by researchers.

CMW involves:

participant-researchers  
→ individual memory-scene generation  
→ shared text  
→ collective analytic work  
→ recursive rewriting/meaning-making.

### Critical rule

Group discussion alone does not establish CMW.

The written/self-generated memories and participant-led collective analysis materially change the architecture.

---

# 40. CMW vs Oral History

D29 established Oral History as a historical source-producing methodology.

## Oral History

- historical question;
- narrator;
- recorded interview;
- memory as testimony;
- preservation;
- archive;
- future reuse.

## CMW

- current participant-researcher group;
- short self-written memory-scenes;
- collective textual scrutiny;
- recursive analysis;
- socialisation/power/praxis.

### Critical rule

CMW is not designed primarily to create a durable historical testimony archive.

Memory is used as material for collective present-day analysis.

---

# 41. CMW vs collective/cultural memory studies

Memory studies may ask:

- how nations remember;
- how communities commemorate;
- how archives construct public memory;
- how media shape shared memory.

CMW instead asks a group to work collectively on members’ individual memories.

### Project rule

Term matching is insufficient.

Verify the actual procedure.

---

# 42. CMW vs collaborative autoethnography

Both may involve:

- self-experience;
- multiple researchers;
- shared reflection;
- culture/power;
- collaborative writing.

## CAE

Multiple researcher-participants jointly analyze their own experiences in relation to culture/social life.

## CMW

Has a more distinctive procedural emphasis on:

- short memory-scenes;
- trigger construction;
- textual dissection;
- recursive rewriting;
- group analysis;
- Haugian socialisation/power logic.

### Project rule

If the Haugian memory-working architecture is explicit:

→ route to CMW.

If shared self-experience is analyzed collectively without that architecture:

→ CAE remains the stronger parent.

Hybrid labels can be preserved.

---

# 43. CMW vs participatory research / PAR

CMW is strongly participatory.

But participation alone does not identify CMW.

## PAR

Typically includes:

problem  
→ collective inquiry  
→ action  
→ reflection  
→ further action.

## CMW

Uses:

memory-scene  
→ collective textual analysis  
→ reconstruction of meaning/socialisation  
→ possible expanded action capacity.

### Critical rule

CMW can support praxis and action.

But an iterative action cycle is not required as the parent architecture.

---

# 44. CMW vs Collective Biography

Contemporary sport literature notes overlap between:

- collective memory work;
- memory work;
- collective biography.

Some authors use these labels closely or as related traditions.

However, collective biography has also developed poststructuralist/posthuman variants that may depart from classic Haugian CMW.

### Project rule

Preserve the author's exact label.

Route to CMW only when the core memory-working architecture is actually present.

Confidence: **MODERATE-HIGH**.

---

# 45. CMW vs Post-Qualitative Inquiry

CMW predates PQI and is not intrinsically identical to post-qualitative research.

However, the 2023 sport paper and 2025 chapter demonstrate a productive hybrid/bridge.

The 2025 chapter argues CMW can support PQI because it can:

1. weaken conventional researcher-participant power relations;
2. move beyond purely discursive experience;
3. trouble static/self-contained concepts of memory, identity and being;
4. foreground sensory, affective and material dimensions.

### Project rule

For the future D38 PQI pass:

- do not collapse CMW into PQI;
- store **CMW parent/method layer** and **PQI ontological/analytic orientation** separately when both are explicit.

# 46. Sport/PA exemplar — young women’s sporting memories

Clift, Francombe-Webb & Merchant 2023 conducted a collaborative memory-work project with:

- six young women aged 19–22;
- two researchers;
- childhood sport/physical activity/play as the substantive domain.

The project examined:

- gendered power;
- bodily awareness;
- bodily regulation;

# 52. What CMW can establish

Depending on implementation, CMW can support contextual claims about:

- socialisation processes;
- gendered/body norms;
- identity construction;
- how language shapes self-understanding;

# 53. What CMW cannot establish by itself

It does not establish:

- prevalence;
- incidence;
- population frequency;
- diagnostic accuracy;
- predictive accuracy;
- average treatment effect;
- intervention efficacy;
- biological mechanism;
- causal mediation;
- objective historical chronology;
- accuracy of another person's remembered motives.

### Critical rule

Depth of collective interpretation is not a substitute for a causal or epidemiologic design.

# 54. “Universal in the particular” must not be overread

Haugian language can speak of revealing:

> the universal in the particular.

For RES-02 this must **not** be silently translated into:

> statistical universal generalization.

A CMW group can reveal how a particular everyday scene is structured by wider social relations.

It does not estimate how common that structure is in a population.

# 60. Confidence

## HIGH

Well-supported:

- CMW is a distinct group- and text-based research/learning methodology developed by Frigga Haug/Frauenformen.
- Its roots are feminist, Marxist and political/praxis-oriented.
- The group works collectively with individual memories, not “collective memory” as a cultural-memory object.
- Participant-researchers generate and collectively analyze written memory-scenes.
- Short concrete scenes are preferred over smooth autobiography.
- Classic Haugian memory writing uses third person, detailed description and avoidance of premature explanation.
- Contemporary CMW can adapt the classic rules; first-person writing is explicitly allowed in current sport/PA guidance.
- Collective textual scrutiny, recursive discussion and rewriting are constitutive.
- Contradictions, language, socialisation, agency/structure and power are major analytic targets.
- Researcher/researched boundaries are intentionally weakened.
- CMW is praxis-oriented and can seek expanded capacity for action.
- CMW is explicitly not therapy.
- Online CMW is methodologically viable.
- Direct sport/physical-activity use is established, including current methodological guidance.
- Current work connects CMW with PQI without making them identical.
- CMW alone cannot establish prevalence, causal treatment effects or objective historical fact.

## MODERATE-HIGH

Project routing choices:

- treating CMW/memory-work as one parent family while preserving “collective biography” as a related/overlapping author label rather than universal synonym;
- requiring participant collective analysis as a parent-family criterion;
- treating third-person narration as lineage-specific but non-mandatory;
- using the 36-point quality router above;
- routing Haug-inspired sport-history papers without full participant-researcher architecture as adaptations rather than canonical CMW.

## MODERATE

- exact boundary across all literature between CMW, memory work and collective biography.

Reason:

authors sometimes treat them as:

- synonyms;
- related methods;
- historically connected but theoretically divergent traditions.

The project therefore preserves author terminology and classifies by architecture.
