### LEA-03 — Reinforcement learning and prediction error
Canonical block:
`research/psychology/qualification/03_cognitive_psychology/LEA-03_reinforcement_learning_prediction_error_state_representation_dopamine.md`

Established:

1. RL model fit != biological mechanism;
2. scalar RPE != all forms of prediction mismatch/surprise;
3. TD-RPE depends on represented state and temporal structure;
4. state representation/state aliasing can mimic learning-rate differences;
5. `alpha` is a model-dependent update weight, not stable learning ability;
6. volatility and stochasticity require opposite adaptive updating responses;
7. learning rates can be adaptive/nonlinear rather than fixed;
8. real-time reward spacing can control per-experience behavioral and dopaminergic learning;
9. reward representation can itself require goal-dependent computation/compression;
10. working memory and rich trial-history memory can be mistaken for standard RL updating;
11. perseveration != model-free learning;
12. inverse temperature/value sensitivity != a pure exploration trait;
13. model-free/model-based computation != habit/goal-directed behavior;
14. MF/MB arbitration changes with WM load and environmental demand;
15. intermediate/compressed/abstract world models prevent a complete binary MF/MB taxonomy;
16. two-step-task signatures can mix transition knowledge, WM, perseveration, exploration, heuristics, and latent-state inference;
17. state prediction errors != reward prediction errors;
18. parameter recovery, model recovery, identifiability, predictive accuracy, and test-retest reliability are separate validation targets;
19. within-task reliability does not imply cross-task generalizability of same-named parameters;
20. dopamine RPE evidence remains strong but does not justify a homogeneous scalar dopamine = RPE identity;
21. dopamine can encode feature-specific/generalized and value-free action prediction errors;
22. neural model-free/model-based signals need not equal actual behavioral control;
23. a 52-question Reinforcement Learning Inference Gate is canonical.

### LEA-04 — Habit formation and goal-directed control measurement
Canonical block:
`research/psychology/qualification/03_cognitive_psychology/LEA-04_habit_formation_goal_directed_arbitration_devaluation_contingency_automaticity_validity.md`

Established:

1. goal-directed action requires R–O knowledge and current outcome-value sensitivity;
2. devaluation/contingency insensitivity alone does not identify the S–R mechanism;
3. devaluation manipulation effectiveness must be validated before assigning habit scores;
4. overtraining effects in humans are paradigm-dependent and sometimes fail replication;
5. automatic response preparation may emerge before later goal-directed correction;
6. response initiation, preparation, outcome devaluation, and remapping are not interchangeable measurements;
7. within-task reliability need not imply cross-task convergence;
8. frequency, routine, automaticity, skill, computational model-free control, and S–R habit are distinct constructs;
9. human 2025–2026 tests strongly constrain claims about habit measurement validity;
10. a 48-question Habit Inference Gate and 18/18 closure audit are canonical.

### LEA-05 — Extinction, return of responding, and reconsolidation
Canonical block:
`research/psychology/qualification/03_cognitive_psychology/LEA-05_extinction_renewal_reinstatement_inhibitory_learning_reconsolidation_validity.md`

Established:
1. extinction attenuation does not uniquely prove original memory erasure;
2. spontaneous recovery, renewal (ABA/ABC/AAB), reinstatement, reacquisition and instrumental resurgence are separable recovery tests;
3. inhibitory learning is a leading explanation but not a unique implementation;
4. retrieval, destabilization, and reconsolidation are distinct inferential steps;
5. prediction error can participate in extinction/state inference and does not uniquely establish reconsolidation;
6. reminder timing, memory strength/age, context and probe structure constrain interpretation;
7. classic animal molecular reconsolidation effects should not be confused with inconsistent human retrieval-extinction efficacy;
8. successful updating and low relapse are behavioral outcomes, not proof of a specific trace modification;
9. a 54-question Extinction/Reconsolidation Inference Gate and 18/18 audit are canonical.

## Phase B / MOT completed

### MOT-01 — Motivation foundations
Canonical block:
`research/psychology/qualification/06_motivation_and_behavior/MOT-01_motivation_foundations_needs_incentives_valuation_effort_goals_boundaries.md`

Established:
1. motivation is separated from learning, valuation, executive control and performance;
2. NIMH Reward Responsiveness, Reward Learning and Reward Valuation are distinct research constructs;
3. SDT autonomy, competence and relatedness are separated from physiological needs, desires, incentives and goals;
4. intrinsic, identified, integrated, introjected and external regulations are distinct;
5. wanting, liking and reward learning must not be conflated;
6. perceived value, probability, delay, cost, opportunity, effort and ability can independently affect behavior;
7. cognitive effort and physical effort, boredom and demand avoidance, and effort cost versus effort value are distinguished;
8. intention is not behavior; goal selection, implementation and adaptive disengagement differ;
9. stable individual motivational traits require reliability, validity and transfer evidence;
10. 50-question Motivation Inference Gate and 18/18 closure audit are canonical.

### MOT-02 — Reward valuation, effort allocation and measurement
Canonical block:
`research/psychology/qualification/06_motivation_and_behavior/MOT-02_reward_valuation_probability_delay_effort_opportunity_cost_measurement_validity.md`

Established:
1. reward magnitude, subjective utility, reward probability, uncertainty, delay and effort represent different valuation dimensions;
2. reward delay, effort delay, task duration and effort sequence must not be conflated;
3. physical and cognitive effort measures have distinct ability and cost confounds;
4. effort-demand tasks must control perceived success, performance errors, and effort–delay confounds;
5. opportunity-cost and boredom accounts are conditional, with 2025 counterevidence against a single universal cost explanation;
6. discounting functions are model hypotheses, not direct psychological trait or neural identifiers;
7. reliability, parameter recovery, cross-task validity and ecological generalization are independent requirements;
8. 50-question Reward Valuation and Effort Inference Gate with 18/18 internal audit is canonical.

### MOT-03 — Goal pursuit, self-determination and adjustment
Canonical block:
`research/psychology/qualification/06_motivation_and_behavior/MOT-03_goal_pursuit_self_determination_intention_action_persistence_disengagement_measurement.md`

Established:
1. goal value, attainability, commitment, intention, implementation and progress are separable;
2. autonomous and controlled regulation can coexist and differ from intrinsic pleasure;
3. approach/avoidance and self-determination are distinct dimensions;
4. 2023 physical-activity meta-analysis quantified a 47.6% intention–behavior gap among intenders under its domain-specific definitions;
5. 2024 implementation-intentions meta-analysis examined 642 tests, without implying universal compliance or habit;
6. goal specificity, difficulty, feedback and experience moderate action results;
7. disengagement, reengagement and flexibility are distinct, and goals have different hierarchical levels;
8. 2025/2026 goal-adjustment meta-analysis covered 235 studies with important heterogeneity, publication-bias and causal-inference limitations;
9. persistence and disengagement require context/attainability checks rather than character judgments;
10. 52-question Goal Pursuit Inference Gate and 18/18 internal closure audit are canonical.
