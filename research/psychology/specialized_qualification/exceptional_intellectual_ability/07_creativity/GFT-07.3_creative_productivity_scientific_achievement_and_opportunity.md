## 3. Realization and evaluation gate (PRACTICAL_HEURISTIC)
Separate ability (conditional capacity), expertise (acquired domain knowledge/skill), creative potential indicators, goal/interest, engagement and exploration, evaluation/revision, implementation, completed valid products, external visibility/recognition. No fixed stage sequence or identified causal network; feedback may change opportunity, preferences, training and future actions. Publication count, patent count and prizes are not interchangeable. Validity/reproducibility, novelty, usefulness, contribution, uptake, recognition and time/cost all different dimensions; one product may be valuable without recognition, another visible but invalid. Credits in collaborative work not automatically equivalent contributions; field, career stage, authorship, publication/access and evaluation windows matter. Do not rank human worth by output or treat unrealized potential as a demonstrated exceptional ability. 
| Claim | Minimum relevant observation | Alternative explanation |
|---|---|---|
| High ability | Appropriate valid measures and repeated performance under defined conditions | Domain knowledge or measurement/selection |
| Creative quality | Independent rubric for novelty, appropriateness and validity | Rarity, confidence or evaluator halo |
| Productivity | Completed comparable products/time, quality and exposure | Task difficulty, team size, resources, field |
| Realization | Engagement, progress, barriers, revisions, completed outputs | Changed goals, inaccessible tools, cost, knowledge |
| Recognition/impact | Dissemination, evaluator/uptake and attribution evidence | Visibility, network, prior status, time lag, topic |
| Sustainable development | Repeated comparable outcomes, costs, voluntary goals, transfer | One-off opportunity, selection or unsustainable effort |
Do not equate ratio count/time with total creative value, or adjust per-time when time opportunity measured poorly. A metric can change with incentive without improving target; check independent quality criteria. Zero output may indicate no opportunity, no interest, delayed projects, invalid attempted work, private work, or limited competence; observation underdetermines cause. High output may reflect expertise, resources, collaboration or lower standard, not necessarily superior intelligence. Preserve self-defined goals: exceptional ability does not create an obligation to maximize productivity or public recognition.
Illustrative stochastic distinction: if each of n hypothetical independent projects has equal probability p=.1 of a defined success, P(at least one)=1−(1−p)^n; n=2 -> .19, n=10 -> .6513. Not a scientific estimate of publication success, p may vary with quality, feedback, opportunity and correlated tasks. An increase in total successes/max can coexist with unchanged or reduced mean quality. More work not necessarily optimal under costs. Compare within-person change with between-person volume, distinguish selection of already productive creators and survival/censoring. Hot-streak findings are aggregate models of observed career products, not a recipe or proof of cognition. 
Model minimal candidate chain: conditions and capabilities -> choices/actual activity -> quality-controlled completed work -> exposure/evaluation -> recognized outcome, feedback to earlier terms. Distinguish predictive associations from intervention effects and selection/collider (e.g. conditioning on being published or awarded may distort capacity/opportunity associations). Do not assume all paths needed; add a proposed interaction/feedback only with contrasting predictions and evidence (07.2). No numerical person-specific probabilities without validated basis.

## 4. Applied protocol
1. Define the goal actually valued, outcome/time/domain and acceptable constraints; clarify whether question concerns capacity, interest, activity, quality, output, recognition or their relationship. Don't substitute institutional recognition for goal.
2. Reconstruct observable timeline (opportunities, training, resources, engagement, work-in-progress, completion, evaluation, follow-up). Record evidence separately from interpretation, not retrospective selection of best episodes.
3. Form competing explanations: competence gap vs opportunity; interest/goal change vs implementation barrier; slow high-quality work vs inefficient strategy; low visibility vs quality; individual vs team contribution. State independent distinguishing observations before new evidence, use safe matched tasks or existing records where warranted. Do not infer underlying trait from absence or presence of achievement.
4. Evaluate interventions at relevant stage: skill practice with transfer, resource access, task structuring, feedback, quality criteria, choice and incentives. Test actual effects, include costs and adverse outcomes; don't impose persistence or productivity goals. Evidence for lab-task change does not establish career effect.
5. Update narrow dependent claim when data change, preserve verified accomplishments and uncertainty. No adult IQ estimate, diagnosis or claims of hidden exceptional potential by default. Route motivation to GFT-09, development to GFT-08, tail to GFT-16/17/20, model integration to GFT-18/19.
