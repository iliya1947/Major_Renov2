## 3. Thermoregulation audit

PASS.

Exercise heat balance depends on:

- metabolic heat production;
- external mechanical work;
- radiation;
- convection;
- conduction;
- evaporation.

Environmental load depends on more than air temperature:

- radiant/solar load;
- humidity;
- airflow;
- clothing;
- water exposure.

Canonical rule:

**ambient temperature alone does not define thermal strain.**

No blocker.

## 4. Sweat production vs evaporative cooling audit

PASS.

Sweating is only useful for heat loss to the extent that sweat evaporates.

The 2025 human cycling study at 33°C showed increasing humidity:

- reduced skin-to-air vapor-pressure gradient;
- reduced environmental evaporative capacity;
- reduced sweating efficiency;
- increased thermal strain;
- reduced self-paced power at high/very-high humidity.

Canonical rule:

**sweat produced ≠ sweat evaporated ≠ heat removed.**

No blocker.

## 5. Heat-acclimation audit

PASS.

The 2025 quantitative review/meta-regression included 211 papers and found average heat-acclimation adaptations including:

- lower resting/end-exercise HR;
- lower resting/end-exercise core temperature;
- plasma-volume expansion;
- increased whole-body sweat rate;
- improved exercise capacity/performance in heat.

Protocol characteristics influenced adaptation magnitude.

The 2026 comparative meta-analysis further found greater adaptation from exercise in hot versus thermoneutral conditions for several thermoregulatory/cardiovascular outcomes and heat performance, while thermoneutral training could still improve some outcomes.

Canonical rule:

**heat acclimation is dose/protocol dependent, not a one-size-fits-all event.**

No blocker.

## 6. Cold-response audit

PASS.

Cold exposure can produce:

- cutaneous vasoconstriction;
- reduced peripheral skin blood flow;
- thermogenesis/shivering when required;
- sympathetic activation;
- increased thermal discomfort.

The 2025 repeated cold-air study showed 8 days of 8°C exposure did not restore extremity perfusion, skin temperature, thermal comfort or manual dexterity.

Canonical rule:

**core-temperature defense can coexist with peripheral performance impairment.**

Cold-air and cold-water exposures remain physiologically distinct because heat transfer differs substantially.

No blocker.

## 7. Fluid-balance / hydration audit

PASS WITH CERTAINTY BOUNDARY.

The 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis of 24 randomized endurance studies found:

- no clear time-trial/fixed-duration benefit for hydration versus minimal/no fluid in the limited evidence;
- time-to-exhaustion estimates favored hydration but were imprecise;
- lower mean core temperature and HR with hydration, with substantial heterogeneity;
- no clear performance advantage for programmed/individualized drinking versus self-regulated drinking;
- all critical/important estimates were rated **very low certainty**.

Therefore the closure does not retain:

- a universal dehydration-performance cutoff;
- one universally superior drinking strategy;
- “more fluid is always better.”

Canonical rule:

**hydration can modify thermophysiological strain, but exact performance benefit and optimal dose are context dependent.**

Hydration also cannot remove the environmental evaporative ceiling.

No blocker.

## 8. Cardiovascular-drift audit

PASS.

During prolonged exercise, especially in heat:

- HR tends to rise;
- stroke volume tends to fall;
- relative physiological intensity rises despite unchanged external workload.

The 2020 heat study found substantial HR increase and stroke-volume decline from 15 to 45 min, with associated reduction in VO2max.

Heat/hyperthermia and dehydration can amplify drift, but drift is not specific to dehydration alone.

Canonical rule:

**cardiovascular drift is an integrated prolonged-exercise response, not a dehydration meter.**

No blocker.

## 9. Heat/humidity-performance audit

PASS.

High humidity reduces the water-vapor pressure gradient between skin and environment and therefore environmental evaporative capacity.

The 2025 trained-cyclist study directly demonstrated:

- lower Emax;
- lower sweating efficiency;
- higher peak core temperature;
- lower self-paced power at higher humidity.

Current pooled heat evidence also supports impaired exercise capacity/performance during heat exposure.

Canonical rule:

**humidity is a physiological performance variable because it constrains evaporation.**

No blocker.

## 10. Heat-illness clinical-boundary audit

PASS.

ACSM 2023 states exertional heat stroke is a true medical emergency requiring rapid recognition, exercise cessation and rapid total-body cooling.

WMS 2024 provides graded recommendations for prevention, recognition and treatment of heat illness.

PHY-20 therefore does not diagnose or treat:

- exertional heat exhaustion;
- exertional heat injury;
- exertional heat stroke;
- exercise-associated hyponatremia;
- heat-related renal injury;
- medication-related heat intolerance.

Canonical rule:

**suspected exertional heat illness crosses immediately into MED/emergency management.**

No blocker.

## 14. Closure criterion test

Normalized-tree question:

> как heat/cold/hydration меняют response/performance?

Result: **PASS.**

Canonical synthesis:

### Heat

`metabolic heat + environmental heat load`
`→ skin blood flow + sweating`
`→ evaporation-dependent cooling`.

If:

`required evaporation > environmental evaporative capacity`

then:

`heat storage ↑ → thermal/cardiovascular strain ↑ → sustainable performance ↓`.

### Hydration

`fluid loss - replacement`
modifies:

`plasma volume + cardiovascular strain + thermoregulation`

but does not eliminate the environmental evaporative limit.

### Cold

`cold exposure`
`→ vasoconstriction + thermogenesis`
`→ core protection`

while:

`peripheral cooling`
can reduce
`dexterity / local force / neuromuscular performance`.
