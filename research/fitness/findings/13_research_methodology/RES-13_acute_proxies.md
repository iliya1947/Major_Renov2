# RES-13 — Acute proxies: EMG, MPS, hormones, lactate, pump и soreness

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v1.0**  
Дата: 2026-11-16  
Область: 13 — Методология исследований

## 0. Question Card

### Decision
Когда acute/mechanistic measurements можно использовать для понимания training stimulus, а когда они не позволяют предсказывать chronic hypertrophy/strength outcomes.

### Question type
Mechanistic / methodological / surrogate-outcome interpretation.

### Critical outcomes
1. Distinguish mechanistic evidence from validated longitudinal proxy.
2. EMG limitations.
3. Acute MPS limitations.
4. Acute systemic hormone limitations.
5. Lactate/metabolic stress/cell swelling/pump limitations.
6. DOMS/soreness limitations.
7. Define project proxy-evidence rules.

### Closure criterion
Есть explicit hierarchy preventing acute proxies from substituting longitudinal outcomes unless validation exists.

---

## 1. Core principle

Acute response can be:
- biologically real;
- mechanistically informative;
- reproducible;

and still be a poor predictor of long-term adaptation.

Therefore:

**acute response ≠ validated longitudinal proxy by default.**

To use acute marker as surrogate for chronic outcome, need evidence that:
1. marker lies on relevant causal pathway;
2. intervention-induced changes in marker predict outcome changes;
3. competing pathways do not dominate;
4. relationship generalizes across interventions/populations;
5. measurement itself is valid/reliable.

## 2. Mechanism vs prediction

Mechanistic evidence answers:
> что происходит сразу после stimulus?

Longitudinal outcome evidence answers:
> что изменилось после repeated training over time?

These are related but not interchangeable.

Example:
exercise A creates larger acute signal than B.
This does **not** establish:
exercise A causes more hypertrophy after 12 weeks.

## 3. Surface EMG

Surface EMG amplitude reflects electrical activity detected near electrodes.

Useful for:
1. timing of activation;
2. relative within-muscle comparisons under controlled conditions;
3. motor-control questions;
4. fatigue/electrical behavior questions.

### Not directly equal to
- muscle force;
- fiber recruitment count;
- mechanical tension;
- hypertrophy stimulus;
- future muscle growth.

### Why acute EMG → hypertrophy inference is weak
Signal affected by:
1. electrode placement;
2. adipose tissue;
3. muscle geometry;
4. cross-talk;
5. normalization;
6. conduction velocity;
7. fatigue;
8. joint position;
9. dynamic shortening/lengthening.

2022 Sports Medicine analysis specifically concluded acute surface EMG amplitude is not a validated predictor of longitudinal hypertrophy.

### Project rule
**EMG can support biomechanics/neurophysiology, never serve as sole exercise-ranking tool for hypertrophy.**

## 4. EMG and exercise comparisons

“Exercise A has 20% higher EMG than B” does not automatically mean:
- more force;
- more tension;
- more growth;
- better exercise.

Potential explanations:
- worse leverage requiring more activation;
- different muscle length;
- instability;
- normalization artifact;
- fatigue;
- smaller active muscle volume.

Interpretation must remain mechanism-specific.

## 5. Acute Muscle Protein Synthesis (MPS)

Resistance exercise and protein intake can acutely increase myofibrillar protein synthesis.

This is biologically relevant because chronic muscle growth requires net protein accretion.

But:
**single early acute MPS response is not a simple hypertrophy forecast.**

Evidence:
- early post-exercise MPS in untrained state has not consistently correlated with later hypertrophy;
- training status changes acute MPS response;
- early MPS can partly reflect repair/remodeling rather than net new contractile tissue.

### Project rule
MPS informs mechanism/nutrition physiology.
Do not infer program superiority from one acute MPS study unless supported by longitudinal adaptation evidence.

## 6. Timing problem in MPS

MPS varies over:
- hours;
- days;
- training status;
- feeding state.

One biopsy window may miss:
- duration;
- integrated response;
- remodeling vs hypertrophic synthesis.

Therefore:
acute 1–4 h response cannot be treated as total anabolic response.

## 7. Acute molecular signaling

Markers:
- mTOR-related phosphorylation;
- p70S6K;
- ribosomal signaling;
- gene expression.

These can identify pathway activation.

But stronger acute phosphorylation does not necessarily yield greater chronic hypertrophy.

Example longitudinal evidence has shown acute signaling differences without corresponding group differences in hypertrophy.

### Project rule
Molecular signaling = mechanism support, not direct prescription outcome.

## 8. Acute systemic hormones

Common acute measures:
- testosterone;
- growth hormone;
- IGF-related signals;
- cortisol.

Resistance exercise can produce transient systemic changes.

Current reviews indicate acute post-exercise systemic hormone spikes are not supported as meaningful drivers/predictors of hypertrophy magnitude in the simplistic “bigger spike = bigger growth” sense.

Important distinction:
- endogenous hormones are physiologically necessary;
- transient exercise-induced serum spike is not equivalent to causal hypertrophy dose-response.

### Project rule
Do not select program by maximizing acute testosterone/GH response.

## 9. Lactate

Lactate is:
- metabolite;
- transportable fuel/intermediate;
- marker of glycolytic demand in context.

It is not:
- waste product causing DOMS;
- direct hypertrophy score;
- proof of effective set.

High lactate can reflect:
- long sets;
- short rest;
- large muscle mass;
- high glycolytic demand.

### Project rule
Lactate informs bioenergetics, not direct growth ranking.

## 10. Metabolic stress

“Metabolic stress” historically proposed as hypertrophy mechanism.

Current mechanistic review literature questions treating metabolites/metabolic stress as an independent major causal driver of hypertrophy.

Potential metabolic signals may interact with training physiology, but practical prescription should not equate:
more burn/lactate = more growth.

## 11. Cell swelling / pump

Acute “pump” reflects:
- blood flow;
- fluid shifts;
- metabolite/osmotic changes.

It can indicate that tissue was active and perfused.

But current reviews do not support pump/cell swelling as a validated independent determinant of chronic hypertrophy.

### Project rule
Pump may be:
- subjective feedback;
- enjoyable;
- temporary;
but not a primary efficacy metric.

## 12. DOMS / soreness

DOMS reflects complex response to unfamiliar/loading stress.

Affected by:
- novelty;
- eccentric work;
- exercise selection;
- training status;
- repeated-bout effect.

DOMS does not directly measure:
- hypertrophy;
- quality of stimulus;
- adequate volume.

More soreness can coexist with:
- same growth;
- worse adherence;
- greater damage/fatigue.

### Project rule
DOMS is recovery/context information, not effectiveness score.

## 13. Muscle damage

Damage can occur with resistance training, especially novel/eccentric loading.

But hypertrophy does not require maximizing damage.

Early training may direct MPS toward repair.
Repeated-bout adaptation can reduce damage while hypertrophy continues.

### Project rule
Do not deliberately chase muscle damage as hypertrophy target.

## 14. Acute strength/power potentiation

Some acute performance measures are useful when target itself is acute:
- post-activation performance enhancement;
- warm-up effectiveness;
- caffeine effect.

Here acute performance is not “proxy”; it is the actual outcome.

Therefore:
**proxy problem depends on target outcome.**

## 15. Acute fatigue markers

Velocity loss, force decline, RPE, lactate can quantify acute fatigue/load characteristics.

They may be useful for:
- set termination;
- fatigue management;
- dose description.

They cannot automatically rank chronic adaptation without longitudinal validation.

## 16. When an acute marker becomes useful for prescription

Stronger case if:

1. mechanism well established;
2. marker responds specifically to relevant intervention dimension;
3. within-person measurement reliable;
4. acute marker predicts chronic outcome across studies/individuals;
5. manipulating marker changes outcome;
6. no simpler direct measure exists.

Most popular hypertrophy acute proxies do not meet all these conditions.

## 17. Validated surrogate concept

A surrogate outcome should capture intervention effect on the real outcome sufficiently reliably.

High correlation between marker and outcome across people is not enough.

Need intervention-level validation:
changes caused by treatment in surrogate should track changes caused by treatment in target outcome.

Project rule:
do not call a mechanistic biomarker “surrogate” casually.

## 18. Exercise-science proxy hierarchy

### Level 1 — Direct chronic outcome
Examples:
- longitudinal MRI muscle volume;
- standardized 1RM change for strength target;
- actual adherence.

Highest decision relevance.

### Level 2 — Validated/strong intermediate
Only if evidence establishes predictive/causal connection for target decision.

### Level 3 — Mechanistic acute marker
Examples:
- MPS;
- molecular signaling;
- EMG;
- lactate;
- hormones.

Useful for explanation, not replacement.

### Level 4 — Subjective correlate
Examples:
- pump;
- burn;
- soreness.

Can inform experience/recovery but not chronic efficacy.

## 19. Triangulation use

Acute mechanisms can increase confidence when:
1. longitudinal outcome supports effect;
2. mechanism direction is coherent;
3. multiple methods converge.

Example:
longitudinal hypertrophy difference + biomechanical rationale + mechanistic support.

Mechanism strengthens interpretation.
It does not create outcome effect by itself.

## 20. Practical examples

### Example A — curls
Exercise A shows higher biceps EMG than B.
No longitudinal data.

Conclusion:
A produces higher measured EMG under study conditions.
Not:
A grows biceps more.

### Example B — short rest
Short rest creates more lactate/pump.
Long-term hypertrophy equal or better with longer rest in some contexts.

Conclusion:
acute metabolic response does not determine growth superiority.

### Example C — protein dose
Dose B causes larger acute MPS than A.
Need longitudinal evidence before claiming more long-term muscle gain.

### Example D — DOMS
New exercise causes severe soreness.
This can signal novelty/damage, not superior stimulus.

## 21. Project language standard

Allowed:
- “increased acute EMG amplitude”;
- “increased MPS over measured window”;
- “greater lactate accumulation”;
- “larger acute hormone response”;
- “greater soreness”.

Not allowed without longitudinal validation:
- “more anabolic”;
- “better for growth”;
- “recruits more therefore grows more”;
- “causes hypertrophy because pump/lactate high”.

## 22. Proxy evidence record

For any acute marker:

1. target outcome;
2. marker;
3. biological rationale;
4. measurement validity;
5. acute intervention effect;
6. individual correlation with chronic outcome;
7. intervention-level prediction;
8. longitudinal validation;
9. competing mechanisms;
10. practical status:
   - explanatory;
   - promising;
   - validated;
   - rejected as proxy.

## 23. Confidence

### HIGH
- acute surface EMG is not a validated hypertrophy predictor;
- acute MPS response, especially early/untrained, cannot be assumed to predict chronic hypertrophy;
- acute systemic hormone spikes should not be used as simple hypertrophy proxy;
- lactate/pump/soreness are not validated direct measures of chronic hypertrophy;
- mechanistic evidence and longitudinal outcome evidence answer different questions.

### MODERATE
- exact causal role of some intermediate molecular processes remains active scientific research; project should not overstate absence of any biological role simply because practical proxy validity is poor.

### PRACTICAL_HEURISTIC
- four-level proxy hierarchy and proxy record.
