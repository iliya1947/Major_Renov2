# 2. Central synthesis correction: the “38 items” are not 38 equal designs

The D44 coverage list contains a mixture of:

- parent methodologies;
- methodology families;
- cross-cutting paradigmatic routes;
- governance architectures;
- temporal architectures;
- quality architectures;
- nested-study architectures.

Therefore it would be wrong to produce a flat list such as:

> phenomenology = longitudinal = reflexivity = Indigenous methodology = PQI = case study = Photovoice

as if all were one ontological type.

### Canonical rule

Every qualitative study should be represented as a **multi-layer record**.

No single label is expected to encode the whole design.

# 3. Canonical qualitative study architecture

For RES-02, a qualitative study is represented through the following layers.

## Layer Q0 — Parent empirical study / evidence-production context

Examples:

- standalone qualitative primary study;
- RCT + qualitative component;
- cohort + qualitative component;
- intervention-development study;
- process evaluation;
- mixed-methods study;
- case-study program;
- implementation study.

This layer answers:

> What larger empirical architecture contains the qualitative work?

### Critical rule

A qualitative component embedded in an RCT does **not** inherit randomized causal inference.

# 5. Cluster A — experience / interpretation / conception

## Phenomenology

Scientific target:

- lived experience;
- meaning/structure of experience.

Routes:

- descriptive;
- interpretive/hermeneutic;
- IPA as distinct idiographic interpretive phenomenological methodology.

Supports:

- experiential/meaning claims.

Does not establish:

- prevalence;
- causal effects.

---

## Phenomenography

Scientific target:

- qualitative variation in ways people experience/understand the same phenomenon.

Canonical output:

- categories of description;
- outcome space.

Supports:

- conception/variation claims.

Does not establish:

- distribution/prevalence of conceptions.

---

## Standalone Hermeneutic Inquiry

Scientific target:

- historically/linguistically situated interpretation and understanding.

Core:

- pre-understanding;
- hermeneutic circle;
- dialogue;
- fusion of horizons.

Distinct from:

- hermeneutic phenomenology;
- IPA.

---

## Heuristic Inquiry

Scientific target:

- phenomenon directly lived by researcher and co-researchers.

Core:

- researcher experience as constitutive resource;
- tacit knowing;
- immersion;
- incubation;
- illumination;
- explication;
- creative synthesis.

Distinct from:

- autoethnography;
- phenomenology;
- narrative inquiry.

---

## Interpretive Interactionism

Scientific target:

- consequential biographical/interactional experiences linked to social/public/institutional context.

Core:

- epiphany;
- thick description/interpretation;
- deconstruction/capture/bracketing/construction/contextualization.

Supports:

- contextualized personal ↔ public/institutional interpretation.

---

# 6. Cluster B — descriptive / applied interpretive

## Qualitative Description

Scientific target:

- comprehensive low-inference description close to participant language.

Low inference does not mean:

- interpretation-free.

---

## Interpretive Description

Scientific target:

- practice-oriented conceptual interpretation.

Especially relevant to:

- clinical;
- rehabilitation;
- applied health practice.

Distinct from:

- QD;
- generic thematic study.

---

## Generic Qualitative Research

Permissible only when authors genuinely use a generic approach and report:

- epistemic position;
- sampling;
- collection;
- analysis;
- reflexivity;
- methodological congruence.

### Critical rule

Methodology-unclear must not be silently “repaired” into generic qualitative research.

---

# 7. Cluster C — theory / process / situation generation

## Grounded Theory

Scientific target:

- generate explanatory/process theory grounded in iterative empirical analysis.

Core:

- constant comparison;
- memoing;
- theoretical sampling;
- category integration.

Routes:

- Glaserian;
- Straussian;
- constructivist/Charmaz.

### Critical rule

Open–axial–selective coding is not universal GT.

---

## Dimensional Analysis

Schatzman-derived GT methodology.

Scientific target:

- dimensions/relationships leading to situation-specific explanatory theory.

Core:

- dimensionalization;
- designation;
- differentiation;
- integration;
- explanatory matrix;
- perspective selection.

Distinct from:

- generic dimensional coding;
- Straussian GT;
- Situational Analysis.

---

## Situational Analysis

Clarke-derived GT extension.

Scientific target:

- complex situation including human/nonhuman/material/discursive elements.

Core:

- situational maps;
- social worlds/arenas maps;
- positional maps;
- relational mapping.

The situation itself is the analytic unit.

---

## Ethnography

Scientific target:

- shared practice;
- culture;

Core:

Subtypes/modes include:

- digital/netnography;

- critical ethnography;
- rapid ethnography;

### Critical rule

These are not automatically separate parent families.

Scientific target:

- how everyday work is coordinated through translocal ruling relations.

Core:

- standpoint;
- problematic/disjuncture;
- work knowledge;
- text-mediated coordination;
- local → translocal tracing.

Distinct from:

- ethnography inside an institution;
- organizational case study;
- discourse analysis.

---

## Ethnomethodology

Scientific target:

- members’ methods for producing recognizable local social order.

Core:

- accountability;
- indexicality;
- ethnomethodological reflexivity;
- documentary method;
- practical competence;
- unique adequacy where relevant.

Conversation Analysis is a major related/distinct program, not the whole of EM.

---

## Video-Reflexive Ethnography

Scientific target:

- situated practice made visible and reflexively reworked through video.

Canonical architecture:

ethnographic familiarisation

Core principles:

- exnovation;
- collaboration;
- reflexivity;
- care.

Distinct from:

- ordinary video ethnography;
- stimulated recall;
- Participatory Video;

# 9. Cluster E — case / incident / pattern architecture

## Case Study

Scientific target:

- in-depth understanding of a bounded real-world case.

Structures:

- single/multiple;
- holistic/embedded.

Can be:

- qualitative;
- quantitative;
- mixed.

### Critical rule

Case Study is broader than the qualitative branch and should be retained as a cross-paradigm bounded-case architecture.

Distinct from:

- case report;
- case-control;
- N-of-1/SCED.

---

## Critical Incident Technique

Scientific target:

- significant/critical incidents relative to a defined activity/aim.

Core analytic unit:

- incident, not participant.

Output:

- categories of helpful/hindering/effective/ineffective incidents or other author-defined criticality structure.

CIT can be:

- full methodology;
- nested method.

Store author enactment.

---

## Consensual Qualitative Research

Scientific target:

- intensive within-case interpretation + systematic cross-case patterns.

Core:

- domains;
- core ideas;
- cross-analysis;
- analyst consensus;
- auditor.

### Critical rule

Consensus is constitutive in CQR specifically and is not a universal qualitative-quality requirement.

- eurhythmia;
- arrhythmia;

### Critical rule

# 17. Cross-cutting route — Indigenous/decolonizing methodology

No pan-Indigenous universal methodology is created.

Canonical routing requires:

specific Nation/community/tradition  
+ exact ontology/epistemology/axiology  
+ governance/authority  
+ methodology  
+ empirical design  
+ methods/analysis  
+ data sovereignty/stewardship  
+ dissemination/reuse.

### Mandatory rules

- Indigenous participants alone ≠ Indigenous methodology.
- Generic cultural adaptation ≠ Indigenous methodology.
- consultation ≠ governance.
- individual consent ≠ community governance.
- yarning / Dadirri / Talking Circles are not universal interchangeable methods.
- Indigenous methodology can govern qualitative, quantitative or mixed empirical designs.

### Consequence

This route sits **above/across** parent methodology rather than replacing every empirical-design field.

# 18. Cross-cutting route — Post-Qualitative / posthuman-new-materialist inquiry

PQI is not represented as one atomic design.

### Route 1 — STRICT_PQI

- explicit poststructural/posthuman onto-epistemology;
- refusal of pre-existing humanist qualitative methodology;
- theory/concepts constitutive;
- conventional data/analysis/representation categories challenged.

### Route 2 — PQI_INFORMED_HYBRID

- named methodology remains;
- postqualitative theory materially reconfigures inquiry.

### Route 3 — POSTHUMAN_NEW_MATERIALIST_ORIENTATION

- posthuman/new-materialist ontology is central;
- strict PQI refusal is not necessarily claimed.

Associated practices stored separately:

- thinking with theory;
- plugging in;
- diffraction;
- assemblage thinking;
- affective inquiry;
- writing-as-inquiry;
- worlding.

### Critical rule

Posthuman vocabulary alone ≠ PQI.

# 19. Cross-cutting temporal architecture — longitudinal qualitative research

Longitudinal qualitative research requires alignment of:

1. longitudinal question;
2. time-structured/repeated empirical material;
3. explicit analysis of change/continuity.

Store:

- prospective vs retrospective;
- same-case trajectory vs recurrent cross-sectional;
- fixed-clock vs event-contingent timing;
- attrition/missing waves;
- longitudinal entity.

### Critical rule

Repeated interviews alone ≠ longitudinal qualitative inquiry.

# 25. Visual/video router

When images/video are used, classify first:

## Photo elicitation

Researcher/participant images used to prompt discussion.

→ data-generation method.

## Photovoice

Participant-generated photos  
+ participant interpretation/dialogue  
+ participatory/action orientation.

→ dedicated participatory-visual route.

## Participatory Video

Participants materially shape video production/message/editing/audience.

→ dedicated participatory-video route.

## Video-Reflexive Ethnography

Situated practice is filmed and returned to implicated practitioners for collaborative reflexive analysis/exnovation.

→ dedicated VRE route.

## Digital Storytelling

Participant-centered multimodal story production.

→ method/representation layer unless a stronger parent architecture is independently present.

### Critical rule

The presence of a camera does not determine design.

# 30. Claim-limit matrix

Qualitative methodology primarily supports claims about:

- meaning;
- experience;
- practice;
- process;
- culture;
- discourse;
- social action;
- context;
- identity;
- institutional organization;
- practical order;
- participant priorities;
- theory/concepts;
- historical reconstruction;
- relational/power structures;
- situated change;
- embodied/material relations.

It generally does **not by itself** estimate:

- prevalence;
- incidence;
- average treatment effect;
- comparative efficacy;
- diagnostic accuracy;
- prediction performance;
- biological mechanism;
- causal mediation;
- population representativeness.

### Critical rule

A study may contain additional design layers that support stronger inference.

Example:

RCT  
+ qualitative process study.

The RCT may support treatment-effect inference.

The qualitative component may support implementation/context/experience interpretation.

Each claim must be linked to the layer that supports it.

# 32. Sampling / adequacy synthesis

No universal qualitative sample-size threshold exists.

Adequacy should be routed to:

- scientific aim;
- methodology;
- sample specificity;
- quality of dialogue/material;
- analysis intensity;
- claim scope.

Important distinctions:

- code saturation vs meaning saturation;
- theoretical saturation in GT;
- outcome-space adequacy in phenomenography;
- case adequacy in Case Study;
- trajectory adequacy in longitudinal qualitative work;
- incident/category coverage in CIT;
- participation/action fidelity in participatory visual work;
- practical-order/collection adequacy in EM/CA.

### Rule

If a study stopped for convenience:

report pragmatic stopping.

Do not retroactively call it saturation.

# 33. Quality synthesis

The cross-family umbrella is:

**methodological integrity / methodological coherence.**

Ask:

1. Is the scientific question coherent with the methodology?
2. Is sampling coherent with the object?
3. Are empirical materials sufficient for the claim?
4. Does analysis enact the stated methodology?
5. Is researcher influence reflexively examined?
6. Are contradictions/negative cases handled?
7. Is representation traceable to empirical material?
8. Are ethics and power aligned with the method?
9. Are claims calibrated to the design?

### Important non-universal practices

Do not require all studies to have:

- saturation;
- member checking;
- multiple coders;
- intercoder reliability;
- triangulation;
- prolonged engagement.

Each is methodology/purpose dependent.
