# ANA-01 — Анатомическая терминология, положение, плоскости, оси и движения

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-18**  
Область: 02 — Анатомия

## 0. Decision

Создать единый язык, на котором дальше описываются структуры, движения, техника и биомеханика без бытовой неоднозначности и без смешивания anatomical reference language с детальной 3D-механикой суставов.

## 1. Current terminology standard

FIPAT/IFAA maintains the international standard terminology for human anatomical sciences.

Current published adult gross-anatomy framework:
**Terminologia Anatomica, second edition (TA2)**.

Current IFAA/FIPAT pages state:
- TA2 was published online in 2019;
- TA2 has IFAA approval;
- TA2 remains the published Terminologia Anatomica listed by IFAA/FIPAT.

FIPAT's 2025 end-of-year report, published 2026-02-08, states that work is underway on a future **TA2.5** and that the plan is to publish it when complete.

Therefore project rule:
**use TA2-compatible terminology now; recheck version if IFAA publishes TA2.5 or a later replacement.**

Do not repeat the legacy exact wording "adopted in 2020"; that date was not confirmed in the current official pages checked during revalidation.

## 2. Anatomical position

Reference orientation:

1. body upright;
2. head/gaze forward;
3. upper limbs at the sides;
4. palms face anteriorly;
5. lower limbs oriented forward.

In anatomical position:
- radius is lateral to ulna;
- thumb is lateral;
- palm is anterior;
- forearm is supinated.

The reference position standardizes description; it is not a required exercise posture.

## 3. Directional terminology

Core:
- superior / inferior;
- anterior / posterior;
- medial / lateral;
- proximal / distal;
- superficial / deep;
- ipsilateral / contralateral;
- unilateral / bilateral.

Useful region-specific terms:
- radial / ulnar;
- palmar / dorsal;
- tibial / fibular;
- plantar / dorsal.

Project rule:
prefer precise anatomical directions over vague "inside/outside", especially when limb orientation changes.

## 4. Planes

### Median plane
Single vertical midline plane dividing the body into right and left halves.

### Sagittal planes
Planes parallel to the median plane.

### Frontal / coronal plane
Vertical plane dividing anterior and posterior portions.

### Transverse / axial / horizontal plane
Plane dividing superior and inferior portions.

### Oblique plane
Any plane not aligned with the three standard orthogonal planes.

Project rule:
planes are **reference categories**. Real exercise trajectories may be oblique/multiplanar.

## 5. Conventional axes

Elementary pairing:

1. sagittal-plane movement → mediolateral axis;
2. frontal-plane movement → anteroposterior axis;
3. transverse-plane movement → longitudinal/superior-inferior axis.

Typical examples:
- flexion/extension → sagittal;
- abduction/adduction and lateral flexion → frontal;
- axial rotation → transverse.

Boundary:
these are communication/teaching conventions, **not proof of one fixed real joint axis through ROM**. Actual 3D joint axes/coordinate systems belong to MEC.

## 6. Core movement terms

### Flexion / extension
Use joint-specific anatomical definitions. The shortcut "flexion always decreases the angle" is not sufficiently universal for all regions.

### Hyperextension
Extension beyond neutral/anatomical reference. The term alone does not mean pathology or danger.

### Abduction / adduction
Movement away from / toward the relevant reference axis. For digits, local axes matter.

### Medial / lateral rotation
Rotation around a longitudinal segment axis. Internal/external rotation are accepted common equivalents.

### Circumduction
Composite sequential movement combining movements such as flexion, abduction, extension and adduction; not a separate single-axis rotation.

### Lateral flexion
Side-bending of neck/trunk.

### Horizontal abduction / adduction
Useful shoulder descriptors when the humerus moves approximately in the transverse plane.

### Opposition / reposition
Thumb-specific complex movement pair.

## 7. Forearm pronation and supination

Pronation/supination are **radioulnar forearm movements**, not wrist rotation.

### Supination
Radius and ulna become relatively parallel; in anatomical position the palm faces anteriorly.

### Pronation
Radius rotates/crosses relative to the ulna; in anatomical position the palm faces posteriorly.

With the elbow flexed around 90°, "palm up/down" is a useful shortcut but posture-dependent.

Training rule:
describe elbow movement, forearm orientation and wrist position separately.

## 8. Wrist

Use:
- flexion;
- extension;
- radial deviation;
- ulnar deviation.

Radial/ulnar deviation is preferred project wording over wrist abduction/adduction because it is less ambiguous.

## 9. Ankle and foot

Use:
- dorsiflexion;
- plantarflexion;
- inversion;
- eversion.

These are anatomical descriptors; detailed multi-joint foot mechanics belong to MEC.

## 10. Scapular movement

Keep scapular motion distinct from humeral/glenohumeral motion.

Project vocabulary:
1. elevation;
2. depression;
3. protraction;
4. retraction;
5. upward rotation;
6. downward rotation;
7. anterior/posterior tilt;
8. internal/external rotation when 3D detail is required.

Avoid ambiguous "shoulder retraction" when the intended structure is the scapula.

Detailed scapular coordinate conventions and coupled shoulder mechanics → ANA-04/MEC.

## 11. Position vs movement

Always distinguish:

1. starting position;
2. segment orientation;
3. active movement;
4. final position.

Example:
a forearm may be pronated while the elbow is flexing.

## 12. Training-description standard

For later exercise analysis:

1. name joint/segment;
2. specify starting position;
3. specify segment orientation;
4. state main anatomical movement;
5. give approximate plane if useful;
6. describe forearm/grip orientation separately;
7. describe scapular movement separately;
8. specify ROM endpoints only when decision-relevant.

Example:
**"elbow flexion with forearm supinated"** is preferable to "curl the hand upward".

## 13. Common errors prohibited

1. Calling pronation/supination "wrist rotation".
2. Calling scapular retraction "shoulder retraction" without clarification.
3. Using internal/external rotation without naming the joint/segment.
4. Treating all abduction as movement relative to whole-body midline.
5. Treating standard planes as literal constraints on real motion.
6. Treating hyperextension as automatically pathological.
7. Equating neutral grip with one exact forearm angle.
8. Confusing anatomical position with exercise technique.
9. Confusing joint position with current movement direction.

## 14. Evidence / confidence

### HIGH

1. IFAA/FIPAT is the international anatomical terminology authority.
2. TA2 remains the current published Terminologia Anatomica framework on the official pages checked 2026-11-18.
3. Anatomical position, directional terms and standard planes are foundational conventions.
4. Pronation/supination are radioulnar forearm movements.
5. Standard anatomical planes/axes are reference language, not a complete 3D joint-mechanics model.
6. Precise shoulder description requires distinguishing scapular from humeral motion.

### MODERATE / convention-dependent

Exact English/Russian synonym choice and some 3D scapular rotation wording vary with teaching/coordinate-system convention; later biomechanics must state its coordinate system.
