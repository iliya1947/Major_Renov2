# NEU-07 — Bilateral effects / cross-education

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-19**  
Область: 04 — Neurophysiology / Motor Learning

## 0. Core decision

Unilateral and bilateral strength performance are task specific.

Canonical model:

`unilateral vs bilateral task structure`
+
`neural + biomechanical context`
→
`bilateral deficit / equivalence / facilitation`.

With unilateral training:

`trained limb`
→
`direct neural + muscular adaptation`

and

`contralateral untrained limb`
→
`predominantly neural strength transfer`
without requiring
`equal contralateral hypertrophy`.

Project rule:

**bilateral deficit/facilitation and cross-education are real but context-specific phenomena, not universal nervous-system traits.**

## 1. Bilateral deficit

Bilateral deficit means bilateral performance is lower than expected from corresponding unilateral performances.

Project rule:

**BLD is task/test/training specific.**

It can depend on:

- movement type;
- joint angle/ROM;
- stabilization;
- synergist contribution;
- shortening velocity;
- population;
- training history.

Do not interpret BLD as a global score of neural efficiency.

## 2. Bilateral facilitation

The bilateral index can also show facilitation.

Project rule:

**bilateral facilitation is not a universally superior neural state.**

Force, velocity and power can even show different bilateral-index signs in the same task.

## 3. Training specificity

Unilateral and bilateral resistance training show strength specificity.

Project rule:

**unilateral training tends to favor unilateral strength; bilateral training tends to favor bilateral strength.**

Current meta-analytic evidence does not show a clear hypertrophy superiority of either mode.

## 4. Cross-education

Cross-education is improved performance in the contralateral untrained limb after unilateral training.

Project rule:

**unilateral resistance training can transfer some strength to the opposite untrained limb.**

Magnitude is variable and is not guaranteed for one individual.

## 5. Contralateral neural adaptation

Ordinary cross-education is predominantly neural.

Observed/candidate adaptations include:

- shared synaptic input;
- motor-unit recruitment thresholds;
- cortical inhibition/excitability;
- descending drive;
- bilateral motor-network engagement;
- task representation/motor learning.

Project rule:

**there is no single established master mechanism of cross-education.**

## 6. Dominant vs non-dominant limb

Current evidence does not justify a reliable practical rule that dominant→nondominant transfer is greater than the reverse.

Project rule:

**do not choose the trained side solely from dominance.**

## 7. Neural transfer vs hypertrophy

Current evidence supports contralateral strength gains without corresponding contralateral muscle-thickness gains.

Project rule:

**contralateral strength transfer ≠ contralateral hypertrophy.**

Direct mechanical loading remains the primary route to hypertrophy of a healthy trainable limb.

## 8. Immobilization boundary

Training the available limb during contralateral immobilization can attenuate:

- strength loss;
- some muscle-size loss.

Project rule:

**preservation during disuse is not the same as ordinary hypertrophy of an active untrained limb.**

## 9. Limited-equipment application

Unilateral exercise can be useful when external load is limited because the same available load can create a higher relative challenge per active limb.

Project rule:

**direct per-limb loading and cross-education are separate mechanisms.**

When both limbs are healthy and trainable:

- use unilateral exercise for load distribution, unilateral strength and task specificity;
- treat cross-education as secondary rather than the main overload strategy.

## 10. Testing implications

Unilateral and bilateral strength tests are not interchangeable.

Testing should specify:

- unilateral vs bilateral condition;
- stabilization;
- load;
- ROM/joint angle;
- force/velocity/power outcome;
- dominant/non-dominant side;
- familiarization.

Project rule:

**a bilateral index belongs to a defined protocol, not to the person in general.**

## 11. Canonical project rules

1. Bilateral deficit is not universal.
2. Bilateral facilitation can occur.
3. Deficit/facilitation are task/test/training specific.
4. Unilateral vs bilateral strength adaptation follows specificity.
5. Neither unilateral nor bilateral training is globally superior.
6. Cross-education of strength exists.
7. Cross-education magnitude is variable.
8. Ordinary cross-education is predominantly neural.
9. No single neural marker explains all cross-education.
10. Dominant→nondominant superiority is not an established practical rule.
11. Contralateral strength gain does not imply contralateral hypertrophy.
12. Immobilization preservation is distinct from ordinary muscle growth.
13. Unilateral exercise can increase relative per-limb challenge under limited load.
14. Cross-education is a secondary benefit when both limbs can be directly trained.

## 12. Training-relevant implications

1. **Do not treat a bilateral deficit as a defect that must always be corrected.**
2. **Do not treat bilateral facilitation as globally better.**
3. **Match unilateral/bilateral training to the strength task being developed.**
4. **Use unilateral variants when limited external load makes bilateral work too easy.**
5. **Do not expect the untrained limb to hypertrophy because its strength increased.**
6. **Do not choose the trained side solely from dominance.**
7. **Cross-education becomes especially useful when one limb cannot be loaded normally.**
8. **Do not replace direct healthy-limb training with cross-education when direct training is available.**

## 13. What NEU-07 does not prescribe

Not owned here:

- exact unilateral/bilateral exercise ratio;
- exact asymmetry threshold;
- rehabilitation loading after injury/surgery;
- side-specific progression;
- testing thresholds;
- optimal contraction mode for cross-education;
- fatigue effects on bilateral performance.

These remain NEU-08/09, TST, TEC, PRO/GPP, MED/REC and IND.

## 14. Confidence

### HIGH
- BLD is context dependent rather than universal;
- unilateral/bilateral strength adaptation follows specificity;
- cross-education of strength exists;
- contralateral strength transfer does not imply equal contralateral hypertrophy;
- dominance asymmetry is insufficient for a practical rule.

### MODERATE-HIGH
- ordinary cross-education is predominantly neural;
- contralateral motor-unit/shared-input adaptations can occur;
- training can alter the bilateral index;
- unilateral training is a valid strength-training mode.

### MODERATE / CONTEXT-DEPENDENT
- exact cross-education magnitude;
- exact cortical/spinal/motoneuron contribution;
- exact immobilization-preservation magnitude;
- relation of BLD to athletic performance;
- optimal unilateral/bilateral program mixture.

### LOW / NOT ESTABLISHED AS UNIVERSAL
- one master mechanism of cross-education;
- dominant→nondominant superiority;
- bilateral deficit as stable personal trait;
- bilateral facilitation as globally superior;
- meaningful ordinary hypertrophy of a healthy untrained limb from contralateral training alone.
