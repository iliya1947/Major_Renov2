E1 Thompson et al. 2021 https://doi.org/10.1111/spc3.12584 indexed author abstract; direct403. Reviews operational variation in emotion differentiation (ED), including repeated ratings and ICC computation, and differences between construct and measure. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9686340/ indexed author text describes multilevel extensions of undifferentiated-affect indices. ED usually indexes within-person pattern of ratings across occasions, often inverse ICC of same-valence emotion items. Low differentiation can arise from actual co-occurrence, similar exposures, sparse ratings, scale usage, mean intensity, variability, language and reporting strategy. An inverse ICC is not direct classification accuracy, inner biological specificity or a measure of overall intelligence. Distinguish between-person trait from occasion-specific differentiation, positive from negative; specify item set, sampling interval, ICC type, missing/negative values and uncertainty. No universal clinical cutoff or validated extreme-tail norm.

## Practical gate (generic heuristic)
Separate first-person feeling, perceived intensity, precision of label, conceptual knowledge, accuracy on a specified recognition/understanding task, self-perceived competence, regulatory choice and enacted behavior. Recognition of another person's expression is probabilistic and context-dependent; target report and test key also have uncertainty. Distinguish emotion knowledge and actual use when tired/stressed, compare familiar vs novel scenarios, relevant verbal ability and opportunities, accuracy and calibration, not confidence alone. Low differentiation score is a measurement observation; examine event distribution, co-occurrence, intensity, item range, language and sampling before interpreting. Compare repeated voluntary ordinary observations, not induced distress or diagnosis. Consider mixed emotions legitimate rather than errors. High cognitive performance can coexist with low emotional clarity, high knowledge with unsuccessful regulation, or modest vocabulary with context-appropriate action. No single EI score implies empathy, moral conduct, emotional health or IQ. Use component-level evidence and recognize limits. 
## Internal cases (wholly invented; rubric set before search)
A. Fictional community radio host uses varied emotion words in scripts and gives nearly identical ratings of sadness and frustration after difficult broadcasts. Observer infers high vocabulary guarantees precise experience, or high ICC proves poor emotional competence. Rival models: repeated genuinely mixed events, rating habit, language and limited sampling. New: varied daily contexts show more distinct reports without increased vocabulary, and original events contain both appraisals. Revise measurement/context explanation; retain vocabulary skill, withdraw global competence inference. 
B. Fictional mediation workshop participant excels on written emotion scenarios but struggles to identify a partner's feelings in a noisy live interaction. Alternatives: verbal knowledge, auditory constraints, ambiguous cues, pressure and behavior. Compare contextual recognition with target feedback, accuracy and confidence, matched quiet/loud conditions. New: recognition improves when context and audio clarified; written performance unchanged. Retain knowledge, revise global EI deficit to task/context hypothesis; no inference of actual internal state beyond evidence.
C. Fictional selection program high reasoning-score group has higher scenario recognition but indistinguishable self-rated competence and lower apparent flexibility in one role. Analyst concludes universal gifted EI superiority or inferiority. Separate score types, selection and role demands, null vs equivalence, actual action and cost. New: matched training comparison removes recognition contrast on familiar stimuli, but novel cases remain uneven by individual; self-report poorly tracks behavior. Withdraw single global group explanation; retain original bounded contrast and uncertainty, no causal training or IQ inference.
