1. Dependency: avoid attributing emotional intensity or sensory sensitivity to high intelligence without construct-specific evidence.
2. Claim types: measurement, description, association, prediction; local causal evidence only where design supports it.
3. Constructs: emotional response threshold, amplitude, duration, recovery, frequency, affective variability, sensory processing sensitivity, overexcitability (OE), self-report vs observed response.
4. Population: general adults, children, selected/identified gifted, measured gifted adults, measured adult IQ150–160+ separately.
5. Phenomena: intensity, sensitivity, reactivity to positive/negative events, perceived sensory burden; five proposed OE domains.
6. Comparators: matched or community samples, self-report vs task/physiological evidence, trait overlap and selection, within-person context vs between-person differences.
7. Outcomes: distinct affective/sensory measures, functioning and costs; not diagnosis or IQ inference.
8. Time: state, repeated episode, trait and development; distinguish initial response from recovery.
9. Eligible evidence: comparative primary studies, psychometric studies, meta-analysis, contradictory/null results and direct adult evidence.
10. Boundaries: no universal gifted emotional phenotype, no clinical classification from OE, no unmarked extreme-tail extrapolation.
11. Privacy: public science and wholly invented tasks only.
