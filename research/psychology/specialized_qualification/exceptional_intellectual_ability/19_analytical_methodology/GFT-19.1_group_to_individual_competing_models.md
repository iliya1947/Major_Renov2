Конструкты: межиндивидуальные и внутрииндивидуальные зависимости, гетерогенность, индивидуальная прогнозируемость, competing hypotheses. ## Synthesis and operational transfer
Separate (a) between-person cross-sectional differences, (b) within-person covariation across time/context, (c) distribution of individual within-person slopes, (d) within-person prediction, (e) causal intervention, (f) mechanism. E01 supports uncertainty of transfer; E02 qualifies all-or-none reading; E03 demonstrates added value in a particular comparison, while E04 challenges universality. Neither proves that intelligence determines these patterns. No direct adult measured IQ 150–160+ evidence was established in this bounded search; this is not a systematic absence claim. GFT-02 psychometric guardrail applies. GFT-16/17/18 results remain prerequisites for specific synthesis. General principles and rules below are PRACTICAL_HEURISTIC, not validated universal instruments.
1. Define target proposition, timeframe, observable outcome, source/proxy, and purpose. Distinguish stable individual difference from a temporary change; same term on two levels need not measure the same thing. Keep contextual units and timestamps. Group evidence informs prior/plausibility, never assigns a specific slope by itself.
2. Compare minimal baseline (personal average or persistence, with context/time if justified), pooled association, group-informed personal baseline, and person-specific/context-dependent slope. For quantitative repeated data, distinguish two illustrative specifications (corrected 27.11.2026 in GFT-19.4): (A) unrestricted person fixed intercepts, Y_it = a_i + b_Wi (X_it - mean_i X) + e_it; time-invariant mean_i X is absorbed by a_i, so no separate b_B is identifiable. (B) a within-between multilevel model Y_it = alpha + u_i + b_W (X_it - mean_i X) + b_B mean_i X + e_it, with suitable conditional mean restrictions on u_i (e.g., E[u_i | mean_i X]=0 in this simplified specification), adequate within/between variation and correctly specified conditional errors. The between coefficient is a conditional between-person association, not causal. Person-varying within slopes require additional data and justified model assumptions; alternatively estimate a separate between-person regression with its own assumptions. Do not use unrestricted person intercepts together with an independently identified person-mean coefficient. See GFT-19.2 §Operational method item 7 and [GFT-19.4](GFT-19.4_dependent_integration_and_sufficiency_audit.md) for algebraic regression check. These are illustrative statistical models, not identified causal mechanisms. Include trend, temporal autocorrelation, error and changing regimes when consequential. With few observations and appropriate exchangeability, shrink noisy slopes toward group estimates; if group unrepresentative or heterogeneous, shrinkage may bias, compare alternatives. No universal number of observations. Group and personal observations can be dependent; do not count same report or same series twice.
3. Prefer more complex model only if it explains relevant contradictions or improves predeclared held-out prediction beyond a fair simple baseline, with uncertainty, similar missingness/coverage and comparable outcomes. Chronological splitting for future time, participant splitting for new people, preprocess within training, no leakage. For personal analysis without sufficient quantitative series, use qualitative discriminating predictions and context variation, not pseudo-estimated coefficients. A stable baseline can explain better fit without changing mechanism. Association/prediction do not identify intervention or causal mechanism; inspect common causes, reverse direction, measurement, selection and time.
4. Competing hypotheses should specify what each predicts when conditions change and what would downgrade it. Preserve robust observations when explanation fails; revise dependent claims and confidence/use permissions per INT-02/03, route C0/C1/C2/C3/C11/C12 of INT-04 as appropriate. If alternatives indistinguishable, state ambiguity instead of multiplying speculative causes. No covert monitoring, invasive data collection, diagnosis or treatment recommendations; voluntary minimal observations adequate for purpose. Uncertainty does not justify dismissing experience or demanding unnecessary records.
5. Output for self-understanding: “In conditions A, B was observed; explanations H1 and H2 remain plausible; C would differentiate; current evidence supports only [claim type].” If observation is enough for practical insight, avoid unnecessary testing. Never infer an individual mechanism, trait or rank from IQ alone. New information may revise a part or architecture, not erase independent facts.
## Internal assessment (predeclared rubric above; fully invented examples)
A. A fictional choir member reports fewer mistakes on rehearsals with louder accompaniment. Group survey: people with louder rehearsals report more mistakes. H1 within-person auditory cue aids timing, H2 easier pieces use louder accompaniment; compare same pieces and conditions, keep group relation separate. New: same pieces recorded at matched difficulty show no consistent difference but rehearsal timing changes with conductor; downgrade accompaniment explanation, consider cueing/conductor or measurement, retain original reports as observations. No causal effect established. 
B. A fictional gardener wants to understand fluctuations in fatigue during greenhouse shifts; analyst fits 12 variables to 10 daily ratings and calls it an individual mechanism. Compare simple personal baseline, shift-length/time model, pooled or partially pooled model only if comparable external data, and personal slope with prospective data. New: chronological holdout simple shift-length model predicts equally well within uncertainty; keep simple for prediction, do not conclude shift length causes fatigue or complex model definitively false. New regime requires re-evaluation. No invented threshold or diagnosis.
C. A fictional museum guide reports better route recall when a map is present, and a high test score is asserted as explanation. H1 visual cue supports recall, H2 route familiarity/selection, H3 score reflects other test construct; separate direct route observations from unverified score and label. New: familiar routes recalled without maps, unfamiliar with maps only after practice; revise map-alone explanation, compare familiarity × cue under matched routes; preserve observed differences, do not infer ability/diagnosis. A cross-sectional association cannot identify within-person mechanism. Practical output: map and practice may help under specific conditions, not a fixed trait.
