# RES-24 — Practical significance, decision thresholds и meaningful effects

## 0. Question Card

### Decision
Как отличать эффект, который статистически существует, от эффекта, который достаточно велик, чтобы изменить тренировочное решение с учётом времени, fatigue, cost, adherence и measurement error.

### Question type
Decision threshold / interpretation / evidence-to-decision.

### Critical outcomes
1. Explicit outcome-specific thresholds.
2. MID/MCID/SWC distinctions.
3. Threshold vs measurement error.
4. Equivalence/noninferiority relevance.
5. Benefit-burden trade-off.
6. Pre-specification before results.

### Closure criterion
Есть project threshold framework usable for strength/hypertrophy/adherence/safety decisions.

## 1. Statistical significance is not decision significance

Question 1:
> Is effect distinguishable from null under model?

Question 2:
> Is effect large enough to change what we should do?

These are different.

Example:
method A adds +0.2% strength vs B with narrow CI and p<.001.
This may be real but practically irrelevant if:
- doubles session time;
- requires expensive equipment;
- worsens adherence.

## 2. Decision threshold

Current GRADE guidance defines decision thresholds as explicit, outcome-specific points separating:
- trivial/minimal;
- small;
- moderate;
- large effects
and where decisions may change.

As of October 2026 GRADE Book explicitly emphasizes thresholds as central to:
- effect magnitude;
- certainty;
- benefit-harm balance;
- recommendations. citeturn469391search5

## 3. Null is only one threshold

Null:
- 0 for differences;
- 1 for ratios.

But decision may depend on threshold away from null.

Example:
+0.1 kg strength effect:
not zero,
but below any practical relevance.

Project rule:
**“different from zero” is never the sole practical threshold unless zero itself is genuinely decision-critical.**

## 4. Minimal Important Difference / Change

MID/MIC:
smallest outcome difference/change considered important to target person/stakeholders.

Common in clinical outcomes.

Fitness complication:
validated MID often absent for:
- arm circumference;
- muscle thickness;
- 1RM improvement;
- hypertrophy.

Therefore:
do not invent universal MCID.

## 5. Smallest Worthwhile Change

Sports-performance literature sometimes uses smallest worthwhile change, often based on:
- performance context;
- between-athlete variability;
- competitive consequences.

Useful for elite performance where tiny changes matter.

Not automatically transferable to:
- novice muscle gain;
- home fitness;
- health.

## 6. Measurement threshold vs meaningful threshold

### MDC/SDC
How much change is needed to exceed measurement error.

### MID/SWC
How much change matters.

These can conflict:

#### Case A
Meaningful threshold = 2 kg,
measurement error = 5 kg.
Tool cannot reliably detect meaningful change.

#### Case B
MDC = 0.2 kg,
meaningful threshold = 3 kg.
Tool is precise, but tiny detected changes still irrelevant.

Need both.

## 7. Outcome-specific thresholds

Potential decision thresholds differ for:
- maximal strength;
- hypertrophy;
- adherence;
- pain;
- injury;
- time burden;
- cost.

Example:
small increase in serious harm risk may matter more than equally small increase in soreness.

GRADE 2026 explicitly ties threshold magnitude to outcome importance/severity. citeturn469391search5

## 8. Perspective matters

Threshold depends on decision-maker.

For Ilya:
potential values:
- practical visible progress;
- strength gain;
- minimal friction;
- free/cheap implementation;
- ADHD sustainability.

A professional athlete may value 1% performance gain much more than a home trainee.

Therefore threshold must be target-specific.

## 9. Pre-specification

Threshold should ideally be defined before looking at effect estimate.

Otherwise:
- favorable result can motivate lower threshold;
- unfavorable result can motivate higher threshold.

Project rule:
Question Card or Decision Card records threshold before interpretation when feasible.

## 10. Empirical vs normative thresholds

Threshold can come from:

### Empirical
- validated patient-important anchor;
- athlete performance data;
- known measurement/performance relation.

### Elicited
- user/stakeholder preferences.

### Decision-analytic
- expected utility / trade-offs.

### Project heuristic
When no validated threshold exists, define transparent context-specific threshold and label it heuristic.

## 11. Effect magnitude categories

“Small/medium/large” generic SMD labels are not enough.

GRADE’s threshold-based approach is preferable:
effect magnitude categories should map to consequences for specific outcome.

Project rule:
no final decision from Cohen-style generic magnitude labels alone.

## 12. Precision relative to thresholds

CI interpretation:

### Entire CI below meaningful threshold
Likely no practically important advantage.

### CI spans trivial and meaningful benefit
Decision uncertainty remains.

### Entire CI beyond meaningful benefit
Stronger practical evidence.

### CI spans meaningful benefit and harm
High decision uncertainty.

This is more useful than p<.05.

## 13. Equivalence

If question:
> Are A and B close enough that simpler A can replace B?

Need equivalence framework.

Define:
- acceptable difference ±Δ.

Then assess whether CI lies inside equivalence margins.

“No significant difference” does not establish equivalence.

## 14. Noninferiority

Question:
> Is simpler/safer/cheaper A not unacceptably worse than B?

Define noninferiority margin before trial.

Useful future fitness questions:
- short session vs long session;
- home dumbbell method vs gym method;
- fewer sets vs more sets;
provided other benefits justify accepting small performance difference.

## 15. Benefit-burden trade-off

Practical value of method includes:

### Benefits
- strength;
- hypertrophy;
- health;
- enjoyment.

### Undesirable/burdens
- time;
- fatigue;
- soreness;
- complexity;
- cost;
- injury risk;
- setup friction;
- adherence loss.

A +2% hypertrophy advantage may be unattractive if burden +100%.

## 16. Opportunity cost

Time/resources spent on one intervention displace another.

Example:
20 extra weekly arm sets may:
- add small growth;
- consume time;
- impair whole-body training;
- reduce adherence.

Practical significance requires marginal value, not isolated effect.

## 17. Reversibility

When uncertainty exists:
low-cost reversible choices can tolerate lower confidence.

Example:
trying a different curl variation for several weeks.

High-cost/irreversible/risky decisions require stronger evidence.

This becomes explicit in RES-28.

## 18. Thresholds for harm

Threshold for serious adverse outcome can be very low.

For mild temporary soreness:
higher tolerance may be acceptable.

Outcome severity changes decision threshold.

Do not aggregate all harms into one “side effects” category.

## 19. Thresholds for adherence

A program only works if implemented.

Potential practical threshold:
difference in completion probability large enough to outweigh marginal physiological advantage.

Need data, not intuition alone.

For Ilya this will be central, but exact threshold is not yet defined.

## 20. Thresholds for cost

User prefers free solutions.

If two options similar:
- free option gets strong practical advantage.

But if paid equipment materially unlocks strength progression beyond current ceiling:
cost-benefit can change.

Threshold must compare real marginal value.

## 21. Practical-significance record

For each decision:

1. outcome;
2. effect estimate;
3. CI;
4. null;
5. meaningful benefit threshold;
6. meaningful harm threshold;
7. measurement error;
8. time burden;
9. fatigue/recovery burden;
10. adherence effect;
11. cost/equipment;
12. reversibility;
13. final practical classification:
   - trivial;
   - small but possibly worthwhile;
   - clearly worthwhile;
   - uncertain;
   - unfavorable.

## 22. Project threshold classes

Project-specific:

### T0 — Trivial
Would not change action.

### T1 — Small/conditional
Could matter if cost/burden negligible.

### T2 — Decision-relevant
Large enough to prefer one option under normal burden.

### T3 — Dominant
Large enough to outweigh substantial burden/cost or strongly affect safety.

Not official GRADE categories; engineering layer for individual decision.

## 23. Example — two hypertrophy programs

A vs B:
+0.3 mm muscle thickness.

If:
- measurement error ±0.5 mm;
- B takes twice as long;

effect is neither measurement-secure nor clearly worthwhile.

Do not recommend B from point estimate.

## 24. Example — strength

A gives +5 kg vs B, CI +3 to +7 kg.

If goal is strength and burden similar:
decision-relevant.

If exercise itself irrelevant to target movement:
applicability can reduce value.

## 25. Example — adherence

A and B similar hypertrophy.
A completion 90%, B 60%.

For real-world use A may dominate despite same physiological efficacy among completers.

## 26. Threshold uncertainty

The threshold itself can be uncertain.

Project should distinguish:
- uncertainty in effect estimate;
- uncertainty in value/threshold.

A close call may legitimately remain preference-sensitive.

## 27. Current GRADE direction

GRADE Book’s 2026 Decision Thresholds guidance explicitly moves assessment away from null-only thinking toward outcome-specific effect thresholds tied to decision consequences. citeturn469391search5

This aligns strongly with project needs.

## 28. Project rules

1. Null is not automatically practical threshold.
2. Define threshold before interpreting evidence where possible.
3. Separate measurement error from meaningful change.
4. Use natural units where possible.
5. Generic SMD labels do not decide importance.
6. “No significant difference” ≠ equivalence.
7. Account for time/fatigue/adherence/cost.
8. Severe harms use different thresholds from mild burdens.
9. Report uncertainty in both effect and threshold.
10. Prefer decision thresholds over p-value thresholds.

## 29. Confidence

### HIGH
- decision thresholds should be explicit and outcome-specific;
- statistical significance and practical significance are distinct;
- equivalence/noninferiority require pre-specified margins;
- measurement detectability and meaningfulness are distinct;
- trade-offs affect recommendations.

### PRACTICAL_HEURISTIC
