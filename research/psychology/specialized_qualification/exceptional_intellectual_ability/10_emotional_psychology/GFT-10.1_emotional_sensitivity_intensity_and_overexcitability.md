## Operational gate (heuristic)
Separate (1) stimulus detection/discrimination, (2) appraisal and perceived burden, (3) threshold/ease of emotion activation, (4) response amplitude and valence, (5) duration/recovery, (6) exposure frequency, (7) expressive behavior, physiology and subjective experience, (8) interference with functioning. Example: strong but brief joy is not slow recovery; frequently exposed person can report frequent emotions without lower response threshold. Aesthetic sensitivity not noise intolerance; accuracy of detecting a signal not distress caused by it. A high OE intellectual score may overlap curiosity and cognitive self-concept rather than maximum ability. No diagnosis, IQ inference, universal neural mechanism or characterological explanation from OE/SPS/ERS/PERS. Self-report, informant and laboratory measures answer different questions; disagreements are data, not proof someone is inaccurate. Compare similar natural events and contexts, baseline, repeated timing, appraisals, exposure, sleep, health, workload and environmental demands; separate subjective discomfort from measured threshold and functional costs. Consider independently supported clinical alternatives where relevant; avoid deliberate distress or unnecessary testing. Optional low-risk accommodations based on actual needs, not IQ label. No inference from group means to an individual without individual observations; no statistical nonsignificance as equivalence.
