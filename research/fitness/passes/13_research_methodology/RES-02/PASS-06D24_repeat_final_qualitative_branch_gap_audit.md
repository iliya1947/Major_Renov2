# 4. New BLOCKER 1 — Consensual Qualitative Research (CQR)

## Audit finding

**BLOCKING — dedicated pass required.**

CQR is not adequately represented by:

- generic qualitative research;
- ordinary thematic analysis;
- generic “multiple coders” metadata.

Foundational CQR has a specific architecture:

1. open-ended data;
2. small intensive case sample;
3. research team;
4. analyst consensus;
5. auditor;
6. domains;
7. core ideas;
8. cross-analysis;
9. categories;
10. frequency labels across cases.

### Why this matters

PASS-06D12 established:

> intercoder agreement / consensus is not a universal qualitative quality requirement.

CQR is an important **methodology-specific exception** where consensus and auditing are constitutive rather than generic ritual.

### Direct exercise relevance

A 2026 Frontiers in Sports and Active Living paper explicitly used CQR to study willingness to participate in exercise-science research.

Therefore omission would create a direct fitness/exercise classification error.

### Needed pass

**PASS-06D25 — Consensual Qualitative Research structures**, including:

- philosophical position;
- team consensus;
- auditor;
- domains/core ideas/cross-analysis;
- general/typical/variant frequency labels;
- CQR-M / CQR-C;
- member checking boundary;
- frequency ≠ prevalence;
- exercise/sport applications.

# 5. New BLOCKER 2 — Moustakas heuristic inquiry

## Audit finding

**BLOCKING — dedicated pass required.**

Heuristic inquiry is phenomenologically oriented but was not covered in D2.

It materially differs from ordinary phenomenology because the primary researcher’s autobiographical experience of the phenomenon is integral to the inquiry.

Canonical Moustakas phases:

1. initial engagement;
2. immersion;
3. incubation;
4. illumination;
5. explication;
6. creative synthesis.

Its output/representation can include:

- individual depictions;
- composite depictions;
- exemplary portraits;
- creative synthesis.

### Why D2 is insufficient

D2 covered:

- descriptive phenomenology;
- hermeneutic phenomenology;
- IPA.

It did not cover:

- researcher-as-experiencer architecture;
- tacit knowing / indwelling;
- incubation / illumination;
- creative synthesis.

### Direct rehabilitation relevance

Current physiotherapy scholarship explicitly uses Moustakas heuristic inquiry and discusses how its reflexive/representational logic changes knowledge production.

### Needed pass

**PASS-06D26 — heuristic inquiry structures.**

# 6. New BLOCKER 3 — Co-operative Inquiry

## Audit finding

**BLOCKING — dedicated pass required.**

D8 mentioned cooperative inquiry only briefly within PAR sources; it did not provide taxonomy-level coverage of Heron/Reason Co-operative Inquiry.

Current 2025 methodology literature explicitly presents it as a distinct qualitative participatory methodology.

Key architecture includes:

- participants as co-researchers/co-inquirers rather than data providers;
- inquiry cycles;
- practical action + reflection;
- shared inquiry ownership;
- Heron/Reason extended epistemology:
  - experiential knowing;
  - presentational knowing;
  - propositional knowing;
  - practical knowing.

### Why generic PAR is insufficient

Co-operative Inquiry specifies a particular epistemology and co-researcher process that cannot be recovered from:

> PAR = shared power + iterative action.

### Direct rehabilitation relevance

A 2026 national rehabilitation-hospital study explicitly used cooperative inquiry for organizational learning/leadership change.

### Needed pass

**PASS-06D27 — Co-operative Inquiry structures.**

# 8. New BLOCKER 5 — Oral History

## Audit finding

**BLOCKING — dedicated pass required.**

D7 explicitly deferred oral history and life-history/biographical approaches to later audit.

Current OHA standards show oral history has a research lifecycle not captured by narrative inquiry alone:

1. preparation;
2. recorded interview;
3. preservation;
4. access/use.

Additional defining features:

- narrator rather than merely “interview participant”;
- historical context;
- memory as both evidence and meaning;
- preservation/archiving;
- rights/release/access;
- future secondary use;
- factual verification where claims are historical facts;
- ethical responsibility extending beyond one analysis/manuscript.

### Why narrative inquiry is insufficient

Narrative inquiry can study:

- plot;
- identity;
- storytelling;
- temporality.

Oral history additionally creates a **historical primary source intended for preservation/access**, with explicit provenance and archive/use obligations.

### Direct sport relevance

Sport-history scholarship uses oral history extensively, including work with athletes/coaches and sport communities.

### Life-history boundary

Life history / biographical narrative methods remain more plausibly routable as:

> narrative/biographical methodology or ethnographic method

unless a later pass shows a scientific target not captured there.

The blocker is specifically **oral history**, not every life-history interview.

### Needed pass

**PASS-06D29 — Oral History primary-research structures.**

These two new blockers should not be merged.

- phenomenologically oriented;
- researcher autobiographical experience is central;
- tacit/intuition/immersion/incubation;

# 29. Oral history vs narrative interview

A long narrative interview is not automatically oral history.

- historical purpose/context;
- recorded testimony as primary source;
- preservation;

- narrator permissions/releases;
- future use;
- provenance;
- factual verification where appropriate.

This distinction materially affects both ethics and inference.
