# BIO-11 — Metabolic fatigue and limiting factors

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-19**  
Область: 05 — Bioenergetics / Exercise Metabolism

## 0. Core decision

Metabolic fatigue is not one metabolite, one threshold or one fixed mechanism.

Project model:

`task demand > momentary sustainable capacity`

where the metabolic contribution to sustainable capacity depends on interacting:

- PCr / high-energy-phosphate state;
- Pi;
- glycogen/glucose availability;
- H+/pH;
- Na+/K+/Cl−/Ca2+ regulation;
- excitation–contraction coupling / Ca2+ handling;
- O2 delivery/distribution/extraction;
- mitochondrial oxidative capacity/control;
- thermal and hydration state.

Project rule:

**the limiting architecture is task-, state-, time- and environment-dependent.**

---

## 1. Limiter vs correlate vs consequence

A variable that changes during fatigue is not automatically the limiting factor.

Separate:

- causal limiter;
- correlate of task strain;
- consequence of fatiguing work.

Project rule:

**mechanism inference requires evidence at the relevant physiological compartment and task.**

---

## 2. PCr depletion

PCr can decline rapidly during high ATP-turnover work.

Lower PCr means:

- less immediate high-rate phosphate-buffering reserve;
- altered energetic state before a repeated bout when recovery is incomplete.

Project rule:

**PCr depletion can constrain repeated/high-power work but is not ATP depletion and is not a complete fatigue diagnosis.**

No universal PCr fatigue threshold is retained.

---

## 3. PCr vs Pi

PCr decline and Pi rise are linked through high-energy-phosphate turnover but are distinct variables.

Project rule:

**PCr depletion ≠ Pi accumulation.**

One cannot be used as a universal numeric proxy for the other across tasks/states.

---

## 4. Glycogen depletion

Low muscle glycogen can reduce exercise capacity in some tasks.

Current human evidence supports impairment of ~5-min high-intensity one-legged exercise with low glycogen, while resting MVC/twitch/RFD were not simply lower.

Project rule:

**low glycogen can limit performance without acting as a universal on/off contractile fuel threshold.**

---

## 5. Glycogen heterogeneity

Whole-muscle glycogen is an average.

Different:

- fibres;
- recruited regions;
- muscles;
- subcellular pools

can differ materially.

Project rule:

**whole-muscle glycogen ≠ every local glycogen pool.**

Low glycogen ≠ zero glycogen.

---

## 6. Glycogen and E–C / Ca2+

Human evidence supports a relationship between glycogen state and SR Ca2+ release during glycogen-depleting exercise/recovery.

Project rule:

**glycogen state can contribute to E–C/Ca2+ behavior in some contexts, but no single universal glycogen→Ca2+ fatigue pathway is retained.**

---

## 7. Inorganic phosphate

Pi rises during intense ATP turnover and can affect:

- cross-bridge force;
- myofilament Ca2+ sensitivity;
- Ca2+-related processes.

Project rule:

**Pi is a major fatigue-relevant metabolite, but no universal human Pi failure threshold is established.**

---

## 8. Pi vs H+

Current human and mechanistic evidence can show Pi more consistently associated with fatigue than H+ in specific protocols.

This is not a universal hierarchy.

Project rule:

**“Pi > H+” is task/model specific, not a general law of fatigue.**

---

## 9. H+ / pH

Severe intracellular acidosis can reduce:

- force;
- shortening velocity;
- power.

Moderate acidosis can have smaller direct effects, especially depending on temperature, fibre type and coexisting metabolic state.

Project rule:

**severe acidosis can contribute to fatigue, but H+/pH is neither necessary nor sufficient to explain all task failure.**

Lactate ≠ H+.

---

## 10. Pi + H+ interaction

Pi and H+ can jointly affect:

- Ca2+ sensitivity;
- cross-bridge mechanics;
- contractile function.

Project rule:

**Pi and H+ interact; their whole-task contributions cannot be assigned as universal percentages.**

---

## 11. Ionic disturbances

Fatiguing contractions alter:

- K+;
- Na+;
- Cl−;
- Ca2+;

across multiple compartments.

These changes interact with:

- Na+/K+-ATPase;
- ion channels;
- membrane excitability;
- T-tubular action potentials;
- SR Ca2+ release.

Project rule:

**ionic fatigue is a multi-ion homeostasis/excitability problem, not a one-ion switch.**

---

## 12. Potassium boundary

K+ can contribute to both potentiation and force depression depending on:

- compartment;
- magnitude;
- activation frequency;
- Na+/Cl− state;
- channel regulation;
- metabolic stress.

Project rule:

**any rise in K+ ≠ automatic fatigue.**

Plasma K+ ≠ muscle interstitial K+.

---

## 13. Excitation–contraction effects

Metabolic/ionic state can influence:

- membrane/T-tubule excitability;
- SR Ca2+ release;
- myoplasmic Ca2+;
- myofilament Ca2+ sensitivity;
- cross-bridge force.

Project rule:

**metabolic fatigue can be expressed through E–C/Ca2+ impairment.**

Detailed E–C physiology remains PHY-10/NEU.

---

## 14. Central vs peripheral boundary

Peripheral metabolic/E–C limitation can coexist with central fatigue.

Project rule:

**evidence for a metabolic contributor does not prove central contribution is zero.**

BIO-11 does not assign central/peripheral percentages.

---

## 15. Oxygen-delivery limitation

O2 delivery depends on:

- pulmonary gas exchange;
- cardiac output;
- arterial O2 content;
- muscle perfusion;
- diffusion/distribution.

In maximal aerobic/elite endurance contexts, systemic O2 delivery can be the primary VO2max limiter.

Project rule:

**O2 delivery can be a major limiter, but not for every task/population.**

---

## 16. Oxygen cascade

Keep separate:

1. systemic O2 delivery;
2. local perfusion/diffusion/distribution;
3. extraction;
4. mitochondrial utilization.

Project rule:

**“oxygen-limited” must specify which level of the cascade is limiting.**

---

## 17. Mitochondrial limitation

Mitochondrial oxidative capacity can constrain sustained oxidative ATP support.

Relevant constructs include:

- mitochondrial content;
- respiratory capacity;
- intrinsic mitochondrial function;
- coupling;
- substrate/ADP/Pi control;
- local O2 availability.

Project rule:

**mitochondrial capacity is important but is not a universal sole endurance limiter.**

---

## 18. Mitochondrial limitation ≠ pathology

In a healthy athlete, a mitochondrial rate limitation can be physiological.

Project rule:

**physiological rate limitation ≠ mitochondrial disease/dysfunction.**

---

## 19. Mitochondrial markers

Keep separate:

- mitochondrial content;
- mass-specific respiration;
- intrinsic respiration;
- citrate synthase;
- whole-body VO2max;
- performance.

Project rule:

**one mitochondrial marker cannot establish the limiter of a whole-body task.**

---

## 20. Heat effects

Heat can alter:

- cardiovascular strain;
- thermal strain;
- perception;
- substrate use;
- fluid loss;
- central/peripheral function.

Project rule:

**heat-related performance loss is multisystem.**

Heat ≠ dehydration.

---

## 21. Hypohydration / dehydration

Hypohydration can impair performance in some contexts, especially with heat/high strain.

However current pooled endurance evidence does not support one universal effect size or body-mass-loss threshold.

Project rule:

**dehydration is context dependent; no universal % body-mass-loss performance threshold is retained.**

---

## 22. Heat vs hydration state

Possible states include:

- heat with maintained hydration;
- hypohydration in temperate conditions;
- combined heat + hypohydration.

Project rule:

**heat stress and hydration state must be measured separately before being interpreted together.**

---

## 23. Cooling / hydration interventions

Cooling can improve performance in hot conditions on average in some task classes.

Hydration can reduce some physiological strain.

But:

**intervention benefit does not identify one molecular fatigue mechanism and does not guarantee individual benefit.**

---

## 24. Medical boundary

Heat-illness recognition, diagnosis, emergency treatment and return-to-play medical clearance belong MED.

BIO-11 owns only performance/fatigue interpretation of heat and hydration state.

---

## 25. Task specificity

The limiting architecture depends on:

- force/power requirement;
- duration;
- duty cycle;
- muscle mass;
- rest;
- exercise mode;
- substrate state;
- training status;
- environment;
- pacing.

Project rule:

**ask what this task must sustain, for how long, under what starting state and environment.**

---

## 26. Brief maximal / near-maximal work

Likely contributors can include:

- rapid PCr use;
- Pi accumulation;
- ion/excitability disturbance;
- E–C/Ca2+ impairment.

Project rule:

**brief high-power failure is not automatically glycogen depletion.**

This is a profile, not a fixed mechanism law.

---

## 27. Repeated high-power work

Later bouts can begin with:

- incomplete PCr restoration;
- altered Pi/ion state;
- higher oxidative contribution;
- local glycogen use.

Project rule:

**the metabolic state before bout 2+ is not the state before bout 1.**

---

## 28. High-density resistance work

High-repetition/high-density resistance work can produce:

- high ATP turnover;
- PCr use;
- glycolytic contribution;
- Pi/H+/ion disturbance;
- local glycogen use;
- E–C impairment.

Project rule:

**greater metabolic stress ≠ automatically greater training effectiveness.**

Exact resistance-training energetics belongs BIO-12.

---

## 29. Prolonged endurance

Potential limiting families include:

- systemic O2 delivery;
- local O2 distribution/extraction;
- mitochondrial capacity;
- muscle/liver carbohydrate availability;
- glucose flux;
- heat/hydration;
- neuromuscular/perceptual factors.

Project rule:

**prolonged endurance limitation is not universally glycogen-only or O2-only.**

---

## 30. Limiter migration

The dominant limiter can change during the same task.

For example:

- early PCr relevance;
- accumulating Pi/ion disturbance;
- rising oxidative contribution;
- increasing glycogen/glucose relevance with duration;
- progressive heat/hydration strain.

Project rule:

**one task can have changing limiting architecture over time.**

---

## 31. State dependence

The same external protocol can produce different limitations depending on:

- starting glycogen;
- PCr/creatine status;
- prior exercise;
- recovery interval;
- training status;
- altitude;
- heat;
- hydration.

Project rule:

**same protocol ≠ same fatigue mechanism in every athlete/state.**

---

## 32. Measurement boundaries

### PCr / Pi / pH
Local 31P-MRS; protocol/model dependent.

### Glycogen
Biopsy / histochemistry / 13C-MRS; local heterogeneity matters.

### Ions
Plasma ≠ interstitial ≠ intracellular.

### VO2
Pulmonary VO2 ≠ direct local mitochondrial ATP production.

### Mitochondria
Content ≠ mass-specific function ≠ intrinsic function.

### Hydration
Body-mass change ≠ plasma osmolality ≠ total-body water.

### Temperature
Core ≠ skin ≠ local muscle temperature.

Project rule:

**limiter inference cannot exceed the construct and compartment actually measured.**

---

## 33. Canonical non-equivalences

1. fatigue ≠ task failure.
2. metabolic fatigue ≠ lactate accumulation.
3. changing marker ≠ proven limiter.
4. PCr depletion ≠ ATP depletion.
5. PCr depletion ≠ complete fatigue diagnosis.
6. PCr recovery ≠ full performance recovery.
7. low glycogen ≠ zero glycogen.
8. low glycogen ≠ universal short-set failure.
9. whole-muscle glycogen ≠ every local pool.
10. glycogen–Ca2+ relationship ≠ one universal pathway.
11. Pi relevance ≠ Pi-only fatigue.
12. one Pi relationship ≠ universal threshold.
13. Pi > H+ in one model ≠ universal hierarchy.
14. H+ ≠ lactate.
15. severe acidosis can matter ≠ all acidosis is limiting.
16. blood pH ≠ muscle pHi.
17. plasma K+ ≠ muscle interstitial K+.
18. K+ disturbance ≠ one-ion fatigue switch.
19. peripheral metabolic fatigue ≠ absence of central fatigue.
20. E–C impairment ≠ primary central failure.
21. O2 delivery ≠ extraction.
22. extraction ≠ mitochondrial utilization.
23. VO2max ≠ isolated cardiac limitation.
24. VO2max ≠ isolated mitochondrial limitation.
25. mitochondrial limitation ≠ mitochondrial pathology.
26. mitochondrial content ≠ intrinsic function.
27. heat ≠ dehydration.
28. body-mass loss ≠ universal performance threshold.
29. cooling benefit ≠ proof of one thermal mechanism.
30. hydration benefit ≠ proof dehydration was sole limiter.
31. normalized metabolic marker ≠ full readiness.
32. same workload ≠ same internal metabolic strain.
33. same protocol ≠ same limiter across individuals.
34. one task ≠ one fixed limiter from start to finish.

---

## 34. Trainer-level interpretation

Use task structure to generate plausible mechanism families.

Do not diagnose mechanism from:

- blood lactate alone;
- one pH value;
- one plasma K+ value;
- one glycogen measure;
- one VO2 result;
- one mitochondrial marker;
- one hydration/body-mass number.

Project rule:

**mechanism hypotheses should be tested against task-specific performance and relevant measurements.**

---

## 36. Confidence

### HIGH

- fatigue is task specific and multi-mechanistic;
- PCr state matters for rapid/repeated high-power work;
- PCr depletion ≠ ATP depletion;
- Pi is fatigue relevant;
- severe acidosis can contribute but is not the sole cause;
- ionic disturbances affect excitability/homeostasis;
- E–C/Ca2+ pathways are fatigue relevant;
- glycogen availability can limit some tasks;
- O2 delivery/extraction/utilization are distinct;
- O2 delivery can be a major maximal-aerobic limitation;
- mitochondrial capacity is important but not sufficient alone;
- heat can impair performance;
- heat ≠ dehydration;
- no universal limiter ranking or threshold is retained.

### MODERATE-HIGH / CONTEXT DEPENDENT

- Pi can be more consistently associated with fatigue than H+ in some models;
- low glycogen can impair ~5-min high-intensity capacity;
- glycogen state can influence SR Ca2+ behavior;
- hypohydration can impair performance in heat/high-strain contexts;
- systemic O2 delivery can be primary for VO2max in elite endurance populations.

### MODERATE / LOW FOR EXACT QUANTIFICATION

- exact PCr/Pi/H+/ion mechanism shares;
- exact glycogen threshold;
- exact human Pi threshold;
- exact individual O2-delivery vs mitochondrial limitation;
- exact dehydration/body-mass-loss threshold;
- exact temperature threshold;
- exact dominant limiter in mixed/self-paced tasks.
