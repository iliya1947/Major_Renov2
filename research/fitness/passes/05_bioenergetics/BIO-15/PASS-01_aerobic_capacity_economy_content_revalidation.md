- treat VO2peak as automatically equal to VO2max;

- turn one economy value into a universal athlete trait;

- equate capillarization with VO2max by itself;
- prescribe exact endurance zones;
- use VO2max as a universal rest-interval/recovery predictor for resistance training.

## 1. Qualification purpose / Question Card

A trainer needs BIO-15 to avoid:

- equating “aerobic fitness” with VO2max alone;
- interpreting VO2peak as automatically maximal physiological capacity;
- using lactate threshold and ventilatory threshold as one exact biological point regardless of method;
- treating critical power/speed and MLSS as interchangeable individual thresholds;
- confusing economy with VO2max;
- confusing economy with mechanical efficiency;
- comparing running-economy values across speeds/surfaces/protocols as if context-free;
- assuming better mitochondria automatically mean higher VO2max;
- assuming capillary density alone determines endurance;
- treating central vs peripheral adaptation as mutually exclusive;
- assuming elite-athlete VO2max limitation applies to all populations;
- assuming higher VO2max automatically means faster recovery between resistance sets.

Primary later decisions supported:

- interpret VO2max/VO2peak as one part of aerobic performance;
- interpret threshold-related speed/power as method-specific sustainable-intensity landmarks;
- interpret economy as submaximal task cost;
- separate mitochondrial/capillary adaptations from whole-body VO2max;
- understand central/peripheral integration;
- understand the limited but real relevance of aerobic metabolism to resistance-training recovery.

Question type:

- **capacity + sustainable-intensity landmark + economy + adaptation integration + recovery-transfer boundary**.

## 2. Core aerobic-performance model

Aerobic/endurance performance is not one variable.

Useful conceptual components include:

- maximal oxygen uptake/capacity;
- sustainable fraction / threshold-related speed or power;
- exercise economy / energetic cost;
- task mechanics;
- substrate state;
- environmental conditions;
- durability/resilience during prolonged work.

Canonical rule:

**VO2max sets an important upper aerobic ceiling, but performance at a given task also depends on how much of that capacity can be used and how costly the task is.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 3. VO2max — content PASS

BIO-09 remains authoritative.

VO2max is:

**the maximal attainable whole-body oxygen uptake under defined test conditions.**

By the Fick framework it integrates:

- cardiac output;
- arterial oxygen content;
- peripheral oxygen extraction.

It also depends on:

- pulmonary gas exchange;
- vascular distribution/diffusion;
- muscle oxidative use.

Canonical rule:

**VO2max is an integrated oxygen-cascade capacity, not an isolated cardiac or mitochondrial measurement.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 4. VO2peak — content PASS

VO2peak is:

**the highest VO2 observed in the specific test.**

Project rule:

**VO2peak ≠ automatically confirmed VO2max.**

Why:

- protocol;
- mode;
- motivation;
- local muscular limitation;
- plateau/verification behavior;
- test duration

can influence the observed peak.

Detailed maximality criteria remain TST.

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 5. VO2max ≠ endurance performance

Athletes with similar VO2max can differ in:

- threshold speed/power;
- economy;
- biomechanics;
- substrate use;
- fatigue durability;
- technical performance.

2026 review PMID **42686133** questions the strength of fractional utilization of VO2max at LT/competition intensity as a strong independent endurance-performance predictor in trained athletes.

2026 large dataset PMID **42071093** reported that VO2max and economy explained most of the variance in speed/power at LT/LTP across a heterogeneous sample, while fractional utilization contributed much less.

Canonical rule:

**VO2max is important but insufficient as a complete endurance-performance score.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 6. VO2max vs economy

VO2max answers:

- how high whole-body oxygen uptake can rise.

Economy answers:

- how much energetic/oxygen cost is required for a defined submaximal external task.

Project rule:

**VO2max ≠ exercise economy.**

An athlete can improve economy without increasing VO2max.

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 7. Lactate/ventilatory thresholds — content PASS

Threshold-related constructs can identify physiological transitions during incremental or constant exercise.

Relevant families include:

- lactate threshold(s);
- gas-exchange threshold(s);
- ventilatory threshold(s);
- maximal lactate steady state;
- critical power / critical speed;
- related metabolic landmarks.

Canonical rule:

**“threshold” is a family of operational constructs, not one universally identical biological point.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 8. First vs second threshold concept

Applied models often identify:

- a lower transition associated with a shift away from low-intensity steady behavior;
- a higher transition associated with the heavy-to-severe boundary / loss of sustainable metabolic steady state.

But exact:

- naming;
- algorithm;
- sampling;
- stage duration;
- physiological signal

changes the estimated value.

Canonical rule:

**threshold number and meaning must be tied to the method used.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 9. Lactate threshold ≠ ventilatory threshold by definition

Blood lactate and ventilatory/gas-exchange responses are physiologically related but measure different signals.

They can occur near one another under some protocols.

They are not mathematically or physiologically identical by definition.

2026 work PMID **41699863** explicitly evaluated agreement among derived, lactate and ventilatory thresholds and showed method/protocol dependence.

Canonical rule:

**LT and VT may approximate related transitions but are not automatically interchangeable individual values.**

Confidence: **HIGH boundary**.

---

## 10. Critical power / MLSS boundary

2025 systematic review/meta-analysis PMID **40124604**:

- 10 studies included in quantitative synthesis;
- power at CP was on average higher than MLSS by ~12 W;
- limits of agreement were wide;
- VO2, HR and blood lactate values did not show clear mean differences;
- moderators/protocols affected agreement.

Canonical rule:

**CP and MLSS are related heavy–severe-boundary constructs but are not perfectly interchangeable individual thresholds.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 11. Lactate threshold / critical speed boundary

2026 study PMID **42120287** assessed agreement between laboratory lactate threshold and field critical speed in trained through world-class runners.

Project interpretation:

**agreement between threshold methods is an empirical property of the method/population, not an assumption.**

No one threshold method is canonized as universally equivalent.

Confidence: **MODERATE-HIGH**.

---

## 12. Threshold adaptation ≠ one universal training zone law

2026 meta-analysis PMID **42928904** reported that training intensity relative to the metabolic threshold moderated threshold-related adaptations, with larger pooled improvements in supra-threshold groups in the available studies.

However:

- heterogeneity was substantial;
- threshold-identification methods varied;
- background training varied;
- study-level categories do not establish an individual optimum.

Canonical rule:

**threshold-related training effects are real but do not justify one universal threshold-training prescription.**

Exact prescription remains PRO.

Confidence: **MODERATE-HIGH**.

---

## 13. Fractional utilization boundary

Traditional endurance models often include the fraction of VO2max sustainable at LT/competition intensity.

2026 review PMID **42686133** found:

- only a minority of studies showed a substantial relationship between fractional utilization and performance;
- evidence for systematic trainability in trained athletes was limited;
- measurement differences confounded interpretation.

Canonical rule:

**fractional utilization is conceptually useful but should not be treated as a universally strong independent performance determinant.**

Confidence: **MODERATE-HIGH**.

---

## 14. Threshold speed/power is a composite outcome

Speed/power at a threshold depends on more than “threshold physiology.”

It can reflect:

- VO2max;
- economy/efficiency;
- threshold method;
- task mechanics.

2026 dataset PMID **42071093** reported strong relationships of LT/LTP speed/power with VO2max and economy and much smaller independent contribution from fractional utilization.

Canonical rule:

**threshold speed/power is a composite performance-related output, not a pure lactate/ventilation trait.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 15. Exercise economy — content PASS

Exercise economy describes the energetic/oxygen cost of performing a standardized submaximal task.

Examples:

- oxygen cost at a fixed running speed;
- energy cost per distance;
- oxygen/energy cost at a fixed cycling workload.

Canonical rule:

**better economy = lower physiological/energetic cost for the same defined external task.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 16. Economy ≠ efficiency

Economy:

- cost required to perform a task.

Efficiency:

- external mechanical work output relative to metabolic energy input.

Running often lacks a simple complete external-work denominator, so economy is commonly used.

Cycling allows clearer gross/net efficiency calculations.

Canonical rule:

**economy and mechanical efficiency are related but non-equivalent constructs.**

Confidence: **HIGH conceptual**.

---

## 17. Oxygen cost vs energy cost

Running economy is often expressed using VO2.

However oxygen cost can be affected by:

- substrate mix;
- RER;
- non-steady behavior;
- measurement method.

Energy-cost expressions incorporate caloric equivalent assumptions.

Canonical rule:

**oxygen cost and energetic cost are closely related but not automatically numerically interchangeable.**

Confidence: **HIGH conceptual**.

---

## 18. Economy is speed/workload specific

Economy can vary with:

- running speed;
- cycling power;
- incline/grade;
- fatigue state;
- environment.

Therefore:

**one economy value does not fully describe economy across all task intensities.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 19. Economy is mode/environment specific

2026 review PMID **42561452** concluded that a fixed 1% treadmill incline does not universally reproduce overground energetic cost.

2026 study PMID **42607059** found higher energetic cost overground than treadmill at 1% under the tested conditions.

Canonical rule:

**treadmill economy ≠ automatically overground economy.**

Environment and protocol must be specified.

Confidence: **HIGH boundary / MODERATE exact magnitude**.

---

## 20. Economy determinants

Economy can be influenced by:

- biomechanics;
- elastic energy storage/return;
- muscle-tendon properties;
- neural coordination;
- technique;
- footwear/equipment;
- body dimensions;
- training state.

2025 review PMID **40066739** supports meaningful biomechanical influence on running economy.

Canonical rule:

**economy is an integrated metabolic-mechanical outcome, not a purely mitochondrial trait.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 21. Strength training and economy

2025 umbrella review PMID **40153564** reported that strength-training interventions can improve running economy and endurance performance in trained endurance athletes while generally not increasing VO2max.

2025 RCT PMID **40016936** reported improved running-economy durability / fatigued high-intensity performance after strength training in well-trained male runners.

2026 RCT PMID **42413900** found no significant running-economy improvement after an 8-week strength program in recreational runners.

Canonical rule:

**strength training can improve economy in some endurance populations/protocols, but the effect is not universal and does not require VO2max improvement.**

Confidence: **MODERATE-HIGH**.

---

## 22. Economy durability

Economy measured fresh can differ from economy after prolonged exercise/fatigue.

This matters because race performance occurs under accumulating fatigue.

Canonical rule:

**fresh economy ≠ economy durability.**

BIO-15 recognizes the construct; detailed durability programming remains PRO/TST.

Confidence: **MODERATE-HIGH**.

---

## 23. Footwear/equipment boundary

2026 meta-analysis PMID **41586014** reported lower running oxygen/energy cost with carbon-plated footwear across pooled crossover studies.

This supports:

**equipment can alter measured economy without changing the athlete's intrinsic VO2max or mitochondrial capacity.**

Canonical rule:

**economy is partly system-level athlete × equipment × environment performance.**

Confidence: **MODERATE-HIGH**.

---

## 24. Mitochondrial adaptations — content PASS

Endurance/interval training can increase skeletal-muscle mitochondrial content and oxidative characteristics.

2025 systematic review/meta-regression PMID **39390310** included very large evidence bases for mitochondrial and capillary outcomes.

After covariate adjustment, pooled mitochondrial-content increases were similar for:

- ET ~23%;
- HIT ~27%;
- SIT ~27%.

Canonical rule:

**multiple endurance-training intensity formats can increase mitochondrial content; no one format owns mitochondrial adaptation.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 25. Mitochondrial content ≠ mitochondrial function

BIO-06 remains authoritative.

Keep separate:

- mitochondrial content;
- mass-specific respiration;
- intrinsic function;
- enzyme markers;
- OXPHOS proteins;
- ATP-production capacity.

Canonical rule:

**a rise in one mitochondrial marker is not equivalent to a proportional rise in all mitochondrial functions.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 26. Mitochondrial adaptation depends on training state/dose

PMID **39390310** reported:

- greater relative adaptation with lower initial fitness;
- training frequency and load associated with mitochondrial/VO2max change;
- different time-efficiency patterns among ET/HIT/SIT.

But meta-regression associations are not individual causal prescriptions.

Canonical rule:

**initial fitness and accumulated training exposure modify mitochondrial adaptation; study-level dose associations are not individual optima.**

Confidence: **HIGH boundary**.

---

## 27. Mitochondria ≠ VO2max

2026 review PMID **42730845** concluded that mitochondrial characteristics are important and often necessary for endurance performance but are not sufficient by themselves.

Whole-body VO2max can remain limited by:

- cardiac output;
- arterial O2 content;
- pulmonary factors;
- diffusion/perfusion.

Canonical rule:

**mitochondrial capacity ≠ VO2max.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 28. Mitochondrial adaptation ≠ performance guarantee

Training can increase mitochondrial markers without proportional improvement in:

- VO2max;
- economy;
- race performance.

Conversely, performance/economy may improve without large VO2max change.

Canonical rule:

**mitochondrial adaptation is one component of endurance adaptation, not a complete performance surrogate.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 29. Concurrent training and mitochondrial adaptation

2026 systematic review/meta-analysis PMID **42732577** directly compared concurrent resistance+endurance training with endurance training alone for VO2max and skeletal-muscle mitochondrial adaptation.

BIO-15 uses this evidence only to reinforce:

**mitochondrial adaptation can occur in mixed training contexts; concurrent training is not automatically metabolically incompatible.**

Exact interference/programming conclusions remain PRO/BIO-16.

Confidence: **MODERATE-HIGH**.

---

## 30. Capillary adaptations — content PASS with PHY ownership

Capillarization influences:

- O2 diffusion distance;
- perfusion distribution;
- substrate/metabolite exchange.

Exercise training can increase capillary measures.

PMID **39390310** reported:

- capillaries per fiber increased after ET/HIT/SIT in pooled analyses;
- capillary-density responses differed by training format and muscle-fiber-size changes;
- adaptation was influenced by training status/time.

Canonical rule:

**capillary adaptation supports peripheral oxygen delivery/extraction but is not a single direct VO2max meter.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

Detailed angiogenesis/microvascular physiology remains PHY.

---

## 31. Capillary metric non-equivalence

Different measures include:

- capillary density;
- capillary-to-fiber ratio;
- capillary contacts;
- capillary-to-fiber perimeter exchange index.

These can change differently when muscle-fiber size changes.

Canonical rule:

**capillary density ≠ capillary-to-fiber ratio ≠ total microvascular adaptation.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 32. Direct central + peripheral adaptation evidence

2025 study PMID **40173327**:

- n=14 young + older adults;
- 12 weeks vigorous cycling;
- VO2max increased ~20% on average;
- maximal cardiac output/stroke volume increased;
- capillary measures and citrate-synthase activity increased;
- VO2max changes correlated with Qmax, SVmax, capillary density and CS activity.

Canonical interpretation:

**central, vascular/peripheral and mitochondrial adaptations can occur together.**

Boundary:

- small sample;
- one cycling program;
- correlations do not prove one exclusive causal pathway.

Confidence: **MODERATE-HIGH**.

---

## 33. Peripheral vs central adaptations — content PASS

Central adaptations can include:

- blood/plasma volume;
- stroke volume;
- maximal cardiac output;
- pulmonary/arterial O2 support.

Peripheral adaptations can include:

- muscle perfusion/distribution;
- capillarization;
- diffusion/extraction;
- mitochondrial content/function;
- oxidative enzymes.

Canonical rule:

**aerobic adaptation is integrated; central and peripheral changes can coexist.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 34. Training-intensity-specific central/peripheral response

2025 RCT PMID **40047443**:

- 84 young healthy participants;
- 6 weeks;
- multiple intensity-domain cycling groups;
- largest VO2max change in HIIT;
- higher-heavy and HIIT increased blood/plasma volume and maximal cardiac output;
- measured peripheral NIRS oxidative-capacity/a-vO2diff changes were not significant.

Canonical interpretation:

**in this short intervention, higher-intensity VO2max gains were supported predominantly by central adaptation.**

Do not infer:

**high-intensity training always adapts centrally and not peripherally.**

Confidence: **MODERATE-HIGH**.

---

## 35. Central vs peripheral dominance is state dependent

2026 review PMID **41845944** emphasizes:

- systemic O2 delivery as a primary VO2max limiter in elite endurance athletes;
- high mitochondrial density/capillarization as important to extraction and fractional utilization.

2026 review PMID **43004664** further emphasizes that limiting steps shift with:

- training status;
- mode;
- environment;
- biological sex;
- pulmonary constraints.

Canonical rule:

**there is no universal central-vs-peripheral limiter/adaptation split for every athlete.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 36. Elite-athlete limitation ≠ general-population limitation

Findings in elite endurance athletes should not automatically be transferred to:

- sedentary adults;
- resistance-trained athletes;
- older adults;
- clinical populations.

Canonical rule:

**population and training state alter the oxygen-cascade bottleneck.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 37. VO2max adaptation ≠ one mechanism

An increase in VO2max can arise through combinations of:

- increased cardiac output;
- blood-volume changes;
- capillarization;
- extraction;
- mitochondrial adaptation;
- task-specific changes.

Project rule:

**same VO2max gain ≠ same underlying adaptation architecture.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 38. Economy adaptation ≠ VO2max adaptation

Running-economy improvements after strength training can occur without VO2max change.

This directly demonstrates:

**submaximal cost and maximal aerobic capacity can adapt independently.**

Confidence: **HIGH boundary**.

---

## 39. Threshold adaptation ≠ VO2max adaptation

Threshold-related speed/power can change through:

- VO2max change;
- economy change;
- local metabolic adaptation;
- method/test changes.

Project rule:

**threshold outcome change does not identify one mechanism.**

Confidence: **HIGH conceptual**.

---

## 40. Recovery relevance for resistance training — content PASS

Aerobic/oxidative metabolism is relevant to resistance-training recovery because it supports:

- PCr resynthesis;
- ATP-dependent ion homeostasis;
- continued metabolic restoration.

BIO-09/12/14 remain authoritative.

Canonical rule:

**between-set recovery has an oxidative component.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 41. VO2max ≠ between-set recovery speed

Direct study PMID **38347629**:

- 28 resistance-trained participants;
- 14 men / 14 women;
- squat and isokinetic knee-extension protocols;
- VO2max was weakly related to within-set fatigue and between-set recovery;
- sex-related recovery patterns were present.

Canonical rule:

**whole-body VO2max is not a strong universal proxy for recovery between resistance sets.**

Confidence: **MODERATE-HIGH direct evidence**.

---

## 42. Mechanism vs proxy boundary

It is possible for oxidative metabolism to be mechanistically important to PCr recovery while whole-body VO2max is only weakly associated with set-to-set recovery.

These are non-contradictory because:

- local muscle oxidative capacity;
- local perfusion;
- PCr kinetics;
- task mechanics;
- neuromuscular factors

are not captured completely by VO2max.

Canonical rule:

**mechanistic relevance of aerobic metabolism ≠ predictive validity of VO2max for resistance-set recovery.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 43. Aerobic fitness does not replace rest prescription

Higher aerobic fitness does not authorize:

- automatically shorter resistance-training rest;
- one universal rest interval;
- assumption of complete PCr/neuromuscular recovery.

Exact rest decisions remain PRO.

Canonical rule:

**aerobic capacity is context for recovery, not a direct rest timer.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 44. Resistance training can alter aerobic/cardiorespiratory outcomes

2026 meta-analysis PMID **40501026** in healthy adults ≥60 years found aerobic, resistance and combined exercise interventions improved VO2max/VO2peak compared with control.

This does not mean resistance training is equivalent to endurance training for all aerobic outcomes.

Canonical rule:

**training-mode labels do not perfectly determine aerobic adaptation, but specificity still matters.**

Confidence: **MODERATE-HIGH population-specific**.

---

## 45. Economy / aerobic capacity / resistance training integration

Strength training can improve economy in endurance athletes without increasing VO2max.

Conversely, VO2max can improve without an economy improvement.

Project rule:

**resistance and endurance adaptations can interact through distinct mechanisms; one outcome should not be used as a surrogate for another.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

## 46. Practical trainer implications — non-prescriptive layer

1. Treat VO2max as an upper-capacity variable, not a complete endurance score.
2. Keep VO2peak operationally distinct unless maximality is established.
3. Name the threshold method used; do not say “the threshold” as though all methods are identical.
4. Do not substitute CP, MLSS, LT and VT numerically without evidence of agreement.
5. Define economy at a specific speed/power, mode and environment.
6. Distinguish economy from efficiency.
7. Recognize that economy can improve without VO2max.
8. Treat mitochondrial and capillary measures as peripheral adaptations, not whole-body aerobic scores.
9. Interpret VO2max adaptation as central + peripheral integration.
10. Do not infer the individual limiting step from VO2max alone.
11. Recognize oxidative metabolism as part of between-set resistance recovery.
12. Do not use VO2max alone to predict or prescribe resistance-set rest.
13. Exact testing and prescription remain TST/PRO.

## 47. Applicability to the academic

No personalized aerobic test battery or conditioning prescription is assigned in this qualification PASS.

## 48. Measurement boundaries

### VO2max
Integrated whole-body oxygen uptake capacity; not one-organ output.

### VO2peak
Highest observed test VO2; not automatically confirmed max.

### LT / VT
Method-specific physiological landmarks; not automatically identical.

### CP / CS
Performance-model boundaries; not automatically MLSS/LT.

### Economy
Submaximal task cost; speed/mode/environment dependent.

### Efficiency
Mechanical output relative to metabolic input; distinct from economy.

### Mitochondrial content
Peripheral tissue construct; not VO2max.

### Capillary density
One vascular metric; not total capillarization.

### VO2max and RT recovery
Whole-body capacity; weak direct proxy for set-to-set recovery in current direct evidence.

Canonical rule:

**measurement construct must match the claim.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

## 51. Evidence-quality interpretation

### Stronger trainer-level evidence

- VO2max is integrated whole-body aerobic capacity rather than a single-organ trait;
- VO2peak is operationally distinct;
- VO2max alone is insufficient to characterize endurance performance;
- LT/VT/CP/MLSS are method-defined related constructs rather than one exact interchangeable threshold;
