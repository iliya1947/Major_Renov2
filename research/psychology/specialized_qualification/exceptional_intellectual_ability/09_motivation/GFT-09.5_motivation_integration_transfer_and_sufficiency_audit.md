## 3. Integrated architecture (practical heuristic; not identified causal model)
Distinguish current state, stable preference, maximal ability, domain knowledge, actual opportunity and demands, value and reasons, intended goal, chosen action, implementation, feedback, outcomes and cost. Candidate process: available options and context -> appraised meaning, feasibility, novelty/demand and costs -> goal selection/regulation -> effort and action -> outcome and feedback -> revise beliefs, strategy or goals. These relations can be reciprocal; no fixed obligatory sequence or latent gifted motive. Multiple goals can compete. For a given observation, keep at least two explanations where plausible; choose simpler sufficient model; only add interactions if they predict distinct observations.
- Low engagement: compare task relevance, difficulty, support, fatigue, knowledge and consequences. Boredom not equivalent to underchallenge.
- High intellectual interest but low output: compare interest and value separately from initiation, barriers, resources, effort allocation and competing goals; distinguish behavior from intention. 
- Preference for independence: distinguish volition from independent action and external structure. Help can support autonomously endorsed goals.
- Repeated revision: compare required quality and marginal improvement with perceived uncertainty, fear of error, evaluation, time and downstream costs. Neither high standards nor self-criticism implies IQ.
- Persistence: distinguish means from purpose and attainable goal from blocked goal; assess opportunity cost and outcomes, not mere duration. 
- Test competing predictions in voluntary comparable low-risk tasks/settings, recording baseline, task demands, subjective reports, behavior, outcome and costs separately. Avoid manipulation of distress or lowering safety-critical criteria. Do not identify a causal mechanism from an uncontrolled observation, clinical diagnosis from motivation or intelligence from behavior. Preserve useful support independent of gifted label. 
- Transfer boundary: youth/selected samples and general adult associations inform generic possibilities, not a necessary gifted profile. Work engagement, school achievement, creativity and wellbeing are distinct outcomes, not proxies for all motivation.

## 4. New integrated cases and explicit revisions
A. Fictional museum conservation assistant enjoys material-science reading, avoids novel complex forms at work, reports boredom and spends long hours revising labels. Candidate model A: underchallenge; B: insufficient form-specific instruction plus evaluation concern; C: task value/competing workload. Predict A would improve with higher challenge at matched relevance; B with structured examples and clarified acceptance criterion; C with relevant purpose and access. New: complex forms with examples are completed promptly, interesting simple labels remain engaging, extra revisions occur only with vague approval rules. Retain scientific interest; withdraw general underchallenge and low-effort claims. Context/task support and uncertainty remain plausible; no claim of a clinical perfectionism mechanism or giftedness.
B. Fictional cooperative weather-monitoring project participant endorses long-term purpose, chooses exciting new sensors but misses repetitive calibration, accepts a partner's checklist. Analyst calls externally supported action controlled motivation and seeks novelty as cure. Separate interest/state novelty, personally endorsed purpose, effort choice, access, competence and constraints. Predictions: motivation-only model expects low voluntary action even when access is restored; implementation/resource model predicts improvement when prerequisites and cues available. New: access window becomes reliable and calibration is sustained without new sensors, participant freely chooses partner checklist. Revise global motivation deficit to conditional implementation/access explanation; preserve novelty preference and autonomous endorsement as separate observations, not causal proof.
C. Fictional community theatre costume workshop member meets high standards, continues polishing rejected design despite material shortage, and reports exhaustion. Analyst calls high standards universal gifted advantage or pathology. Distinguish skill, chosen standards, objective quality, self-evaluation, goal feasibility and tradeoffs. New: independent review shows accepted garments require no further polishing; a substitution meets performance purpose, while participant retains ambitious standards for new designs. Withdraw all-or-nothing perfectionism explanation and general persistence virtue; revised means substitution preserves purpose. If distress persists, assess independent causes and actual function rather than infer diagnosis.
