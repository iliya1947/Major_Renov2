- treating observed variation as proof of stable responder types;

## 3. True response heterogeneity vs noise audit

PASS WITH CAUSAL RESTRAINT.

PMID **41465870** does not prove that true inter-individual treatment-response heterogeneity never exists.

It shows that:

- many studies do not adequately partition measurement error and within-person variability;
- evidence for true variance beyond random variation is often weak/inconclusive for the outcomes analyzed.

Canonical rule:

**failure to prove stable response heterogeneity ≠ proof that all people respond identically.**

No blocker.

## 4. Stable responder phenotype audit

PASS.

PMID **42241643** directly found limited within-person reproducibility of several adaptation magnitudes across repeated similar endurance-training blocks.

Canonical rule:

**one training block does not establish a stable responder/non-responder trait.**

Repeated measurement remains necessary.

No blocker.

## 35. Hypoxia evidence-quality audit

PASS WITH DOWNGRADE.

PMID **42043079** overview reports potential aerobic/hematological benefits, but most included systematic reviews were low/critically low AMSTAR-2 quality.

Current 2026 meta-analyses are mixed:

- PMID **42891559** found small time-trial improvement and higher hemoglobin but inconsistent VO2max/economy effects;
- PMID **42302183** found no clear superiority for RE, VO2max or TT vs normoxic training, though TTE improved.

Canonical rule:

**hypoxic-training benefits are protocol/outcome specific and evidence quality is heterogeneous; hypoxia is not a universal conditioning upgrade.**

No blocker.

## 38. Stable trait vs current state ordering audit

PASS AS A COACHING HEURISTIC, NOT CAUSAL LAW.

The practical sequence:

1. verify task equivalence;
2. verify measurement;
3. inspect current state/context;
4. inspect training history;
5. then slower-changing traits

is retained because:

- current-state variables are more changeable;
- measurement/context errors are common;
- immutable-trait explanations are easy to overuse.

Canonical rule:

**this is a diagnostic heuristic for reducing attribution error, not a biological causal hierarchy.**

No blocker.
