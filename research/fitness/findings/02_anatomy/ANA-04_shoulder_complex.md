# ANA-04 — Плечевой комплекс

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-18**  
Область: 02 — Анатомия

## 0. Decision

Создать structural-functional карту плечевого комплекса, достаточную для последующего анализа press/pull/raise/arm work без:

- GH-only simplification;
- fixed scapulohumeral-ratio myth;
- one “correct” scapular trajectory;
- подмены anatomy техникой или диагнозом.

## 1. Canonical shoulder-complex model

Для проекта:

1. **SC joint** — true synovial joint;
2. **AC joint** — true synovial joint;
3. **GH joint** — true synovial joint;
4. **Scapulothoracic articulation** — functional gliding interface, not a conventional synovial joint.

Project rule:

**“shoulder” ≠ GH joint alone.**

Total upper-limb motion is distributed through clavicle, scapula, humerus and thorax.

## 2. Structural chain

Canonical path:

`sternum / first-rib region → SC → clavicle → AC/CC complex → scapula/glenoid → GH → humerus`

Functional ST interface:

`scapula ↔ soft-tissue/gliding layers ↔ thoracic wall`

Clavicle is the principal bony axial–appendicular bridge.

Scapula is a mobile glenoid/orientation platform.

## 3. Clavicle

Relevant structural roles:

1. links upper limb to axial skeleton through SC;
2. acts as a strut holding shoulder girdle laterally;
3. transmits load between upper limb/scapula and sternum;
4. provides muscle/ligament attachments.

Relevant landmarks:
- sternal end;
- acromial end;
- shaft;
- conoid tubercle;
- trapezoid line;
- costal tuberosity;
- subclavian groove.

Normal clavicular length/curvature varies.

## 4. Scapula

Key landmarks:

- superior/medial/lateral borders;
- superior/inferior/lateral angles;
- spine;
- acromion;
- coracoid;
- glenoid cavity;
- supraspinous/infraspinous/subscapular fossae;
- supra-/infraglenoid tubercles;
- suprascapular/spinoglenoid notches.

Structural roles:
- glenoid socket base;
- muscle attachment platform;
- mobile orientation platform for humerus.

## 5. Proximal humerus

Relevant structures:

1. humeral head;
2. anatomical neck;
3. surgical neck;
4. greater tubercle;
5. lesser tubercle;
6. intertubercular groove;
7. deltoid tuberosity distally.

Rotator-cuff attachments:
- supraspinatus → greater tubercle;
- infraspinatus → greater tubercle;
- teres minor → greater tubercle;
- subscapularis → lesser tubercle.

## 6. Sternoclavicular joint

SC joint:
- medial clavicle ↔ manubrium / first-costal-cartilage region;
- synovial saddle-type articulation;
- contains fibrocartilaginous articular disc;
- provides the main skeletal upper-limb ↔ axial connection.

Main stabilizing structures:
- anterior SC ligament;
- posterior SC ligament;
- interclavicular ligament;
- costoclavicular ligament.

Clavicular motion includes combinations of:
- elevation/depression;
- protraction/retraction;
- axial rotation.

Exact angles → MEC.

## 7. Acromioclavicular / coracoclavicular complex

AC joint:
- lateral clavicle ↔ acromion;
- planar synovial joint.

Relevant stabilizing structures:
- AC capsule/ligament;
- coracoclavicular complex:
  - conoid;
  - trapezoid.

CC ligaments maintain clavicle–scapula spatial relationship but are not a separate synovial joint.

## 8. Coracoacromial arch

Coracoacromial ligament spans coracoid ↔ acromion and contributes to the superior arch over the cuff/humeral-head region.

It is not a separate joint.

Detailed subacromial contact mechanics → MEC/MED.

## 9. Scapulothoracic articulation

ST articulation = functional gliding of scapula over posterolateral thoracic wall.

It is not a true synovial joint.

Relevant intervening layers include:
- subscapularis;
- serratus anterior;
- bursae/gliding planes.

Project rule:

**scapula is not “pinned” to the thorax.**

It moves dynamically on a variable 3D thoracic surface.

## 10. Scapular motion

Useful descriptors:

1. upward/downward rotation;
2. internal/external rotation;
3. anterior/posterior tilt;
4. elevation/depression;
5. protraction/retraction.

These movements combine in 3D.

“Protraction” and “retraction” are not pure one-axis slides.

## 11. Glenohumeral joint

GH joint:
- humeral head ↔ glenoid;
- ball-and-socket;
- multiaxial;
- highly mobile;
- relatively limited osseous congruence.

Stability depends on integrated:
- bony geometry;
- labrum;
- capsule/ligaments;
- pressure mechanisms;
- rotator cuff and other dynamic stabilizers.

Project wording:

**high mobility with limited osseous congruence**, not “fragile by design”.

## 12. Glenoid labrum

Labrum = fibrocartilaginous rim around glenoid.

Roles:
- deepens/enlarges effective socket;
- contributes capsulolabral stability;
- interfaces with capsule/ligament structures;
- relates superiorly to long-head biceps attachment complex.

Labrum ≠ meniscus.

## 13. GH capsule / ligaments

Core capsular restraints:

1. SGHL;
2. MGHL;
3. IGHL complex;
4. coracohumeral ligament.

Their tension depends on:
- arm elevation/abduction;
- humeral rotation;
- translation direction.

Project rule:

**GH ligaments are position-dependent restraints, not one-action ropes.**

## 14. Rotator interval

Region between:
- supraspinatus superiorly;
- subscapularis inferiorly;

with associated:
- capsule;
- coracohumeral / superior GH ligament structures;
- long-head biceps relationship.

Detailed pathology → MED.

## 15. Bursae

Relevant:
- subacromial/subdeltoid;
- subscapular;
- subcoracoid.

Role:
reduce friction / permit tissue gliding.

Bursa presence ≠ bursitis.

## 16. Rotator cuff

Four muscles:

1. supraspinatus;
2. infraspinatus;
3. teres minor;
4. subscapularis.

Their tendons blend with GH capsule.

Core project model:

**cuff contributes both to humeral movement and dynamic humeral-head stabilization/centering.**

### Supraspinatus
- elevation/abduction contribution;
- stabilization.

Do not encode “owns first 15°”.

### Infraspinatus
- external rotation;
- stabilization.

### Teres minor
- external rotation;
- stabilization;
- position-dependent adduction contribution.

### Subscapularis
- internal rotation;
- anterior stabilization;
- position-dependent adduction contribution.

Exact force vectors → MEC.

## 17. Deltoid

Broad regions:
- anterior/clavicular;
- middle/acromial;
- posterior/spinal.

Origin:
- lateral clavicle;
- acromion;
- scapular spine.

Insertion:
- deltoid tuberosity.

Broad tendencies:
- anterior → flexion / internal-rotation contribution;
- middle → elevation/abduction;
- posterior → extension / external-rotation contribution.

Moment arms vary with position → MEC.

## 18. Scapular muscles

### Trapezius
Contributes to combinations of:
- elevation;
- depression;
- retraction;
- upward rotation;
- stabilization.

Upper trapezius ≠ shrug-only muscle.

### Serratus anterior
Contributes to:
- protraction;
- holding scapula against thoracic wall;
- upward rotation.

### Rhomboids
Contribute to:
- retraction;
- downward-rotation component;
- stabilization.

### Levator scapulae
Contributes to:
- elevation;
- downward-rotation component.

### Pectoralis minor
Acts on scapula:
- anterior/inferior motion;
- protraction/depression patterns depending task.

### Subclavius
Anchors/depresses clavicle and contributes to SC stability.

## 19. Axiohumeral / boundary muscles

### Pectoralis major
Broad actions:
- adduction;
- internal rotation;
- horizontal adduction;
- clavicular fibers can contribute to flexion.

### Latissimus dorsi
Broad actions:
- extension;
- adduction;
- internal rotation.

### Teres major
Broad actions:
- extension;
- adduction;
- internal rotation.

Teres major is **not** rotator cuff.

### Coracobrachialis
Boundary muscle contributing to GH:
- flexion;
- adduction.

Detailed arm anatomy → ANA-05.

## 20. Long head of biceps at shoulder

Long-head biceps tendon:
- attaches at supraglenoid/superior-labral complex;
- passes through GH region;
- continues through intertubercular groove.

Project rule:

**biceps is not anatomically elbow-only.**

Detailed biceps elbow/supination function → ANA-05.

## 21. Scapular upward-rotation coordination

Main contributors include:
- serratus anterior;
- trapezius regions.

Other periscapular muscles provide task-dependent opposing/stabilizing contributions.

Do not model scapular upward rotation as one-muscle action.

## 22. Scapulohumeral coordination

Current 2025 systematic review/meta-regression supports:

- substantial interindividual variability;
- angle-dependent scapular kinematics;
- no universal “setting phase”;
- no constant universal scapulohumeral ratio.

Canonical rule:

**scapulohumeral rhythm = dynamic coordination pattern, not a fixed gear ratio.**

Project does not replace old 2:1/3:1 teaching with another universal number.

## 23. Humerothoracic vs GH elevation

Total arm elevation can combine:

1. GH motion;
2. scapular rotation/translation;
3. AC motion;
4. SC/clavicular motion;
5. thoracic contribution depending task.

ROM measurements must specify reference frame.

## 24. Scapular plane

Scapula lies obliquely on thoracic wall.

Natural elevation commonly occurs in an oblique scapular plane.

“Scaption” is useful descriptive terminology.

No single universal scapular-plane angle is encoded.

## 25. Normal variation

Relevant variation includes:

- clavicle length/curvature;
- glenoid morphology/version;
- humeral-head morphology;
- scapular resting orientation;
- acromial morphology;
- ligament/labral variants;
- muscle attachment variation;
- serratus digitations;
- scapular kinematics.

Project rule:

**population-average anatomy/kinematics does not define one visually correct shoulder trajectory.**

## 26. Anatomy vs technique / biomechanics / pathology

### Anatomy
structures, attachments, broad action tendencies, relationships, variation.

### Biomechanics
moment arms, force vectors, contact forces, angle-specific coupling.

### Technique
exercise path, grip, elbow position, ROM, scapular strategy.

### Medicine
instability, cuff/labral pathology, bursitis, pain, clinical dyskinesis.

Normal anatomy alone does not diagnose pathology or prescribe one technique.

## 27. Training-relevant structural consequences

Anatomy supports only these broad statements:

1. presses/pulls are not GH-only movements;
2. scapula and clavicle normally participate in many arm movements;
3. rotator cuff stabilizes dynamically while also producing movement;
4. shoulder position changes multi-joint muscle geometry;
5. arm-isolation exercises may still involve shoulder structures;
6. “shoulders pinned back” is not a universal anatomical law;
7. one observed scapular pattern is not automatically abnormal.

## 28. Evidence / confidence

### HIGH

- SC/AC/GH + functional ST architecture;
- clavicle as axial–appendicular bridge;
- labrum/capsule/ligament structure;
- rotator-cuff composition;
- cuff capsule integration and dynamic stabilization;
- major periscapular / axiohumeral muscle map;
- long-head biceps shoulder relationship;
- distributed total arm motion.

### HIGH for variability / MODERATE for exact application

- scapulohumeral coordination is variable;
- fixed universal ratio/setting phase is not supported.

### MODERATE / individual-dependent

- exact personal scapular-plane angle;
- exact muscle contribution by angle/task;
- exact individual kinematic trajectory.
