## 2. Corrections and extensions

R1 — Qualitative confidence, identity and wider parental pathways. Finlayson primary text reports 16/22 high or moderate CERQual confidence, leaving six not in these categories; this is confidence in qualitative findings, not a participant proportion or prevalence of identity integration. Two Swiss papers and three Brazilian papers appear to report common underlying studies, so 36 papers not 36 independent cohorts. Do not turn themes into universal stages, required fulfillment, or causal effects. Additional non-gestational evidence: British adoptive-parent prospective study https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph16245153, indexed original abstract and methods: 96 adoptive parents followed four times during first four years after placement; most reporters were mothers, but not a female-only sample. Direct publisher 429; PMC browser verification blocked. No preplacement baseline or specific symptom coefficients verified here. Another comparison https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3433270/ (indexed original extracts) reports lower selected anxiety symptoms and higher well-being in adoptive vs postpartum women, restricted to mothers recruited through one private international adoption agency. This evidence establishes relevance of distinct parental pathways and measurement beyond gestation, not equivalence, causal differences or clinical prevalence. Additional maternal self-efficacy prospective study PMID36271681 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36271681/ indexed original abstract N292 socially/culturally disadvantaged families; distinguishes perceived parenting capability as an outcome worthy of separate study. No detailed effects asserted. Root cause: original qualitative synthesis generalizes broadly and explicitly leaves diverse pathways open; added concrete scope evidence and narrowed confidence interpretation. Retest against original source and WOM-05 role-vs-physiology distinction; remaining diversity gaps open.

R2 — Self-control trajectory, sample dependence and actual time window. Direct Table1 maternal self-control T-score means 53.05,51.14,50.81,50.12; nonmother 50.38,50.58,50.57,50.44. Descriptive maternal decline2.93 points, most of it between baseline and six months. In the specific T2–T4 growth model authors preferred an intercept-only specification by BIC, rather than linear change, for both mothers and fathers. Thus original note wording 'continuing during follow-up' is limited to observed descriptive scores and full-period nonlinear model; no established continuing linear decline after six months. Daily aggregated self-control declines for mothers51.52->49.48 and fathers50.26->48.86; daily measure not collected for nonparents, so no daily parent/nonparent contrast. Pregnancy stress concurrent maternal intercept b=-3.43 CI[-4.96,-1.91], but linear slope p=.887 and quadratic p=.659; no evidence it explains change. Late-pregnancy baseline cannot distinguish preexisting difference, anticipation or return to prepregnancy level. Wehner goal paper N542,373 completed both,169 dropped (31.2%); same VENI grant451-13-025 as self-control study, similar baseline schedule, sample sizes and authors. Shared project lineage verified; exact respondent overlap not established; do not count independent cohorts without confirmation. Reuse of Wehner across WOM-02 and WOM-05 already noted. Goal importance and effort, trait self-description and actual capacity remain distinct; no neurological claim. Root cause: incomplete time-window/model distinction and missing project dependency. Retest Tables1/3/4, methods and funding; arithmetic section3.

R3 — Matching and multidimensional well-being. Voznyuk et al. final indexed full-page excerpts now available, but direct open still cache miss. Table1 matched groups differ on baseline standard-of-living satisfaction p=.024 and survey wave p<.001; relationship satisfaction p=.053 and life satisfaction p=.062. Nonsignificance is not proof of balance. Women comprise69.3% vs66.7%, p=.172. Prefer standardized covariate balance and overlap diagnostics to p-value-based balance; these diagnostics and post-match adjustments not fully inspected, so do not assert model failure or adequate control. Authors report no detected gender differences in changes in wellbeing/relationship quality (additional analyses); exact interaction precision not verified. Overall estimates are not maternal-specific effects. Matching on observed baseline covariates and outcome levels helps address measured differences, not unobserved confounding, anticipation, different trends, attrition or selection into the eligible sample. GFS/NZAVS distinction: this study and WOM-04.3 E4 distress/support study both use NZ panel descriptions; exact shared respondent lineage not established here, so no assumption of independence across overlapping claims. Tran sample has pregnancy rather than prepregnancy reference, no verified nonparent comparator; mean increases in positive affect and meaning can coexist with decreases in life/relationship satisfaction without establishing that every participant experiences all changes. No evidence-based equivalence after 2–3 months. Original authors' exploratory daily predictor patterns not identified mechanisms. Root cause: earlier matching description lacked residual imbalance and explicit moderation result. Retest published Table1 and Additional Findings, preserve multidimensional method.

R4 — Cohort, time and personal goals. Graham's methods explicitly include the Seattle Longitudinal Study; Brandt uses SLS too. This upgrades prior possible overlap to confirmed shared source cohort, not known exact overlapping person count. Graham harmonized models are not 16 independent replications of one precise female trajectory; model-specific availability and measures vary. Keep age/cohort/period nonidentifiability (period = birth year + age) and distinguish rank stability, mean trajectory and individual variability. Do not use a fitted cohort-average turning point as a prediction of individual timing. Buchinger E4/E5 share SOEP source; exact overlap not confirmed. E5 age endpoints differ (40 for women,50 for men), so a sex contrast is not necessarily a same-age contrast; early goal valuation and later realized parenthood status are distinct, neither childlessness nor parenthood reveals autonomous choice, infertility, regret, goal adjustment or fulfillment. HILDA relationship-status heterogeneity reinforces conditional rather than universal statements; different measures and comparison groups not direct contradiction. No causal interpretation of matching. Carry WOM-01 SWAN coefficient/model-test ambiguity forward unchanged: neither age nor menopausal status is a direct hormonal assay; no new biological conclusion. Root cause: missing explicit cohort dependency and different endpoint interpretation. Retest original indexed methods and abstracts; not a complete age-period-cohort reanalysis.

R5 — Support measures, causal evidence and later parenting. Khademi/Kaveh explicitly excludes longitudinal and trial studies; final synthesis includes 11 cross-sectional studies, not heterogeneous longitudinal causal evidence. Actual studies observed up to18 months even though eligible up to24. Different instruments, narrative synthesis; no pooled estimate, causal direction or mediation. 5 low/6 medium risk by authors' adapted tool; this is not an independent risk audit. Schobinger's 26 participants include dyad members, not 26 independent families; purposive Swiss hospital sample (healthy term infants, French-speaking, all cohabiting/married and employed), not general population or every form of parenthood. Themes about formal support do not establish actual preferred help for a different individual. Erratum funding addition only. Historical Cochrane 2013 pooled risk ratio pertains to heterogeneous psychosocial/psychological prevention interventions, not proof social support alone mediated effect; older 2004 version different conclusion, do not cite as same estimate. No current clinical recommendation derived. E7 indexed primary abstract newly verifies actor-partner result; stress measured twice, coparenting support only at second wave, so no prospective coparenting-to-stress feedback test from this design alone. E8 five waves enable studying later parenting, not proof stress causes school adjustment. Clinical distress, symptoms, diagnosis and safety remain separate; no diagnosis or relationship inference from role or help-seeking. Root cause: original support review omitted actual design restriction and later coparenting source left as lead. Retest original methods and abstract; strengthened evidence without causal inflation.

R6 — Relationship satisfaction meta-analysis: heterogeneity, comparator and reporting ambiguity. Bogdan Table1 first-year mothers g=-.31 CI[-.38,-.25] I2=90.91%, fathers -.29 CI[-.36,-.22] I2=81.56%; nonparent women -.12 CI[-.24,-.01], men -.13 CI[-.24,-.02], each k4,N165. These are separate pooled pre/post standardized changes, not a randomized parenthood contrast. Arithmetic differences -.19 and -.16 are descriptive contrasts, not inferential effect estimates; parent and nonparent samples very imbalanced, no matched causal comparison established. Direct gender subgroup test Q=.20,p=.65 (not equivalence); second-year Q=.12,p=.72, no sufficient nonparent data for second year. High heterogeneity precludes assuming common individual trajectory. Article calls g=.31/.29 medium, inconsistent with usual .2/.5/.8 landmarks stated by authors; use numerical effect rather than categorical magnitude. Table2 first-year DAS g=-.24 CI[-.44,.05] with p=.000: CI includes zero but displayed p-value appears highly significant. Do not reconcile or use this subgroup significance without clarifying test definition or correction. Table3 second-year age estimate -.008 with z=.57 (opposite sign), relationship-length estimate first year SE=.004 in table vs .003 in prose; unresolved reporting inconsistencies. Cross-partner meta-regression reports b=-.68 for one sex and +.87 for other while prose interprets both as steeper decline with partner decline; coding/estimation unclear, no verified dyadic causal relation. Exact article code, study-level influence and overlap with Mitnick not audited. Total sample N145139 vs listed sex counts4790+139956=144746, residual393 may reflect unclassified/combined samples; not resolved. Earlier WOM-05 note only gave pooled changes, not these limitations. Retest direct Tables1–3 and Cross-Partner Associations; robust bounded conclusion: average satisfaction often declines around transition, but also declines in nonparents; no unique cause, individual prognosis or assignment of blame established. Carry these restrictions into REL-X03/04.

## 4. Revised operational method
Define actual parental path, timing, previous and current roles, and precisely measured outcome. Separate identity centrality, self-esteem, self-efficacy, autonomy, preference, goal valuation, effort, resources, caregiving behavior and relationship-specific motive. Identify reference period and available opportunities; require actual pretransition evidence and appropriate comparators for causal claims, with attention to anticipation, regression to mean, measurement, selection, time-varying confounding and dependency. Distinguish prenatal and preconception baseline, short-term adaptation and durable within-person change. Do not reduce development to age, parenthood, reproductive state or universal stages. For support distinguish perceived availability, desired form, actual assistance and observed load reduction, appraisal, coping response and outcome. Respect autonomy; no obligation of affection, consent or stereotyped duties inferred from support, role or distress. Clinical/safety concerns require distinct scope. Use source-specific effects, uncertainty, real evidence and competing conditional predictions under IND-03. No calibrated personal model, causal pathway or universal female profile claimed.
