## 3. Corrections and qualifications

R1 — Base-rate dependence and response tendency. Bond & DePaulo's aggregate 54% overall equals the arithmetic average of 47% lie and 61% truth classifications for equal weighting, not a validated real-world conditional-rate pair. Levine et al. 2014 direct abstract reports linear base-rate effects in all three interactive experiments, mean r=.61. This supports dependence of overall accuracy on stimulus prevalence; does not demonstrate improved discrimination. Levine & Serota 2025 reanalyse earlier data and report truth bias correlations +.88 with truth accuracy, -.88 with lie accuracy, and overall r<.03 in balanced designs. The underlying quantities are mathematically coupled; do not construe correlations as independent psychological mechanisms. For a balanced reference set, let T and L be conditional correct-truth and correct-lie rates: accuracy=(T+L)/2, truth-response fraction=(T+1-L)/2. Thus truth response and the T-L difference are algebraically linked. In another population, accuracy depends on both prevalence and conditional rates, which themselves may vary. No actual prevalence or individual test-performance claim.

R2 — Weak cues and confidence are not evidence of total impossibility. Original meta-analyses support weak/heterogeneous individual behavioral associations, and the lens-model study distinguishes actual from perceived evidence; these analyses may share studies. DePaulo et al. 1997 author abstract confirms weighted accuracy-confidence r=.04 across 18 independent samples, not significantly different from zero, not a universal inverse relationship or equivalence. Audible versus visible judgments in Bond & DePaulo do not isolate acoustic tone from lexical content. Gaze-aversion null does not establish zero information in all gaze contexts. Behavioral indicators, factual corroboration, confidence and validated accuracy must be separately assessed. No fixed cue rule, personal classifier or operational deception-detection technique.

R3 — Relational dishonesty, disclosure rules and actual motive. Roggensack & Sillars indexed original results support discrepancy between confidence/assumed agreement and actual measured rule understanding; stronger commitment need not imply greater actual agreement. Unilateral assumed duties and agreed disclosure obligations differ. Cross-sectional associations do not establish whether understanding increases conflict or vice versa. Cole's dyadic questionnaire evidence supports studying competing motivations, not identifying private reasons. Bibliographic correction: Mazzini et al. 2025 institutional record lists Lau Lilleholt (not Harpviken, as erroneously named in SOC-X03.1 E8), alongside Rachele Mazzini, Anna Louise Malfilâtre, Séamus A. Power and Ingo Zettler. N=656=455 current+175 former+26 hypothetical future accounts. These are selected narratives, not verified events or frequencies in the population. The taxonomy's reported self-/partner-protective motives are accounts, not proof of intent or justification. Treat the new DIRS publication as an open primary-source follow-up; no diagnostic threshold or individual classification asserted.

R4 — Emotion perception, context and criterion. Barrett review and Aviezer original abstract oppose an invariant one-to-one facial emotion interpretation, not the presence of any usable facial information. Steward direct abstract: both contextual influence on facial labels and facial influence on contextual labels are substantial (g_av>1.23); clarity and congruency moderate these effects. After accounting for these factors, face versus body/body-plus-scene influence is not detectably different in reported comparison. This is not an equivalence test or evidence of equal accuracy in recovering experienced emotion. Outcome is observer label. Indexed methods indicate RVE was not originally preregistered and direction of estimates was reportedly unchanged without it; full sensitivity analysis not reproduced. Official Barrett corrigendum is retained. No duplication of constituent studies as independent corroboration.

R5 — Aggregation and cross-cultural generalization. Cowen direct Results distinguish mean predictions by eliciting video and individual experience. Reported average-response prediction correlations: Japanese expression to US experience r=.65 (SE .03) versus US expression r=.70 (SE .02); US expression to Japanese experience r=.72 (.03) versus Japanese expression r=.80 (.05). These are average-response analyses, not individual accuracy or direct comparison tests of correlations. The study distinguishes at least 21 dimensions of reported experience and 12 differentiated by facial information under its analysis; do not treat them as one-to-one labels. Unequal repeated observations and the separate survey samples (English n=1332, Japanese n=61, averages 30.7/274.3 video responses) matter; survey response counts and recorded facial reactions are distinct denominators, not interchangeable. Full model and dependence analysis not audited. Laukka & Elfenbein's cross-cultural recognition tasks support context-specific above-chance label recognition and group-familiarity differences, not motive inference or universal accuracy. No demographic inference from isolated behavior.

R6 — Live response timing, asynchronous delay and deception are distinct. Templeton 2022 measures live turn transitions and reported social connection; manipulating intervals for third-party listeners changes perceived connection, not necessarily actual relationship state. The 2023 study's contrast of long gaps between friends and strangers is a counterexample to a universal monotonic interpretation; indexed methods refer to recordings from original studies, so possible dataset overlap remains unresolved and papers are not counted as independent replications. Mapala direct text reports a direction counterexample to usual latency hypothesis in a small experimental sample; its literature review cites Sporer & Schwandt d=.18,p=.004 and DePaulo d=.02, both heterogeneous and not independently verified from original tables in this audit. Sporer original indexed abstract supports average associations for some vocal features, not fixed thresholds. Different experimental tasks and speech vs asynchronous opportunity/time are not interchangeable. No intent or veracity conclusion from timing alone.

R7 — Previously excluded multicue synthesis and actual validation. Hartwig & Bond 2014 original extracted abstract reports 144 samples, 9380 senders, 26866 messages. Results report pooled multiple R=.52, 95% CI [.49,.56], heterogeneous across studies Q(143)=299.58; selected sample-level correlations range .01–.87. Authors explicitly distinguish same-data optimization of behavioral features from expected cross-sample performance and calculate expected cross-validity R_c=.42 using mean values. This is an analytical estimate, not observed prospective held-out validation. Their headline nearly 70% accuracy should not be compared directly with unaided human 54% as if both were identical measured test outcomes. Non-significant moderators do not establish invariance across contexts. No original data reanalysis or case-specific classifier. Some primary samples likely overlap earlier reviews. PDF screenshot failed; exact numerical claims are limited to extracted text. Scientific relevance is that jointly assessed signals may contain information while in-sample fit overstates transfer; this is not a license to aggregate dependent observations into a numerical verdict.

## 4. Revised reasoning rules

1. Separate the literal proposition, pragmatic meaning, independently known facts, contemporaneous belief, inferred intent, actual disclosure duty, actual choice and consequences. Demonstrated factual error/nonperformance alone does not establish deliberate deception; private nondisclosure and changed intentions need separate evidence.
2. Use context and multiple kinds of evidence, not body-language dictionaries. Experienced emotion, expressive display, observer label, veracity and motive are different targets. Missing cue is not proof of missing emotion.
3. Define reference criterion, prevalence, conditional error rates and evidence independence before discussing classifier performance. In an illustrative balanced dataset, (T,L)=(.61,.47) gives accuracy .54 and truth-response fraction .57; arithmetic is not an empirical model of a new setting. More confidence, more suspected lies or better fit to already labeled data is not automatically more accurate.
4. Assess cue diagnosticity against distinct credible explanations and consider dependencies, sampling, channel, opportunity, time scale and measurement. Separate prospective prediction, factual corroboration and causal attribution. No personal numeric confidence calibration from population summaries.
5. Prioritize legitimately accessible facts, existing agreements and voluntary clarification where appropriate. Respect privacy and explicit choices. Do not use surveillance, deceptive testing, coercion, individualized susceptibility scoring, or methods of bypassing refusal. Serious documented harm or breach can warrant action without proving hidden intent.
6. Preserve disconfirming information, uncertainty and unresolved source issues. Correct narrow established claims; do not overgeneralize null results or imply all interpersonal judgment is worthless.
