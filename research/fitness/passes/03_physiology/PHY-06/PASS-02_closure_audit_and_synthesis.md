## 3. Hypertrophy / swelling audit

PASS.

Retained hypertrophy is defined by chronic increase in muscle/fiber size measured after sufficient resolution of acute swelling.

Valid outcomes include:

- whole-muscle volume;
- anatomical CSA;
- muscle thickness;
- fiber CSA.

Critical boundaries:

1. early size increase can include edema;
2. one measurement site does not represent whole muscle;
3. fiber CSA and whole-muscle size are related but not identical;
4. regional adaptation can be nonuniform.

Canonical rule:

**hypertrophy = retained tissue enlargement, not postexercise swelling.**

Detailed molecular mechanisms remain PHY-07.

## 4. Hypertrophy time-course audit

PASS.

Current evidence does not support one universal onset week.

Detectability depends on:

- program;
- training status;
- muscle;
- method;
- measurement error;
- edema control;
- sampling schedule.

A current 12-week within-subject trial confirms progressive mCSA increase across repeated exposure and differential late-phase growth under maintained relative effort.

Canonical rule:

**hypertrophy occurs across repeated weeks-to-months; exact first-detectable time is protocol/method dependent.**

No blocker.

## 5. PCSA / anatomical CSA / specific tension audit

PASS.

These constructs remain separate:

- anatomical CSA = geometric cross-sectional size;
- PCSA = contractile tissue effectively arranged in parallel, derived from volume/architecture assumptions;
- specific tension = force per contractile cross-sectional area.

Specific-tension estimation in humans is methodologically difficult because torque contribution, activation, tendon compliance and moment arms must be estimated/measured.

Canonical rule:

**size, PCSA and force quality are not interchangeable.**

No blocker.

## 6. Fascicle-length audit

PASS.

Fascicle length is training-plastic in some contexts, but evidence is heterogeneous.

Current boundaries:

- muscle-specific;
- region-specific;
- protocol-specific;
- method-dependent;
- not mandatory for hypertrophy.

A 2025 meta-analysis in older-adult interventions found no pooled fascicle-length response, reinforcing that architecture change cannot be assumed universally.

Canonical rule:

**do not require fascicle-length increase as proof of successful adaptation.**

No blocker.

## 7. Pennation-angle audit

PASS.

Pennation angle may increase with hypertrophy/RT in some muscles and populations, but pooled intervention evidence can be small or null depending on sample and method.

Canonical rule:

**pennation angle is a context-dependent architectural adaptation, not a universal quality marker.**

No blocker.

## 8. Muscle-quality audit

PASS.

“Muscle quality” remains an umbrella term that can mean:

- strength per muscle size/mass;
- specific tension;
- ultrasound echo intensity;
- CT/MRI attenuation;
- intramuscular adipose-related metrics;
- other composition indices.

Canonical rule:

**always state the operational definition before interpreting muscle-quality change.**

No blocker.

## 9. Capillarization audit

PASS.

RT-specific evidence supports increased capillary supply in some populations/protocols.

Examples retained:

- older men: increased capillary contacts and CFPE after 12 weeks;
- young men: capillary-to-fiber ratio increase detectable after 2 weeks in a 12-week RT intervention.

Critical metric boundary:

- capillary contacts;
- capillary-to-fiber ratio;
- CFPE;
- capillaries/mm²

are not interchangeable.

Hypertrophy can change denominator-based density independently of absolute/per-fiber capillary supply.

Important evidence-boundary correction:

The broad 2025 mitochondrial/capillary meta-regression primarily synthesizes ET/HIT/SIT, so it is used only as broader exercise context, not as direct RT effect estimation.

Canonical rule:

**capillary supply can adapt with RT; capillary density alone can mislead.**

No blocker.

## 10. Mitochondrial audit

PASS.

RT can alter mitochondrial:

- respiratory capacity;
- ETC protein abundance;
- fusion/fission machinery;
- redox handling;
- relative content/volume density.

Current evidence supports two simultaneous possibilities:

1. mitochondrial function/protein machinery can improve;
2. relative mitochondrial volume density can decrease through hypertrophy-related dilution without net mitochondrial loss.

Canonical rule:

**mitochondrial function, absolute content and relative volume density are distinct outcomes.**

No blocker.

## 11. Enzyme-profile audit

PASS.

RT can alter glycolytic and oxidative enzymes, but no universal one-direction profile is established.

Canonical rule:

**one enzyme cannot define the entire metabolic phenotype; enzyme adaptation is pathway/protocol specific.**

Detailed metabolism remains BIO.

No blocker.

## 12. Substrate-store audit

PASS WITH MODERATE CONFIDENCE.

Training can alter resting:

- glycogen;
- phosphocreatine/creatine pool;
- high-energy phosphate stores.

But magnitude depends strongly on:

- diet;
- carbohydrate availability;
- creatine intake;
- timing;
- program.

Evidence is less contemporary/robust than for hypertrophy.

Canonical rule:

**chronic storage capacity must be separated from acute depletion and interpreted with nutrition context.**

No blocker.

## 13. ECM audit

PASS WITH PRESERVED UNCERTAINTY.

Evidence supports training-responsive ECM biology through:

- collagen turnover;
- MMP/TIMP remodeling;
- matrix-related transcription;
- structural/adhesion signatures.

However:

- acute collagen remodeling is not proof of chronic matrix architecture;
- direct human chronic structural quantification remains less mature than fiber hypertrophy evidence.

Current 2026 transcriptomic synthesis supports conserved ECM remodeling responses and resistance-training-associated structural maturation/reinforcement signatures.

Canonical rule:

**ECM is a real adaptation compartment, but exact chronic collagen quantity/crosslinking/architecture requires direct evidence.**

No blocker.

## 14. Damage-resistance audit

PASS.

Repeated exposure produces repeated-bout protection.

Current 2026 meta-analysis supports reduced subsequent:

- MVC loss;
- DOMS;
- CK response.

Canonical rule:

**greater resistance to exercise-induced damage is a chronic protective adaptation distinct from hypertrophy.**

Less soreness after familiarization does not imply less effective training.

No blocker.

## 15. Regional-adaptation audit

PASS.

Hypertrophy and architecture can be regionally nonuniform.

Current methodological guidance supports measuring multiple sites/regions where whole-muscle inference matters.

The 2025 longer-vs-shorter muscle-length meta-analysis found mostly trivial regional between-condition differences, with high probability of practical equivalence across regions.

Canonical rule:

**regional adaptation exists, but large regional targeting effects must be demonstrated rather than assumed.**

No blocker.

## 16. Multi-clock audit

PASS.

Different domains operate on different timelines.

### Early
Can include:
- repeated-bout protection;
- angiogenic/capillary remodeling;
- early neural adaptation outside primary PHY-06 ownership.

### Weeks
Can include:
- retained hypertrophy becoming measurable;
- some architecture/PCSA changes;
- capillary/metabolic/mitochondrial remodeling.

### Months and longer
Can include continued:
- hypertrophy;
- architecture;
- ECM remodeling;
- substrate/metabolic phenotype;
- tissue-specific structural maturation.

Canonical rule:

**there is no single skeletal-muscle adaptation clock.**

Each outcome requires its own reassessment interval and measurement-error model.

No blocker.
