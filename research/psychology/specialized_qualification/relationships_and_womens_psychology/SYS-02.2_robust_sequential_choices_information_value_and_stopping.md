## 3. Construct and normative map
Distinguish uncertainty about available outcomes, their probabilities, consequences, preferences, measurement and model structure. More information may reduce uncertainty without changing the action. Information can also have intrinsic, emotional or expressive value distinct from instrumental value. In a fixed finite decision problem with coherent probabilities, consistent utility, unchanged feasible actions and cost-free optional use of information, its gross expected decision value is nonnegative because it can be ignored. Net value may be negative once acquisition, delay, privacy, attention and opportunity costs or changing feasible options are included. Incorrect or poorly interpreted information can harm real decisions; normative ideal assumptions need not hold.

With state s, action a, utility U(a,s), probability p(s), baseline V0=max_a sum_s p(s)U(a,s). EVPI=sum_s p(s)max_a U(a,s)-V0. For a sample signal z with known conditional distribution, EVSI=sum_z p(z)max_a sum_s p(s|z)U(a,s)-V0. If the information leaves the same action optimal for all signals, instrumental EVSI=0. Under the idealized assumptions and when perfect information concerns all uncertainties relevant to the sample, EVSI cannot exceed EVPI. The distinction between gross and net value matters. No personal probabilities or utilities estimated here.

Under deep uncertainty, use scenarios that cover materially different plausible assumptions rather than invent likelihoods. Maximin maximizes worst-case utility over the chosen scenarios; minimax regret minimizes worst-case forgone utility compared with the best feasible action in each scenario. Satisficing asks whether explicit acceptable constraints/thresholds hold across relevant scenarios; these thresholds come from actual decision-maker values, not arbitrary analyst numbers. Expected utility with a justified distribution, minimax regret, maximin and robust satisficing address distinct goals. Scenario counts are not event probabilities; sensitivity to scenario bounds, included options and criteria must be exposed. Neither worst case nor regret has universal priority.

Sequential decisions compare a terminal action now against continuing acquisition/deferral with possible future outcomes and feasible contingent actions. A one-step rule can be insufficient if later information has complementary value. In a known finite model a recursive value equation can represent stop versus continue: V(h)=max{V_stop(h), max_q[-c(q,h)+E[V(h,z)|h,q]]}, where h is available history, q an admissible information action, z the result, and terminal conditions/horizon and time costs are defined. This is an idealized decision recursion, not a fitted personal policy. Avoid infinite waiting, arbitrary deadlines or moving criteria; establish legitimate review conditions from actual costs, constraints and evidence. A freely expressed refusal determines feasible options; no search policy is a reason to seek ways around it.

## 5. Operational procedure
1. Define the actual decision, feasible alternatives, end goals, constraints, horizon and relevant consequences. Distinguish another person's independent choice from one's own available actions.
2. Separate what is known from uncertain states, uncertain causal models and uncertain preferences; identify assumptions that could reverse the decision. Avoid false precision.
3. Compare alternatives by relevant outcomes under plausible scenarios. Apply real hard constraints first, then test sensitivity to criteria, scenario set and costs. Do not silently choose a risk criterion or trade away safety/autonomy.
4. Ask what information could change a feasible action, how reliable it would be, and its total acquisition and delay costs. If relevant information is not legitimately available, retain uncertainty rather than manufacture a test or invade privacy.
5. Compare act now, feasible provisional action, wait, stop and revise, including opportunity costs and irreversible effects. Consider future decision opportunities; define observable review conditions and a reasonable horizon based on the decision, not a universal deadline.
6. Track disconfirming evidence and update assumptions; evaluate decision process separately from outcome. Avoid treating new feedback as automatically improving judgment or redundant observations as independent evidence.
7. Preserve voluntary choices, privacy and safety. No surveillance, deception, pressure, exploitative tactics or psychological experimentation. Protective action can be warranted before all uncertainty is resolved.
