## 1. Что считать плечевым комплексом

Для проекта каноническая модель включает:

1. **Sternoclavicular (SC) joint** — истинный синовиальный сустав.
2. **Acromioclavicular (AC) joint** — истинный синовиальный сустав.
3. **Glenohumeral (GH) joint** — истинный синовиальный сустав.
4. **Scapulothoracic (ST) articulation** — функциональное скольжение лопатки по грудной клетке, не отдельный истинный синовиальный сустав.

Некоторые источники описывают дополнительные функциональные «articulations», например subacromial interface. Чтобы не путать уровни, проект использует четыре перечисленные единицы, отдельно описывая bursae/subacromial space.

## 2. Почему плечо — комплекс, а не один сустав

Подъём/перемещение руки требует согласованного движения:

`sternum/ribs ↔ clavicle ↔ scapula ↔ humerus`

GH joint не может объяснить весь humerothoracic ROM самостоятельно.

Положение glenoid меняется вместе со scapula, а scapula зависит от:
- AC motion;
- clavicular motion at SC;
- thoracic surface;
- muscular control.

Поэтому «плечо» в тренировочном языке почти всегда механически шире GH joint.

## 3. Clavicle

Clavicle — S-образная длинная кость плечевого пояса.

Основные роли:
1. единственная костная связь верхней конечности с axial skeleton через SC joint;
2. действует как распорка, удерживая плечевой пояс латерально от грудной клетки;
3. передаёт силы между scapula/upper limb и sternum;
4. даёт места прикрепления мышц/связок.

Ключевые области:
- sternal/medial end;
- acromial/lateral end;
- shaft;
- conoid tubercle;
- trapezoid line;
- costal tuberosity;
- subclavian groove.

Мышечные прикрепления включают:
- pectoralis major;
- deltoid;
- trapezius;
- sternocleidomastoid;
- subclavius. citeturn750734view2

## 4. Scapula

Scapula — плоская треугольная кость на заднелатеральной поверхности thoracic cage.

Ключевые ориентиры:

1. superior, medial и lateral borders;
2. superior, inferior и lateral angles;
3. scapular spine;
4. acromion;
5. coracoid process;
6. glenoid cavity;
7. supraspinous fossa;
8. infraspinous fossa;
9. subscapular fossa;
10. supraglenoid tubercle;
11. infraglenoid tubercle;
12. suprascapular notch;
13. spinoglenoid notch.

Scapula служит:
- socket base для humeral head;
- большим attachment platform для мышц;
- подвижной опорой, ориентирующей glenoid в пространстве. citeturn750734view3

## 5. Proximal humerus

Ключевые структуры:

1. humeral head;
2. anatomical neck;
3. surgical neck;
4. greater tubercle;
5. lesser tubercle;
6. intertubercular/bicipital groove;
7. crests/lips of groove;
8. deltoid tuberosity дистальнее.

Rotator cuff attachments:
- supraspinatus → greater tubercle;
- infraspinatus → greater tubercle;
- teres minor → greater tubercle;
- subscapularis → lesser tubercle. citeturn919369search3

## 6. Sternoclavicular joint

SC joint:
- между medial clavicle, manubrium и first costal cartilage region;
- synovial saddle-shaped joint;
- функционально multiaxial;
- primary skeletal connection upper limb ↔ axial skeleton. citeturn311795search1

Внутри:
- fibrocartilaginous articular disc;
- fibrous capsule.

Disc улучшает congruence и разделяет joint space на compartments.

## 7. SC ligaments

Основные стабилизирующие структуры:

1. anterior sternoclavicular ligament;
2. posterior sternoclavicular ligament;
3. interclavicular ligament;
4. costoclavicular ligament.

Costoclavicular ligament связывает clavicle с first rib/costal cartilage region и существенно ограничивает/направляет clavicular motion.

## 8. SC movements

Clavicle относительно sternum выполняет комбинации:

1. elevation/depression;
2. protraction/retraction;
3. axial/posterior rotation при подъёме руки.

Это не «движения руки отдельно» — они необходимы для перемещения scapula.

Exact angles and coupling → MEC, не anatomical constant.

## 9. Acromioclavicular joint

AC joint:
- lateral clavicle ↔ acromion;
- planar synovial joint;
- fibrous capsule + synovial lining;
- fibrocartilage на articulating surfaces;
- intra-articular fibrocartilaginous disc может присутствовать. citeturn750734view1

AC позволяет scapula менять orientation относительно clavicle.

## 10. AC/CC ligaments

### Acromioclavicular ligament
Capsular/intrinsic stabilizer, особенно horizontal stability.

### Coracoclavicular complex
Не пересекает AC joint напрямую, но удерживает spatial relation clavicle–coracoid.

Состоит из:
1. **conoid ligament** — более posteromedial;
2. **trapezoid ligament** — более anterolateral.

Это критическая structural suspension связь scapula с clavicle. citeturn750734view1turn919369search14

## 11. Coracoacromial arch

Coracoacromial ligament соединяет:
- coracoid;
- acromion.

Вместе с acromion/coracoid формирует superior arch над humeral head/rotator cuff region.

Это не отдельный сустав.

## 12. Scapulothoracic articulation

ST articulation:
- не true synovial joint;
- функциональное скольжение anterior scapula по posterolateral thoracic wall.

Между scapula и ribs лежат muscular/soft-tissue layers, прежде всего:
- subscapularis;
- serratus anterior;
с bursae/gliding interfaces в соответствующих областях.

Scapula часто лежит примерно на уровне ribs 2–7, но положение индивидуально и зависит от thoracic/scapular geometry.

## 13. Scapular rotations

3D scapular motion включает:

1. upward/downward rotation;
2. internal/external rotation;
3. anterior/posterior tilt;
4. translational descriptions such as elevation/depression, protraction/retraction.

Эти движения накладываются друг на друга.

«Protraction = только прямое движение вперёд» — упрощение: реальная scapular motion 3D.

## 14. Glenohumeral joint

GH:
- humeral head ↔ glenoid cavity;
- ball-and-socket;
- diarthrodial, multiaxial;
- высокая mobility при сравнительно shallow glenoid. citeturn750734view0

Главный structural trade-off:
- large humeral head;
- relatively shallow/small glenoid;
- broad ROM;
- stability depends heavily on soft tissue + dynamic control.

## 15. Articular surfaces

Humeral head и glenoid покрыты articular cartilage.

Glenoid osseous socket относительно неглубок.

Эта геометрия отличается от deep acetabulum hip joint.

Не делать из этого вывод «shoulder is unstable by design and therefore fragile» — это уже чрезмерная интерпретация. Комплекс имеет развитые static/dynamic stabilizers.

## 16. Glenoid labrum

Labrum:
- fibrocartilaginous rim вокруг glenoid;
- углубляет socket;
- увеличивает effective articular interface;
- связан с capsule/ligament complexes.

Superiorly labrum связан с origin/tendon complex long head biceps. citeturn750734view0

Labrum ≠ meniscus:
геометрия и роль различаются.

## 17. GH capsule

Fibrous capsule идёт:
- от glenoid rim region;
- к anatomical neck humerus.

Она относительно loose, что допускает большой ROM.

В capsule вплетены:
- glenohumeral ligaments;
- rotator cuff tendons in multiple regions.

Inferior capsule более свободна при arm adducted и разворачивается при elevation.

## 18. Glenohumeral ligaments

Основные capsular thickenings:

1. superior glenohumeral ligament (SGHL);
2. middle glenohumeral ligament (MGHL);
3. inferior glenohumeral ligament complex (IGHL).

Их натяжение зависит от:
- abduction angle;
- humeral rotation;
- direction of translation.

Следовательно, нельзя описывать их как простые верёвки, которые «всегда ограничивают одно движение». citeturn750734view0

## 19. Coracohumeral ligament и rotator interval

Coracohumeral ligament идёт от coracoid region к proximal humerus/cuff complex.

Rotator interval:
анатомический region между:
- supraspinatus superiorly;
- subscapularis inferiorly,
с capsuloligamentous structures и long-head biceps relationship.

Это важная stability/containment zone, но глубокая pathology → MED.

## 20. Long head of biceps at shoulder

Long head biceps:
- proximal attachment связан с supraglenoid tubercle/superior labral complex;
- tendon courses through GH joint region;
- затем проходит в intertubercular groove.

Функционально biceps — прежде всего тема ANA-05, но shoulder anatomy должна учитывать:
- superior labral relationship;
- dynamic stabilizer contribution in some contexts. citeturn750734view0turn750734view5

## 21. Bursae

Крупные shoulder bursae:

1. subacromial/subdeltoid;
2. subscapular;
3. subcoracoid.

Subacromial bursa:
- между deltoid/acromial region и supraspinatus/capsular structures;
- обычно не communicates with GH joint.

Bursae уменьшают friction между движущимися layers. citeturn750734view0

Наличие bursa ≠ bursitis.

## 22. Static vs dynamic stabilizers

### Static
1. bony geometry;
2. labrum;
3. capsule;
4. glenohumeral ligaments;
5. negative intra-articular pressure concept.

### Dynamic
1. rotator cuff;
2. deltoid and coordinated shoulder musculature;
3. long-head biceps contribution;
4. scapular stabilizers/rotators indirectly by orienting glenoid.

Static/dynamic is useful functional classification, not strict anatomical separation.

## 23. Rotator cuff

Four muscles:
1. supraspinatus;
2. infraspinatus;
3. teres minor;
4. subscapularis.

All originate from scapula and insert on proximal humerus.

Their tendons blend with capsule to form a musculotendinous cuff around anterior/superior/posterior GH joint. citeturn750734view5

## 24. Supraspinatus

Origin:
- supraspinous fossa.

Insertion:
- superior facet/region greater tubercle.

Innervation:
- suprascapular nerve, predominantly C5–C6.

Functional anatomy:
- contributes to humeral elevation/abduction;
- compresses/stabilizes humeral head.

Common teaching says it “initiates first 15° of abduction”, but project will **not** treat 0–15° as an exclusive phase boundary. Shoulder muscle contributions overlap continuously and are load/angle dependent.

## 25. Infraspinatus

Origin:
- infraspinous fossa.

Insertion:
- middle/posterior facet region greater tubercle.

Innervation:
- suprascapular nerve, mainly C5–C6.

Functions:
- lateral/external rotation;
- dynamic GH stabilization. citeturn919369search11

## 26. Teres minor

Origin:
- lateral border/posterior scapula.

Insertion:
- inferior facet greater tubercle.

Innervation:
- axillary nerve, mainly C5–C6.

Functions:
- lateral rotation;
- stabilization;
- may contribute to adduction depending position.

Do not confuse with teres major.

## 27. Subscapularis

Origin:
- subscapular fossa/anterior scapula.

Insertion:
- lesser tubercle and adjacent capsule/crest fibers.

Innervation:
- upper/lower subscapular nerves, C5–C7 contributions.

Functions:
- medial/internal rotation;
- anterior dynamic stabilization;
- contributes to adduction in some positions. citeturn919369search3

## 28. Cuff as force-couple system

Anatomically, cuff surrounds humeral head from:
- anterior;
- superior;
- posterior aspects.

Functional implication:
co-contraction can compress/center head against glenoid while larger muscles create rotation/elevation.

Exact force vectors/angle-specific roles → MEC/PHY, not fixed anatomical facts.

## 29. Deltoid

Three broad regions:

1. anterior/clavicular;
2. middle/acromial;
3. posterior/spinal.

Origin:
- lateral clavicle;
- acromion;
- scapular spine.

Insertion:
- deltoid tuberosity humerus.

Innervation:
- axillary nerve, predominantly C5–C6.

Broad actions:
- anterior fibers: flexion + medial rotation contribution;
- middle fibers: abduction/elevation contribution;
- posterior fibers: extension + lateral rotation contribution. citeturn311795search1turn311795search7

Actual moment arms change with arm position → MEC.

## 30. Trapezius

Origin broadly:
- occipital/nuchal structures;
- C7–T12 spinous processes.

Insertion:
- lateral clavicle;
- acromion;
- scapular spine.

Innervation:
- spinal accessory nerve (motor);
- cervical nerves C3–C4 sensory/proprioceptive contributions.

Functional regions:
- upper;
- middle;
- lower.

Collectively contributes to:
- elevation/depression;
- retraction;
- upward rotation;
- scapular stabilization. citeturn311795search8

Avoid simplistic “upper traps = shrug only”.

## 31. Serratus anterior

Origin:
- lateral surfaces of upper ribs, commonly ribs 1–8/9.

Insertion:
- anterior surface of medial scapular border, with strong inferior-angle attachment.

Innervation:
- long thoracic nerve, C5–C7.

Functions:
- protraction;
- holds scapula against thoracic wall;
- major upward-rotation contributor. citeturn311795search2

This is a key scapular-control muscle for overhead elevation.

## 32. Rhomboid major/minor

Origin:
- lower cervical/upper thoracic spinous processes depending muscle.

Insertion:
- medial scapular border.

Innervation:
- dorsal scapular nerve.

Functions:
- retraction;
- downward rotation contribution;
- stabilize scapula against thorax.

Exact contributions depend on arm/scapula position.

## 33. Levator scapulae

Origin:
- transverse processes C1–C4.

Insertion:
- superior medial border/superior angle scapula.

Innervation:
- dorsal scapular nerve + cervical contributions.

Functions:
- elevation;
- downward rotation contribution.

Also couples cervical/scapular regions anatomically.

## 34. Pectoralis minor

Origin:
- ribs 3–5 near costal cartilages.

Insertion:
- coracoid process.

Innervation:
- predominantly medial pectoral nerve.

Acts on scapula, not humerus directly.

Contributes to:
- anterior/inferior scapular motion;
- protraction/depression patterns depending fixed segment. citeturn311795search11turn311795search16

## 35. Subclavius

Origin:
- first rib/costal cartilage.

Insertion:
- inferior middle clavicle.

Innervation:
- nerve to subclavius C5–C6.

Function:
- anchors/depresses clavicle;
- contributes to SC stability. citeturn311795search1

## 36. Pectoralis major

Two broad heads:
1. clavicular;
2. sternocostal.

Origin:
- medial clavicle;
- sternum/costal cartilages and adjacent aponeurotic regions.

Insertion:
- lateral lip of intertubercular sulcus/crest of greater tubercle region.

Innervation:
- lateral and medial pectoral nerves.

Functions:
- humeral adduction;
- medial rotation;
- horizontal adduction;
- clavicular head contributes to flexion;
- sternocostal fibers can contribute differently depending humeral position. citeturn311795search9

Detailed angle-specific function → MEC.

## 37. Latissimus dorsi

Broad origin:
- lower thoracic spine;
- thoracolumbar fascia;
- iliac crest;
- lower ribs, with variation.

Insertion:
- floor/intertubercular groove region of humerus.

Innervation:
- thoracodorsal nerve.

Functions:
- humeral extension;
- adduction;
- medial rotation;
- links upper limb to trunk/pelvis connective structures. citeturn311795search8

## 38. Teres major

Origin:
- inferior angle/lower lateral scapula.

Insertion:
- medial lip/crest of lesser tubercle region near intertubercular groove.

Innervation:
- lower subscapular nerve.

Functions:
- adduction;
- medial rotation;
- extension contribution.

Teres major is **not** rotator cuff despite proximity to teres minor.

## 39. Coracobrachialis — boundary muscle

Origin:
- coracoid process.

Insertion:
- medial mid-humerus.

Innervation:
- musculocutaneous nerve.

Acts at GH:
- flexion;
- adduction contribution.

Detailed arm anatomy → ANA-05.

## 40. Scapular upward-rotation force couple

Main muscular contributors commonly include:
- serratus anterior;
- upper/lower trapezius regions.

Rhomboids/levator contribute opposing/downward-rotation components and stabilization.

Do not reduce this to “one muscle pulls, one muscle relaxes”; scapular control is coordinated and task-dependent.

## 41. Scapulohumeral rhythm

Concept:
ratio/coordination between:
- glenohumeral elevation;
- scapulothoracic upward rotation
during arm elevation.

Historical teaching often cites ~2:1 or similar fixed ratios.

Modern systematic evidence shows:
- substantial interindividual/angle-dependent variability;
- scapula begins moving from the start rather than after a fixed “setting phase”;
- fixed universal ratio is not supported. citeturn311795search0

Project rule:
**scapulohumeral rhythm = dynamic coordination pattern, not a constant gear ratio.**

## 42. Humerothoracic vs glenohumeral elevation

When someone says:
“arm elevated 150°”,
that can refer to humerus relative to thorax, not 150° pure GH motion.

Total arm elevation combines:
- GH;
- scapular rotation;
- clavicular motion;
- thoracic contribution depending task.

This distinction becomes mandatory in biomechanics and ROM measurement.

## 43. Scapular plane

Scapula rests obliquely on thoracic wall.

Therefore many natural humeral elevation trajectories occur in an oblique **scapular plane**, not strict frontal plane.

“Scaption” = elevation approximately in scapular plane.

Exact plane varies individually; no single angle is anatomical law.

## 44. Shoulder movement vocabulary

GH movements:
1. flexion/extension;
2. abduction/adduction;
3. medial/lateral rotation;
4. horizontal abduction/adduction;
5. circumduction.

Scapular movements:
1. elevation/depression;
2. protraction/retraction;
3. upward/downward rotation;
4. anterior/posterior tilt;
5. internal/external rotation.

Clavicular:
1. elevation/depression;
2. protraction/retraction;
3. axial rotation.

Avoid naming all of these “shoulder rotation”.

## 45. Shoulder ROM is distributed

Anatomy implies:
restriction/variation in one component can be compensated partially by others.

Example:
less GH elevation may be accompanied by more scapular/thoracic contribution.

This is description, not diagnosis.

Do not infer “dyskinesis” from visual variation alone.

## 46. Anatomical variation

Relevant variants include:
- glenoid inclination/version;
- acromial morphology;
- clavicle shape;
- scapular orientation;
- ligament morphology;
- muscle attachment variation;
- labral variants.

Population average cannot define one visually “correct” shoulder trajectory.

## 47. Scapular kinematics variability

2025 systematic review of asymptomatic and rotator-cuff-related shoulder pain populations found broad normal variability in:
- upward rotation;
- tilt;
- internal/external rotation.

This weakens simplistic diagnosis from a single observed scapular pattern. citeturn311795search0

Clinical dyskinesis/pathology remains MED topic.

## 48. Innervation map — minimum required

Key nerves:

1. suprascapular → supraspinatus/infraspinatus;
2. axillary → deltoid/teres minor;
3. upper/lower subscapular → subscapularis; lower also teres major;
4. long thoracic → serratus anterior;
5. dorsal scapular → rhomboids, levator contribution;
6. spinal accessory → trapezius motor;
7. thoracodorsal → latissimus dorsi;
8. medial/lateral pectoral → pectoral muscles;
9. nerve to subclavius → subclavius.

Detailed brachial plexus architecture → ANA-12.

## 49. Structural chain during arm elevation

Simplified sequence:

1. humerus elevates relative to glenoid;
2. cuff/deltoid and other muscles generate/control GH force;
3. scapula changes upward rotation/tilt/rotation;
4. AC joint permits scapula-clavicle relative motion;
5. clavicle elevates/retracts/rotates at SC;
6. thoracic cage provides moving support surface.

This is coordinated continuum, not stepwise phases.

## 50. Structural chain during pressing/pulling

Press/pull does not use isolated pectoral/back muscles.

Depending movement:
- humerus moves at GH;
- scapula protracts/retracts/rotates;
- clavicle responds at SC/AC;
- trunk/rib cage provides base;
- cuff maintains GH control.

Exact force distribution → MEC.

## 51. Arm-isolation relevance

Even a curl or triceps exercise may involve shoulder structures because:
- biceps long head crosses GH;
- triceps long head originates on scapula;
- shoulder position changes muscle length;
- scapula/trunk stabilize upper arm.

Therefore “arm exercise” is not anatomically elbow-only.

Detailed biceps/triceps → ANA-05.

## 52. Anatomy vs technique boundary

Anatomy can say:
- what structures exist;
- where they attach;
- what movements they can contribute to.

It cannot alone say:
- exact safest grip;
- optimal elbow angle;
- best press path;
- “shoulders must be pinned back”;
- one universal scapular position.

Those require biomechanics + training outcome evidence.

## 53. Anatomy vs pathology boundary

Do not infer:
- impingement;
- labral tear;
- instability;
- bursitis;
- rotator-cuff pathology
from normal structure or one movement observation.

Pathology → MED.

## 54. Project canonical shoulder model

1. Three true synovial joints: SC, AC, GH.
2. One principal functional articulation: ST.
3. Clavicle is axial–appendicular bony bridge.
4. Scapula is mobile socket/orientation platform.
5. GH trades bony congruence for mobility and relies on soft-tissue/dynamic stabilization.
6. Cuff forms capsule-blending dynamic stabilizing sleeve.
7. Deltoid produces large humeral moments.
8. Serratus/trapezius and other periscapular muscles orient/stabilize scapula.
9. Total arm motion is distributed across GH + scapula + clavicle + thorax.
10. Scapulohumeral rhythm is variable, not fixed 2:1.

## 55. Confidence

### HIGH
- basic SC/AC/GH/ST architecture;
- clavicle/scapula landmarks;
- labrum/capsule/ligament anatomy;
- cuff composition/attachments/innervation;
- major periscapular/axiohumeral muscle anatomy;
- scapula moves from early arm elevation rather than after a universal setting phase.

### MODERATE/HIGH
- exact scapulohumeral ratios: evidence strongly supports variability, so project deliberately avoids a universal number.

### MODERATE
- angle-specific muscle roles/moment arms vary with position and require biomechanics; anatomical action labels are broad tendencies rather than fixed force contributions.
