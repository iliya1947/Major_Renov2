# BIO-13 — Energy-system development

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-19**  
Область: 05 — Bioenergetics / Exercise Metabolism

## 0. Core decision

Energy-system development means **biasing an integrated energetic demand profile**, not isolating one ATP pathway.

Training demand is shaped by:

- intensity;
- work-bout duration;
- recovery duration/mode;
- repetitions/total exposure;
- exercise mode;
- frequency;
- starting state;
- target-task mechanics.

Project rule:

**“phosphagen-, glycolytic-, or oxidative-focused” describes relative energetic bias, never exclusive biochemical supply.**

---

## 1. Acute response vs chronic adaptation

Acute responses can include:

- PCr depletion/recovery;
- VO2;
- lactate;
- tissue oxygenation;
- power decrement;
- tVO2max.

Chronic outcomes can include:

- VO2max;
- sprint/repeated-sprint ability;
- power/capacity;
- task performance;
- metabolic adaptations.

Project rule:

**acute energetic response ≠ chronic adaptation dose.**

---

## 2. Intensity

Intensity changes:

- ATP-turnover rate;
- sustainable duration;
- PCr/glycolytic demand;
- oxidative demand;
- recruitment;
- fatigue rate.

Project rule:

**intensity changes relative pathway contribution; it does not assign one exclusive energy system.**

---

## 3. Absolute vs relative intensity

The same absolute speed/power can represent different physiological intensity across people/states.

Project rule:

**absolute intensity ≠ relative physiological intensity.**

Threshold/testing implementation remains TST/PRO.

---

## 4. Duration

Work-bout duration changes how long high ATP demand must be sustained.

Shorter bouts can preserve:

- peak power;
- sprint quality;
- brief high-rate output.

Longer bouts can increase:

- cumulative oxidative contribution;
- metabolic accumulation;
- time for VO2 to rise.

Project rule:

**duration must be interpreted together with intensity and recovery.**

---

## 5. Work:rest ratio

Work:rest affects:

- PCr restoration;
- residual metabolites/ions;
- VO2;
- power maintenance;
- density.

Project rule:

**work:rest changes the starting state of the next bout.**

---

## 6. Ratio ≠ complete prescription

A ratio such as 1:4 is incomplete without absolute durations.

Project rule:

**same ratio ≠ same physiology.**

Always distinguish:

- 5 s : 20 s;
- 10 s : 40 s;
- 30 s : 120 s.

---

## 7. Shorter recovery

Shorter recovery can:

- increase VO2/internal load;
- reduce restoration;
- reduce repeated-bout power.

Project rule:

**greater acute strain from short rest ≠ better chronic adaptation by definition.**

---

## 8. Exercise mode

Mode changes:

- active muscle mass;
- contraction pattern;
- force/velocity profile;
- mechanics;
- skill;
- achievable power;
- local demand.

Project rule:

**mode is part of the stimulus, not a neutral container.**

---

## 9. Cross-training transfer

Current running/cycling evidence does not establish:

- full interchangeability;
- zero transfer.

Project rule:

**absence of a detected between-mode difference ≠ proof of equivalence.**

Transfer must be judged against the target task.

---

## 10. Frequency

Frequency distributes exposure and recovery.

It interacts with:

- per-session dose;
- weekly volume;
- fatigue;
- adherence;
- target outcome.

Project rule:

**frequency is a dose-distribution variable, not an independent energy-system switch.**

---

## 11. More intervals ≠ more adaptation

In current untrained cycling SIT evidence, adding sprint repetitions did not show a meaningful linear VO2max benefit.

Project rule:

**more work inside one session ≠ automatically more adaptation.**

This finding is population/outcome specific.

---

## 12. Phosphagen-focused training

Phosphagen-focused work emphasizes:

- very high power;
- brief efforts;
- output quality;
- enough recovery to preserve intended performance.

Project rule:

**phosphagen-focused ≠ PCr-only.**

Glycolytic and oxidative contribution remain present.

---

## 13. Power maintenance boundary

Preserved sprint power can be useful for task quality.

Project rule:

**power maintenance ≠ direct PCr measurement.**

Do not infer muscle PCr concentration from performance alone.

---

## 14. Glycolytic-focused training

Glycolytic-focused work can be biased through:

- sustained high ATP turnover;
- longer intense bouts;
- incomplete recovery;
- repeated work.

Project rule:

**glycolytic-focused ≠ glycolysis-only and ≠ lactate chasing.**

Blood lactate is not direct glycolytic ATP flux.

---

## 15. Oxidative-focused training

Oxidative-focused work creates substantial sustained oxidative ATP demand.

This can include:

- continuous exercise;
- long HIIT;
- repeated shorter intervals;
- SIT across repeated exposure.

Project rule:

**oxidative-focused ≠ low-intensity-only.**

---

## 16. tVO2max boundary

Longer HIIT intervals often create more acute time at high VO2.

Project rule:

**tVO2max is an acute exposure metric, not a validated standalone chronic adaptation dose.**

More tVO2max does not automatically mean more VO2max gain.

---

## 17. SIT and aerobic adaptation

SIT can improve VO2max/cardiorespiratory fitness.

Project rule:

**brief all-out work is not aerobically irrelevant.**

This does not establish SIT as universally superior to continuous or HIIT training.

---

## 18. Mixed demands

Many tasks require combinations of:

- peak power;
- repeated-sprint ability;
- glycolytic demand;
- oxidative recovery;
- sustained aerobic support.

Project rule:

**mixed-demand tasks should not be reduced to one energy-system label.**

---

## 19. Mixed adaptation ≠ one protocol optimizes everything

HIIT can improve multiple aerobic and anaerobic-related outcomes.

Repeated-sprint training can improve intermittent high-intensity performance.

Project rule:

**multi-quality adaptation does not mean one protocol maximizes every quality simultaneously.**

---

## 20. Concurrent training boundary

Current SIT + resistance-training evidence does not show a clear pooled strength/power interference signal.

But:

- evidence base is limited;
- design, dose, sequencing and population matter.

Project rule:

**no clear pooled interference signal ≠ proof of no interference in every concurrent-training design.**

---

## 21. Specificity

Specificity includes:

- movement mode;
- contraction pattern;
- velocity;
- force/power;
- work duration;
- recovery;
- duty cycle;
- active muscle mass;
- energetic state;
- technical demands.

Project rule:

**energy-system specificity is only one layer of task specificity.**

---

## 22. Specificity ≠ exact mimicry

Training can overload one component or use a lower-mechanical-cost mode.

Project rule:

**specificity is graded transfer, not mandatory exact competition reproduction.**

---

## 23. General physiology vs target performance

Improved VO2max does not guarantee proportional improvement in:

- sprint ability;
- economy;
- repeated-sprint ability;
- race performance;
- sport skill.

Project rule:

**general physiological adaptation ≠ guaranteed target-task transfer.**

---

## 24. Meta-regression boundary

Study-level meta-regression can identify population-level associations.

It does not establish an individual's optimal:

- interval duration;
- work:rest;
- frequency;
- session volume.

Project rule:

**meta-regression optimum ≠ individual optimum.**

---

## 25. Volume construct

Training volume can mean:

- intervals;
- work time;
- distance;
- mechanical work;
- session duration;
- weekly exposure;
- time above an internal threshold.

Project rule:

**state the volume construct before interpreting dose-response.**

---

## 26. Canonical non-equivalences

1. energy-system focus ≠ energy-system isolation.
2. dominant system ≠ only active system.
3. intensity ≠ energy-system identity.
4. absolute intensity ≠ relative intensity.
5. high intensity ≠ anaerobic-only.
6. low/moderate intensity ≠ oxidative-only.
7. work duration ≠ complete training dose.
8. work:rest ratio ≠ complete interval prescription.
9. same ratio ≠ same physiology.
10. shorter rest ≠ universally better adaptation.
11. acute VO2 ≠ chronic VO2max adaptation.
12. blood lactate ≠ glycolytic ATP flux.
13. high lactate ≠ better glycolytic adaptation.
14. tVO2max ≠ chronic adaptation dose.
15. more sprint repetitions ≠ more VO2max adaptation.
16. more sessions ≠ guaranteed more adaptation.
17. mode ≠ neutral container.
18. no mode difference detected ≠ equivalence.
19. phosphagen-focused ≠ PCr-only.
20. power maintenance ≠ PCr measurement.
21. glycolytic-focused ≠ glycolysis-only.
22. oxidative-focused ≠ low-intensity-only.
23. SIT ≠ aerobically irrelevant.
24. mixed demand ≠ all qualities maximized at once.
25. no pooled interference signal ≠ no interference ever.
26. general adaptation ≠ target-task transfer.
27. specificity ≠ exact mimicry.
28. meta-regression optimum ≠ individual optimum.
29. acute response ≠ chronic adaptation.
30. energy-system percentage ≠ training prescription.

---

## 27. Trainer-level implications

1. Start from the target task, not an energy-system label.
2. Manipulate intensity, work duration and recovery together.
3. Report absolute work/rest durations as well as ratio.
4. Preserve output when high-power quality is the intended bias.
5. Do not chase lactate as a “glycolytic score.”
6. Use long intervals as one route to high oxidative exposure, not a universal optimum.
7. Treat mode as part of specificity.
8. Treat frequency as distribution of exposure/recovery.
9. Use mixed training when the target itself is mixed.
10. Judge transfer using target outcomes rather than acute markers alone.
11. Exact prescription remains PRO.

---

## 28. Confidence

### HIGH

- energy systems can be biased but not isolated;
- intensity/duration/recovery jointly shape demand;
- ratio alone is incomplete;
- shorter rest can increase acute internal load and reduce output;
- mode is part of the stimulus;
- frequency is dose distribution;
- phosphagen/glycolytic/oxidative “focus” is relative;
- tVO2max is not a validated chronic adaptation dose;
- SIT can improve aerobic fitness;
- specificity is multidimensional;
- acute response is not chronic adaptation;
- no universal interval/frequency optimum is retained.

### MODERATE-HIGH

- shorter better-recovered sprints preserve output better in selected protocols;
- longer/denser sprint work produces greater metabolic accumulation in selected protocols;
- longer HIIT intervals increase acute high-VO2 exposure;
- team-sport HIIT can improve multiple aerobic/anaerobic-related outcomes;
- field-based RST can improve repeated-sprint/intermittent performance;
- current SIT+RT evidence does not show a clear pooled strength/power interference signal.

### MODERATE / CONTEXT DEPENDENT

- exact intensity for one target;
- exact work duration;
- exact work:rest;
- exact frequency;
- exact cross-mode transfer;
- exact pathway fractions;
- exact mixed-demand distribution.
