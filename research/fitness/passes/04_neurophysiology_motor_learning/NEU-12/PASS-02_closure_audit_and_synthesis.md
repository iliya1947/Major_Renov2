## 3. Fitts–Posner stages audit

PASS WITH MODEL-LIMITATION BOUNDARY.

The classic cognitive → associative → autonomous progression remains useful for describing a broad trend:

- early learning often requires more deliberate problem solving;
- practice can reduce error and stabilize task relations;
- well-practiced execution can demand less high-level control.

However the model cannot be treated as:

- three discrete neural states;
- a universal one-way sequence;
- stages with fixed durations;
- a claim that one learning mechanism is active at a time;
- a claim that the autonomous stage is attention-free.

The contemporary 3R framework (PMID 39087986) is closure-critical because it explicitly emphasizes parallel processes:

- reasoning;
- refinement;
- retrieval.

This provides a better modern boundary than interpreting motor skill acquisition as a single serial process.

Upstream NEU-11 independently established:

- automaticity is graded;
- task/context changes can reintroduce deliberate control.

Canonical rule:

**Fitts–Posner is retained as a coarse descriptive heuristic for changing control demands, not a literal universal three-state biology.**

No blocker.

## 4. Explicit-learning audit

PASS.

Explicit motor learning is retained operationally as learning in which task-relevant:

- knowledge;
- rules;
- hypotheses;
- action–outcome reasoning;
- strategy

are accessible enough to deliberate awareness to be intentionally used.

Explicit contribution can support:

- rapid early compensation;
- direct use of safety/task rules;
- strategy switching;
- hypothesis testing;
- deliberate error correction.

The 2024 3R framework reinforces that reasoning and explicit cognitive processes can make meaningful contributions to sensorimotor learning.

Important non-equivalences remain:

- explicit learning ≠ verbal instruction alone;
- explicit learning ≠ internal focus;
- explicit learning ≠ large rule checklist;
- explicit contribution ≠ harmful by definition.

Canonical rule:

**explicit learning is a legitimate deliberate strategy/knowledge contribution, not an intrinsically inferior mode.**

No blocker.

## 5. Implicit-learning audit

PASS WITH CONSTRUCT-PURITY LIMIT.

The Delphi study (PMID 24968228) reached consensus on broad definitions of implicit and explicit motor learning but did not reach consensus that commonly used strategies reliably induce one form or the other.

That finding remains closure-critical.

Implicit learning is therefore retained as:

> motor-skill change with relatively reduced dependence on accessible/verbalizable task rules or deliberate strategy.

It is not retained as:

- complete unconsciousness;
- absence of attention;
- absence of any strategy;
- absence of error perception;
- a process guaranteed by a particular coaching label.

The measurement review (PMID 34214514) reinforces that different explicit/implicit measures have important assumptions and limitations.

Canonical rule:

**implicit learning is a construct about the learner's process, not a synonym for a named teaching method.**

No blocker.

## 6. Explicit + implicit coexistence / additivity audit

PASS — IMPORTANT MEASUREMENT CORRECTION.

The field often decomposes motor adaptation into explicit and implicit contributions.

However:

- PMID 39191485 found that common measures of explicit and implicit adaptation did not linearly add;
- PMID 42012055 re-examined the additivity assumption in 2026 and retains the interaction/decomposition problem as unresolved.

Therefore:

`total adaptation = measured explicit + measured implicit`

cannot be treated as a universal identity.

This does **not** mean explicit and implicit processes do not exist.

It means:

- measurement procedures matter;
- contributions can interact;
- process decomposition is model dependent;
- both components should be directly assessed when mechanistic separation is the research question.

Transfer boundary:

most of this evidence comes from visuomotor adaptation and cannot be numerically mapped onto a squat, power clean or other complex resistance skill.

Canonical rule:

**explicit and implicit contributions can coexist, but common proxy measures do not justify a simple process-pure additive decomposition.**

No blocker.

## 7. “Implicit methods” comparative-efficacy audit

PASS WITH HETEROGENEITY LIMIT.

The 2025 sport-biomechanics systematic review (PMID 40209584):

- included 25 studies / 60 comparisons;
- found a small positive aggregate effect for interventions classified as implicit versus control;
- described the conclusion as premature;
- included multiple very different intervention classes under the implicit umbrella.

This prevents a clean claim that:

`implicit coaching > explicit coaching`.

The 2018 systematic review of automaticity (PMID 30183763) likewise found that most implicit-vs-explicit comparisons did not show clear differences in automaticity, with unclear risk of bias.

### Analogy

The analogy systematic review/meta-analysis (PMID 39419241) supports potential benefit in some tasks/populations but reports:

- high heterogeneity;
- important comparison limitations;
- high risk of bias.

Thus analogy is a useful candidate tool, not a universal superior method.

### Errorless learning

The 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis (PMID 41953321; 29 studies / 31 experiments / 1,509 participants) found:

- no significant overall movement-performance advantage over errorful learning;
- subgroup benefits in some children / impaired populations;
- substantial heterogeneity;
- low to very low certainty.

Thus:

**error minimization is not a universal motor-learning law for healthy adult strength coaching.**

Canonical rule:

**method labels must be evaluated by delayed task-relevant outcomes; no universal implicit-over-explicit hierarchy is supported.**

No blocker.

## 8. Discovery / exploration audit

PASS WITH VARIABILITY BOUNDARY.

Exploration is retained as:

> search among candidate movement solutions to discover actions that better satisfy the task goal under current constraints.

Movement variability can reflect:

- exploration;
- motor noise;
- fatigue;
- unstable control;
- redundant task-successful solutions;
- changing constraints.

The 2025 systematic review (PMID 39547333) found that relations between initial variability and later learning/adaptation depend on:

- task;
- reward- versus error-based structure;
- amount versus temporal structure of variability;
- initial-performance bias.

Therefore:

`high variability`
does not imply
`high-quality exploration`.

The 2026 kettlebell-swing study (PMID 42617501) provides direct strength-adjacent evidence:

- N=12;
- one week of practice;
- solution variability was separated from error variability using UCM analysis;
- higher early solution variability with low error variability was associated with fewer errors under the water-filled-kettlebell transfer challenge.

Important causal boundary:

- participants were not randomized to “high exploration”;
- grouping was based on observed early variability;
- the study does not prove that forcing more variability causes better transfer.

Canonical rule:

**useful exploration is structured search among informative/task-successful solutions, not random error or maximal variability.**

No blocker.

## 9. Constraints-led / nonlinear pedagogy audit

PASS WITH COMPARATIVE-EFFICACY LIMIT.

The core framework remains coherent:

movement solutions emerge from interaction among:

- individual/organism constraints;
- task constraints;
- environmental constraints.

The 2025 systematic review of linear versus nonlinear pedagogy in team-invasion sports (PMID 40759833) found:

- nine included studies;
- most technical outcomes showed no significant difference;
- nonlinear approaches sometimes favored tactical outcomes;
- evidence base remained limited.

This supports:

- practical legitimacy;
- possible context-specific advantages.

It does **not** support:

- universal superiority;
- transfer of tactical team-sport findings directly to closed lifting technique;
- removal of all direct instruction.

The 2024 critical review of coaching approaches also argues that the coach's problem is not selecting one ideology as universally “better,” but matching principles to learner/task needs.

Canonical rule:

**constraints-led learning is a valid practice-design strategy, not a universal replacement for direct instruction.**

No blocker.

## 10. Direct power-clean comparative evidence audit

PASS WITH IMPORTANT OUTCOME LIMIT.

PMID 35499570 is the strongest direct resistance-exercise comparison in this topic.

Design:

- 16 beginners;
- randomized to linear pedagogy versus nonlinear pedagogy;
- seven lessons over four weeks;
- linear group received explicit prescribed movement-form instruction;
- nonlinear group received analogy instruction plus two task constraints.

Result:

- both groups improved performance accuracy;
- no significant difference in quantity of exploration;
- no significant difference in barbell-trajectory-type distribution.

Therefore the study supports:

- both approaches can improve training-period movement-form performance;
- prescribed-form coaching is not uniquely required for improvement;
- nonlinear coaching is not demonstrated as universally superior.

Closure correction:

the accessible study report does not establish a separate delayed-retention test.

Therefore it must **not** be cited as evidence that both methods produced equally durable retention.

Canonical rule:

**direct power-clean evidence supports comparative feasibility/effectiveness during training, not universal or retention superiority of either pedagogy.**

No blocker.

## 11. Task-constraint audit

PASS.

Task constraints include variables defining:

- goal;
- success criterion;
- rules;
- load;
- implement;
- ROM;
- timing;
- target;
- allowed movement options;
- support conditions.

Changing a task constraint changes the motor problem.

Important distinction:

**task constraint ≠ cue.**

A cue provides information to the learner.

A task constraint changes the problem/environment the learner must solve.

These can be combined, but they are analytically different.

Canonical rule:

**task constraints should be manipulated to improve task-relevant information/solution search, then learning must be tested when the target condition is restored.**

No blocker.

## 12. Organism / individual-constraint audit

PASS WITHOUT TYPOLOGY.

Individual constraints can include:

- anthropometry;
- morphology;
- strength;
- mobility;
- previous motor experience;
- fatigue;
- sensory capability;
- cognitive/attentional capacity;
- pain/discomfort state;
- arousal.

These can alter the functional difficulty and available movement solutions.

Important boundary:

individual constraints do not justify deterministic learner types.

Not supported:

- “long femurs require one exact squat technique”;
- “ADHD requires one exact learning method”;
- fixed visual/auditory/kinesthetic learning-style prescription.

Canonical rule:

**the learner's capability/state changes the functional learning problem without dictating one inevitable solution.**

No blocker.

## 13. Environmental-constraint audit

PASS WITH CATEGORY-FLEXIBILITY LIMIT.

Environmental constraints can include:

- surface;
- space;
- visual conditions;
- obstacles;
- noise;
- social context;
- equipment arrangement;
- contextual pressure.

Some variables can reasonably be classified differently across frameworks.

For example, an implement may be considered:

- part of the task;
- part of the action environment.

The closure-critical issue is not the label.

It is:

1. what changed;
2. how it changed information/action possibilities;
3. whether the skill transfers when the target context returns.

Canonical rule:

**constraint categories are useful analytic bins, not rigid ontological compartments.**

No blocker.

## 14. Constraint-interaction audit

PASS.

The same nominal task can have very different functional difficulty depending on:

- learner strength;
- prior skill;
- fatigue;
- anthropometry;
- feedback;
- environment;
- target precision;
- movement speed.

Therefore:

`same external exercise`
does not imply
`same learning challenge`.

This interaction is consistent with both:

- ecological/dynamical constraints reasoning;
- Challenge Point's nominal-versus-functional-difficulty distinction.

Canonical rule:

**functional difficulty emerges from learner × task × context interaction rather than the nominal exercise label alone.**

No blocker.

## 15. Challenge Point Framework audit

PASS AS FRAMEWORK, NOT QUANTITATIVE LAW.

The original Challenge Point Framework (PMID 15130871) proposes that learning-relevant challenge depends on:

- task difficulty;
- learner skill;
- available information/practice conditions.

The 2025 scoping review (PMID 40568842):

- identified 100 papers applying/discussing the framework;
- found generally favorable use;
- explicitly noted limitations including lack of practical-application research.

This supports conceptual utility.

It does not validate:

- one numerical optimum;
- one success-rate target;
- one difficulty prescription across tasks.

Canonical rule:

**Challenge Point is a framework for reasoning about functional difficulty, not an equation that yields one universal optimal challenge.**

No blocker.

## 16. Universal optimal-difficulty audit

PASS — UNIVERSAL SWEET SPOT REJECTED.

The 2026 golf-putting RCT (PMID 41400972):

- N=150;
- randomized participants to high, medium and low practice-success ranges;
- used 50 practice trials;
- tested retention after 24 h.

Success ranges:

- high: 60–70%;
- medium: 20–30%;
- low: <10%.

Result:

- no significant difference in pattern of improvement;
- no significant difference in magnitude of learning.

The authors also identify unresolved field-level problems:

- no standard metric of task difficulty;
- inconsistent dependent variables;
- no universally agreed definition of optimal difficulty.

Therefore:

- no fixed “85% rule”;
- no fixed “70% success rule”;
- no universal desirable-error rate

is retained.

This single RCT cannot prove that optimal difficulty never exists.

It does, however, strongly support rejecting a universal numeric sweet spot.

Canonical rule:

**functional difficulty should be adapted, but no universal success-rate optimum is established.**

No blocker.

## 17. Progression / regression audit

PASS WITH SPECIFICITY BOUNDARY.

Progression/regression is retained as task-demand manipulation.

Possible progression dimensions:

- load;
- speed;
- precision;
- complexity;
- range;
- support;
- environmental uncertainty;
- target specificity.

Regression can temporarily:

- reduce demand;
- improve safety;
- allow interpretable attempts;
- make relevant information perceptible;
- isolate a coordination problem.

But regression success is not target-task learning.

### Sequential easy→hard progression

PMID 30905405 directly challenges universality.

In a novel gait task:

- criterion-task-specific practice outperformed sequential progression;
- sequential and random progression did not differ;
- target-task practice amount appeared more important than difficulty progression under that protocol.

This does not show that progression is useless.

It shows that:

**progression can trade off against specificity.**

Canonical rule:

**progression/regression is task dependent and should serve eventual target-task performance, not obey a universal easy→hard sequence.**

No blocker.

## 18. Specificity-vs-scaffolding audit

PASS.

A useful training design must balance:

1. enough simplification/support to make practice informative;
2. enough target-condition exposure to learn the actual required task.

Excessive regression can create a different skill.

Examples:

- permanent light-load practice may not capture working-load coordination;
- permanent support may not prepare unsupported execution;
- partial-ROM practice may not transfer automatically to full target ROM;
- constrained drills may teach the drill without transferring to the final lift.

This is fully consistent with NEU-11:

**retention of a regression/drill does not prove transfer to the target task.**

Canonical rule:

**every regression/progression structure ultimately requires target-condition practice and transfer/retention verification.**

No blocker.

## 19. Acute analogy-vs-explicit lifting audit

PASS AS PERFORMANCE EVIDENCE ONLY.

PMID 41558250 studied 20 novice participants across three consecutive days and compared:

- analogy instruction;
- explicit instruction;
- control.

Measured outcomes included:

- EMG;
- force;
- RFD;
- declarative knowledge.

The study found instruction-dependent acute changes in motor-control/force variables.

However the design does not establish a delayed retention or transfer advantage.

Therefore its correct project role is:

- evidence that instructional framing can acutely alter lifting performance/control strategy;
- **not** proof of durable motor learning superiority.

Canonical rule:

**acute force/EMG/RFD effects after instruction are performance/control effects unless delayed learning is demonstrated.**

No blocker.

## 20. Adaptation-to-skill transfer audit

PASS WITH STRICT BOUNDARY.

The strongest mechanistic explicit/implicit decomposition evidence comes from:

- visuomotor rotation;
- force-field adaptation;
- reaching adaptation.

These paradigms provide useful process information.

But complex resistance-exercise skill differs in:

- multi-joint coordination;
- force production;
- load;
- task goal;
- perceptual information;
- prior movement repertoire;
- safety constraints.

Therefore the project allows transfer of:

- construct distinctions;
- measurement cautions;
- coexistence of deliberate and non-deliberate learning processes.

It does **not** allow direct transfer of:

- exact component percentages;
- exact learning-rate parameters;
- exact implicit-explicit balance;
- adaptation-specific aftereffect magnitudes.

Canonical rule:

**adaptation evidence constrains theory but does not numerically define how a complex lift is learned.**

No blocker.

## 27. Closure criterion test

Topic:

> NEU-12 — Explicit / implicit / constraints-based learning

Result: **PASS.**

Canonical synthesis:

Motor-skill acquisition is not well represented by one exclusive teaching ideology.

A defensible model is:

`learner state/capability`
+
`task constraints`
+
`environment/context`
→
`functional learning problem`.

Learning can involve parallel contributions from:

- deliberate reasoning / explicit strategy;
- sensorimotor refinement;
- retrieval;
- exploration;
- practice-driven implicit processes.

Coaching can alter the learning problem through:

- concise instruction;
- analogy;
- task/environment constraints;
- guided exploration;
- progression/regression.

But learning quality must still be judged through:

- delayed retention;
- transfer to the target condition;
- task-relevant performance.

No universal law was established for:

- implicit > explicit;
- constraints-led > linear;
- errorless > errorful;
- more variability > less variability;
- one optimal success percentage;
- easy→hard progression.
