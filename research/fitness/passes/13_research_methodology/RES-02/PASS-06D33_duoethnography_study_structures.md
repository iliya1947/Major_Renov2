# 3. Lineage

Duoethnography emerged from work by Joe Norris and Richard Sawyer.

Oxford's account describes the named methodology as first appearing in **2004**, when Norris and Sawyer produced a dialogic autoethnographic inquiry and adopted the term “duoethnography.”

By the 2012 Oxford monograph it had become an explicitly articulated collaborative research methodology.

The field subsequently expanded through:

- education;
- social justice;
- professional identity;
- health professions;
- qualitative methodology;
- applied social sciences.

### Project lineage rule

Store:

**dialogic autoethnographic origin  
→ Norris/Sawyer duoethnography  
→ evolving collaborative/dialogic methodology family.**

Do not treat current Duoethnography as merely a writing style derived from autoethnography.

---

# 4. Canonical scientific target

Duoethnography studies how two or more researcher-participants:

- have lived a shared/common phenomenon differently;
- give different/similar meanings to it;
- interrogate those meanings through dialogue;
- expose cultural/social/meta-narrative influences;
- alter or reconceptualize prior understandings through the research act itself.

### Canonical project representation

researcher A as site of inquiry  
↕ dialogue  
researcher B as site of inquiry  
+  
disparate/situated histories  
+  
deliberate juxtaposition  
+  
difference preserved and interrogated  
+  
polyvocal meaning-making  
+  
reconceptualization / praxis  
+  
relational ethics / trust.

### Critical rule

The scientific object is not simply:

> two people's opinions.

It is the **relationship between divergent situated histories, meanings and the transformations generated through dialogic juxtaposition**.

---

# 5. Researchers are simultaneously researchers and participants/sites of inquiry

Burleigh & Burm describe Duoethnography as inviting researchers to serve as **sites of inquiry**.

The researchers are simultaneously:

- authors;
- participants;
- data generators;
- interlocutors;
- analysts/meaning-makers;
- witnesses/challengers to one another.

This differs from a conventional qualitative interview in which:

researcher asks  
→ participant answers  
→ researcher later analyzes.

In Duoethnography:

researcher-participant A  
↔  
researcher-participant B

generate and interrogate meaning together.

### Project rule

At least two collaborating voices must be epistemically active.

A passive interviewer who merely elicits one participant’s story does not automatically create canonical Duoethnography.

---

# 6. “Duo” does not impose a strict N=2 rule

The foundational Oxford definition explicitly allows **two or more researchers**.

Therefore:

> Duoethnography = exactly two people

is too rigid.

However, larger collaborative groups introduce different relational dynamics and may be labeled:

- collaborative autoethnography;
- collective autoethnography;
- multi-voice inquiry;
- other collaborative forms.

### Project rule

Do not classify by headcount alone.

Classify by **methodological architecture**.

Exact author terminology must be retained.

---

# 7. Life as curriculum

One foundational tenet is **life as a curriculum**.

Life history is not treated as inert background.

Past experience becomes material that can be:

- revisited;
- narrated;
- juxtaposed;
- questioned;
- reinterpreted;
- connected to social/cultural structures.

The same past event may acquire a different meaning after being placed beside another person’s history.

### Critical rule

Duoethnography does not merely retrieve an assumed fixed autobiographical truth.

It examines how meaning itself can change through inquiry.

---

# 8. The nine-tenet architecture

Burleigh & Burm summarize Norris/Sawyer's expanded nine tenets:

1. life as a curriculum;
2. polyvocal and dialogic;
3. deliberate juxtaposition;
4. differences are articulated and discussed to interrogate/disrupt stories;
5. meanings held about the past are questioned to invite reconceptualization;
6. universal truths are not sought;
7. inquiry is a form of praxis;
8. ethical stance is negotiated space;
9. deep trust grows over time and enables disclosure and rigorous conversation.

### Project taxonomy decision

These nine tenets are the strongest available canonical architecture for distinguishing a genuine Duoethnography from:

> two researchers talking about themselves.

They should be stored as methodology-level coherence criteria, not as a mechanical checklist requiring identical procedural enactment in every paper.

---

# 9. Deliberate juxtaposition is constitutive

Juxtaposition is more than comparison.

The inquiry deliberately places:

- experience A beside experience B;
- memory A against memory B;
- interpretation A against interpretation B;
- positionality A beside positionality B.

The analytic value comes from what becomes visible **between** them.

Possible outcomes:

- contradiction;
- tension;
- hidden assumption;
- different socialization;
- changed meaning;
- newly visible power relation;
- previously unnoticed similarity.

### Critical rule

If two autobiographical accounts are merely placed side-by-side without mutually interrogating one another, the study is methodologically weaker as Duoethnography.

---

# 10. Difference is an analytic resource

Duoethnography does not treat disagreement as measurement error that must be eliminated.

Difference can reveal:

- positionality;
- culture;
- social class;
- race/ethnicity;
- gender;
- disability;
- profession;
- institutional location;
- historical context;
- embodied difference;
- divergent assumptions.

### Canonical logic

difference  
→ dialogue  
→ disruption of taken-for-granted meaning  
→ reconceptualization.

### Critical rule

**Consensus is not the goal.**

A high-quality result may preserve unresolved difference.

---

# 11. Similarity alone is insufficient

Two researchers may have:

- similar careers;
- similar diagnoses;
- similar sport backgrounds;
- the same technology;
- the same institution.

Duoethnographic value does not come from demonstrating:

> “we both experienced the same thing.”

Similarity becomes useful when it is interrogated for:

- hidden divergence;
- different meaning;
- different histories;
- different consequences;
- different positionality.

### Failure mode

A paper that merely collects common themes across two autobiographical accounts can collapse into small-N collaborative thematic reflection.

---

# 12. Polyvocality

The methodology seeks to keep voices distinguishable.

Foundational writing guidance emphasizes:

- amplified individual voices;
- multiple perspectives;
- avoidance of a single fused narrator.

Typical representation:

A: ...  
B: ...

or other visibly differentiated forms.

### Critical rule

The final manuscript should not automatically compress divergent voices into:

> “we found that…”

when that erases meaningful disagreement.

---

# 13. Polyvocality does not require one universal script format

The 2012 monograph strongly foregrounds conversational/play-script representation.

Later literature also uses:

- dialogic scripts;
- reconstructed conversations;
- themed exchanges;
- narrative sections;
- conventional analytic sections mixed with dialogue;
- creative forms.

Duoethnography is explicitly non-prescriptive and evolving.

### Project rule

Script/dialogue format is **characteristic**, not a universal minimum criterion.

The stronger criterion is:

> are distinct voices and their interaction actually preserved?

---

# 14. Dialogue is constitutive

Dialogue is not merely a way to collect two pre-existing stories.

It is an epistemic mechanism.

Through dialogue, each researcher can:

- ask;
- challenge;
- clarify;
- provoke;
- witness;
- interrupt;
- reframe;
- disagree;
- revise their own prior understanding.

### Critical rule

The meaning is partly **produced in the relationship**, not only retrieved from individual memory.

---

# 15. Data and meaning-making can occur simultaneously

Burleigh & Burm explicitly describe Duoethnography as a methodology in which the boundary between:

- data generation;
- analysis;
- interpretation;

can be porous.

Dialogue may simultaneously:

1. generate new autobiographical material;
2. analyze earlier material;
3. challenge an interpretation;
4. generate a new interpretation.

### Project implication

Do not require a false sequence:

> collect all data → stop → analyze later.

But transparency about the actual process is still required.

---

# 16. What can count as data

Burleigh & Burm identify dialogue as central and describe archived material such as:

- emails;
- text messages;
- audio/video recordings;
- audio notes;
- artwork.

Other Duoethnographies can incorporate:

- diaries;
- written memories;
- photographs;
- documents;
- cultural artifacts;
- fitness-tracker records;
- theoretical texts;
- prompts;
- field notes.

### Critical rule

External artifact ≠ Duoethnography by itself.

The artifact must enter the dialogic/juxtaposition process.

---

# 17. Dialogue can be both raw material and final representation

In some Duoethnographies:

recorded dialogue  
→ transcript  
→ iterative dialogue/analysis  
→ edited dialogic text.

The published dialogue should not automatically be assumed to be:

> a literal verbatim raw transcript.

Current literature explicitly documents analytic re-storying, condensation and reordering while preserving polyvocality.

### Project extraction

Record separately:

1. raw dialogue captured?;
2. transcription?;
3. editing/re-storying?;
4. coding?;
5. thematic organization?;
6. final script reconstructed?;
7. changes disclosed transparently?

---

# 18. No single mandatory analysis method

Duoethnography does not require one universal coding algorithm.

Observed strategies include:

- iterative dialogic meaning-making;
- rereading/revisiting dialogue;
- thematic identification;
- open coding;
- reflexive thematic analysis;
- re-storying;
- theory-informed interpretation;
- arts-based/creative representation.

### Critical rule

“No fixed analysis protocol” does **not** mean:

> no analysis is necessary.

The study should make visible how raw interaction became the presented scholarly account.

---

# 19. Analysis must not erase difference

A conventional thematic analysis can be added to a Duoethnography.

But if the analytic process reduces:

A's story  
+  
B's story

into a single consensus theme and discards the tension between them, it can undermine the methodology’s core logic.

### Project rule

When coding is used, inspect whether it preserves:

- divergent voice;
- contradiction;
- relational meaning;
- shifts in interpretation;
- dialogic sequence where relevant.

---

# 20. Reconceptualization is central

Foundational and later methodology sources repeatedly emphasize **change of perspective**.

The inquiry asks not only:

> What did we believe?

but:

> What changed when our histories encountered each other?

Possible reconceptualization:

- changed self-understanding;
- revised professional identity;
- changed moral interpretation;
- altered understanding of a social phenomenon;
- recognition of privilege/power;
- changed relation to technology/body/health.

### Critical rule

Transformation can be subtle and incomplete.

Duoethnography does not require dramatic conversion.

---

# 21. Praxis

The methodology conceptualizes inquiry as potential praxis:

reflection  
↔  
dialogue  
→  
changed consciousness/meaning  
→  
possible changed action.

### Boundary

Praxis is not equivalent to an intervention effect.

A duoethnographic dialogue may change how researchers think or act.

It does not establish:

> the dialogue is an effective intervention for the population.

---

# 22. Universal truths are explicitly not the goal

One canonical tenet states that universal truths are not sought.

Duoethnography instead privileges:

- situated accounts;
- plural meanings;
- relational knowledge;
- contextual transformation;
- multiple possible interpretations.

### Project claim rule

Do not translate:

> “our dialogue exposed this cultural pattern”

into:

> “everyone with this identity experiences this pattern.”

---

# 23. The reader has an active interpretive role

Duoethnographic writing can invite readers to place their own narratives:

- alongside;
- against;
- between;

the researchers' stories.

The representation can deliberately avoid closing meaning into one final authoritative statement.

### Project implication

Reader resonance is a possible contribution.

It is not a measure of prevalence or validity by itself.

---

# 24. The process is non-prescriptive

Burleigh & Burm explicitly reject one preferred step-by-step procedure.

Possible differences include:

- structured vs emergent prompts;
- synchronous vs asynchronous dialogue;
- face-to-face vs online interaction;
- audio/video/text modes;
- regular schedule vs intermittent inquiry;
- formal coding vs dialogic sense-making;
- script vs hybrid representation.

### Critical rule

Flexibility does not eliminate the methodological core.

A paper cannot justify absence of:

- dialogue;
- difference;
- juxtaposition;
- reflexive challenge;

simply by saying Duoethnography is non-prescriptive.

---

# 25. Trust is methodology-level

Deep trust is one of the nine tenets.

Why?

High-value dialogue may require disclosure of:

- shame;
- uncertainty;
- contradiction;
- privilege;
- professional mistakes;
- illness;
- trauma;
- identity conflict;
- moral conflict.

Without enough relational safety, researchers may produce polished but shallow dialogue.

### Critical rule

Trust supports candor.

Trust does not guarantee epistemic correctness.

---

# 26. Safe dialogical space

Burleigh & Burm emphasize:

- naming discomfort/emotions;
- attending to nonverbal cues;
- allowing timeouts;
- allowing pauses/respite;
- negotiating expectations;
- recognizing different communication needs.

### Project implication

Quality appraisal should ask not only:

> Was dialogue recorded?

but also:

> What relational conditions made rigorous disagreement/disclosure possible?

---

# 27. Power asymmetry

Power can suppress the methodological mechanism itself.

Examples:

- supervisor ↔ doctoral student;
- manager ↔ employee;
- senior academic ↔ junior researcher;
- clinician ↔ trainee;
- coach selector ↔ athlete;
- assessor ↔ student.

Burleigh & Burm explicitly raise the concern that a partner with career/life authority can reduce how “real” the dialogue becomes.

### Project rule

Extract:

1. formal power relation;
2. dependency;
3. authorship authority;
4. decision rights;
5. publication veto;
6. withdrawal process;
7. how dissent was protected.

---

# 28. Ethics cannot be reduced to one approval form

Hunting & Hammond's 2024 analysis argues that Duoethnography’s deeply personal and ongoing relational character requires ethical attention beyond a one-time formal approval process.

They propose attention to:

1. relationship dynamics;
2. commitment to the project;
3. vulnerabilities;
4. consent and confidentiality;
5. working with differences.

### Critical rule

Formal IRB/ethics approval and relational ethics answer different questions.

One does not replace the other.

---

# 29. Ongoing consent

Duoethnographic disclosure evolves.

A collaborator may initially consent to discuss a topic but later decide:

- a detail is too revealing;
- a family member becomes identifiable;
- publication changes the risk;
- professional consequences become clearer.

### Project rule

For sensitive Duoethnography, consent should be treated as potentially:

- iterative;
- renegotiated;
- publication-specific.

Do not assume:

> initial agreement to participate = permanent agreement to publish every disclosure.

---

# 30. Confidentiality inside the duo

Researchers need enough confidentiality to take interpersonal risks.

Possible questions:

- What remains off-record?
- Can one researcher quote the other without renewed agreement?
- Who controls recordings?
- Who can see draft text?
- What happens to withdrawn material?
- What happens if the relationship deteriorates?

These are methodology-level concerns because the quality of dialogue depends on the relationship.

---

# 31. Implicated third parties

Autobiographical stories may reveal:

- partners;
- family;
- students;
- athletes;
- patients;
- supervisors;
- colleagues;
- institutions.

These people are not necessarily formal participants, but can become identifiable.

### Project extraction

Record:

- third-party identifiability;
- pseudonymization;
- composites;
- redaction;
- consent where appropriate;
- whether detail was altered;
- whether alteration changes meaning.

# 32. Duoethnography vs solo autoethnography

## Solo autoethnography

Researcher’s own experience  
→ cultural/social interpretation.

## Duoethnography

Researcher A experience  
↔  
Researcher B experience  
→ dialogic juxtaposition  
→ disruption/reconceptualization  
→ polyvocal representation.

### Critical distinction

Duoethnography makes **difference in relation** an analytic engine.

Adding a second author who edits a solo autoethnography does not create Duoethnography.

# 33. Duoethnography vs collaborative autoethnography (CAE)

This boundary is genuinely fuzzy.

The literature contains:

- explicit differentiation;
- partial synonymy;
- Duoethnography described as a variant of collaborative autoethnography;
- collaborative sport work that calls itself CAE while noting that similar work is sometimes termed Duoethnography.

Therefore a categorical statement:

> CAE and Duoethnography are completely unrelated methods

would be false.

### Strongest operational distinction

Duoethnography has a specific methodological core emphasizing:

- researchers as sites of inquiry;
- deliberate juxtaposition of disparate histories;
- polyvocal dialogue;
- difference as analytic resource;
- disruption of prior narratives;
- reconceptualization;
- non-pursuit of universal truth;
- negotiated relational ethics.

CAE is a broader autoethnographic family in which multiple researchers jointly analyze their own experiences and connect them to culture.

### Project routing rule

If a study explicitly claims Duoethnography **and** enacts the duoethnographic core above:

→ route parent methodology to **Duoethnography**.

If it primarily performs joint self→culture analysis without the distinct juxtaposition/dialogic-change architecture:

→ route to **collaborative autoethnography (D17)**.

If authors explicitly use both:

→ preserve both labels and explain the overlap.

Confidence: **MODERATE-HIGH**, because terminology remains heterogeneous.

---

# 34. Headcount cannot solve the CAE boundary

“Two authors = Duoethnography; three authors = CAE” is not reliable.

Foundational Duoethnography definitions allow:

> two or more researchers.

CAE also allows two or more.

### Better classifier

Ask:

1. Are researchers themselves sites of inquiry?
2. Are different histories deliberately juxtaposed?
3. Is dialogue constitutive?
4. Is difference preserved/interrogated?
5. Is reconceptualization/change explicit?
6. Is polyvocal representation retained?

---

# 35. Duoethnography vs narrative inquiry

Duoethnography often uses stories and autobiographical narrative.

But narrative inquiry is broader.

## Narrative inquiry can study

- participant stories;
- plot;
- temporality;
- identity;
- narrative construction.

The researcher need not be the empirical participant.

## Duoethnography requires

- researcher-participants;
- relational dialogue;
- juxtaposed life histories;
- difference/challenge;
- reconceptualization.

### Rule

Narrative form ≠ Duoethnography.

---

# 36. Duoethnography vs focus group

Both can involve dialogue among multiple people.

But a focus group typically has:

- researcher/moderator;
- participants;
- group discussion as data;
- no requirement that participants become co-researchers;
- no requirement that researchers’ own life histories are the site of inquiry.

Duoethnography typically has:

- co-researcher-participants;
- mutual questioning;
- shared analytic responsibility;
- self-disclosure;
- dialogic transformation.

### Critical rule

Two people interviewed together ≠ Duoethnography.

---

# 37. Duoethnography vs paired interview

A paired interview can collect data from two people simultaneously.

The interviewer may compare accounts.

But unless the pair:

- act as co-researchers;
- interrogate each other’s histories;
- generate meaning through mutual dialogue;
- enact reflexive transformation;

it should not be classified as Duoethnography.

---

# 38. Duoethnography vs generic dialogic inquiry

Dialogue is a broad research principle used in:

- dialogic qualitative research;
- deliberation;
- participatory inquiry;
- collaborative inquiry;
- coaching/supervision;
- focus groups.

Duoethnography adds a more specific architecture:

**autobiographical/life-history sites  
+ deliberate juxtaposition  
+ polyvocal researcher-participant dialogue  
+ difference  
+ reconceptualization.**

---

# 39. Duoethnography vs Self-Study of Practice / S-STEP

D32 concerns practitioner self-in-practice.

Duoethnography concerns juxtaposed life histories/perspectives around a shared phenomenon.

They can overlap if two practitioners critically juxtapose their practice histories.

### Routing rule

Ask what gives the study its primary logic.

If:

> practitioner improvement/self-in-practice

dominates → S-STEP.

If:

> dialogic juxtaposition of divergent histories and reconceptualization

dominates → Duoethnography.

Hybrid coding is allowed when both are explicitly enacted.

---

# 40. Data quality: dialogue depth matters more than conversation volume

Many hours of conversation do not guarantee rigorous Duoethnography.

A long dialogue can remain:

- agreeable;
- repetitive;
- superficial;
- confirmatory.

Stronger inquiry shows:

- challenges;
- clarifications;
- contradiction;
- reconsideration;
- evolution of meanings;
- evidence of why the interaction produced something unavailable to either researcher alone.

---

# 41. Reflexivity must be relational

Solo reflexivity asks:

> How do my assumptions shape this inquiry?

Duoethnographic relational reflexivity also asks:

- How does your story change how I understand mine?
- What power do I hold relative to you?
- Why does your interpretation unsettle me?
- What are we avoiding?
- Where are we too similar?
- Where am I projecting onto you?

### Critical rule

Two parallel reflexive essays are not enough.

---

# 42. Disagreement is not a quality failure

A Duoethnography can end without:

- agreement;
- synthesis;
- one final explanation.

In fact, forced consensus can erase exactly what the methodology is designed to reveal.

### Project extraction

Record:

- disagreements retained?;
- how handled?;
- any unresolved tensions?;
- whether final text falsely merges voices?

---

# 43. Ending is not conventional saturation

Burleigh & Burm describe their own process as tapering when:

- new insights become less frequent;
- new questions shift outside scope;
- the inquiry reaches a natural culmination;
- collaborators begin orienting toward future inquiry/public dialogue.

This is not equivalent to:

- theoretical saturation in grounded theory;
- statistical stopping;
- fixed sample sufficiency.

### Project rule

Do not import another methodology's stopping criterion mechanically.

---

# 44. Representation is part of the method

Because the reader needs access to multiple perspectives and their encounter, writing form is epistemically important.

Possible representational tools:

- speaker labels;
- dialogue;
- alternating voices;
- juxtaposed narratives;
- creative/performative text;
- analytic commentary around dialogue;
- re-storied excerpts.

### Critical rule

Representation should make the interaction inspectable enough for readers to see:

- difference;
- change;
- challenge;
- relational meaning.

---

# 45. Re-storying must be transparent

Current Duoethnographic work may:

- condense;
- reorder;
- rephrase;
- curate excerpts.

This can strengthen readability and analytic focus.

But the reader should not be misled into believing a polished dialogue is necessarily a verbatim chronological transcript.

### Project rule

Extract:

- verbatim?;
- edited?;
- reconstructed?;
- composite?;
- temporal reordering?;
- author transparency?

---

# 46. Quality / trustworthiness router

No single universal quality checklist was established in this pass.

For project appraisal, the following router is supported by the foundational tenets plus 2016/2022/2024 methodological clarification.

Check:

1. explicit Duoethnography rationale;
2. researchers/co-researchers are sites of inquiry;
3. shared phenomenon clearly defined;
4. relevant differences/positionalities visible;
5. deliberate juxtaposition occurs;
6. dialogue is constitutive rather than decorative;
7. individual voices remain distinguishable;
8. disagreement/difference is preserved;
9. evidence of interrogation/disruption;
10. evidence of changed/reconsidered meaning where claimed;
11. data sources/process transparent;
12. dialogue recording/transcription/editing transparent;
13. meaning-making/analysis process transparent;
14. final representation coherent with polyvocality;
15. power dynamics addressed;
16. trust/safe-space conditions considered;
17. vulnerability managed;
18. consent/confidentiality ongoing where needed;
19. implicated others considered;
20. claims remain contextual rather than universal/causal.

### Status

This is a **project synthesis/router**, not a claimed universally published checklist.

# 47. What Duoethnography can establish

Depending on implementation, it can support contextual claims about:

- how differently situated people understand a common phenomenon;
- how histories shape current perspectives;
- identity formation/revision;
- cultural/social narratives;
- professional assumptions;
- power/privilege;
- embodied meaning;
- technology-mediated experience;
- how dialogue changes understanding;
- tensions that disappear in single-voice accounts;
- conceptual/design insights generated by juxtaposition.

---

# 48. What Duoethnography cannot establish by itself

It does not establish:

- prevalence;
- incidence;
- average treatment effect;
- comparative treatment efficacy;
- diagnostic accuracy;
- prediction performance;
- biological mechanism;
- causal mediation;
- population frequency of a perspective;
- representativeness of all people sharing an identity.

### Critical rule

Two deeply researched lives can produce important conceptual knowledge.

They do not become a population sample by depth alone.

# 49. Direct 2025 physical-activity exemplar

Gómez Ortega & Vincenzi (DIS 2025) conducted a **six-week Duoethnography** focused on:

- physical activity;
- chronic health;
- Garmin wearable devices;
- datafication/self-tracking.

The two researchers identified themselves as women living with chronic health conditions and amateur athletes.

They regularly engaged in:

- running;
- cycling;
- swimming;
- strength training.

### Data architecture

They used:

- weekly individual diaries;
- weekly prompts;
- weekly dialogue meetings;
- video recording;
- automated transcripts;
- Garmin activity data/screenshots;
- digital and paper-based diary media.

Their process was incremental and cumulative.

---

# 50. Physical-activity exemplar: juxtaposition in practice

The six-week sequence deliberately moved between perspectives.

Early weeks emphasized:

- what each person’s own activity data captured;
- what was missing.

Middle weeks emphasized:

- looking at one another’s activity records;
- comparing what could be inferred;
- discussing what the data failed to communicate.

Later weeks examined:

- motivation;
- symptoms/context;
- what personally meaningful metrics might look like.

### Methodological lesson

The dataset was not simply:

> Garmin numbers.

The scientific value arose from:

**embodied experience  
↔ data representation  
↔ other researcher's interpretation  
↔ dialogue  
↔ critical/feminist reconfiguration.**

---

# 51. Physical-activity exemplar: analysis

The authors:

- moved diary materials to a shared Miro board;
- inductively coded the dataset;
- identified broader patterns;
- revisited entries through reflexive dialogue;
- developed preliminary themes;
- planned further refinement toward reflexive thematic analysis.

### Important lesson

Duoethnography can coexist with formal coding/thematic analysis.

The dialogic methodology remains the parent architecture.

---

# 52. Physical-activity exemplar: ethics

The DIS 2025 project reported institutional ethical approval.

It also used:

- regular check-ins;
- supportive discussion;
- breaks;
- autonomy over disclosure;
- pseudonymization;
- secure storage.

### Methodological lesson

Researcher = participant does **not** imply:

> ethics are irrelevant.

This direct movement-domain example supports the broader relational-ethics findings from the 2024 methodology literature.

---

# 53. Physical-activity exemplar: supported claims

The study can illuminate:

- what fitness trackers capture/miss;
- how chronic illness changes the meaning of “missing” activity;
- tensions between quantified and embodied experience;
- how two active people interpret physical-activity data differently;
- design considerations for more context-sensitive data systems.

It cannot establish:

- prevalence of these experiences among all athletes with chronic illness;
- Garmin's clinical accuracy;
- exercise efficacy;
- causal impact of tracker use on health;
- comparative effectiveness of tracking systems.

# 54. Disability-sport boundary exemplar

Maher et al. studied exclusion/inclusion/belonging in mainstream and cerebral-palsy youth football.

The paper's **parent methodology is presented as narrative**, with creative non-fiction representation.

However, its data-generation account explicitly invokes Duoethnography because:

- Jack and Joanne co-constructed interview guides;
- Joanne served as “the Other” in Jack's attempts to make meaning;
- the discovery process was framed as collaborative, mutual and reciprocal.

### Project lesson

This is a useful **boundary/hybrid example**, not the cleanest canonical Duoethnography.

Do not code a paper as full parent Duoethnography merely because one stage is described as “in keeping with duoethnography.”

Preserve:

- parent narrative methodology;
- duoethnographic/dialogic element.

# 55. Distance-running boundary exemplar

Allen-Collinson's distance-running injury/rehabilitation project with John Hockey was described in different publications as:

- collaborative autoethnography;
- sometimes termed Duoethnography;
- later referred to as a Duoethnography of distance running.

### Project lesson

Sport literature directly demonstrates the CAE ↔ Duoethnography nomenclature overlap.

Therefore taxonomy must classify architecture, not only terminology.

This is one reason the D33 routing rule is kept **MODERATE-HIGH** rather than absolute.

# 56. Fitness/sport questions suited to Duoethnography

Examples:

- How do two athletes with different injury histories reconstruct what “training through pain” means?
- How do two strength coaches from different coaching cultures understand autonomy and discipline?
- How do two people with different chronic conditions experience the same fitness-tracker metrics?
- How do disabled and nondisabled sport researchers differently understand “inclusion”?
- How do two athletes from different gender/class/cultural positions understand gym belonging?
- How does dialogue between two coaches alter what each believes constitutes a “good” athlete?
- How do different athletic biographies change interpretation of fatigue, missed sessions or recovery?

### Critical condition

The methodology is justified when **difference + dialogue + reconceptualization** are scientifically central.

---

# 57. Bad-fit questions

Duoethnography is usually a poor parent design for:

- What percentage of lifters use creatine?
- Does 3 vs 5 sets cause more hypertrophy?
- What predicts ACL reinjury?
- Is wearable X valid against indirect calorimetry?
- What is the prevalence of exercise addiction?
- What biomechanical mechanism causes this injury?

These require other designs.

---

# 58. Common failure modes

1. Two autobiographies with no mutual interrogation.
2. Agreement treated as the primary result.
3. Difference edited out.
4. Dialogue appears only as illustrative quotes after conventional analysis.
5. One collaborator dominates epistemic/authorial authority.
6. Power relation is ignored.
7. Trust is assumed because collaborators are friends.
8. No explanation of how dialogue was captured/edited.
9. Polished script presented as though it were raw transcript.
10. Third parties exposed through autobiographical disclosure.
11. Personal transformation inflated into social universal.
12. Small-N lived experience inflated into prevalence/causal claim.
13. CAE and Duoethnography separated only by author count.
14. “Non-prescriptive” used to justify methodological opacity.

# 60. Do-not-confuse matrix

| Do not confuse | Why |
|---|---|
| two authors vs Duoethnography | headcount does not establish methodology |
| two autobiographies vs Duoethnography | dialogic juxtaposition must do analytic work |
| similarity vs finding | difference and changed meaning are central |
| disagreement vs failure | difference is an analytic resource |
| dialogue vs ordinary conversation | inquiry requires systematic scholarly purpose/transparency |
| dialogue as quotes vs dialogic methodology | dialogue must be constitutive |
| polyvocality vs merged “we” | separate voices should remain inspectable where difference matters |
| script format vs methodology | script is characteristic but not the full design |
| non-prescriptive vs no methodological core | flexibility does not erase tenets |
| researcher-participant vs solo autoethnography | relational juxtaposition changes the architecture |
| Duoethnography vs CAE | substantial overlap exists; classify by architecture, not headcount |
| Duoethnography vs focus group | co-researcher life histories and mutual inquiry differ |
| Duoethnography vs paired interview | interviewer/participant roles alone are insufficient |
| Duoethnography vs narrative inquiry | story form is broader than researcher-site dialogic juxtaposition |
| Duoethnography vs S-STEP | life-history/dialogic reconceptualization vs self-in-practice improvement |
| dialogue vs consensus | consensus is not required |
| transformation vs causal effect | researcher meaning-change ≠ treatment efficacy |
| reader resonance vs representativeness | resonance is not prevalence |
| edited dialogue vs verbatim transcript | re-storying/editing must be transparent |
| ethics approval vs complete ethics | ongoing relational ethics still matters |
| trust vs absence of power | trusted relationships can still contain power asymmetry |

# 61. Confidence

## HIGH

Well-supported:

- Duoethnography is a distinct collaborative/dialogic qualitative methodology.
- Norris/Sawyer are the foundational developers and the named methodology emerged from early-2000s dialogic autoethnographic work.
- Researchers function simultaneously as researchers/participants/sites of inquiry.
- Polyvocal dialogue is constitutive.
- Deliberate juxtaposition is constitutive.
- Difference is treated as an analytic resource.
- The methodology aims to interrogate/disrupt prior meanings and enable reconceptualization.
- Universal truths are explicitly not the goal.
- Praxis/transformation is part of the methodological logic.
- Dialogue can be both data generation and meaning-making.
- Multiple data forms can be incorporated.
- No one fixed analysis method is mandatory.
- Trust, safe dialogical space, relational ethics and power dynamics are methodology-level concerns.
- The 2025 Garmin/chronic-health study provides a direct contemporary physical-activity / strength-training relevant exemplar.

## MODERATE-HIGH

Project routing choices:

- using the nine tenets as the main taxonomy coherence architecture without treating them as a rigid checklist;
- treating script format as characteristic but not universally mandatory;
- classifying Duoethnography separately from D17 collaborative autoethnography when the deliberate-juxtaposition/polyvocal-reconceptualization core is explicitly enacted;
- treating two-or-more-person Duoethnography as valid despite the “duo” label;
- using the 20-point project trustworthiness router above.

- exact boundary between Duoethnography and collaborative autoethnography across the entire literature.

authors and fields sometimes use the terms as:

The project therefore preserves author labels and uses architecture rather than terminology alone.

# 64. Status after PASS-06D33

## Established

1. Duoethnography is a distinct dialogic/collaborative qualitative methodology.
2. Researchers are simultaneously researcher-participants/sites of inquiry.
3. The foundational core is not number of authors but life-history juxtaposition, polyvocal dialogue, difference, disruption and reconceptualization.
4. “Duo” can include two or more researchers in foundational definitions.
