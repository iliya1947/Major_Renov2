## Phase B / EXE completed

### EXE-01 — Executive functions and cognitive control: architecture, scope, and core distinctions

Canonical block:

`research/psychology/qualification/04_executive_functions_and_metacognition/EXE-01_executive_functions_cognitive_control_architecture.md`

Established:

1. cognitive control is a family of processes, not one executive capacity;
2. executive function is an umbrella term and current ontology remains unsettled;
3. inhibition/updating/shifting are useful but incomplete;
4. EF shows both unity and diversity without implying one latent factor is literal mental anatomy;
5. task impurity is fundamental;
6. goal selection, representation, maintenance, updating, and hierarchy are distinct targets;
7. response selection != motor stopping != interference suppression;
8. common stop-signal inhibition measures require strong caution for stable trait inference;
9. response inhibition != impulsivity;
10. switch cost != generic flexibility;
11. cognitive stability and flexibility need not be strict opposites;
12. proactive and reactive control are context-dependent modes, not a better/worse hierarchy;
13. error detection, performance monitoring, and adaptive adjustment are separable;
14. control implementation != control allocation;
15. cognitive effort != amount of cognitive control;
16. attention, working memory, motivation, metacognition, emotion regulation, and intelligence remain neighboring but distinct constructs;
17. control can be insufficient, excessive, or maladaptively configured;
18. laboratory tasks, self-report, and real-world executive behavior are not interchangeable;
19. stable EF trait inference requires reliable repeated cross-task/context evidence;
20. a 48-question Executive Control Inference Gate is canonical.
