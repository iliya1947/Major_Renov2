## 1. Time is part of outcome definition

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

Cochrane recommends predefining timing of outcome measurement.

A complete outcome is not only:
- domain;
- measurement tool;

but also:
- **when it is measured**.

Project consequence:
“strength” is underspecified.

Examples:
- immediate post-intervention strength;
- 8-week strength;
- 6-month retained strength

are not automatically one outcome question.

## 2. Exact time point vs time window

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

Reviews commonly use prespecified time windows rather than one exact day because primary studies measure outcomes at heterogeneous times.

Examples:
- short term;
- medium term;
- long term.

Cochrane explicitly recommends defining those windows in advance when used.

### Project rule

For each critical longitudinal outcome, define either:

1. exact target time point; or
2. prespecified time window.

If a window is used, its width must be justified by:
- biology/adaptation time course;
- intervention duration;
- decision relevance;
- expected study timing.

Do not merge 2-week and 24-week effects simply because both are “post-training”.

## 3. Time windows change the scientific question

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

If timing differs enough that:
- adaptation mechanism;
- retention;
- adverse-event exposure;
- adherence decay
can differ, the effects may answer different questions.

Project consequence:
time is not always just a subgroup variable; it can define separate synthesis questions.

## 4. Is T mandatory in PICOT?

### Conclusion of this pass
**Project decision: YES as an explicit Question Card field for longitudinal/effect questions.**

Reason:
Cochrane PICO does not require a separate letter T, but its methods require predefining outcome timing where relevant.

Therefore project standard will use:

**PICOT where T is materially relevant**, with T meaning:
- intervention duration and/or
- outcome assessment horizon,
clearly distinguished.

For truly atemporal structural/anatomical questions, T can be marked:
- `N/A`.

This is a project operational rule, not a claim that Cochrane universally requires the acronym PICOT.

## 5. Intervention duration and outcome horizon are different

Example:

- intervention duration: 8 weeks;
- outcome assessment: week 8;
- retention follow-up: week 20.

These must not be collapsed into one ambiguous “T”.

### Project Question Card change to be made at synthesis stage

Separate fields:

1. **Intervention/exposure duration**
2. **Outcome time horizon**

This is more precise than one single T field.

## 6. Outcome list should be limited and decision-relevant

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

Cochrane recommends:
- meaningful outcomes;
- excluding trivial outcomes;
- limiting critical/important outcomes;
- prioritizing those essential to decisions.

GRADE categories cited by Cochrane:

1. **critical**;
2. **important**;
3. **not important** for decision making.

Project consequence:
do not collect every measurable variable simply because a paper reports it.

## 7. Critical outcomes

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

Critical outcomes are those essential to the decision and should largely drive:
- conclusions;
- summary evidence;
- certainty assessment.

Cochrane summary tables normally contain no more than seven critical/important outcomes.

Project adaptation:
the project does not need to mechanically cap every topic at seven research variables, but the **decision-facing critical outcome set should remain small**.

### Project rule

A critical outcome must answer:
> Could this outcome plausibly change the practical decision?

If no, it is not critical.

## 8. Important but non-critical outcomes

Useful when they:
- explain secondary trade-offs;
- affect implementation;
- matter to user but not enough alone to determine choice.

Examples in this project could include:
- session duration;
- DOMS;
- enjoyment;
- perceived difficulty;
depending on the question.

Their importance is decision-specific.

## 9. Not-important / exploratory outcomes

Outcomes can be scientifically interesting while not decision-relevant.

Examples:
- a molecular marker;
- acute hormone spike;
- isolated EMG difference
when target decision is chronic hypertrophy.

Such outcomes can still be used for:
- mechanism;
- hypothesis generation;

but should not crowd out direct outcomes.

This connects to later RES-13/RES-14 revalidation.

## 10. Benefit and harm must both be represented

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

Cochrane explicitly requires consideration of both:
- beneficial outcomes;
- adverse effects.

A review that considers only benefits risks a one-sided conclusion.

Project rule:
every intervention-effect Question Card asks separately:

1. What are the desired outcomes?
2. What are the plausible adverse/undesirable outcomes?

If no material harm is plausible, record the reasoning rather than silently omit the category.

## 11. Adverse event vs adverse effect

Cochrane Chapter 19 distinguishes:

### Adverse event
Unfavourable event occurring during/after intervention, not necessarily caused by it.

### Adverse effect / harm
Adverse event for which a causal relation to intervention is at least reasonably possible.

Project rule:
do not label every injury/pain/event observed in intervention group as caused by intervention.

## 12. Harms can require different evidence than benefits

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

Rare or long-latency harms may be missed in:
- small;
- short RCTs.

Cochrane notes that non-randomized evidence may be necessary for some adverse effects.

Project consequence:
question framing can contain:
- one evidence design for efficacy;
- another evidence stream for rare/long-term harms.

This is not methodological inconsistency if justified by outcome type.

## 13. Confirmatory vs exploratory harms

Cochrane Chapter 19 identifies three approaches:

1. confirmatory — prespecified harms;
2. exploratory — capture reported harms broadly;
3. hybrid — both.

### Project default
**Hybrid** for training/intervention questions when safety is relevant:

1. predefine plausible important harms;
2. also record credible unexpected harms found during review;
3. label post-hoc exploratory harms as exploratory.

Reason:
training-related harms can be incompletely and inconsistently reported.

## 14. “No adverse events reported” ≠ “safe”

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

Absence of reported harms may reflect:
- no active monitoring;
- short follow-up;
- incomplete reporting;
- insufficient sample size.

Project wording rule:
do not infer safety from silence.

Use:
> “No relevant harm signal was detected in the available evidence, but ascertainment was limited”

when that is what evidence actually supports.

## 15. Burden is an outcome class

Cochrane explicitly includes burdens such as:
- restrictions;
- testing demands;
and resource/cost outcomes where relevant.

For this project, burden may include:

1. time;
2. fatigue;
3. setup complexity;
4. tracking burden;
5. equipment cost;
6. adherence friction.

These are not automatically “harms” in medical terminology, but they can be critical decision outcomes.

## 16. Surrogate and interim outcomes

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

Cochrane warns that surrogate/interim outcomes can mislead if they do not predict decision-important outcomes accurately.

Project rule:

For each outcome mark:
- direct/decision-important;
- surrogate/intermediate;
- mechanistic.

Do not give a surrogate critical status merely because it is easier to measure.

## 17. Missing critical outcome is itself informative

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

An outcome can remain critical even if primary studies did not measure/report it.

Project consequence:
do **not** downgrade an outcome's importance because literature neglected it.

Instead record:
- evidence gap.

Example:
if adherence sustainability is critical but hypertrophy trials do not report it, that is a gap rather than a reason to delete adherence from the decision model.

## 18. Outcome measure ≠ outcome domain

Example domain:
- maximal strength.

Possible measures:
- 1RM bench;
- dynamometry;
- isometric MVC.

The domain is broader than one instrument.

Project rule:
first specify outcome/domain, then define acceptable measurements and grouping.

Measurement validity is a later layer.

## 19. Multiplicity across time points

A study may report the same outcome:
- week 4;
- week 8;
- week 12.

Selecting the most favorable time point after seeing results creates bias.

Project rule:
predefine:
1. target time point/window;
2. selection hierarchy if multiple measures fall inside it.

Detailed multiplicity statistics remain RES-07 revalidation.

## 20. Fitness-specific default outcome framing

This is **not a universal final outcome set**.

For resistance-training efficacy questions, likely candidate domains include:

### Desired
- strength;
- hypertrophy;
- task performance;
- adherence/retention where relevant.

### Undesirable/burden
- pain/injury/adverse events;
- excessive fatigue;
- time burden;
- dropout;
- interference with other goals.

Each topic must still justify its own critical set.

## 21. Practical rules produced by PASS-03

1. Explicitly specify outcome time horizon.
2. Separate intervention duration from outcome assessment timing.
3. Project Question Card will use PICOT logic when time is relevant; otherwise T = N/A.
4. Predefine time windows before result inspection.
5. Limit decision-critical outcomes.
6. Classify outcomes:
   - critical;
   - important;
   - exploratory/not decision-important.
7. Include at least one benefit domain and relevant harm/undesirable domain for intervention decisions.
8. Use hybrid harm strategy by default when safety matters:
   - prespecified plausible harms;
   - exploratory unexpected harms.
9. Do not infer safety from missing harm reports.
10. Allow harms to require different study designs/follow-up than efficacy.
11. Mark surrogate/intermediate outcomes explicitly.
12. Missing critical outcomes remain evidence gaps, not reasons to remove them.
13. Predefine selection rules for multiple time points/measures.
