## 3. “All systems work simultaneously” audit

PASS WITH TERMINOLOGY REFINEMENT.

The project keeps the trainer-level statement that phosphagen, glycolytic and oxidative ATP-resynthesis pathways are **overlapping and concurrently available**.

However “all systems work simultaneously” must not be interpreted as:

- identical non-zero flux from every pathway in every fibre at every instant;
- equal contribution;
- direct measurement of all local fluxes;
- absence of compartment/fibre heterogeneity.

The 2026 systematic review PMID **41965479** describes phosphagen, glycolytic and oxidative pathways as working together to meet ATP demand.

Canonical wording:

**at whole-muscle/task level, ATP resynthesis is supported by overlapping interacting pathways whose relative flux changes continuously; no pathway requires another pathway to switch off first.**

No blocker.

## 4. Dominant system audit

PASS.

“Dominant” remains an integrated relative-contribution label.

It does not imply:

- exclusive contribution;
- zero oxidative ATP during short maximal work;
- zero glycolysis during prolonged oxidative work;
- one fixed share across the entire bout.

Canonical rule:

**dominant pathway ≠ only active pathway.**

No blocker.

## 5. Intensity audit

PASS.

Increasing intensity raises total ATP turnover and increases the need for high-rate ATP resynthesis.

Retain:

- large early PCr contribution at onset/high power;
- rapid increase in glycolytic ATP production;
- concurrent rise in oxidative ATP production;
- increasing carbohydrate/high-rate non-mitochondrial reliance as demand rises.

Do not retain:

- “high intensity = no aerobic contribution”;
- one universal intensity threshold separating systems.

Canonical rule:

**intensity changes relative pathway flux and total ATP demand; it does not define exclusive pathway ownership.**

No blocker.

## 6. Duration / 78.6 s crossover audit

PASS WITH STRONG MODEL BOUNDARY.

The 2026 systematic review PMID **41965479** included:

- **102 studies**;
- **311 study-mean data points**;
- adult maximal exercise;
- samples reported as ~78% male, 12% female, 11% mixed;
- literature search through **January 2020**.

Its nonlinear model estimated equal anaerobic/aerobic contribution at **78.6 s**.

Important limitations:

- it is a regression estimate from pooled study means;
- anaerobic contribution is method-derived rather than directly measured;
- measurement method and pacing strategy had significant effects;
- the literature was male-dominant;
- publication year 2026 does not mean the evidence search extended to 2026.

Therefore:

**78.6 s is an aggregate model estimate, not a biological switch point, not an individual threshold, and not a universal value for every modality/population.**

The durable conclusion is only:

**short maximal exercise is more anaerobic dominated; oxidative contribution increases as maximal exercise duration increases.**

No blocker.

## 7. Transition audit

PASS.

At exercise onset:

1. ATP demand rises immediately;
2. stored ATP/PCr provides rapid buffering;
3. glycolytic ATP production increases;
4. oxidative ATP production accelerates;
5. relative contributions evolve continuously.

PMID **9124295** showed substantial oxidative ATP synthesis can be reached within seconds during brief maximal muscle exercise.

Canonical rule:

**transition = continuous redistribution of flux, not sequential handoff.**

Detailed VO2 kinetics and oxygen deficit remain BIO-09.

No blocker.

## 8. Stored ATP vs phosphagen audit

PASS WITH DEFINITION BOUNDARY.

Stored ATP and PCr are closely linked in the immediate high-energy-phosphate response but are not chemically identical.

Project convention:

- “phosphagen” may be used as applied shorthand for the ATP-PCr/CK immediate system;
- when quantitative accounting matters, stored ATP and PCr-derived ATP must be distinguished.

Canonical boundary:

**stored ATP ≠ PCr, even when both are grouped educationally within the immediate phosphagen response.**

No blocker.

## 9. Measurement-method audit

PASS WITH STRONG LIMITATION.

The 2025 systematic review/best-evidence synthesis PMID **41078873** identified five main approaches:

1. MAOD;
2. PCr-La-O2;
3. critical power;
4. gross efficiency;
5. bioenergetic models.

It found:

- **strong evidence for MAOD** reliability/validity;
- **limited-to-strong** evidence for CP and PCr-La-O2;
- **limited-to-conflicting** evidence for gross efficiency and bioenergetic models;
- no universally accepted gold standard for anaerobic contribution.

MAOD:

- estimates total anaerobic contribution;
- does not directly partition phosphagen vs glycolytic components.

PCr-La-O2:

- can partition alactic/phosphagen and lactic/glycolytic estimates using physiological measures;
- has a smaller validation/reliability evidence base.

Canonical rule:

**energy-system percentages are method-conditioned estimates; method identity is part of the result.**

No blocker.

## 10. Blood lactate / glycolytic ATP audit

PASS.

Blood lactate is not a direct calorimeter of glycolytic ATP flux.

Problems include:

- production vs appearance/disposal;
- transport between compartments;
- oxidation/recycling;
- timing of blood sampling;
- assumed O2-equivalent conversion factors.

Therefore:

**blood lactate concentration or delta lactate ≠ direct measurement of glycolytic ATP production.**

If lactate-derived estimates are used within a method such as PCr-La-O2, they remain model-dependent estimates.

No blocker.

---

## 11. VO2 / oxidative contribution audit

PASS.

Exercise VO2 provides important information about oxidative metabolism, but:

- pulmonary VO2 is a whole-body measurement;
- muscle O2 utilization has kinetics/delivery delays;
- non-steady-state integration requires assumptions;
- VO2 does not capture total non-oxidative ATP production.

Therefore:

**VO2-derived oxidative contribution is useful but not equivalent to direct total cellular ATP-flux measurement.**

Detailed kinetics remain BIO-09.

No blocker.

---

## 12. Repeated-set audit

PASS WITH TASK-DEPENDENCE BOUNDARY.

A subsequent set begins with a different internal state when recovery is incomplete.

Potential changes include:

- lower PCr;
- altered Pi;
- altered pH;
- elevated VO2;
- changed glycolytic response;
- changed power/work.

PMID **22808209** directly showed that different repetition configurations with the same initial load/rest structure produced materially different PCr, ATP, lactate and power responses.

Canonical rule:

**same external load or repetition target does not guarantee the same metabolic contribution across sets.**

However BIO-08 does not claim that oxidative contribution must increase in every later set.

Exact direction depends on:

- set duration;
- rest;
- work performed;
- fatigue;
- exercise;
- active muscle mass;
- training state.

No blocker.

---

## 13. Repeated-sprint audit

PASS WITH GENERALIZATION BOUNDARY.

PMID **8964751** showed in two 30-s cycle sprints separated by 4 min:

- PCr remained incompletely restored before sprint 2;
- anaerobic ATP turnover fell;
- glycolytic contribution fell by ~45%;
- oxidative contribution increased.

The 2024 study PMCID **PMC11549394** in **12 male amateur soccer players** using repeated 20 m sprints and PCr-La-O2 also found:

- declining glycolytic relative contribution across later repetitions;
- increasing relative oxidative contribution;
- persistent important ATP-PCr contribution.

But this 2024 protocol is:

- small;
- male-only;
- soccer-specific;
- method-dependent.

Therefore:

**repeated-sprint pathway redistribution is well supported, but exact percentages and the exact repetition at which oxidative contribution exceeds glycolytic contribution are protocol specific.**

No blocker.

---

## 14. Maximal intent vs pathway flux audit

PASS.

Repeated-sprint evidence confirms:

**maximal effort/intent does not guarantee constant maximal glycolytic ATP flux.**

Pathway flux can decline because the metabolic starting state and regulatory environment change even when the athlete continues maximal effort.

No blocker.

---

## 15. Long low-intensity activity audit

PASS.

During prolonged low/moderate-intensity steady-state work:

- oxidative phosphorylation supplies most ATP;
- carbohydrate/fat source mix can shift;
- glycolysis still processes carbohydrate and can provide substrate-level ATP;
- CK/PCr remains part of local ATP buffering;
- accelerations/terrain changes can transiently increase non-oxidative contribution.

Canonical rule:

**oxidative-dominant ≠ oxidative-only.**

No blocker.

---

## 16. Resistance-training audit

PASS WITH MEASUREMENT BOUNDARY.

Resistance exercise is intermittent, non-steady-state and metabolically mixed.

PMID **22808209** confirms simultaneous PCr depletion and lactate accumulation under demanding leg-press work.

PMID **23487002** estimated a large anaerobic fraction during exhaustive 80% 1RM exercise, but the study itself reported imperfect accuracy and called for more validation.

The 2024 scoping review PMID **38896201** included **166 studies** and found:

- indirect calorimetry was most common;
- energy-expenditure estimates varied widely;
- substantial anaerobic contribution makes resistance-exercise energy expenditure difficult to quantify;
- indirect calorimetry alone is limited for glycolytic contribution;
- gas exchange combined with blood lactate may better approximate total energy expenditure, while still remaining an estimate.

Canonical rule:

**resistance exercise uses mixed phosphagen/glycolytic/oxidative metabolism; universal percentage splits are not retained.**

No blocker.

---

## 17. Recovery-interval audit

PASS.

Recovery is metabolically active.

Oxidative ATP production supports:

- PCr resynthesis;
- restoration of oxygen stores;
- ion-gradient/homeostatic processes;
- substrate redistribution/oxidation;
- other ATP-dependent restoration.

Therefore:

**a rest interval is part of the metabolic state of the next bout.**

No blocker.

---

## 18. PCr-recovery audit

PASS WITH IMPORTANT REFINEMENT.

PCr resynthesis is primarily powered by oxidative ATP production.

Evidence:

- PMID **8457428** supports PCr recovery as an estimate of net oxidative ATP synthesis under defined conditions;
- PMID **10368368** showed hypoxia slowed and hyperoxia accelerated PCr recovery in trained humans;
- PMID **9124295** relates post-exercise PCr recovery to muscle oxidative capacity;
- PMID **9241025** showed incomplete PCr repletion after repeated sprints even at 3 min.

But closure rejects:

**PCr recovery is universally limited only by oxygen.**

The 2025 systematic review/meta-analysis PMID **40189235** included **128 studies** and found:

- substantial methodological/laboratory heterogeneity;
- more acidic end-exercise pH was associated with longer PCr recovery in healthy individuals;
- demographic/disease factors can also affect kinetics.

The older review PMID **12238940** also supports multi-factor control involving ADP/CK equilibrium, oxidative phosphorylation and pH-dependent slower recovery.

Canonical rule:

**PCr resynthesis is oxidative but its measured kinetics are state- and method-dependent; O2 availability is one regulator, not a universal sole limiter.**

No blocker.

---

## 19. “Three minutes fully restores PCr” audit

PASS — rejected as universal.

PMID **9241025** found approximately:

- 90% of pre-exercise PCr after 3 min following one 6-s sprint;
- 84% after 3 min following repeated 5 × 6-s sprints.

Therefore:

**one fixed recovery duration does not guarantee full PCr restoration across protocols.**

No programming prescription is made here.

No blocker.

## 20. Recovery metabolism vs EPOC audit

PASS.

BIO-08 retains:

**oxidative metabolism supports restoration during recovery.**

BIO-08 does not equate:

- PCr resynthesis;
- excess post-exercise oxygen consumption;
- lactate oxidation;
- thermogenic/cardiovascular post-exercise cost;
- total recovery energy expenditure.

Canonical rule:

**PCr resynthesis is one recovery process; EPOC is a broader post-exercise oxygen-consumption construct.**

Detailed EPOC belongs BIO-09.

No blocker.

## 21. Integrated contribution vs instantaneous ATP flux audit

PASS.

A percentage reported for an entire bout is an **integrated energy contribution**.

It does not necessarily describe:

- contribution in the first second;
- peak ATP-production rate;
- contribution at exercise termination;
- local fibre-specific flux.

Canonical rule:

**integrated pathway percentage ≠ instantaneous ATP-production rate.**

No blocker.

## 25. Closure decision

**BIO-08 = CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0.**

Meaning:

1. ATP-resynthesis pathways overlap and interact rather than operate as sequential isolated engines;
2. dominance is relative, not exclusive;
3. intensity and duration continuously redistribute contribution;
4. no universal time-based switching chart is retained;
5. short maximal work can be anaerobic dominated while oxidative ATP is already active;
6. the ~78.6 s pooled maximal-exercise crossover estimate is not treated as an individual physiological boundary;
7. contribution percentages are method-conditioned estimates;
8. blood lactate is not direct glycolytic ATP flux;
9. VO2 is not total cellular ATP production;
10. repeated sets/sprints begin from altered metabolic states;
11. repeated-sprint glycolytic contribution can fall despite maximal intent;
12. long low/moderate-intensity activity is oxidative-dominant, not oxidative-only;
13. resistance exercise is metabolically mixed and protocol dependent;
14. recovery intervals are metabolically active;
15. PCr resynthesis is oxidative but not universally O2-limited;
16. no universal fixed PCr recovery duration is retained;
17. PCr resynthesis and EPOC remain distinct;
18. integrated contribution percentages remain distinct from instantaneous ATP flux.
