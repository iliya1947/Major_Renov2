## Evidence checkpoint — targeted public searches (not systematic review)
1. CDC https://www.cdc.gov/autism/hcp/diagnosis/index.html diagnostic sections directly read, pediatric-focused public DSM-5 summary, not full DSM-5-TR or update audit. Distinct social-communication/interaction and restricted/repetitive domains, developmental history, actual impact, differential; no single diagnostic instrument. NICE CG142 https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg142/chapter/recommendations indexed recommendations: comprehensive adult developmental, functional, differential and needs assessment; possible IQ-adaptive discrepancy; direct access 403. WHO ICD-11 CDDR 6A02 indexed extract only, full manual not directly verified in this unit. No DSM symptom counts imported into ICD.
2. Doobay et al. 2014 https://doi.org/10.1007/s10803-014-2082-1, indexed author abstract https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24584434/ (direct CAPTCHA): 40 high-ability youth with ASD, 41 high-ability youth without psychological diagnosis. Lower processing speed, adaptive and broad psychosocial functioning in ASD group. Boschi et al. 2016 review https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01605 directly inspected intro/methods and comparison section: reports WISC-IV VCI and PRI selection >=130 and Vineland subscale differences; exact original inclusion logic and effects not independently checked, do not treat as FSIQ>=130 or diagnostic cutoffs. Review reports few direct comparative studies; it incorporates Doobay and is not independent replication. Selected youth and unusually healthy comparison group constrain differential specificity. No adult effect inferred.
3. Johnston et al. 2019 https://doi.org/10.1007/s10803-019-04133-7 publisher abstract/introduction inspected: 110 autistic adults vs 31 age/IQ-matched controls, intended average intellectual range, not gifted sample; EF task differences, 35.8% of ASD group not impaired on tested EF measures. Everyday dysexecutive behavior not related to tested EF performance in reported sample. Group association does not establish universal deficit, mechanism or diagnostic criterion; exact test cutoffs/denominators and comorbidity analysis not fully rechecked.
4. Orm et al. 2024 https://doi.org/10.1007/s10802-024-01265-y publisher abstract/introduction inspected; longitudinal sample with IQ>70, not gifted-only. Emerging adults with ASD reported greater impairment on WFIRS (reported g=.92); childhood/adolescent EF difficulties predicted later adaptive functioning. Predictive association not causal intervention effect. WFIRS self-report not identical to directly measured adaptive skill or Vineland. Exact attrition, adjustment and effects not yet inspected. EU-AIMS LEAP https://doi.org/10.1002/aur.2081 indexed primary results only (403), reports higher IQ associated with greater IQ-adaptive discrepancy in selected analyses; distinguish absolute adaptive level from a difference score containing IQ, mathematical coupling and selection. 2026 longitudinal counterexample https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13377276/ indexed text reports limited discrepancy and challenges deterministic expectations; full results and eligibility pending. Do not infer inevitable worsening or a causal cognitive-adaptive gap.
5. Ashwood et al. 2016 https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291716001082 indexed author abstract, PMC direct CAPTCHA: 476 adults consecutively referred for suspected ASD, AQ-10 sensitivity .77 and specificity .29 (reported 95% CI .72–.82 and .20–.38; cutpoint >=6 confirmed in 2025 paper's retrospective discussion, not direct methods). Predictive values depend on referral base rate, not population or high-IQ strata. A questionnaire can help identify assessment questions but cannot confirm/exclude ASD by itself. Do not apply clinical threshold as self-diagnosis.
6. Lai et al. https://doi.org/10.1177/1362361316671012 indexed author methods: 60 autistic adults without ID, age/IQ-matched sex groups, composite discrepancy between ADOS, AQ and RMET proposed to operationalize camouflaging. Discrepancy not direct measure of intent/effort, not universal or uniquely autistic, no causal IQ effect. Detailed compensation reserved for GFT-14.
7. 2026 adult qualitative study https://doi.org/10.3390/educsci16081254 indexed primary methods/abstract (publisher 429): 17 adults, 15 formally diagnosed and 2 self-identified, historical psychometric records OR TOPF >90th percentile. Retrospective perceived educational experiences, not a controlled adult high-IQ group comparison, not uniformly measured adult FSIQ>=130. No prevalence, general effect or extreme-tail inference. 2026 self-rated intelligence https://doi.org/10.1177/13623613261495453 indexed author text measures self-estimated IQ categories, not directly measured tail. Youth academic trajectories https://doi.org/10.1177/1362361318804019 indexed author abstract describes higher initial performance and subsequent gains in gifted ASD students, not uniform adaptive problems. 2025 differential child study https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0045-1806998 direct cache miss; no effects adopted.

## Provisional transfer rules
- Two independent axes: evidence of measured general ability and evidence of ASD developmental criteria. Specific talent, high verbal score, low PSI, sensory sensitivity, strong interests, introversion, preference for intellectual peers, deliberate social strategies or screening scores do not independently establish either.
- Separate capacity (standardized task), behavior (context and partner), adaptive functioning (actual everyday demands), internal effort and support. High output and real support needs can coexist; relative weakness alone is not impairment. Neither infer empathy deficit from ASD nor exclude ASD from good social reasoning.
- Competing models: person–environment mismatch, social anxiety/learned avoidance, ADHD, developmental language/pragmatic difference, sensory environment, autism or combinations. Identify early developmental history, both diagnostic domains, context variability, onset and functional consequences, and independently corroborate with consent. Context improvement can reflect support in autistic as well as non-autistic people; do not deliberately remove helpful supports. Referral if clinically relevant; no diagnosis or treatment prescription.
- Research design: high-IQ autistic vs IQ-matched non-autistic; ideally comparable non-high-IQ groups and clinical differentials; measure IQ with valid norms, independent functional outcomes, developmental and recruitment history, access and accommodations. Cross-sectional association cannot establish causal compensation, compare interactions with uncertainty, not significance differences. Retain source dependency and range restrictions.
