# NEU-04 — Cognitive/affective neuroscience integration and sufficiency audit

Status: **COMPLETE_FOR_CURRENT_SCOPE — 20/20 PASS**
Evidence cutoff: **2026-11-19**
Scope: public neuroscience evidence and generic inference methodology only; no individualized psychological material.

## Purpose

NEU-01 established method-specific inference boundaries for localization, networks, fMRI/BOLD, functional connectivity, lesions, stimulation, EEG/MEG, reverse inference, decoding, neural prediction and individual biomarkers.

NEU-02 established neurochemical inference boundaries for neurotransmitters, receptor/projection heterogeneity, PET binding and displacement, receptor/transporter occupancy, pharmacology, pharmacokinetics/pharmacodynamics, and downstream efficacy.

NEU-03 established systems/plasticity inference boundaries for excitation/inhibition, oscillations and synchrony, synaptic plasticity, homeostasis, structural MRI, myelin, adult human neurogenesis, and cross-scale plasticity.

NEU-04 asks the final domain question:

**Do NEU-01…03 jointly provide a sufficiently coherent, current, and reusable framework for generic cognitive/affective neuroscience inference, or is another foundational neuroscience block required before applied psychometrics?**

This is an integration/sufficiency audit, not an encyclopedia of neuroscience.

## Core rule

**A psychological construct, a computational operation, a neural representation, a network, a circuit, a cell population, a neuromodulatory system, and a plasticity mechanism are different explanatory targets. Evidence can connect these targets, but one level must not be silently substituted for another. The strongest cognitive/affective neuroscience inference specifies the psychological construct, neural level, directly measured variable, causal status, timescale, spatial scale, validation target, and generalization population, then states only the narrowest cross-level claim supported.**

Integrated reasoning:

define psychological construct -> specify computation/behavioral operation -> choose neural level and measurement -> identify what the method directly observes or perturbs -> distinguish association, prediction, representation, necessity, sufficiency and intervention -> model network/circuit/receptor/plasticity alternatives -> separate within-person process from between-person difference -> test reliability, specificity and external generalization -> integrate independent convergent evidence -> preserve cross-domain alternatives -> state the narrowest justified brain–behavior inference.

# Part I — Integrated levels of explanation

## 1. Psychological construct is not a neural label

Psychological constructs such as:
- working memory;
- reward valuation;
- cognitive control;
- emotion regulation;
- empathy;
- confidence;
- habit;
- fear;
- motivation

require psychological definition and construct validation.

A neural correlate does not replace that definition.

## 2. Computation and implementation are distinct targets

A computation specifies an information-processing operation.

A biological implementation specifies how neural systems realize that operation.

The same computation can potentially be implemented through different neural configurations.

The same neural system can contribute to several computations.

Therefore:
**computation = brain region**
is generally too strong.

## 3. Cross-level mapping is many-to-many

One psychological process can involve:
- multiple representations;
- several networks;
- multiple neuromodulators;
- several plasticity mechanisms.

One neural system can participate in:
- cognitive;
- affective;
- motivational;
- social;
- motor
contexts.

Cross-level mapping should be treated as a constrained relation, not an identity.

## 4. Lower biological scale is not automatically deeper explanation

Molecular, synaptic or cellular detail can increase biological specificity.

It does not automatically explain:
- task computation;
- subjective experience;
- behavioral strategy;
- individual difference.

A lower-level explanation is useful when it reduces uncertainty about the target question.

## 5. Localization and distribution coexist

Current evidence supports:
- regional specialization;
- distributed recruitment;
- network interaction;
- graded/overlapping boundaries.

The relevant question is not:
localized or distributed?

It is:
**what degree and type of specialization/distribution does the evidence support for this operation?**

A 2026 Nature Reviews Neuroscience synthesis concludes that cognitive variables can be represented broadly but not ubiquitously across the brain.

## 6. Network label is not a functional ontology

Labels such as:
- default-mode network;
- salience network;
- frontoparietal control network;
- reward network;
- social brain

are useful organizational summaries.

They are not equivalent to one uniquely defined computation.

Their boundaries depend on:
- atlas;
- modality;
- resolution;
- preprocessing;
- clustering/model;
- task/state.

## 7. Circuit topology can preserve specialization and integration simultaneously

A 2026 connectomic review of cortico-basal ganglia–thalamic organization describes:
- modular organization;
- overlapping/graded boundaries;
- segregation;
- convergence;
- re-entrant interactions.

This is a useful general model:
**specialization and integration are architectural properties that can coexist.**

## 8. Cognitive, affective and social domains are not anatomically sealed compartments

A 2026 Nature Reviews Neuroscience perspective on social cognition argues that regions used in social cognition can perform analogous computations in non-social contexts with similar computational demands.

Therefore:
**socially recruited region = social-only mechanism**
is invalid.

The same principle applies broadly to affective and cognitive labels.

# Part II — Neural representation and decoding

## 9. Neural sensitivity is not the whole concept of representation

A 2026 Nature Reviews Neuroscience framework separates at least:
- sensitivity to a feature;
- specificity for that feature;
- invariance to irrelevant variation;
- functionality/downstream use.

These dimensions support different strengths of representation claim.

## 10. Decodability usually establishes sensitivity first

If a classifier predicts feature X from neural activity, this demonstrates information about X in the measured signal under the tested distribution.

It does not automatically establish:
- specificity;
- invariance;
- causal use;
- mechanism;
- subjective content.

## 11. High decoder accuracy does not prove a dedicated code

A decoder can exploit:
- correlated nuisance variables;
- motor responses;
- task structure;
- low-level sensory features;
- global state;
- site/scanner artifacts.

Labels and experimental design define what the classifier can learn.

## 12. Representation claims require stronger tests than detectability

Stronger evidence can include:
- cross-condition generalization;
- cross-task generalization;
- invariance tests;
- feature-specific perturbation;
- downstream-readout evidence;
- competing model comparison.

Above-chance decoding alone is insufficient.

## 13. Representational similarity remains model comparison

Similarity between:
- neural geometry;
- behavioral geometry;
- computational-model geometry

can support a representational hypothesis.

It does not directly read semantic content from the brain.

## 14. Flexible high-capacity models create model-equivalence risk

A 2026 Nature Reviews Neuroscience review emphasizes that high-capacity neural-network models can approximate similar functions, making them difficult to discriminate with ordinary stimuli.

Mechanistic model comparison should therefore seek:
- diagnostic conditions;
- adversarial/model-disagreement stimuli;
- out-of-distribution tests;
rather than relying only on average fit.

# Part III — Process, state, trait and individual difference

## 15. Within-person process effect is not a between-person trait

A neural response can reliably differ between task conditions within the same person.

That does not mean variation in the response across people measures:
- ability;
- capacity;
- personality;
- symptom severity;
- habitual behavior.

These are different statistical and psychological targets.

## 16. Group activation is not individual psychometrics

A robust group contrast can coexist with:
- low between-person reliability;
- weak trait association;
- limited rank-order stability.

Therefore:
**replicable group activation -> useful individual biomarker**
is invalid.

## 17. Emotion-regulation evidence gives a direct 2026 stress test

A preregistered 2026 consortium analysis of 40 task-fMRI datasets with 2,175 participants tested three common reappraisal-capacity indicators:
- trait questionnaires;
- task-based affective ratings;
- amygdala down-regulation.

They did not converge on one shared capacity, and none showed the expected association with canonical prefrontal regulatory circuits.

This is direct evidence against:
**task-process activation = individual regulation capacity.**

## 18. Process validity and trait validity can diverge

The 2026 emotion-regulation result does not invalidate within-person process evidence.

It shows that:
- a task can identify neural implementation of an instructed process;
while
- the same neural signal may not rank people by real-world capacity.

Process inference and individual-difference inference require separate validation.

## 19. Within-person and between-person variance must be separated

A measure may contain:
- large within-person state variance;
- small between-person variance;
- both.

Correlations at one level do not automatically transfer to the other.

This is an ecological/ergodic inference boundary.

## 20. State and trait require timescale evidence

Repeated measurement is needed to distinguish:
- transient neural state;
- context-dependent response;
- stable individual difference.

One scan or one task session cannot establish a stable neural trait by label alone.

# Part IV — Measurement and modality integration

## 21. Every modality measures a different physical quantity

Examples:
- fMRI BOLD: hemodynamic signal coupled indirectly to neural/metabolic activity;
- EEG/MEG: electromagnetic field measurements;
- PET: radioligand kinetics/binding-derived quantities;
- diffusion MRI: water-diffusion-sensitive tissue properties;
- structural MRI: image-derived anatomical/tissue contrast;
- MRS: metabolite-related spectral quantities;
- electrophysiology: electrical activity at defined recording scales.

These quantities are not interchangeable.

## 22. Measurement proxy must not be renamed as mechanism

Invalid shortcuts include:
- BOLD = neuronal firing;
- Glx/GABA = synaptic E/I;
- FA = myelin;
- PET binding potential = transmitter level;
- EEG coherence = communication;
- cortical thickness change = neuronal growth.

The measure should retain its measurement-level name until mechanism-specific validation exists.

## 23. Multimodal convergence is valuable when modalities constrain different uncertainties

Convergence is strongest when methods contribute complementary evidence, for example:
- temporal measurement + spatial localization;
- observational imaging + perturbation;
- target engagement + downstream systems response;
- cellular evidence + behavior;
- structural assay + histological calibration.

## 24. Multimodal convergence is not automatically independent confirmation

Different modalities can share:
- task confounds;
- preprocessing decisions;
- participant selection;
- physiological state;
- construct misspecification;
- circular region selection.

Convergence should be evaluated for independence of failure modes.

## 25. Reliability is target-specific

Reliability must be estimated for:
- the exact derived measure;
- the exact pipeline;
- the relevant population;
- the relevant timescale.

There is no single reliability value for fMRI, EEG, PET, structural MRI or neuroscience generally.

## 26. Reproducibility is not construct validity

A signal can be:
- computationally reproducible;
- statistically reliable;
yet
- measure the wrong construct.

Psychological and biological validity remain independent requirements.

## 27. External validation is stronger than internal cross-validation

A 2026 Nature Methods perspective emphasizes that neuroimaging predictive models often fail external validation.

Independent-dataset evaluation better tests:
- generalizability;
- dataset shift;
- reproducibility;
- hidden leakage.

## 28. External validation is still population-bounded

Successful external validation establishes transport only between:
- the tested training distribution;
- the tested external distribution.

It does not guarantee universal generalization across:
- age;
- culture;
- clinical status;
- scanners;
- tasks;
- preprocessing pipelines.

# Part V — Causal evidence integration

## 29. Association, prediction and causation are different claims

Association:
signals covary.

Prediction:
one variable forecasts another under a validation regime.

Causation:
intervening on one variable changes another under assumptions/design.

These must not be collapsed.

## 30. Temporal precedence is useful but insufficient

A neural signal occurring before behavior can be compatible with:
- causal contribution;
- common upstream cause;
- predictive preparation;
- epiphenomenal covariation.

Timing narrows alternatives but does not eliminate them.

## 31. Lesions provide necessity-like evidence with constraints

Natural lesions can strongly support a causal role.

But interpretation must account for:
- nonrandom anatomy;
- co-damage;
- disconnection;
- diaschisis;
- recovery;
- compensation.

Lesion site is not automatically exact mechanism location.

## 32. Stimulation provides perturbation, not a perfect local switch

TMS and related interventions can strengthen causal inference.

But target interpretation depends on:
- field spread;
- network effects;
- brain state;
- sensory/peripheral effects;
- target engagement.

A stimulation effect means:
**this intervention changed this system and altered this outcome under these conditions.**

## 33. Necessity and sufficiency are distinct

A system can be necessary for a task without being sufficient to generate the full psychological process.

Sufficiency requires a different experimental logic.

## 34. Strong local sufficiency evidence remains scope-limited

Animal circuit experiments can sometimes establish necessity/sufficiency for:
- a defined cell population;
- a defined manipulation;
- a defined behavioral effect.

Such evidence should not be generalized to:
- the whole construct;
- all species;
- all contexts.

## 35. Causal mediation requires intervention-aware assumptions

Neural mediation models based only on observational correlations do not prove:
stimulus -> neural mediator -> behavior.

Unmeasured confounding and measurement error can generate apparent mediation.

## 36. Causal evidence should be claim-matched

Examples:
- region necessity claim -> lesion/perturbation evidence;
- transmitter target claim -> receptor/occupancy/pharmacology evidence;
- representation-use claim -> downstream/perturbation evidence;
- plasticity mechanism claim -> change + persistence + behavioral specificity + manipulation.

The design must target the claim.

# Part VI — Neurochemical integration

## 37. Neurotransmitter names are not psychological explanations

Invalid identities include:
- dopamine = reward;
- serotonin = happiness;
- norepinephrine = arousal;
- acetylcholine = memory.

Each system is heterogeneous across:
- projection;
- receptor;
- region;
- timescale;
- local circuit;
- state.

## 38. Chemical identity does not specify signal semantics

The same transmitter can participate in multiple computations.

Different transmitters can jointly support one psychological operation.

The relevant unit is often:
**transmitter × receptor × projection × circuit × state × timescale.**

## 39. Release, concentration, availability and occupancy are different quantities

PET and pharmacological inference must separate:
- receptor availability;
- transporter availability;
- receptor occupancy;
- endogenous displacement;
- extracellular concentration;
- downstream signaling.

No one quantity should be renamed as a general transmitter level.

## 40. Target engagement is not functional efficacy

High receptor occupancy can occur without proportional:
- neural effect;
- behavioral effect;
- subjective effect.

Dose-response relationships can be:
- nonlinear;
- saturating;
- receptor-reserve-dependent;
- adaptation-dependent.

## 41. Drug effect is not one receptor mechanism by default

Systemic drugs can include:
- multiple targets;
- active metabolites;
- peripheral actions;
- expectancy/blinding effects;
- compensatory responses.

Mechanistic attribution requires target-specific evidence.

## 42. Acute pharmacology does not equal chronic physiology

Repeated exposure can produce:
- receptor regulation;
- transporter adaptation;
- network compensation;
- altered downstream signaling.

Acute challenge findings should not be transferred automatically to chronic states.

# Part VII — Systems dynamics and oscillations

## 43. E/I balance is a family of quantities

Possible E/I targets include:
- synaptic currents;
- conductances;
- cell-population activity;
- metabolite proxies;
- spectral proxies;
- network stability.

There is no single global E/I scalar.

## 44. Proxy agreement does not imply dynamic identity

Two E/I-related modalities can correlate between people and still fail to track the same within-person dynamics.

Cross-modal correlation must therefore specify:
- level;
- region;
- timescale;
- between/within-person target.

## 45. Oscillation measurement is not automatically oscillatory mechanism

Power, phase, coherence and coupling are measurements/statistics.

Interpretation must consider:
- aperiodic activity;
- waveform shape;
- burst structure;
- volume conduction;
- source leakage;
- estimator choice.

## 46. Frequency band is not a psychological dictionary

Theta, alpha, beta and gamma can participate in multiple functions.

A frequency label does not uniquely identify:
- memory;
- inhibition;
- binding;
- attention;
- control.

## 47. Synchrony is not causal communication

Coherence/phase-locking can arise through:
- common input;
- mixing;
- indirect paths;
- volume conduction;
- leakage.

Communication hypotheses require stronger temporal, anatomical and preferably perturbational evidence.

## 48. Oscillatory communication remains a legitimate conditional mechanism

Rejecting the shortcut:
coherence = communication
does not imply:
oscillations never contribute to communication.

Mechanism should be established in a circuit- and task-specific way.

# Part VIII — Plasticity integration

## 49. Neural plasticity is not one mechanism

Plasticity includes:
- synaptic efficacy changes;
- intrinsic excitability changes;
- homeostatic regulation;
- structural remodeling;
- myelin changes;
- glial changes;
- neurogenesis in specific systems.

Different mechanisms operate at different scales/timescales.

## 50. Learning and neural plasticity are related but not identical

Behavioral learning is defined through changed behavior/knowledge/performance under a learning framework.

Neural plasticity refers to biological change.

A learning effect can involve multiple plasticity mechanisms.

A plasticity marker does not by itself identify psychological learning.

## 51. LTP/LTD are not synonyms for memory/forgetting

LTP and LTD are families of synaptic phenomena.

Strong memory-mechanism claims require more than correlation:
- occlusion;
- blocking;
- necessity;
- erasure;
- rescue/sufficiency
where feasible.

## 52. Homeostasis and learning are complementary

Hebbian-like change can alter network states.

Homeostatic mechanisms can stabilize activity and preserve operating ranges.

Stability is not absence of plasticity.

## 53. Structural MRI change does not identify cellular growth

Training-related MRI changes can reflect combinations of:
- neural structure;
- glia;
- vasculature/perfusion;
- extracellular fluid;
- metabolism;
- pipeline effects.

A structural signal can be real while its cellular cause remains unknown.

## 54. Myelin-sensitive MRI is not histological myelin

FA and myelin-related MRI metrics vary in biological specificity.

Human experience-related myelin inference remains more indirect than cellular animal evidence.

## 55. Adult human hippocampal neurogenesis and its function are separate questions

2025/2026 evidence substantially strengthens the existence of adult human dentate-gyrus progenitor/immature neuronal populations.

It does not yet establish:
- a universal production rate;
- mature circuit integration for every identified cell;
- a causal human memory mechanism.

Existence, maturation and cognitive function remain separate inferential steps.

## 56. More plasticity is not universally better

Plasticity can support:
- learning;
- compensation;
- adaptation.

It can also contribute to:
- instability;
- maladaptive learning;
- pathological remodeling.

Adaptive value depends on context and control.

# Part IX — Cognitive/affective/social integration

## 57. Cognitive neuroscience should begin with a cognitive model

A brain signal is interpretable only relative to:
- the task;
- the hypothesized computation;
- alternatives;
- behavioral evidence.

Neural localization cannot repair an invalid cognitive task model.

## 58. Affective neuroscience should preserve channel distinctions

Emotion-related constructs can differ across:
- subjective report;
- autonomic physiology;
- behavior/expression;
- neural response.

Convergence can occur without one channel being a universal gold standard.

## 59. Affective arousal is not autonomic arousal by definition

A 2025 neurofunctional-signature study reported distributed neural prediction of subjective affective arousal and distinguished it from autonomic reactivity/wakefulness in its validation framework.

This supports:
**same everyday label can refer to separable measurement targets.**

## 60. Emotion regulation is not a single top-down prefrontal quantity

The 2026 consortium result shows that:
- within-person reappraisal process;
- task affect reduction;
- trait questionnaire;
- amygdala down-regulation;
- prefrontal activation

should not be treated as interchangeable indicators of one personal capacity.

## 61. Social cognition can reuse general computations

Social prediction, value learning, latent-state inference and confidence can recruit mechanisms also used in non-social settings.

Thus neural evidence should distinguish:
- computational specialization;
from
- contextual specialization.

## 62. Metacognition illustrates legitimate cross-level integration

A 2026 Nature Reviews Neuroscience synthesis integrates:
- behavioral confidence;
- evidence accumulation;
- uncertainty transformations;
- prefrontal systems;
- self-beliefs.

The useful lesson is not a one-region mapping.

It is that computational, behavioral and neural evidence can constrain one another without being identical.

# Part X — Individual inference and biomarkers

## 63. Neural correlate is not a biomarker

A biomarker requires evidence for:
- measurement reliability;
- construct/clinical validity;
- sensitivity/specificity where relevant;
- calibration;
- external validation;
- transportability;
- incremental utility.

Association alone is insufficient.

## 64. Group difference is not individual classification

Two groups can differ on average with substantial distributional overlap.

A statistically significant neural group effect does not establish useful person-level prediction.

## 65. Prediction is not mechanism

A neural model can predict an outcome from:
- causal features;
- consequences;
- confounders;
- proxies.

Predictive validity and mechanistic validity are separate targets.

## 66. Mechanism is not automatically useful prediction

A causal mechanism can be scientifically correct yet provide weak individual prediction if:
- effect size is small;
- measurement is noisy;
- multiple compensatory pathways exist.

Clinical/individual utility requires separate testing.

## 67. Dataset shift is a core neuroscience problem

Neuroimaging models may shift with:
- scanner;
- site;
- preprocessing;
- demographics;
- task;
- disease prevalence;
- recruitment.

External validation should be designed around the intended use population.

## 68. Person-level neural inference routes directly to applied psychometrics

Once neuroscience attempts to rank or classify individuals, questions become psychometric:
- reliability;
- score meaning;
- dimensionality;
- measurement invariance;
- calibration;
- normative reference;
- change sensitivity;
- decision threshold.

This is why PSYCHOMETRICS is the correct next dependency.

# Part XI — Species, scale and translation

## 69. Animal-to-human translation requires mechanism matching

Translation should compare:
- homologous anatomy;
- receptor expression;
- circuit organization;
- task demands;
- behavioral phenotype;
- timescale.

Species similarity cannot be assumed from a shared label.

## 70. Cellular causal evidence and human systems evidence have different strengths

Animal studies often offer:
- cellular specificity;
- genetic access;
- direct perturbation.

Human studies often offer:
- richer cognition;
- direct subjective report;
- population relevance.

Integration should preserve these asymmetric strengths.

## 71. Cross-species convergence is strongest at shared computational constraints

When identical behavior cannot be measured across species, a useful bridge can be:
- computation;
- learning rule;
- decision variable;
- circuit motif.

But computational similarity still requires empirical validation.

# Part XII — Internal contradiction audit

## 72. Localization versus distributed cognition

Apparent conflict:
regions can be specialized, but cognition is distributed.

Resolution:
local selectivity can exist inside distributed networks.

Audit: **compatible**.

## 73. Decoding versus representation caution

Apparent conflict:
decoders contain information about a feature, but representation claims are restricted.

Resolution:
decoding establishes sensitivity under tested conditions; stronger representation claims require specificity, invariance and/or functional-use evidence.

Audit: **compatible**.

## 74. Group process effects versus weak individual differences

Apparent conflict:
task fMRI can show robust process effects but poor trait prediction.

Resolution:
within-person experimental effect and between-person rank-order validity are different estimands.

Audit: **compatible**.

## 75. Lesions/stimulation versus distributed networks

Apparent conflict:
focal perturbation can cause deficits even when cognition is distributed.

Resolution:
distributed systems can contain necessary nodes, bottlenecks or connections.

Audit: **compatible**.

## 76. Neurotransmitter specificity versus multifunctionality

Apparent conflict:
specific receptor/circuit effects exist, but transmitter labels are nonspecific.

Resolution:
specificity emerges at transmitter × receptor × projection × circuit × state, not from transmitter name alone.

Audit: **compatible**.

## 77. Oscillatory communication versus coherence caution

Apparent conflict:
oscillations can contribute to routing, but coherence is not proof.

Resolution:
mechanism can be real while a common observational statistic remains non-identifying.

Audit: **compatible**.

## 78. LTP evidence versus LTP≠memory

Apparent conflict:
synaptic potentiation can be causally important for specific memories, but LTP is not memory by definition.

Resolution:
causal evidence is local to circuit/task/manipulation; identity claims are broader.

Audit: **compatible**.

## 79. Adult neurogenesis existence versus cognitive uncertainty

Apparent conflict:
adult human neurogenesis has stronger cellular evidence, but cognitive role remains unresolved.

Resolution:
existence, maturation/integration and behavioral function are separate inferential steps.

Audit: **compatible**.

## 80. Plasticity versus homeostasis

Apparent conflict:
learning requires change, homeostasis resists change.

Resolution:
systems can combine selective change with stabilizing regulation.

Audit: **compatible**.

**No internal contradiction blocking NEU completion was found.**

# Part XIII — Red-team cases

## 81. Case A — amygdala activation during negative pictures

Supported:
the amygdala signal differs under the contrast.

Not established:
the person has more fear as a stable trait;
the amygdala is a fear center;
the signal is necessary or sufficient for fear.

## 82. Case B — prefrontal activation during reappraisal

Supported:
prefrontal systems participate in the task contrast.

Not established:
greater activation means greater personal regulation capacity.

The 2026 NIDER consortium directly warns against that transfer.

## 83. Case C — dopamine PET displacement after reward task

Supported only under validated tracer/challenge assumptions:
binding changed in a manner compatible with endogenous dopamine competition.

Not established:
exact synaptic dopamine concentration;
pleasure;
motivation;
reward sensitivity as a stable trait.

## 84. Case D — high EEG coherence during memory task

Supported:
signals show a statistical phase/amplitude relationship under the estimator.

Not established:
direct communication;
causal routing;
memory-specific mechanism.

## 85. Case E — increased cortical thickness after training

Supported:
pipeline-derived cortical-thickness estimate changed.

Not established:
new neurons;
new synapses;
adaptive structural growth.

## 86. Case F — a decoder predicts emotion labels at 85%

Need to test:
- label validity;
- leakage;
- nuisance variables;
- independent dataset;
- cross-person transport;
- specificity to emotion rather than arousal/task/motor features.

Accuracy alone is not a neural mechanism.

## 87. Case G — TMS over region X changes behavior

Supported:
the stimulation protocol causally perturbed a system in a way that changed behavior.

Not established:
region X exclusively implements the function;
effect was purely local;
direction of local neural change is known.

## 88. Case H — lesion network map overlaps across disorders

Could reflect:
- shared causal circuitry;
- properties of normative connectivity matrix;
- lesion-distribution structure;
- common network topology.

Shared map alone is not shared disorder mechanism.

## 89. Case I — adult human hippocampus contains immature neuronal populations

Supported:
cellular/multiomic evidence for adult progenitor/immature neuronal populations.

Not established:
their rate across all adults;
their survival/integration;
their causal contribution to a specific human memory ability.

## 90. Case J — a neural signature generalizes to one external cohort

Supported:
generalization to that tested cohort/distribution.

Not established:
universal transport to all populations, scanners, cultures or clinical groups.

# Part XIV — Integrated Neuroscience Inference Gate — 64 questions

### A. Construct and level

1. What exact psychological construct is being explained?
2. Is the construct behaviorally/psychometrically defined before neural interpretation?
3. What computation or information-processing operation is hypothesized?
4. What neural level is targeted: network, circuit, cell, synapse, receptor, molecule?
5. Is any level being silently substituted for another?
6. Is a lower biological level being treated as automatically more explanatory?
7. Are state, process, capacity and trait separated?
8. Is within-person inference separated from between-person inference?

### B. Measurement

9. What physical quantity does the method directly measure?
10. Is the measurement a proxy for the claimed mechanism?
11. What is the spatial scale?
12. What is the temporal scale?
13. Are physiological/non-neural contributions modeled?
14. Is pipeline dependence known?
15. Is reliability estimated for the exact derived measure?
16. Is reproducibility being confused with construct validity?

### C. Localization and networks

17. Is a region label being treated as a psychological ontology?
18. Are local specialization and distributed alternatives both considered?
19. Is network membership method/atlas dependent?
20. Could common input or indirect pathways explain connectivity?
21. Is functional connectivity being called causal?
22. Are disconnection/diaschisis relevant?
23. Is a social/affective/cognitive label being treated as context-exclusive?
24. Are spatial autocorrelation/null-model requirements satisfied?

### D. Representation and decoding

25. Does evidence establish sensitivity, specificity, invariance or downstream use?
26. Is above-chance decoding being called representation mechanism?
27. Could decoder labels contain nuisance structure?
28. Is subject/site leakage excluded?
29. Is cross-task/cross-condition generalization tested where needed?
30. Is the model externally validated?
31. Is dataset shift characterized?
32. Are competing representational/computational models discriminated?

### E. Causal evidence

33. Is the evidence associative, predictive, necessity-like, sufficient or perturbational?
34. Does temporal precedence actually constrain alternatives?
35. Are lesion-selection/co-damage confounds handled?
36. Is stimulation target engagement demonstrated?
37. Are sensory/peripheral stimulation effects controlled?
38. Is necessity being confused with sufficiency?
39. Is mediation intervention-aware?
40. Is causal evidence matched to the exact claim?

### F. Neurochemistry

41. Are transmitter, receptor, projection, region and timescale specified?
42. Is release separated from cell-body firing?
43. Is receptor availability separated from density/concentration?
44. Is occupancy separated from downstream efficacy?
45. Is PET displacement treated as tracer/challenge dependent?
46. Is dose separated from brain exposure/target engagement?
47. Are autoreceptors, biased signaling, polypharmacology and adaptation relevant?
48. Are peripheral measures being mistaken for local brain neurotransmission?

### G. Systems/plasticity

49. Which E/I quantity is actually measured?
50. Is oscillatory measurement separated from causal process?
51. Are source mixing, leakage, aperiodic and waveform alternatives considered?
52. Is synchrony being treated as communication?
53. Is LTP/LTD being identified with learning/memory?
54. Does a plasticity claim include persistence and behavioral specificity?
55. Is structural MRI being assigned a cellular mechanism?
56. Is myelin inference histologically/calibrationally justified?
57. Is neurogenesis existence separated from integration/function?
58. Are homeostatic/metabolic/glial constraints represented?

### H. Individual/generalization

59. Does the signal support individual ranking/classification rather than only a group effect?

# Part XVI — Evidence confidence map

## High-confidence practical boundaries

- psychological, computational and neural levels are distinct but mutually constraining;
- localization and distributed-network organization can coexist;
- region/network labels are not psychological ontologies;
- measurement proxies must not be renamed as mechanisms;
- fMRI BOLD is indirect and functional connectivity is not causal connectivity;
- EEG/MEG source inference remains an inverse problem;
- decoding demonstrates information/prediction, not mechanism by itself;
- representation claims require dimensions beyond mere sensitivity;
- process effects do not automatically provide individual-difference measures;
- external validation is essential for strong neuroimaging prediction claims;
- neurotransmitter labels are receptor/circuit/timescale heterogeneous;
- PET availability/occupancy/release and downstream efficacy are distinct;
- E/I, oscillatory, synaptic, structural, myelin and neurogenesis measures target different scales;
- LTP/LTD are not psychological learning/memory by definition;
- structural MRI cannot uniquely identify cellular remodeling;
- adult human hippocampal neurogenesis existence is better supported than its cognitive function;
- causal evidence must be matched to the claim;
- person-level neural inference requires psychometrics.

## Supported but context-sensitive

- specific regions can be functionally specialized;
- distributed representations can support cognition;
- lesions can provide strong necessity-like evidence;
- stimulation can causally perturb cognitive/affective systems;
- specific dopamine transients can support reward-learning computations;
- validated PET challenges can index relative transmitter release;
- oscillatory coordination can contribute to communication in defined circuits;
- synaptic potentiation can be necessary/sufficient for specific animal memories;
- neural signatures can generalize across independently tested samples;
- multiscale network architecture can combine specialization and integration.

## Not established as universal

- one-to-one mind–brain mappings;
- one canonical network for each psychological construct;
- one neural representation criterion based only on decoding;
- one stable brain signature of psychological capacity across people;
- one transmitter/function mapping;
- one E/I biomarker;
- one frequency–function dictionary;
- one LTP/LTD learning rule;
- one MRI metric that directly identifies cellular plasticity;
- one adult-human-neurogenesis cognitive function;
- one neural biomarker transporting across populations by default.
