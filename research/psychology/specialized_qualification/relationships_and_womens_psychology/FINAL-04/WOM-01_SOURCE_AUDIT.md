## 2. Findings requiring correction or explicit extension

R1 — Distinguish multivariate discrimination, individual trait inference and practical relevance. Hofmann Table1 and results distinguish instrument-level and cross-country targets. Aggregate AUC=.86 is neither 86% correct classification nor probability of an individual's unmeasured trait. Item-level models outperform domain-level on average; ordering between adjacent levels is not universal, and Egypt's rounded equality .72/.72 should not be called a confirmed numerical loss. Age ranges are 10–99 for the two original IPIP samples and 18–119 for HEXACO; these are reported ranges, not validated age values or representative adults. Additional counterpoint Eagly & Revelle https://doi.org/10.1177/17456916211046006 (indexed primary abstract/extract) explicitly explains coexistence of small individual contrasts and larger multivariate distances. Do not infer that group differences are irrelevant just because univariate means overlap; demonstrate incremental validity on the actual target and with measured features, not reverse classification. Under equal-variance independent normal draws, d=.25 gives overlap ~90.05% and rank probability ~57.02%; d=.35 overlap ~86.11%, rank probability ~59.77%; d=.40 overlap ~84.15%, rank probability ~61.14%. These are illustrative theoretical distributions, not observed population or individual probabilities. Root cause: original quantitative illustration sound but lacks explicit conditional predictive utility and contrary synthesis. Retest original formula and multivariate results; no universal female type follows.

R2 — Confirmed data lineage and denominator correction. Hofmann Table1 original IPIP-NEO-300 N307313 and IPIP-NEO-120 N619150 sum exactly 926463; Temizyürek methods describe original combined IPIP-NEO N926463 and Johnson 2014. This confirms shared source lineage beyond earlier 'possible' overlap; exact row overlap after filtering or stratification not determined without matching IDs. Do not count the two analyses as independent replication of underlying population evidence. Temizyürek N831849 is FINAL dataset, not initial data as in WOM-01.1 E4. Reported exclusions 1268 unclear country, 58356 under16, 7869 missing answers, 17142 small countries leave 841828 before further country exclusions; difference to final 9979 consistent arithmetically with further exclusions, not independently verified counts per country. Text states 49 countries but specific results use 42; reason for seven-country difference not established from inspected passages, and figure rendering failed. Keep denominator as published per analysis, do not assume coverage of all 49 or convert 48% into a fraction of people. Article clarifies English administration, binary question labeled sex then analyzed as gender; neither actual gender identity nor sex-specific biology fully measured. Configural/model fit within groups ~52/53%, scalar fit varies, not blanket invalidity of facets or proof of equivalence. Require construct-specific MI, sensitivity to partial invariance and separate score vs latent construct. Root cause: imprecise sample stage and understated data dependency. Retest source methods and arithmetic; distinction between classification of recorded labels and latent mean comparisons preserved.

R3 — Causal share and scope of matched effects. 72/102=70.59% directionally consistent, 8/102=7.84% opposite, 22/102=21.57% neither; consistent-to-opposite ratio=9. The original authors' rule for opposite effects requires both absolute d>.10, not every opposite sign. The 102 are matched outcomes, not independent participants, and selection of significant power effects conditions on existing evidence. Not 71% of psychological sex/gender differences caused by power or 71% of variance explained. The review of Herlitz likewise distinguishes national indicators from causal explanation. Addition: age moderation study https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42095888/ (indexed original abstract only, direct open cache miss) N3038567 aged10–65 across68 countries, reports age moderation and cross-national variability; cross-sectional age comparisons not within-person developmental change; possible dataset overlap with IPIP studies not verified. Use actual exposures, chronology and alternative pathways; country/region or birth cohort cannot serve as individual's actual socialization history. Root cause: existing guardrails correct, explicit denominators, criterion and new age evidence extend them. No new causal share claimed.

R4 — SWAN model-comparison ambiguity. Original WOM-01.2 correctly gives p=.5203 for reported menopausal status coefficient and limits generalization. Direct results additionally report likelihood-ratio p<.00001 for adding menopausal status to adjusted age model, then omission from final model. These two numbers concern potentially different tests and need not logically contradict; however specification, degrees of freedom, identical analysis sample and coefficient table were not provided in the inspected passage (coefficient data not shown). Cannot reconcile whether joint or other fit improvements occur, nor establish a null overall stage effect. Preserve both results; restrict conclusion to no significant reported stage coefficient in the stated adjusted analysis, NOT evidence of absence of any menopause-related change. Specific New Jersey trajectory has only five available visits; no generalization from site trend to person. Perceived stress is not physiological stress or general emotionality; author discussion references other SWAN work, shared cohort not independent until confirmed. Carry this restriction to WOM-05/06. Root cause: prior ledger omitted model-comparison result. Retest direct Results and Discussion, no alteration of broader heterogeneity principle.

R5 — Method-sensitive comparisons and inference of bias. Buck Table6 Study2 trait/state point-biserial correlations with recorded gender: openness -.13/.06; conscientiousness -.16/-.05; agreeableness -.26/.00; emotional stability .40/.12; extraversion .01/.02. Study1 older couples (partner dependence and shared context), unregistered analyses; trait multi-item vs state single-item, state openness absent. Study2 three-day observation, 71.6% women; limited occasions and convenience sampling; both measures self-report. Exact treatment of partner clustering and supplement deviations not audited. Equivalence margin |d|=.10 selected after initial analyses, mostly inconclusive; smaller state differences do not prove equivalence or isolate recall bias. Factor-score comparisons and raw scores are different; trait and state coverage and discriminant validity differ. A direct observation or independent informant measure can test convergence, but is not automatically infallible. Identify time scale and measure before interpreting discrepancies; use independent evidence and do not dismiss explicit report as stereotype solely because group method differs. Root cause: strengthen interpretation of measured discrepancies and dependence; no new biological or social causal claim. Retest primary text and Table6, retains original alternative explanations.

## 4. Revised transfer procedure
Define measured variable (sex-related trait, assigned/recorded category, self-identified gender, role, actual exposure, developmental transition, trait or state); select estimand (within-women variation, group contrast, within-person trajectory, multivariate prediction or causal mechanism); check sample age, site, culture, selection, measurement, shared lineage, effect/dispersion and uncertainty; distinguish absence of evidence from equivalence; use meaningful comparator and actual predictive target; require additional identification for biological/social causal shares; conditional group evidence may inform a hypothesis, but individual inference requires specific evidence and competing explanations under IND-03. Never use a group category as proof of a person's motives, needs, choices or preferences. Within-women trajectory studies are useful independently of male comparison. No clinical or psychophysiological conclusion implied. Other original SPQ units unchanged; transfer limitations documented here for their later audit.
