# 3. Core taxonomy decision: PQI is not an atomic design

St. Pierre is explicit:

> Post Qualitative Inquiry is not another qualitative methodology.

In the strict formulation it:

- does not begin with a pre-existing humanist social-science methodology;
- does not have standard research designs;
- does not have a predefined research process;
- does not have canonical “data collection methods”;
- does not have standard coding/thematic-analysis procedures;
- does not aim to represent a stable real via a separate representation.

### RES-02 decision

Do **not** create:

`study_design = Post-Qualitative Inquiry`

as if it were parallel to:

- phenomenology;
- grounded theory;
- ethnography;
- case study.

Instead create a cross-cutting layer:

`inquiry_orientation = post-qualitative / posthuman-new-materialist / poststructural`

plus exact enacted practices.

# 4. Strict St. Pierre PQI vs broader field usage

The contemporary literature contains a real tension.

## Strict St. Pierre position

PQI:

- begins with poststructuralist onto-epistemology;
- refuses pre-existing social-science methodology;
- treats conventional humanist qualitative methodology as incompatible;
- refuses “qualitative methodology with a twist.”

Under this strict definition:

> a conventional interview study cannot simply add Deleuze/Barad after coding and become PQI.

## Broader sport/health/education usage

Current field literature includes labels such as:

- post-qualitative visual ethnography;
- post-qualitative-informed Collective Memory Work;
- a/r/tography in post-qualitative collections;
- poststructural discourse analysis within a PQI volume;
- affective inquiry;
- assemblage thinking.

### Project decision

Route separately:

1. **STRICT PQI**
   - explicit refusal of pre-existing qualitative methodology;
   - theory/concepts first;
   - no conventional design identity as parent.

2. **POST-QUALITATIVE-INFORMED / POSTHUMAN-INFORMED HYBRID**
   - a conventional or named methodology remains identifiable;
   - posthuman/new-materialist/poststructural concepts materially reconfigure it.

### Confidence

**HIGH** that the distinction is needed.

**MODERATE-HIGH** that this two-route solution is the most stable project representation across the heterogeneous field.

# 5. “Postqualitative” is not one philosophical school

PQI literature draws on multiple philosophical lineages, including:

- poststructuralism;
- posthumanism;
- new materialism;
- feminist materialism;
- Deleuzian philosophy;
- Foucauldian thought;
- Derridean deconstruction;
- Baradian agential realism;
- Harawayan relational thinking;
- Spinozist affect;
- new empiricisms.

### Critical rule

Do not code:

> postqualitative = Baradian

or:

> postqualitative = Deleuzian

by default.

The exact philosophical lineage matters because these traditions are not fully interchangeable.

Monforte & Smith's sport/exercise introduction gives three broad propositions:

# 7. New materialism is important but not mandatory in every PQI

**PQI ≠ new materialism only.**

# 8. Posthuman subject

Conventional humanist qualitative research often assumes:

- autonomous person;
- stable individual experience;
- person as origin of meaning;
- language as report of that experience.

Posthuman/postqualitative inquiry can instead treat subjectivity as:

- relational;
- distributed;
- emergent;
- constituted through material/discursive relations;
- not exclusively human.

### Example

Instead of asking:

> What does the parent think about physical activity?

the inquiry may ask:

> What parent-child-app-space-time-discourse assemblage makes a particular physical-activity possibility?

---

# 9. Decentering the human does not mean deleting humans

A common misunderstanding is:

> posthuman = humans do not matter.

The stronger claim is:

- human agency is not the only agency;
- humans do not exist independently of material, social, technological and ecological relations.

### Sport/PA examples

Potential consequential elements:

- body;
- smartphone;
- tracker;
- weather;
- playground;
- child;
- parent;
- schedule;
- policy discourse;
- fatigue;
- road;
- dog;
- equipment;
- app notifications.

### Critical rule

Do not treat nonhuman objects as decorative context.

In posthuman inquiry they may participate in constituting the phenomenon.

The ontology is often anti-dualist.

Common challenged separations include:

- subject / object;
- mind / body;
- human / nonhuman;
- nature / culture;
- researcher / researched;
- matter / meaning;
- data / theory;
- intervention / context.

### Project implication

The object of inquiry can be:

- relation;
- assemblage;
- phenomenon;
- worlding;
- becoming;
- intra-action;

rather than an isolated individual.

# 11. Assemblage

“Assemblage” is common but should not become a buzzword field.

An assemblage-oriented inquiry can attend to temporary configurations of:

- people;
- bodies;
- affects;
- technologies;
- discourses;
- spaces;
- institutions;
- objects;
- practices.

### Critical rule

Writing:

> “the gym assemblage”

does not establish assemblage thinking.

The study should show how elements:

- connect;
- disconnect;
- constrain;
- enable;
- transform one another.

# 13. Theory comes before or through inquiry, not after “data collection”

Strict PQI rejects the conventional sequence:

question  
→ collect data  
→ code  
→ themes  
→ discuss with theory.

Instead theory/concepts can be active throughout:

- shaping what can be noticed;
- changing the object;
- changing the researcher;
- changing writing;
- changing what counts as an event.

### Critical rule

Theory is not merely a literature-review frame placed around otherwise conventional analysis.

---

# 14. Thinking with theory / concepts

Jackson & Mazzei describe “plugging in” as a way of:

- using theory to think with materials;
- using materials to think with theory;
- resisting coding as the default reduction mechanism.

### Project classification

**thinking-with-theory / plugging-in = analytic-conceptual practice**

not a standalone parent methodology.

### Extraction

Record:

- concept(s) used;
- theorist(s);
- how concept changed reading;
- how material changed concept;
- whether result was predetermined by theory.

---

# 15. Theory should not become a deductive codebook by another name

A failure mode is:

1. choose Deleuze/Barad/Foucault;
2. create pre-labelled categories;
3. place quotations under categories;
4. call this “thinking with theory.”

### Critical distinction

Thinking-with-concepts should do more than classify.

It should generate:

- new relations;
- altered questions;
- conceptual movement;
- unexpected readings;
- changed object.

---

# 16. Refusal of conventional data

Strict St. Pierre PQI questions the idea that:

> data already exist outside the researcher and can be collected.

The term “data collection” presupposes:

- separation of knower and known;
- stable entities;
- pre-existing information waiting to be retrieved.

### Project rule

When authors reject “data” language, do not force it into the extraction.

Use broader fields:

- empirical materials;
- encounters;
- traces;
- stories;
- events;
- artifacts;
- practices;
- assemblages;
- generated relations.

---

# 17. Broader PQI literature may still say “data”

Sport/health PQI is pragmatically heterogeneous.

Beggan's intergenerational PA work used:

- participant stories;
- app-related experience;
- reading/writing;
- creative encounters;
- observation;
- practitioner/parent/child engagements.

Other authors may still use:

- interview;
- transcript;
- data;
- theme

language while materially reframing them.

### Project rule

Terminology alone is insufficient.

Classify what the research **does**, not only what it calls materials.

# 18. “Un-methodology”

Beggan describes a postqualitative **(un)methodology**.

This should not be encoded as a standardized method called:

> un-methodology.

It signals refusal of predefined procedural closure.

### Critical rule

“Un-method” does not mean:

- no work;
- no decisions;
- no empirical engagement;
- no ethics;
- no transparency.

It means the research process is not governed by a pre-existing humanist method template.

---

# 19. Emergence does not excuse opacity

Even strict PQI must permit readers to understand:

- philosophical commitments;
- concepts used;
- empirical encounters;
- writing/making practices;
- how the object changed;
- what cuts/choices were made;
- what ethical consequences followed.

### Project quality rule

**anti-method ≠ anti-accountability.**

---

# 20. Diffraction

Diffraction is derived especially from:

- Haraway;
- Barad.

Rather than reflecting one thing against another to assess similarity/difference, diffraction attends to:

- patterns of difference;
- entanglement;
- intra-action;
- how relations produce effects.

### Project classification

`PQI/posthuman orientation + diffractive analytic practice`

not:

`parent design = diffraction study`.

---

# 21. Diffraction vs comparison

A simple comparison asks:

- how does A differ from B?

Diffractive reading asks more relational questions:

- what is produced when A is read through B?
- how do boundaries emerge?
- what differences matter?
- how do concepts/materials mutually reconfigure one another?

### Critical rule

Putting two theories side by side is not automatically diffraction.

---

# 22. Baradian intra-action

Barad's term **intra-action** differs from interaction.

## Interaction

Assumes entities already exist and then relate.

## Intra-action

Entities/boundaries emerge through relation.

### Project implication

A posthuman PA study may avoid assuming in advance:

- parent;
- child;
- app;
- environment;
- intervention

as completely separate units.

Their roles can emerge through the research apparatus.

---

# 23. Apparatus and cuts

In Baradian thinking, research apparatuses do not neutrally observe a pre-given world.

They participate in making boundaries and phenomena.

### Methodological consequence

Researchers must ask:

- what does this measurement/interview/app/category cut together-apart?
- what becomes visible?
- what becomes excluded?
- what reality does the intervention enact?

### Critical rule

Measurement is not necessarily passive representation.

It can participate in producing the object.

---

# 24. New materialism

New materialist inquiry commonly challenges:

- language-only accounts;
- passive matter;
- exclusively human agency.

It attends to:

- bodies;
- objects;
- infrastructures;
- technologies;
- environments;
- forces;
- affect;
- material-discursive relations.

### RES-02 decision

`new_materialist_orientation` should be a separate field.

Do not infer:

# 25. Affect

Affect in postqualitative work is not necessarily synonymous with:

- emotion;
- self-reported feeling.

Current 2025/2026 sport/PE writing frames affect as:

- relation;
- intensity;
- force;
- capacity to affect / be affected;
- becoming.

### Project classification

`PQI/posthuman orientation + affective inquiry/conceptual practice`

not a new atomic study design.

---

# 26. Affective inquiry vs emotion research

Conventional emotion research can ask:

> What emotion did the athlete report?

Affective inquiry may instead ask:

- what intensities circulate?
- what bodies/materials are affected?
- what capacities emerge?
- what becomes possible or impossible?

### Critical rule

Affective inquiry should not be reduced to:

> interview coding for emotions.

# 27. Representation critique

Strict PQI questions representationalism:

> a real world exists first, then research creates a representation of it.

Instead research itself can be:

- performative;
- world-making;
- constitutive;
- productive.

### Project implication

Outputs may be described as:

- worldings;
- assemblages;
- refrains;
- provocations;
- lines of flight;
- conceptual creations;

rather than only:

- findings;
- themes.

# 30. Boundary with conventional thematic analysis

Conventional thematic analysis typically assumes:

- a dataset;
- coding;
- pattern/theme development;
- researcher interpretation.

A strict PQI does not begin from this standardized process.

### Hybrid possibility

A postqualitative-informed project may still use thematic work, but if coding/themes remain the controlling analytic engine, classify carefully.

### Project router

Ask:

> Did theory/concepts materially reorganize what inquiry was and could do, or were they added after conventional thematic analysis?

# 31. Boundary with Situational Analysis

D19 Situational Analysis shares several features with PQI:

- poststructural influence;
- human/nonhuman elements;
- material/discursive elements;
- complexity;
- relationality;
- silences.

But SA retains:

- a named methodology;
- a defined genealogy;
- recognizable map families;
- systematic mapping/memoing;
- identifiable analytic procedures.

### Taxonomy rule

**Situational Analysis remains its own methodology.**

A study does not become PQI because its situational map contains technologies/material objects.

---

# 32. PQI vs Situational Analysis: decisive question

If the study begins:

> We use Clarke's Situational Analysis and its maps

→ D19 route.

If the study begins from:

> poststructural/posthuman ontology and invents inquiry through concepts without a predefined methodology

→ strict PQI candidate.

If it uses SA but explicitly posthumanizes/reworks it:

→ hybrid:
- SA parent;
- postqualitative/posthuman orientation layer.

# 39. Strict incompatibility vs field pragmatism

# 40. Ethics: researcher is not outside the phenomenon

Posthuman/PQI ethics rejects the fantasy of a fully external neutral observer.

Research practices:

- make cuts;
- privilege relations;
- exclude possibilities;
- materialize some futures rather than others.

### Project implication

Ethical appraisal should include:

- what did the inquiry enable?
- what did it foreclose?
- what beings/materials were ignored?
- what relations were intensified?
- what consequences did the representation/intervention produce?

---

# 41. Response-ability

Haraway/Barad-inspired work often uses **response-ability**:

the capacity/obligation to respond within entangled relations.

This is different from ordinary “researcher responsibility” as rule compliance only.

### Project rule

Extract:

- ethical relation to human participants;
- ethical relation to nonhuman/material worlds where relevant;
- consequences of cuts/assemblages;
- downstream effects of speculative experimentation.

---

# 42. Ethics remains concrete

Relational/posthuman ethics does not replace:

- consent;
- privacy;
- safeguarding;
- data security;
- clinical risk management;
- community governance.

### Critical rule

Philosophical ethics must not be used to bypass ordinary participant protection.

A study can require both:

**institutional ethics + relational/posthuman ethical accountability.**

---

# 43. Speculative experimentation can create risk

Beggan's 2024 physical-activity paper explicitly notes that:

- lines of flight;
- speculative intervention relations

can be politically and ethically risky.

### Project implication

Innovation is not automatically emancipatory.

Researchers should inspect:

- who gains capacity?;
- who loses capacity?;

# 62. Practical classification algorithm

## Route A — strict PQI

Use when the study:

- explicitly identifies PQI;
- starts from poststructural/posthuman onto-epistemology;
- refuses a pre-existing humanist qualitative methodology;
- treats theory/concepts as constitutive;
- challenges conventional data/analysis/representation categories.

Store:

`PQI_STRICT`.

## Route B — postqualitative-informed hybrid

Use when:

- a named methodology remains;
- posthuman/new-materialist/poststructural theory materially reconfigures it.

Examples:

- CMW + postqualitative-informed;
- visual ethnography + postqualitative;
- a/r/tography + posthuman orientation.

Store:

`PQI_INFORMED_HYBRID + parent methodology`.

## Route C — posthuman/new-materialist qualitative study

Use when:

- new materialist/posthuman theory is central;
- authors do not claim/refuse methodology in the strict PQI sense.

Store:

`POSTHUMAN_NEW_MATERIALIST_ORIENTATION + parent design`.

## Route D — vocabulary-only

If conventional methods remain unchanged and posthuman concepts appear only decoratively:

do **not** elevate to PQI route.
