## 1. What makes a review systematic

A systematic review is not defined by:
- many references;
- “meta-analysis” in title;
- journal prestige.

Core features:
1. explicit question;
2. pre-specified methods/protocol;
3. reproducible eligibility criteria;
4. systematic search;
5. transparent study selection;
6. structured data extraction;
7. risk-of-bias assessment;
8. planned synthesis;
9. transparent interpretation;
10. updateability.

Cochrane emphasizes minimizing bias through pre-specified questions and methods.

## 2. Protocol

Protocol should specify before seeing full results:

1. question/PICO;
2. eligibility;
3. study designs;
4. outcomes;
5. search plan;
6. risk-of-bias tool;
7. synthesis method;
8. heterogeneity analysis;
9. subgroup/sensitivity analyses;
10. certainty framework.

Protocol reduces post-hoc decision-making.

Registration/publication also:
- improves transparency;
- reduces duplication;
- records deviations.

But registration alone does not guarantee quality.

## 3. Search strategy

A good search aims to identify all eligible evidence reasonably possible.

Need:
1. multiple relevant databases;
2. appropriate controlled vocabulary + keywords;
3. date limits justified;
4. language restrictions justified;
5. trial registries/grey literature where relevant;
6. citation searching when useful;
7. complete search dates.

Search quality matters because missing studies can bias conclusions.

## 4. Search is question-specific

Hypertrophy RCT review may need:
- MEDLINE/PubMed;
- SPORTDiscus;
- Embase;
- Scopus/Web of Science.

Rare harms may additionally need:
- registries;
- observational databases;
- case literature.

Qualitative ADHD adherence review needs databases appropriate to psychology/behavior.

No universal database list.

## 5. Study selection

Best practice:
- eligibility criteria explicit;
- ideally independent duplicate screening or robust equivalent QC;
- reasons for full-text exclusion recorded.

Why:
selection can drift toward desired evidence.

PRISMA flow diagram helps reconstruct:
1. identified;
2. deduplicated;
3. screened;
4. full-text assessed;
5. excluded;
6. included.

## 6. Data extraction

Need standardized extraction for:
1. participants;
2. intervention;
3. comparator;
4. outcome definitions;
5. timing;
6. effect estimates;
7. variance;
8. adherence;
9. funding/conflicts;
10. risk-of-bias data.

Duplicate/verified extraction reduces transcription and interpretation errors.

## 7. Risk of bias of included studies

Review quality depends heavily on underlying evidence.

A meta-analysis of biased trials can produce precise biased result.

Need:
1. appropriate RoB tool;
2. result/outcome-specific assessment;
3. incorporate RoB into synthesis/interpretation.

Not enough:
> “Most studies scored 7/10 quality.”

See RES-04.

## 8. Synthesis plan

Before pooling, define:
1. which studies belong together;
2. effect measure;
3. fixed/random framework if meta-analysis;
4. handling multiple arms/outcomes;
5. heterogeneity;
6. sensitivity analysis;
7. subgroup analysis;
8. missing data.

Meta-analysis should not be automatic.

## 9. When meta-analysis should not be done

Potential reasons:
1. interventions too different;
2. outcomes not comparable;
3. designs estimate different quantities;
4. extreme clinical/methodological heterogeneity;
5. insufficient usable data;
6. severe bias makes pooled number misleading.

Systematic review can be valid without meta-analysis.

Narrative/synthesis-without-meta-analysis must still be systematic, not vote-counting.

## 10. Vote counting is weak

Counting:
- 5 significant studies;
- 3 nonsignificant studies

is not valid synthesis.

Why:
- ignores sample size;
- effect magnitude;
- precision;
- direction;
- bias.

A small underpowered “negative” study and large precise study should not count equally.

## 11. PRISMA 2020

PRISMA is a **reporting guideline**.

It improves transparency about:
- methods;
- search;
- selection;
- results;
- bias;
- synthesis.

But PRISMA compliance does not prove methodological quality.

PRISMA itself explicitly says it is not intended to assess review conduct/quality.

Project rule:
**PRISMA ≠ quality score.**

## 12. AMSTAR 2

AMSTAR 2 is appraisal tool for systematic reviews of interventions.

Critical domains include:
1. protocol before review;
2. adequate search;
3. justification of excluded studies;
4. risk-of-bias assessment;
5. appropriate meta-analysis;
6. consideration of RoB in interpretation;
7. publication-bias assessment.

AMSTAR 2 explicitly recommends:
- do not sum items into total numeric score;
- judge critical flaws.

## 13. ROBIS

ROBIS assesses risk of bias in systematic reviews.

Useful when question is:
> could review process itself have biased conclusions?

Different from:
- quality of reporting;
- quality of primary studies.

Project may use:
- AMSTAR 2 for rapid methodological appraisal;
- ROBIS for important/high-stakes review when needed.

## 14. Review recency

Fitness evidence changes.

Check:
1. last search date;
2. publication date;
3. major new RCTs;
4. updated guideline/position stand;
5. newer systematic review.

A 2020 review can still be valuable mechanistically but may be outdated for current prescription.

Project rule:
record **search end date**, not only publication year.

## 15. Overlapping reviews

Multiple meta-analyses can include same primary trials.

Do not treat them as independent confirmations.

Need:
1. compare included studies;
2. compare search dates;
3. compare PICO;
4. compare methods;
5. identify newest/highest-quality synthesis.

Umbrella reviews require special attention to overlap.

## 16. Review conclusion can exceed data

Red flags:
1. “proves” from low-certainty evidence;
2. ignores CI;
3. conclusion based on subgroup;
4. ignores high heterogeneity;
5. ignores RoB;
6. uses acute proxies for chronic recommendation;
7. claims equivalence from nonsignificance;
8. extrapolates population beyond evidence.

Always inspect results, not only abstract conclusion.

## 17. Funding/conflicts

Potential conflict can occur at:
- primary-study level;
- review level.

Conflict does not automatically invalidate research.

Need transparency and method appraisal.

AMSTAR 2 includes funding/conflict considerations.

## 18. Grey literature / unpublished evidence

Purpose:
reduce publication bias.

Potential sources:
- trial registries;
- theses;
- conference records;
- preprints where appropriate;
- regulatory databases.

But unpublished sources may have incomplete methods/data.

Inclusion strategy must be pre-defined and quality-assessed.

## 19. Language restrictions

English-only restrictions can miss evidence.

Impact varies by field/question.

If used, should be justified and noted as limitation.

## 20. Duplicate publication

One trial may appear in:
- conference abstract;
- main paper;
- secondary analysis;
- follow-up paper.

Review must link reports to one study to avoid double-counting participants.

## 21. Outcome multiplicity in reviews

Review authors can selectively choose:
- time point;
- muscle site;
- outcome metric;
- analysis.

Need pre-specified hierarchy.

This is review-level analogue of selective reporting.

## 22. Exercise-science review pitfalls

### 22.1 Mixing trained/untrained without plan
May obscure effect modification.

### 22.2 Mixing muscle-thickness/lean-mass/MRI volume as “hypertrophy”
May create construct heterogeneity.

### 22.3 Pooling unmatched interventions
“High volume” may differ in load/failure/frequency simultaneously.

### 22.4 Treating within-group change as controlled effect
Invalid comparative synthesis.

### 22.5 Multiple effects from same participants
Needs dependency handling.

### 22.6 Including acute studies in chronic outcome review
Time-horizon mismatch.

### 22.7 Ignoring adherence
Prescription effect vs completed-dose effect differ.

## 23. Systematic review appraisal card

For each key review:

1. Exact question/PICO?
2. Protocol/registration?
3. Search end date?
4. Databases?
5. Grey/unpublished search?
6. Eligibility pre-specified?
7. Duplicate/verified screening?
8. Excluded-study reasons?
9. Duplicate extraction?
10. Appropriate RoB tool?
11. RoB integrated into conclusions?
12. Effect measures appropriate?
13. Pooling justified?
14. Heterogeneity addressed?
15. Publication bias considered?
16. Certainty/GRADE?
17. Conflicts/funding?
18. New evidence since search?
19. Direct applicability to Ilya?
20. Overall confidence.

## 24. Review hierarchy for project

When several reviews exist:

Prefer:
1. closest Question Card;
2. current search date;
3. stronger methods;
4. proper RoB/certainty;
5. transparent data;
6. direct outcomes/population.

Do not select merely:
- newest;
- largest;
- most favorable conclusion.

## 25. Using systematic reviews as navigation

A high-quality review is useful for:
1. map of primary evidence;
2. pooled estimate;
3. heterogeneity;
4. gaps;
5. important primary trials.

For high-impact personal decisions:
review may be starting point, then inspect key primary studies.

## 26. Project rules

1. “Systematic review” label is not automatic trust.
2. PRISMA is reporting, not quality appraisal.
3. AMSTAR 2 items should not be summed to one score.
4. Review protocol/search/RoB are critical.
5. Meta-analysis is optional, not required.
6. Do not vote-count significance.
7. Check search end date.
8. Check overlap between reviews.
9. Inspect whether conclusion matches pooled estimate/CI/certainty.
10. For key claims, trace back to primary studies when necessary.

## 27. Confidence

### HIGH
- pre-specified protocol/search/eligibility/synthesis reduce review-process bias;
- PRISMA is a reporting guideline, not quality tool;
- AMSTAR 2 emphasizes critical domains and rejects total-score approach;
- meta-analysis is not mandatory for a systematic review;
- primary-study risk of bias must affect interpretation.
