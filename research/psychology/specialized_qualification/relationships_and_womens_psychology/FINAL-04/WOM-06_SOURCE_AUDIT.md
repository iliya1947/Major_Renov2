## 2. Corrections and rechecks
R1 — Measurement and outcome specificity. Jang original Methods distinguish five-phase and three-phase definitions, natural cycles vs OC, excluded diagnosed/anovulatory participants from natural-cycle robustness subset, hormone/LH confirmation rather than calendar/BBT alone. 517 five-phase comparisons; nominal aggregate memory result did not survive multiple-comparison correction. 730 comparisons not independent persons, no equivalence margin/individual prediction. The null robust finding concerns objective cognitive tasks, not effort, symptoms, affect, or desire. Earlier WOM-02 Stern/Koval/Blake https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39804343/ newly checked indexed abstract N757 diary and N257 hormone-assay laboratory sample: possible cycle-linked personality states/affect, not durable trait change. No precise hormone coefficients or exact shared cohort with Stern 2021 mate preference N257 verified; same N and author warrant checking, not proof of overlap. Retain earlier FINAL-04.6 corrected bibliographic meta-analysis/contrary evidence. No universal inference from a person's phase to motive. Root cause: original robustness description lacked explicit eligible population and multiple-comparison scale. Retest directly inspected PLOS Methods/Results.

R2 — Hormone sensitivity is heterogeneous even in selected responders. Schmidt 2017 original indexed text says approximately two thirds responded to suppression, and 7/12 of suppression responders showed symptom provocation with addback. Thus not all 12 had categorical recurrence. No conversion to population prevalence. Additional Schmidt replication/extension https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41030005/ and indexed full-text https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13702036/: 10/34 suppression responders did not meet recurrence criterion during either hormone addback; progesterone vs estradiol contrasts significant for irritability/mood swings, not anxiety/sadness in indexed results. This supports heterogeneity and outcome-specific sensitivity, not universal progesterone effect or a single causal molecular pathway. Exact sample flow, overlap with historical cohort, and full protocol not audited; same research group, do not assume independence. Bloch's 5/8 vs0/8 selected history groups cannot estimate general postpartum incidence. Combined experimental exposure and withdrawal, vulnerability group not randomized. No diagnostic or treatment advice. Root cause: earlier group-level recurrence summary could imply every responder; added specific nonresponse and replication lead. Retest original indexed Results and arithmetic 7/12,10/34,5/8.

R3 — Evidence certainty and conflicting contraception outcomes. de Wit explicitly grades every comparison very low certainty because of bias, indirectness and imprecision; prior note's 'uncertain' insufficiently precise. Outcomes principally self-reported symptom change across about three cycles; limited first-time users and people with previous negative experiences. No proof of absence of harm or equivalent regimens. 2026 diagnosis meta-analysis reports RR1.31 CI[1.07,1.61], I2=94.18%, moderate certainty as rated by authors. The two reviews differ in treatment/exposure, endpoint, duration, population and design; not direct conflicting causal estimates. Additional Swedish register cohort https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34837324/ indexed original abstract: adolescent combined OC RR.96 CI[.93,.98], progestogen-only pill RR1.13 CI[1.07,1.19] under their model; other formulations different. Danish register cohort https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27680324/ indexed abstract: associations and reference-group sensitivity; different users and nonusers, initiation/prescription vs diagnosis. Cohort overlap with 2026 meta-analysis not verified, not independent replication assumed. Relative risk not percentage-point increase or individual predicted risk. No drug selection advice. Root cause: certainty and formulation/reference-comparator limitations not explicit enough. Retest direct Cambridge Discussion and primary abstracts, preserve both average trial and observational evidence.

R4 — Model-derived perinatal trajectories vs actual onset. Kee N11563 across seven cohorts (8704+953+710+329+350+86+431=11563); dominant ALSPAC75.27% of participants, but cohorts analyzed separately. Three K-means cluster patterns are not validated individual diagnoses, prevalence of onset types or proof all trajectories stable. Different assessment timing, instruments, exclusions of medication users and absence of global-south cohorts restrict applicability. Prior symptom history, new onset and episodic course remain possible; sparse observations and cluster averages can hide them. Review PMID35803394 supports prior depression and contextual correlates but exact comparative trajectory prevalence not verified. Do not conflate mean-level longitudinal stability, stable rank, individual trajectory and clinical onset; EPDS/CES-D are screening measures. Pregnancy cognitive meta-analysis and prospective pre-pregnancy study have different reference populations/windows; no universal cognitive decline or inferred biological impairment. Root cause: strengthen limitations of cluster-based interpretation and cohort dominance. Retest JAMA limitations and reported cohort counts. No causal attribution or clinical threshold advice.

R5 — SWAN and trial outcome/precision restrictions. WOM-01 unresolved SWAN stress ambiguity freshly confirmed via original indexed text https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6027744/: LRT p<.00001 adding menopausal status, reported status variable p=.5203 (data not shown), omitted from final model. Potentially different tests; cannot reconcile without specification/dfs/coefficients, no global null conclusion. Depression vs perceived stress are distinct outcomes; clinical depression SWAN Pittsburgh N221 and FMP N1582 share SWAN source, exact row overlap not verified. FMP analytic sample1582 vs1720 excluded out of3302 (47.91% included), selection limits inference; annually measured hormones cannot identify rapid fluctuations. Gordon randomized N86+86, reported 32.3 vs17.3% crossing CES-D16, absolute descriptive difference15pp, treatment/placebo risk ratio approx.536, placebo/treatment odds ratio approx2.28 from rounded percentages; reported OR2.5 is model result, not automatically same unadjusted quantity. Endpoint symptom threshold at least once across visits, not diagnosed depression or lifetime risk. 76% attended all seven visits, differential ascertainment and missingness need assessment; one acute DVT in active group, two severe MDD events in placebo as reported, no overall safety inference. Direct stage interaction p=.03 for symptom-score endpoint, not demonstrated universal stage-dependent effects. Abstract late-transition beta-.9 SEM.3 p=.23 appears inconsistent for a simple Wald test, actual statistic/coding not established. Demographic reporting discrepancy: abstract 70 African American (19%) is arithmetically inconsistent with N172; Table1 active group column shows70 white+70 African American+3 other=143 despite N86. This may be transcription/display error; actual composition not inferred or silently corrected. No full corrigendum or supplement audit performed. Root cause: prior note omitted reporting inconsistency, follow-up coverage and endpoint distinction. Retest JAMA direct abstract/table/adverse-events and SWAN indexed original; preserve bounded evidence of assigned regimen on measured endpoint, no clinical recommendation.

R6 — Sleep, phase validity and biomarkers. Direct sleep-rhythm Methods assign a provisional ovulation day to four low-dose pill users even though ovulation usually suppressed; six other participants have LH-based timing, which is indirect evidence. Phase-labeled rhythm differences cannot be taken as actual ovulation-related hormone changes in all ten. Sleep duration vs timing vs rhythm robustness are distinct; one-year per-person centering does not make repeated days independent or establish hormone mechanism; no direct device validation audit. Schmalenberger cardiac meta-analysis 37 studies/47 effects, average decrease d=-.39, some opposite results; physiological change not emotional change. Progesterone association person-centered observational, table sample visits differ by analysis (112 phase table vs150 hormone regression table); use model-specific denominator, do not infer missingness reason or causal mediation. HRV guideline requires consideration of recording epoch, co-registered respiration, posture/activity, sensor measurement and interpretation; PPG-derived pulse variability not automatically equivalent to ECG-based HRV across settings. Laborde cautions against automatic adjustment removing target variance. Stress cortisol counts across studies not participant proportions, TSST peak/recovery timing not universal stress intensity. Palmer mixed ages/sexes sleep experiments support effects on selected outcomes, not female-specific effect or universal irritability; no new moderator estimate verified. Root cause: mixed physiological exposure labels and insufficiently explicit signal/construct distinction. Retest directly read sleep Methods and indexed original HRV abstracts/guidelines.

## 4. Revised procedure
Identify actual outcome, voluntarily and reliably known exposure, method and timing, population, individual baseline, comparator and uncertainty. Separate bleeding from ovulation, assigned phase from measured hormones, hormone concentrations from change and sensitivity, role transition from endocrine exposure, symptom from function/diagnosis and motive/choice. Separate person-mean and within-person deviation, distinguish state from trait and actual opportunity. Consider sleep, pain, health, context and timing; identify competing pathways and avoid arbitrary adjustment of mediators or colliders. Respect explicit choices regardless of physiological hypothesis; never infer reproductive status, feelings, preferences or consent from social cues or a single biomarker. No covert tracking or clinical diagnosis/treatment. Maintain individual evidence, alternatives and revision triggers under IND-03. Physiological hypotheses can inform analysis only where appropriate evidence exists; no universal female mechanism.
