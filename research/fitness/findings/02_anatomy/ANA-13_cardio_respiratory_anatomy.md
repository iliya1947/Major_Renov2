# ANA-13 — Базовая cardio-respiratory anatomy

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-18**

## 0. Decision

Создать structural cardio-respiratory map для последующего exercise physiology без:

- artery/vein = oxygenation simplification;
- incomplete heart-flow model;
- exhaustive angiology;
- airway = gas-exchange surface simplification;
- anatomy → physiology/diagnosis overclaim.

## 1. Canonical circulation model

### Artery
Carries blood **away from the heart**.

### Vein
Carries blood **toward the heart**.

Therefore:
- pulmonary arteries carry relatively deoxygenated blood from RV to lungs;
- pulmonary veins return oxygenated blood to LA.

Project rule:

**vessel class is defined by direction, not oxygenation.**

## 2. Systemic circulation

`LV → aortic valve → aorta → systemic arteries → arterioles → capillaries → venules → systemic veins → SVC/IVC → RA`

ANA-13 owns the route.

Pressure/flow regulation → PHY.

## 3. Pulmonary circulation

`RV → pulmonary valve → pulmonary trunk/arteries → pulmonary arterioles → alveolar capillaries → pulmonary veins → LA`

Pulmonary circulation is distinct from bronchial/systemic vascular supply to lung tissues.

## 4. Heart

Four chambers:
1. right atrium;
2. right ventricle;
3. left atrium;
4. left ventricle.

Four major valves:
1. tricuspid;
2. pulmonary;
3. mitral;
4. aortic.

### AV valves
- tricuspid;
- mitral;
- chordae/papillary apparatus.

### Semilunar valves
- pulmonary;
- aortic;
- no chordae/papillary apparatus.

Project rule:

**valves permit directional flow structurally; opening/closing is pressure-driven physiology.**

## 5. Great vessels

Project-relevant great vessels:

- aorta;
- superior vena cava;
- inferior vena cava;
- pulmonary trunk/arteries;
- pulmonary veins.

Canonical flow:

`SVC/IVC → RA → RV → pulmonary arteries → lungs → pulmonary veins → LA → LV → aorta`

## 6. Coronary-supply boundary

Right/left coronary arteries arise near the aortic root and supply myocardium.

Most cardiac venous return reaches RA through coronary sinus.

Project rule:

**blood inside heart chambers does not directly nourish myocardium.**

Detailed coronary pathology → MED.

## 7. Upper-limb vascular route

Major arterial route:

`subclavian → axillary → brachial → radial + ulnar → palmar networks`

Major venous organization:

### Deep
- radial/ulnar venae comitantes;
- brachial;
- axillary;
- subclavian;
- brachiocephalic;
- SVC.

### Superficial
- cephalic;
- basilic;
- median cubital connection.

Project rule:

**venous anatomy is not a single mirror-image copy of the arterial tree.**

## 8. Lower-limb vascular route

Major arterial route:

`external iliac → femoral → popliteal → anterior/posterior tibial + fibular systems → foot networks`

Major venous organization:

### Deep
- tibial/fibular;
- popliteal;
- femoral;
- iliac;
- IVC.

### Superficial
- great saphenous;
- small saphenous.

Perforating veins connect superficial and deep systems.

## 9. Capillary / microvascular level

Canonical sequence:

`arteriole → capillary network → venule`

Capillaries:
- thin endothelial exchange structures;
- associated basement membrane/pericytes;
- predominantly continuous in skeletal muscle.

Project rule:

**capillaries are exchange-level vessels, not miniature muscular arteries.**

Hyperemia/recruitment/exchange physiology → PHY.

## 10. Conducting vs respiratory airway

### Conducting portion
`nose/oral entry → pharynx → larynx → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → terminal bronchioles`

### Respiratory portion
- respiratory bronchioles;
- alveolar ducts;
- alveolar sacs/alveoli.

Project rule:

**not every airway segment performs gas exchange.**

## 11. Lungs

Right lung:
- superior;
- middle;
- inferior lobes.

Left lung:
- superior;
- inferior lobes;
- cardiac indentation/lingular region.

Each lung has:
- apex;
- base;
- costal/mediastinal/diaphragmatic surfaces;
- hilum/root.

Detailed bronchopulmonary-segment memorization is not required for current fitness use.

## 12. Alveolar–capillary interface

Gas-exchange interface structurally includes:
- alveolar epithelium;
- basement-membrane/interstitial interface;
- pulmonary capillary endothelium.

Type I pneumocytes form most thin exchange surface.

Type II pneumocytes are structurally distinct secretory alveolar cells.

Diffusion/surfactant physiology → PHY.

## 13. Dual lung blood supply

### Pulmonary circulation
Gas-exchange blood flow.

### Bronchial circulation
Systemic supply to airway/lung supporting tissues.

Project rule:

**lungs have dual vascular supply.**

## 14. Pleura

### Visceral pleura
Covers lung surface/fissures.

### Parietal pleura
Lines thoracic wall, diaphragm, mediastinum and cervical pleural region.

Between them:
- potential pleural cavity with lubricating fluid.

Right and left pleural cavities are anatomically separate.

Project rule:

**pleura is not lung parenchyma.**

## 15. Diaphragm

ANA-07 owns detailed trunk/pressure anatomy.

ANA-13 retains:
- dome-shaped musculotendinous thoracoabdominal partition;
- central tendon;
- principal inspiratory role;
- phrenic motor supply C3–C5.

Project rule:

**detailed diaphragm mechanics/fatigue belong to PHY/MEC.**

## 16. Intercostal muscles

Primary layers:
1. external;
2. internal;
3. innermost.

Broad role:
- span/stabilize intercostal spaces;
- contribute to rib-cage movement.

Project rule:

**“external = inspiration, internal = expiration” is too crude as a universal anatomical rule.**

## 17. Accessory respiratory muscles

Potential inspiratory contributors under higher demand include:
- sternocleidomastoid;
- scalenes;
- pectoral muscles;
- serratus anterior;
- other thoracic muscles depending task.

Forced-expiratory contributors can include:
- abdominal wall;
- rib-depressing thoracic muscles.

“Accessory” is a functional/task label.

## 18. Thoracic cage

Structural components:
- thoracic vertebrae;
- ribs;
- costal cartilages;
- sternum;
- thoracic joints;
- intercostal muscles;
- diaphragm.

ANA-03 owns detailed rib-cage/axial skeleton.

Project rule:

**thoracic cage is a deformable respiratory framework, not a rigid box.**

## 19. Thoracic organization

Useful gross map:

1. central mediastinum;
2. right pleural cavity;
3. left pleural cavity.

Mediastinum contains:
- heart/pericardium;
- great vessels;
- central airway/esophageal structures.

Pleural cavities contain lungs within pleural sacs.

## 20. Circulation–respiration integration

Blood path:

`systemic veins → RA → RV → pulmonary arteries → alveolar capillary bed → pulmonary veins → LA → LV → systemic arteries → tissue capillaries`

Air path:

`environment → conducting airways → respiratory bronchioles → alveoli`

Integration site:
- alveolar–capillary interface.

Project rule:

**ANA-13 establishes where circulation and respiration meet; transfer rates and control are physiology.**

## 21. Training-relevant landmarks

Useful vascular landmarks later include:
- axillary/brachial/radial/ulnar routes;
- cephalic/basilic/median-cubital veins;
- femoral/popliteal/tibial routes;
- great/small saphenous systems.

An anatomical landmark is not a vascular diagnosis.

## 22. Anatomy / physiology / medicine boundary

### ANA
- chambers;
- valves;
- vessels/routes;
- capillary structure;
- airways;
- lungs;
- pleura;
- respiratory muscles;
- thoracic framework.

### PHY/BIO
- cardiac output;
- stroke volume;
- heart-rate response;
- vascular resistance;
- exercise hyperemia;
- ventilation;
- V/Q;
- diffusion;
- O₂ transport/utilization.

### MED
- cardiovascular/pulmonary pathology;
- red flags;
- diagnosis/treatment.

## 23. Normal variation

Relevant variation includes:
- vessel branching;
- superficial veins;
- pulmonary veins;
- bronchial branching;
- lung fissures/lobes;
- heart orientation;
- respiratory-muscle variants.

Project rule:

**textbook anatomy is modal, not identical in every person.**

## 24. Canonical project model

1. Artery/vein = direction relative to heart.
2. Heart = four chambers + four valves + great vessels.
3. Systemic and pulmonary circuits are serially linked.
4. Major limb vascular routes are sufficient; exhaustive angiology is deferred.
5. Venous return uses interacting superficial/deep systems.
6. Capillaries are exchange-level vessels.
7. Conducting and respiratory airway portions are distinct.
8. Lungs have dual pulmonary + bronchial blood supply.
9. Pleura is a separate serous system around lungs.
10. Diaphragm is principal inspiratory muscle.
11. Intercostal/accessory muscles contribute task-dependently.
12. Thoracic cage is deformable mechanical framework.
13. Anatomy establishes structure; exercise responses remain PHY/BIO/MEC/MED/TST.
