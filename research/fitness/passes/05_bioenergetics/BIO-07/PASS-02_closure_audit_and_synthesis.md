## 3. Carbohydrate oxidation audit

PASS.

Carbohydrate supports both:

- substrate-level ATP resynthesis through glycolysis;
- oxidative ATP resynthesis through pyruvate → PDH → acetyl-CoA → TCA/OXPHOS.

The substrate source must remain resolved.

Distinct pools include:

- muscle glycogen;
- liver-derived/blood glucose;
- exogenous carbohydrate;
- carbohydrate-derived intermediates such as lactate.

Therefore:

**carbohydrate metabolism is not synonymous with anaerobic metabolism, and muscle glycogen use is not synonymous with total carbohydrate oxidation.**

No blocker.

---

## 4. Fat oxidation / beta-oxidation audit

PASS WITH CONTROL BOUNDARY.

Retain the multi-step chain:

`triglyceride lipolysis → FA availability/delivery → muscle uptake → intracellular partitioning → mitochondrial entry → beta-oxidation → acetyl-CoA/TCA/OXPHOS`.

The carnitine shuttle is important for long-chain fatty-acid mitochondrial entry.

However the audit rejects a one-site model in which CPT1 or carnitine alone is treated as a universal sole rate limiter.

Fat oxidation is controlled across:

- fatty-acid availability;
- transport;
- intracellular lipid handling;
- carnitine/fatty-acyl import;
- glycolytic/acetyl-CoA state;
- beta-oxidation/TCA interaction;
- respiratory demand/capacity.

Canonical rule:

**lipolysis ≠ plasma FFA ≠ uptake ≠ mitochondrial entry ≠ beta-oxidation ≠ whole-body fat oxidation.**

No blocker.

## 5. Protein/amino-acid contribution audit

PASS WITH QUANTITATIVE BOUNDARY.

The 2025 systematic review/meta-analysis PMID **40135445** included:

- 30 articles;
- 286 healthy adults;
- endurance exercise lasting at least 60 min;
- running, cycling or cross-country skiing.

Its pooled estimate:

- protein contribution = approximately **3.28%** of total exercise energy expenditure;
- protein oxidation rose during endurance exercise;
- oxidation rate was associated with intensity.

The closure audit does **not** promote 3.28% to a universal constant.

The exact value cannot be transferred automatically to:

- short resistance exercise;
- sprint exercise;
- severe energy deficit;
- different glycogen states;
- all training populations.

Canonical rule:

**protein/amino acids are usually a small/subsidiary but non-zero exercise fuel; exact contribution is task-, nutrition- and method-dependent.**

No blocker.

## 6. Intensity audit

PASS.

Across classic tracer work and current reviews:

- carbohydrate use generally rises as exercise intensity rises;
- relative fat contribution is greater at lower intensities;
- absolute fat oxidation often rises from low to moderate work rates;
- absolute fat oxidation then falls as intensity becomes high;
- carbohydrate becomes increasingly important as ATP demand/glycolytic flux rises.

Critical wording:

**the intensity effect is graded, not a binary fat-off/carbohydrate-on switch.**

No blocker.

## 7. Fatmax audit

PASS WITH STRONG METHOD BOUNDARY.

The 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis PMID **42166325** included 42 studies and found Fatmax occurred, on average, below LT/GET.

But the difference depended on:

- fitness;
- age/sex;
- nutritional status;
- protocol;
- exercise mode;
- study design;
- analytical method.

Therefore neither Fatmax nor crossover intensity is retained as one universal percentage of:

- VO2max;
- VO2peak;
- HRmax;
- threshold intensity.

Also:

**Fatmax ≠ LT/GET by definition**, even though they can be close under some protocols/populations.

No blocker.

---

## 8. Duration audit

PASS WITH TIME-DEPENDENCE BOUNDARY.

Classic tracer evidence shows that during prolonged submaximal exercise:

- plasma-derived substrate oxidation can increase over time;
- muscle glycogen contribution can decline;
- intramuscular substrate contribution can shift.

But the time course depends on:

- intensity;
- starting glycogen;
- carbohydrate ingestion;
- training status;
- exercise mode;
- environmental/physiological stress.

Therefore:

**there is no universal “after X minutes fat takes over” rule.**

Duration changes both:

1. macronutrient proportions;
2. source pools within a macronutrient.

No blocker.

---

## 9. Training-status audit

PASS WITH REFINED WORDING.

The audit confirms that the PASS-01 absolute-vs-relative distinction is essential, but one sentence needs refinement.

Evidence:

- PMID **10517772** found trained participants oxidized more fat at the same absolute workload and also higher absolute fat oxidation at matched relative intensity in that protocol.
- PMID **11350771** found endurance training reduced glucose flux at both post-training absolute and relative workloads.
- other longitudinal/cross-sectional studies show heterogeneous changes in relative fuel proportions when matching by %VO2max or other relative markers.

Therefore the canonical rule is not:

“training only increases fat use at the same absolute workload.”

Instead:

**endurance training modifies substrate-selection capacity and source-pool flux; both same-absolute and same-relative comparisons can show changes, but the direction/magnitude is protocol-, sex-, nutrition-, duration- and workload-dependent.**

Absolute workload and relative intensity must both be reported before interpreting a training-status comparison.

No blocker.

## 10. Glycogen-availability audit

PASS WITH SOURCE-POOL BOUNDARY.

Human randomized crossover evidence PMID **31095946** directly supports:

- lower starting muscle glycogen;
- lower endogenous carbohydrate oxidation;
- higher fat oxidation;
- preserved exogenous carbohydrate oxidation during carbohydrate feeding.

Meta-analysis PMID **29923148** supports baseline glycogen and exercise intensity as important determinants of exercise glycogen use.

Recent 2025/2026 studies strengthen the state-dependence model:

- PMID **40922559**: 3-day LCHF vs HCLF changed muscle glycogen, PFO and Fatmax;
- PMID **42801284**: glycogen depletion + low-CHO diet increased PFO/Fatmax without changing mitochondrial coupling control/efficiency.

Canonical rule:

**substrate availability can materially shift measured fuel selection without requiring a change in intrinsic mitochondrial function.**

No blocker.

---

## 11. Glycogen vs energy availability audit

PASS.

BIO-07 must not collapse:

- low carbohydrate availability;
- low glycogen;
- low energy availability.

These can coexist but are distinct exposures.

Current human evidence shows carbohydrate restriction can produce metabolic changes that are separable from energy restriction.

Detailed low-energy-availability physiology belongs MED/NUT/REC rather than BIO-07.

Canonical boundary:

**low glycogen ≠ low energy availability.**

No blocker.

---

## 12. Feeding-state audit

PASS WITH ACUTE/CHRONIC BOUNDARY.

Classic tracer evidence PMID **9357807** showed pre-exercise carbohydrate can suppress lipolysis and fat oxidation at low-intensity exercise.

The 2016 meta-analysis PMID **27609363** found greater acute fat oxidation during fasted aerobic exercise in its included studies.

However the newer 2025 meta-analysis PMID **39921164** did not support a simple claim that acute fasted exercise is globally “better” for glucose/lipid metabolism and showed outcome-dependent effects.

Therefore retain:

**fasting can increase acute fat oxidation in many exercise contexts.**

Reject:

**fasting is metabolically superior in general.**

And reject:

**higher acute fat oxidation proves greater chronic fat loss.**

Chronic body-composition and dietary strategy remain NUT/PRO.

No blocker.

---

## 13. Acute fat oxidation vs chronic fat loss audit

PASS.

Acute substrate oxidation is a within-session physiological outcome.

Chronic fat-mass change depends on longer-horizon variables including:

- energy intake/expenditure;
- diet composition/adherence;
- compensatory behavior;
- training dose;
- recovery;
- repeated-session adaptation.

Therefore:

**session fat oxidation ≠ net 24-h fat balance ≠ chronic adipose-fat loss.**

No blocker.

---

## 14. Metabolic-flexibility audit

PASS WITH MEASUREMENT/VALIDATION BOUNDARY.

Retain metabolic flexibility as:

**capacity to adapt substrate utilization to changes in demand, nutrient availability and physiological state.**

This includes the ability to:

- increase lipid oxidation when appropriate;
- increase carbohydrate use when ATP demand requires it;
- shift with feeding/fasting;
- adapt to glycogen availability.

Current 2026 review PMID **41881712** supports mitochondrial fuel flexibility as switching between lipid and carbohydrate oxidation.

But metabolic flexibility is **not** equivalent to:

- low RER;
- high PFO;
- high Fatmax;
- one fasting value;
- maximal fat oxidation alone.

No blocker.

---

## 15. Metabolic-flexibility test validity audit

PASS WITH IMPORTANT LIMITATION.

PMID **40164840** documents:

- no consensus exercise-testing protocol;
- variation in maximal/submaximal protocols;
- stage length differences;
- different averaging windows;
- different equations;
- effects of nutritional state and exercise mode.

Therefore:

**no single exercise-derived metabolic-flexibility score is retained as a standardized clinical diagnostic surrogate.**

Testing methodology remains TST.

No blocker.

---

## 16. RER / indirect-calorimetry audit

PASS.

RER/VO2/VCO2-derived substrate equations are useful for whole-body estimation under suitable steady-state aerobic conditions.

But:

- bicarbonate-pool changes at higher intensity can distort VCO2;
- buffering-derived CO2 is not identical to substrate-derived CO2;
- equations depend on glucose vs glycogen assumptions;
- protein oxidation is frequently omitted;
- whole-body gas exchange does not directly identify local muscle source pools.

Canonical rule:

**RER is an estimator of whole-body fuel use under defined conditions, not a universally valid direct measure of local muscle carbohydrate/fat flux.**

No blocker.

---

## 17. High-fat adaptation / performance audit

PASS AS SCOPE BOUNDARY.

Current evidence continues to show that low-carbohydrate/high-fat or ketogenic adaptation can markedly increase fat oxidation.

However performance effects remain:

- task dependent;
- intensity dependent;
- athlete dependent;
- method dependent.

The 2024 ISSN position stand retained largely neutral/detrimental performance effects overall despite increased fat oxidation.

The 2026 Burke/Noakes debate consensus explicitly records agreement that athletes can train the capacity to oxidize either fuel while disagreement remains over the performance advantage of maximizing carbohydrate vs fat oxidation.

Therefore BIO-07 does **not** infer:

- higher fat oxidation = superior endurance performance;
- lower carbohydrate oxidation = superior metabolic state;
- high carbohydrate availability = universally superior for every task;
- LCHF/KD = universally inferior for every task.

Canonical boundary:

**fuel-selection phenotype and performance outcome are separate constructs.**

Detailed dietary strategy belongs NUT; performance prescription belongs PRO.

No blocker.

## 21. Closure decision

**BIO-07 = CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0.**

Meaning:

1. carbohydrate and fat are concurrent oxidative fuels rather than mutually exclusive systems;
2. carbohydrate can support both glycolytic substrate-level and oxidative ATP production;
3. carbohydrate source pools remain separated;
4. fat oxidation is a multi-step pathway and is not equivalent to lipolysis or fat loss;
5. beta-oxidation is retained as mitochondrial fatty-acid degradation without a universal single rate-limiting step;
6. protein contribution is small/subsidiary but non-zero;
7. intensity generally shifts fuel use toward carbohydrate without a binary switch;
8. no universal Fatmax/crossover percentage is retained;
9. duration changes both macro-level fuel selection and source-pool contribution;
10. training status changes substrate handling, but absolute/relative workload context is mandatory;
11. glycogen availability changes endogenous carbohydrate/fat selection without eliminating exogenous carbohydrate oxidation;
12. glycogen state can change PFO/Fatmax without an intrinsic mitochondrial-function change;
13. fed/fasted state changes acute substrate selection but does not determine chronic fat loss;
14. metabolic flexibility means adaptive switching, not maximal fat oxidation;
15. no single standardized metabolic-flexibility exercise test/score is retained;
16. RER is a method-dependent whole-body estimator;
17. fuel-selection phenotype and performance outcome remain separate.
