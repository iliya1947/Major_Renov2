# ANA-06 — Предплечье, запястье, кисть и хват

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-18**  
Область: 02 — Анатомия

## 0. Decision

Создать structural-functional карту предплечья, DRUJ/TFCC, запястья, кисти и grip, достаточную для последующего анализа forearm rotation, wrist positioning, grip mechanics and forearm training без:

- wrist-rotation mislabeling;
- TFCC-as-one-ligament simplification;
- wrist-as-one-joint model;
- finger-flexors-only grip model;
- подмены anatomy mechanics/pathology.

## 1. Forearm rotational system

Canonical system:

`PRUJ ↔ radius/ulna + interosseous membrane ↔ DRUJ`

During pronation:
- radius crosses relative to ulna.

During supination:
- radius and ulna become relatively parallel.

Project rule:

**pronation/supination = forearm rotation, not wrist rotation.**

## 2. Radius / ulna / interosseous membrane

### Radius
- lateral/thumb side in anatomical position;
- participates proximally in elbow/PRUJ;
- participates distally in DRUJ and wrist complex;
- moves substantially around ulna during forearm rotation.

### Ulna
- medial/little-finger side;
- major proximal elbow articulation;
- distal ulnar head participates in DRUJ.

### Interosseous membrane
Fibrous syndesmosis between radius and ulna.

Roles:
- links/organizes bones;
- separates compartments;
- provides muscle attachment;
- participates in longitudinal/transverse force transfer;
- contributes rotational stability.

IOM ≠ passive septum only.

## 3. Distal radioulnar joint

DRUJ:
- sigmoid notch of radius ↔ ulnar head;
- synovial pivot articulation;
- part of the forearm-rotation system;
- distinct from radiocarpal wrist motion.

Project rule:

**DRUJ belongs primarily to pronation/supination logic, not wrist flexion/extension.**

## 4. TFCC

TFCC = multi-component ulnar-sided stabilizing/load-sharing complex.

Current 2026 anatomy supports a 3D model including:
- articular disc;
- distal radioulnar ligaments;
- peripheral capsular/ulnocarpal structures;
- associated ECU-sheath and ulnar-sided relationships depending definition.

Project rule:

**TFCC ≠ one triangular ligament/disc.**

Pathology/variant significance → MED/TST/MEC.

## 5. Forearm compartments

### Anterior
Flexor-pronator dominant.

### Posterior
Extensor-supinator dominant.

A lateral/mobile-wad convention may include:
- brachioradialis;
- ECRL;
- ECRB.

Compartment labels organize anatomy; they do not imply one action per compartment.

## 6. Pronator system

Major pronators:

1. pronator teres;
2. pronator quadratus.

### Pronator teres
- medial-epicondylar/coronoid origins;
- inserts on lateral mid-radius;
- median nerve;
- pronation + secondary elbow-flexion contribution.

### Pronator quadratus
- distal ulna → distal radius;
- anterior interosseous nerve;
- pronation;
- contributes distal radioulnar stabilization.

Exact angle/load contribution → MEC.

## 7. Supinator system

Major supinators:

1. supinator;
2. biceps brachii.

Brachioradialis can assist return toward neutral from more extreme positions.

Project rule:

**no one muscle owns forearm rotation across all tasks.**

## 8. Wrist flexor system

### FCR
- wrist flexion;
- radial-deviation contribution.

### FCU
- wrist flexion;
- ulnar-deviation contribution;
- ulnar nerve.

### Palmaris longus
- weak wrist flexion;
- tenses palmar aponeurosis;
- frequently absent as normal variation.

FDS/FDP/FPL also cross wrist and influence wrist/finger coupling.

## 9. Deep anterior finger/thumb flexors

### FDS
- digits 2–5;
- PIP flexion;
- contributes MCP/wrist flexion.

### FDP
- digits 2–5;
- DIP flexion;
- major grip contribution;
- split median/AIN vs ulnar innervation pattern.

### FPL
- thumb IP flexion;
- anterior interosseous nerve.

## 10. Wrist extensor system

### ECRL / ECRB
- extension;
- radial-deviation contribution.

### ECU
- extension;
- ulnar-deviation contribution.

Current 2026 reference explicitly supports wrist-extensor stabilization during forceful grip.

Project consequence:

**wrist extensors are part of grip architecture even though they do not flex fingers.**

## 11. Extrinsic finger / thumb extensors

Core:
- extensor digitorum;
- extensor indicis;
- extensor digiti minimi;
- APL;
- EPB;
- EPL.

Extensor digitorum acts through extensor expansions rather than a simple one-tendon/one-distal-phalanx model.

## 12. Wrist joint complex

Whole-wrist motion is distributed across:

1. radiocarpal joint;
2. midcarpal/intercarpal joints;
3. ulnocarpal/TFCC interface.

DRUJ is adjacent but primarily part of forearm rotation.

Project rule:

**total wrist ROM ≠ pure radiocarpal ROM.**

## 13. Carpal bones

Eight carpals:

### Proximal row
- scaphoid;
- lunate;
- triquetrum;
- pisiform.

### Distal row
- trapezium;
- trapezoid;
- capitate;
- hamate.

Pisiform is sesamoid within FCU tendon architecture.

## 14. Retinacula and tendon sheaths

### Flexor retinaculum
Forms carpal-tunnel roof.

Canonical contents:
- median nerve;
- 4 FDS tendons;
- 4 FDP tendons;
- 1 FPL tendon.

Total:
**9 flexor tendons + median nerve**.

FCR uses a separate fibro-osseous route; palmaris longus is superficial; ulnar nerve/artery use a separate ulnar-sided route.

### Extensor retinaculum
Limits bowstringing and organizes dorsal tendon paths.

Typical six-compartment reference model is retained, while exact tendon-slip/septation anatomy may vary.

### Tendon sheaths
Normal gliding structures reducing friction.

Presence of a sheath ≠ pathology.

## 15. Intrinsic hand muscles

Core groups:

1. thenar;
2. hypothenar;
3. lumbricals;
4. interossei;
5. adductor pollicis / deep thumb muscles.

Intrinsic hand muscles contribute to grip and pinch, not merely “fine motor control”.

## 16. Thenar / thumb system

Main thenar muscles:
- abductor pollicis brevis;
- flexor pollicis brevis;
- opponens pollicis.

Adductor pollicis is a deep intrinsic thumb muscle.

Innervation nuance:
- major thenar motor supply → recurrent median;
- adductor pollicis → ulnar;
- FPB may have mixed/deep-head ulnar contribution.

Project rule:

**thumb grip function spans both median and ulnar territories.**

## 17. Hypothenar system

Core:
- abductor digiti minimi;
- flexor digiti minimi brevis;
- opponens digiti minimi;
- palmaris brevis.

Primarily ulnar-nerve territory.

Contributes:
- fifth-digit positioning;
- palm contour/cupping;
- grip support.

## 18. Lumbricals / interossei

### Lumbricals
- arise from FDP tendons;
- insert into extensor expansions;
- flex MCP and assist IP extension.

Typical innervation:
- lateral two median;
- medial two ulnar.

### Dorsal interossei
- finger abduction;
- MCP flexion;
- IP-extension contribution.

### Palmar interossei
- finger adduction;
- MCP flexion;
- IP-extension contribution.

Count nuance:
classic teaching often counts 3 palmar interossei, but a pollical/first palmar interosseous may be treated as a distinct variable muscle.

Project rule:

**do not use “always three” or “always four” without defining convention.**

## 19. Extensor expansion

Digital extensor mechanism integrates:

1. extensor digitorum;
2. lumbricals;
3. interossei.

Therefore:

**finger extension is an integrated mechanism, not one tendon acting alone.**

## 20. Thumb / finger joints

### Thumb
- first CMC;
- MCP;
- IP.

CMC permits combined:
- flexion/extension;
- abduction/adduction;
- axial rotation;
- opposition/reposition.

Opposition is a composite 3D movement central to grip/pinch.

### Digits 2–5
- MCP;
- PIP;
- DIP.

## 21. Power grip architecture

Power grip requires distributed contribution from:

1. extrinsic finger flexors;
2. thumb flexion/adduction/opposition;
3. intrinsic hand muscles;
4. wrist positioning/stabilization;
5. forearm orientation.

Project rule:

**strong grip ≠ finger flexors alone.**

## 22. Wrist–finger coupling / tenodesis

Because long finger flexor/extensor tendons cross wrist + finger joints:

- wrist extension tends to passively flex fingers;
- wrist flexion tends to passively extend fingers.

This establishes real mechanical coupling.

Anatomy does not establish one optimal wrist angle for force.

That belongs to MEC/TST.

## 23. Wrist flexion / extension / deviation coordination

Broad anatomical force-couple model:

### Flexion
FCR + FCU can combine to reduce net deviation.

### Extension
ECRL/ECRB + ECU can combine to reduce net deviation.

### Radial deviation
radial-side flexor/extensor contributors combine.

### Ulnar deviation
FCU + ECU contribute.

Exact torque → MEC.

## 24. Neural map — minimum

### Median / anterior interosseous
Most flexor-pronator structures + major thenar/lateral lumbrical functions.

### Ulnar
- FCU;
- medial FDP;
- most intrinsic hand muscles.

### Radial / posterior interosseous
Posterior extensor-supinator system.

Detailed branching/entrapment → ANA-12/MED.

## 25. Carpal tunnel vs Guyon route

### Carpal tunnel
- median nerve;
- 9 long flexor tendons.

### Guyon-region route
- ulnar nerve/artery.

These are anatomically separate.

No symptom diagnosis is made here.

## 26. Normal variation

Relevant normal variation includes:

- palmaris longus absence;
- accessory muscle/tendon slips;
- extensor-compartment septation;
- lumbrical variants;
- interosseous variants;
- accessory intrinsic muscles;
- palmar-interosseous count convention.

Project rule:

**atlas anatomy is modal, not identical in every person.**

Variation alone ≠ pathology.

## 27. Training-relevant structural consequences

Anatomy supports later study of:

1. neutral/supinated/pronated curls;
2. wrist flexion/extension;
3. radial/ulnar deviation;
4. loaded pronation/supination;
5. grip strength;
6. wrist position during rows/deadlifts/presses;
7. finger-extensor work;
8. thumb/intrinsic contribution;
9. handle thickness.

But ANA-06 does not determine:
- best wrist angle;
- best handle diameter;
- best forearm exercise;
- hypertrophy superiority of one grip.

Those require MEC/TEC/PHY evidence.

## 28. Canonical project model

1. PRUJ + radius/ulna + IOM + DRUJ = forearm rotational system.
2. TFCC = multi-component ulnar-sided stabilizing/load-sharing complex.
3. Anterior forearm = flexor-pronator dominant.
4. Posterior forearm = extensor-supinator dominant.
5. Pronator teres/quadratus and biceps/supinator are the main rotation systems.
6. Wrist motion is distributed across radiocarpal + midcarpal/intercarpal structures.
7. Retinacula constrain tendon paths and limit bowstringing.
8. Grip requires extrinsic flexors + thumb/intrinsics + wrist stabilization.
9. Wrist position couples mechanically to finger posture/force through multi-joint tendons.
10. Extensor expansion integrates extrinsic extensors + lumbricals + interossei.
11. “Forearm” contains multiple functions; wrist curls do not represent complete forearm training.
12. Normal anatomy is variable.

## 29. Evidence / confidence

### HIGH

- forearm rotational system;
- DRUJ distinction from wrist joint;
- multi-component TFCC architecture;
- major forearm muscle systems;
- wrist-complex model;
- retinacula/tendon pathways;
- power-grip distributed architecture;
- wrist-extensor grip stabilization;
- extensor-expansion integration;
- tenodesis coupling.

### MODERATE / convention-dependent

- exact palmar-interosseous count/classification;
- exact individual muscle share in grip;
- mechanical consequence of specific TFCC variants.
