# RES-01 / PASS-07A — Stakeholder and end-user involvement in question formulation

Статус: **COMPLETE PASS — topic remains RESEARCHING**  
Дата: 2026-11-16  
Topic: RES-01 — формулировка исследовательского вопроса

## Scope этого прохода

Только:
1. зачем intended users / stakeholders участвуют в постановке review question;
2. кто считается stakeholder;
3. какие части вопроса они могут менять;
4. где stakeholder input заканчивается и начинается методологическая/научная проверка;
5. как адаптировать stakeholder involvement к проекту, предназначенному прежде всего для одного конечного пользователя;
6. как документировать участие без ложного заявления о независимом consensus process.

Не покрывается здесь:
- equity/diversity как отдельный cross-question слой;
- formal priority-setting methods;
- consensus methods (Delphi и др.);
- conflict-of-interest governance подробно;
- stakeholder involvement на всех последующих стадиях review;
- final RES-01 gap/audit.

## 1. A review question should matter to its intended users

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

Cochrane states that review questions should address issues important to people expected to:
- use;
- act on;
- implement
the review's conclusions.

This is not merely a dissemination concern after the review is finished.

It affects the initial choice of:
- question;
- outcomes;
- scope;
- relevant comparisons.

Project consequence:
scientific answerability alone is not enough.
A technically valid question can still be low-value if it does not inform a real decision.

---

## 2. Stakeholder involvement can improve question relevance

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

Cochrane recommends involving relevant stakeholders when defining/refining review questions.

Potential stakeholders include:
- consumers/patients/service users;
- carers/family where relevant;
- clinicians/professionals;
- policy makers;
- guideline developers;
- researchers;
- funders;
- people responsible for implementing the intervention.

Stated benefits include:
- greater relevance;
- mutual learning;
- better uptake;
- lower research waste.

---

## 3. The relevant stakeholder set depends on the question

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

Cochrane does not prescribe one fixed stakeholder panel for every review.

Who matters depends on:
1. who is affected by the answer;
2. who will use the answer;
3. who will implement or discontinue the intervention;
4. what setting/system is involved.

Project consequence:
do not mechanically add stakeholder categories that have no role in the target decision.

---

## 4. Stakeholder input can change more than wording

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

Stakeholder involvement may reveal that researchers have prioritized the wrong:
- outcomes;
- comparisons;
- subgroups;
- implementation constraints;
- time horizons;
- meanings of benefit/burden.

Project consequence:
stakeholder input is upstream of evidence synthesis, not cosmetic feedback on final prose.

For this project, end-user input can legitimately alter:
- which outcomes are critical;
- acceptable burden;
- practical comparators;
- implementation setting;
- what constitutes a meaningful result.

---

## 5. Stakeholder preference does not determine empirical truth

### Project methodological boundary
**Confidence: HIGH**

Stakeholders can determine:
- which question matters;
- which outcomes matter;
- what trade-offs are acceptable;
- what context is relevant.

They do **not** determine:
- study validity;
- effect size;
- causal direction;
- certainty of evidence.

Project rule:

**Values choose the decision problem. Evidence constrains the answer.**

Do not modify evidence interpretation to fit a preferred conclusion.

---

## 6. Stakeholder involvement does not replace methodological expertise

### Established finding / project consequence
**Confidence: HIGH**

Cochrane explicitly describes systematic reviews as needing:
- methodological expertise;
- subject expertise;
- stakeholder perspectives.

These are complementary roles.

Project consequence:
user/end-user priorities cannot substitute for:
- evidence appraisal;
- statistics;
- risk-of-bias assessment;
- clinical safety judgement where relevant.

Likewise, methodological expertise cannot decide personal preferences for the user.

---

## 7. Diversity of perspectives matters for general-purpose reviews

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

Cochrane generally recommends input from multiple relevant perspectives because:
- consumers;
- professionals;
- policy makers;
- implementers
may prioritize different questions or outcomes.

The QES guidance specifically warns that consensus exercises can still suppress minority or marginalized perspectives.

Project consequence:
for a review intended to make general recommendations, one user's preferences are not enough to define broad population priorities.

---

## 8. But this project has a different primary decision target

### Project-specific application
**Confidence: HIGH as project logic; not a universal review standard**

The current project is not primarily trying to publish a population-wide clinical guideline.

Its eventual target is:
- an evidence-informed training program for one identified end user.

Therefore the primary intended user is the person whose training decision is being made.

### Consequence

For **personal decision framing**:

the user's stated:
- goals;
- constraints;
- acceptable burden;
- preferences;
- adherence realities;
- equipment/environment

are direct end-user stakeholder input.

We do **not** need to simulate a large public stakeholder panel merely to decide what matters to this one user's eventual program.

---

## 9. Single-user relevance does not make external evidence optional

### Project boundary
**Confidence: HIGH**

Even in a one-user decision project:

1. scientific claims come from external evidence;
2. safety constraints may require professional/clinical evidence;
3. applicability must be assessed separately;
4. personal preference only modifies the decision weights, not the factual evidence.

Thus:

**evidence base = population science**  
**decision weights/context = user-specific**

These layers must remain separate.

---

## 10. General evidence questions vs user-specific decision questions

### Project distinction

### A. General evidence question
Example:
> What training-volume dose-response is supported for hypertrophy in adults?

Stakeholder role:
helps decide which outcomes/comparisons are relevant, but evidence synthesis should remain general enough to support applicability assessment.

### B. User-specific decision question
Example:
> Given limited home resistance and high importance of adherence, which evidence-supported progression strategy is preferable?

Stakeholder role:
the user directly determines:
- goal priority;
- burden tolerance;
- feasible setting;
- equipment constraints;
- unacceptable trade-offs.

Project rule:
do not contaminate the general evidence question by prematurely narrowing all science to one person's current equipment.

This preserves the earlier architecture principle:
**equipment is a late-stage constraint, not the organizing principle of the science base.**

---

## 11. Stakeholder input should be recorded before outcome prioritization is finalized

### Established finding / project consequence
**Confidence: HIGH**

Cochrane MECIR requires outcomes to address issues important to review users.

Therefore when a topic has a decision-facing outcome set, record:
- who the intended user is;
- which outcomes they consider decision-critical;
- why.

Project consequence:
a mechanistic variable should not become critical merely because researchers measure it often if the real decision concerns:
- strength;
- hypertrophy;
- safety;
- burden;
- adherence.

---

## 12. Stakeholder involvement should be transparent

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

Cochrane reporting guidance says reviews should state:
- whether consumers/stakeholders were involved;
- general approach;
- level/role;
- review stage;
- methods used.

If no stakeholder involvement occurred, that should also be stated.

Project consequence:
do not write vague claims such as:
> “stakeholder informed”

without identifying:
- who;
- at what stage;
- what input changed.

---

## 13. Do not falsely label user conversation as formal PPI/consensus

### Project rule
**Confidence: HIGH**

This project includes direct end-user input from the user.

But unless a formal process is actually conducted, do **not** label it as:
- formal patient/public involvement (PPI);
- consensus process;
- Delphi;
- representative stakeholder panel;
- independent stakeholder validation.

Use precise language:

> **Direct end-user input from the target user**

when that is what occurred.

---

## 14. User input can be authoritative about preferences, not about hidden facts

### Project rule
**Confidence: HIGH**

The user is the best source for:
- stated goals;
- subjective preferences;
- acceptable friction;
- perceived burden;
- practical constraints.

But even self-report can have measurement limits for:
- exact historical volume;
- physiological response;
- medical diagnosis;
- objective performance.

Project consequence:
classify user-provided information by type:
1. preference/value;
2. self-reported fact;
3. objectively measured fact;
4. interpretation/hypothesis.

Do not collapse them.

---

## 15. Sensitive personal information should not be copied into the public research repository

### Project governance rule
**Confidence: HIGH**

The GitHub repository is public.

Therefore stakeholder documentation stored there should contain only non-sensitive abstractions necessary for research architecture.

Allowed examples:
- “target user prioritizes strength + hypertrophy”;
- “home-training context matters at application stage”;
- “adherence burden is decision-relevant”.

Do not store:
- detailed psychological history;
- medical records;
- private health information;
- identifying personal details.

Detailed personal assessment belongs outside the public general-research layer.

---

## 16. Stakeholder disagreement is information, not noise

### Established finding / project consequence
**Confidence: HIGH/MODERATE**

In multi-stakeholder reviews, different groups may legitimately value:
- outcomes;
- burdens;
- comparisons
differently.

Do not force artificial consensus merely to simplify the question.

Project rule:
if meaningful disagreement exists:
1. record it;
2. preserve multiple decision-relevant outcomes where feasible;
3. distinguish evidence findings from value judgments.

For this one-user application, external stakeholder disagreement may still matter when it signals:
- safety concerns;
- implementation conflicts;
- different professional perspectives.

---

## 17. Stakeholder involvement can be one-off or ongoing

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

Cochrane says ongoing engagement is optimal, but recognizes that one-off engagement may sometimes be all that is feasible.

Project adaptation:
because the target user is directly participating throughout this project, stakeholder input is naturally **ongoing**.

But scientific question changes must still be logged rather than being silently altered in conversation.

---

## 18. Project Stakeholder/End-user field

Add to Question Card:

1. **Intended user(s)**
2. **Decision owner**
3. **Affected stakeholders**
4. **Implementation actor(s)**
5. **End-user priorities**
6. **Critical outcomes from user perspective**
7. **Unacceptable harms/burdens**
8. **Context constraints**
9. **Source of stakeholder input**
10. **Formal or informal process?**
11. **What changed because of stakeholder input?**
12. **Potential stakeholder conflicts**
13. **Representation limitations**

For this project, common value:
- source = direct user input;
- process = informal/ongoing unless a formal method is actually used.

---

## 19. When external stakeholder input is actually needed

### Project rule

External input becomes more important when:

1. the evidence question aims at population-wide recommendations;
2. professional implementation is required;
3. safety depends on clinical expertise;
4. the user is not the only affected person;
5. relevant outcomes require perspectives the user cannot supply;
6. there is a material conflict between personal preference and professional/safety constraints.

Do not add external stakeholders performatively when they cannot change the decision.

---

## 20. Practical rules produced by PASS-07A

1. Review questions should matter to intended users.
2. Stakeholders help define relevance, outcomes, comparisons and implementation context.
3. Stakeholder input chooses/weights the decision problem; it does not determine empirical truth.
4. Methodological expertise, domain expertise and stakeholder perspectives are complementary.
5. General-purpose reviews should seek relevant diversity of perspectives.
6. For this one-user decision project, direct input from the target user is the primary end-user perspective.
7. Keep general evidence questions broader than the eventual user's equipment/context when scientifically appropriate.
8. Separate population evidence from user-specific decision weights.
9. Record stakeholder involvement transparently.
10. Do not call informal direct user input “formal PPI” or “consensus”.
11. Classify user information as preference, self-report, measurement or interpretation.
12. Do not store sensitive stakeholder details in the public research repository.
13. Preserve meaningful stakeholder disagreement rather than manufacturing consensus.
14. Log question changes caused by stakeholder input.
15. Add explicit stakeholder/end-user fields to the project Question Card at final RES-01 synthesis.
