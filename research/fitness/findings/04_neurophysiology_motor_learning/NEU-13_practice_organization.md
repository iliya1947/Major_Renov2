# NEU-13 — Practice organization

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-19**  
Область: 04 — Neurophysiology / Motor Learning

## 0. Core decision

Practice organization is multidimensional.

A defensible model is:

`frequency`
+
`volume`
+
`spacing`
+
`practice content`
+
`practice order`
+
`whole/part structure`
+
`target specificity`
+
`fatigue state`
→
`acquisition + retention + transfer`.

No single dimension has a universal optimum.

Project rule:

**organize practice around the target skill and the learning outcome being tested, not around slogans such as “always spaced,” “always random,” “always variable,” or “more is always better.”**

---

## 1. Practice frequency

Practice frequency is how often practice occasions recur.

It can refer to:

- repetitions;
- blocks;
- sessions;
- days between sessions.

These time scales must not be conflated.

Frequency effects can be confounded by:

- total volume;
- within-session rest;
- between-session spacing;
- fatigue/recovery;
- number of target-task exposures.

Project rule:

**there is no universal motor-skill practice frequency independent of dose, task, spacing and fatigue.**

For compound-lift technique, NEU-13 does not establish an exact sessions/week prescription.

---

## 2. Practice volume

Practice volume is the amount of task exposure.

Possible units:

- trials;
- repetitions;
- blocks;
- minutes;
- successful target executions.

More practice can increase learning opportunity.

But:

- learning gain per added trial need not remain constant;
- more practice is not always more efficient;
- a plateau does not prove further practice has zero value.

Project rule:

**practice dose must be judged by both total learning and marginal learning value.**

---

## 3. Distributed vs massed practice

### Massed practice
Relatively dense practice with shorter rest/spacing.

### Distributed practice
Practice separated by longer intervals.

The exact time scale must be named.

Current evidence does not justify:

`distributed > massed`

as a universal motor-learning law.

Recent motor-sequence and sport studies show:

- spacing can improve current practice performance without improving later learning;
- longer spacing can produce no retention advantage;
- in some serial tasks shorter inter-trial intervals can produce better delayed learning.

Project rule:

**spacing must be treated as task- and time-scale-dependent.**

Do not import declarative-memory spacing rules directly into motor skill.

---

## 4. Blocked practice

Blocked practice repeats one task/variant before switching.

It can:

- reduce switching demands;
- improve acquisition;
- help stabilize an unfamiliar task;
- simplify early practice.

Better blocked acquisition does not prove better durable learning.

Project rule:

**blocked practice is a legitimate tool, not a synonym for poor learning.**

---

## 5. Random / interleaved practice

Random/interleaved practice changes among relevant task variants more frequently.

It can increase:

- action-plan reconstruction;
- retrieval demands;
- comparison among variants;
- resistance to one fixed practice context.

In many paradigms random practice produces:

- worse acquisition performance;
- better delayed retention.

But the effect is not universal.

Project rule:

**use interleaving when retrieval/variant discrimination is relevant, then verify delayed retention/transfer.**

---

## 6. Contextual interference

Contextual interference (CI) is the interference created by arranging multiple practiced tasks/variants in a less repetitive order.

Meta-analytic evidence supports an average benefit of high CI for retention.

However:

- laboratory effects are clearer;
- applied retention effects can be almost negligible;
- applied transfer effects are smaller and less certain than laboratory transfer effects;
- age/task/context moderate the result.

Project rule:

**CI is a real average effect, not a universal guarantee.**

---

## 7. CI is not chaos

Contextual interference does not mean:

- random exercise selection;
- maximal task variation;
- changing irrelevant variables;
- deliberate error production;
- removing stable practice;
- randomizing safety-critical setup.

Project rule:

**interleave relevant variants to create useful retrieval/reconstruction demands, not noise.**

---

## 8. Constant vs variable practice

### Constant practice
Repeated practice of one criterion condition.

### Variable practice
Practice across multiple relevant task conditions/parameter values.

Variable practice can be useful for transfer/generalization.

But current evidence does not establish a universal retention advantage.

Recent studies show:

- variable practice may not outperform constant practice for one criterion target;
- more variability is not necessarily better;
- adding variability after stabilization does not guarantee a retention benefit.

Project rule:

**vary practice because a transfer dimension matters, not because variability is inherently superior.**

---

## 9. Practice content vs practice order

These are separate dimensions.

### Content
Constant vs variable asks:

> what conditions are practiced?

### Order
Blocked vs random asks:

> in what sequence are multiple conditions practiced?

Therefore:

- variable-blocked;
- variable-random;
- serial/progressive;
- constant

are different schedules.

Project rule:

**do not use “random,” “variable,” and “contextual interference” as synonyms.**

---

## 10. Specificity of practice

Motor learning is constrained by overlap between practice and target conditions.

Relevant dimensions include:

- movement direction;
- load/force;
- speed;
- timing;
- target;
- equipment;
- sensory context;
- task goal.

Selected variability can broaden transfer.

But target-condition exposure remains necessary.

Project rule:

**practice the actual target condition; add variation only for transfer dimensions that matter.**

---

## 11. Whole vs part practice

No universal whole-vs-part winner is established.

### Whole practice is favored when

- integrated coordination matters;
- the whole skill is feasible/safe;
- component interactions are important.

### Part practice can be useful when

- the whole task is too difficult to generate informative attempts;
- a component is meaningfully separable;
- one bottleneck needs temporary isolation;
- setup/safety makes whole practice impractical.

Project rule:

**part practice is useful only if it transfers back to the integrated target skill.**

Evidence for whole/part rules is weaker and older than the main CI evidence.

---

## 12. Repetition without repetition

Skill learning requires repeated exposure to the task problem.

It does not require identical joint trajectories every repetition.

Stable skill can coexist with:

- solution variability;
- contextual adaptation;
- multiple task-successful movement patterns.

Project rule:

**repeat the task-relevant control problem, not an exact universal kinematic template.**

This does not mean maximizing variability.

---

## 13. “Mindless repetition”

“Mindless repetition” is not a validated binary scientific construct.

Operationally, additional practice becomes less defensible when trials mainly produce:

- fatigue-driven deterioration;
- repetitive uncorrected failure;
- trivial repeated success with no retention/transfer target;
- practice of a condition unrelated to the eventual target.

Project rule:

**practice quality is judged by marginal task-relevant information and learning value, not a fixed repetition number.**

---

## 14. Diminishing returns

Several practice-dose datasets show diminishing marginal efficiency.

But this is not a universal law.

Evidence also shows:

- additional practice after an apparent plateau can improve delayed learning in some tasks/populations;
- total trial count can remain predictive of retention.

Therefore:

**plateau ≠ no remaining learning value.**

Project rule:

**do not stop solely because acquisition performance has flattened.**

---

## 15. When to stop or reorganize practice

No universal repetition threshold is established.

Additional practice may still contribute through:

- refinement;
- consolidation;
- retrieval;
- robustness;
- transfer exposure.

A practical stop/reorganization criterion is:

1. target-specific quality;
2. error information;
3. retention/transfer goal;
4. fatigue state;
5. marginal learning value.

Status:

**PRACTICAL_HEURISTIC**, not a universal biological cutoff.

---

## 16. Fatigue as a practice modifier

Fatigue can change:

- force production;
- coordination;
- sensory precision;
- attention;
- error structure;
- current performance.

Current evidence shows heterogeneous effects.

Local neuromuscular fatigue appears more consistently disruptive than cardiovascular fatigue.

But:

- fatigue does not always impair learning;
- practicing fatigued does not automatically create fatigue resilience.

Project rule:

**fresh-state learning and fatigued-state robustness are different outcomes.**

---

## 17. Practice frequency and skill decay

Motor/procedural skills can decay with nonuse.

Decay depends on:

- task;
- outcome metric;
- complexity;
- nonuse duration;
- intermittent performance opportunities.

Therefore no universal refresher interval can be set for compound lifts.

Project rule:

**eventual skill-practice frequency should be informed by observed retention of the actual target skill.**

---

## 18. Direct resistance-exercise evidence

Direct NEU-13 evidence for compound lifts is sparse.

### Squat
A randomized study shows acquisition and delayed retention of some squat variables, but it does not compare practice schedules.

### Isometric knee extension
A 2026 acute study compared constant, serial and random force-practice order:

- random practice had worse acquisition error initially;
- all schedules improved pre-to-post force accuracy;
- no schedule-specific immediate accuracy advantage was established;
- no long-term retention result supports a chronic schedule prescription.

Therefore:

**general motor-learning principles constrain RT coaching logic, but they do not define an exact compound-lift practice schedule.**

---

## 19. Canonical non-equivalences

1. Practice frequency ≠ practice volume.
2. Practice volume ≠ practice distribution.
3. Distributed practice ≠ universally superior learning.
4. Massed practice ≠ universally harmful learning.
5. Blocked practice ≠ poor learning.
6. Random practice ≠ universally superior learning.
7. Random practice ≠ practice chaos.
8. Contextual interference ≠ variability of practice.
9. Variable practice ≠ universally better retention.
10. Constant practice ≠ always poor transfer.
11. Whole practice ≠ universally superior.
12. Part practice ≠ automatic transfer back to the whole.
13. Repetition ≠ identical kinematics.
14. Variability ≠ useful exploration by default.
15. Plateau ≠ zero further learning value.
16. More practice ≠ proportionally more learning.
17. Fresh-state retention ≠ fatigued-state robustness.
18. Practice under fatigue ≠ automatic fatigue resilience.
19. Acquisition performance ≠ durable learning.

## 20. Measurement boundaries

### Frequency
State the time scale.

### Volume
State the unit.

### Distribution
State inter-trial/inter-block/inter-session spacing.

### Blocked/random
Define the variants and their order.

### Constant/variable
Define what task dimension varies.

### Whole/part
Define the decomposition and recombination test.

### Retention
Use delayed testing.

### Transfer
Name the untrained target condition.

### Fatigue
Measure/verify the fatigue state.

### Plateau
Define the outcome window and threshold; do not treat it as a biological endpoint.

Project rule:

**schedule claims are uninterpretable without an explicit practice architecture.**

---

## 21. Practical coaching synthesis

For future resistance-exercise technique work, a defensible heuristic is:

1. establish enough stable exposure to execute the task safely;
2. practice the whole target movement when feasible;
3. isolate parts only for a specific bottleneck;
4. add relevant task variation/interleaving when transfer demands justify it;
5. preserve enough target-condition exposure for specificity;
6. monitor whether fatigue is changing the task into a different coordination problem;
7. judge learning by delayed retention/target transfer, not same-session smoothness;
8. change practice structure when extra repetitions provide little task-relevant information.

Status:

**PRACTICAL_HEURISTIC**, not a proven universal optimal sequence.

---

## 22. What NEU-13 does not prescribe

Not owned here:

- exact weekly training frequency;
- exact sets/repetitions for strength or hypertrophy;
- exact inter-set rest for physical adaptation;
- exact load/intensity prescription;
- exact exercise rotation;
- attentional focus;
- feedback schedule;
- cue wording;
- demonstration/video use;
- error-correction algorithm;
- exact exercise-specific drill progressions.

These remain PRO, TEC, NEU-14…18, TST, COA and IND.

## 23. Confidence

### HIGH

- frequency, volume and distribution are distinct;
- blocked/random and constant/variable are distinct;
- CI has an average retention effect;
- CI applied effects are smaller/more heterogeneous;
- variable practice is not universally superior;
- specificity remains necessary;
- fatigue effects are state/task dependent;
- no universal repetition stopping threshold exists.

### MODERATE-HIGH

- random/interleaved practice can improve delayed retention;
- relevant variability can broaden transfer;
- diminishing marginal efficiency occurs in some practice-dose contexts.

### MODERATE

- whole-vs-part task-organization rule;
- “repetition without repetition” as a useful practice-design principle;
- practical transition from stable practice toward selected interleaving/variation.

### LOW / CONTEXT-DEPENDENT

- exact practice frequency for a compound lift;
- exact optimal spacing;
- exact CI dose;
- exact amount of useful variability;
- exact whole/part decomposition;
- exact fatigue exposure for robustness;
- exact stopping threshold.

### NOT ESTABLISHED AS UNIVERSAL

- distributed > massed;
- random > blocked;
- variable > constant;
- whole > part;
- part > whole;
- more practice is always proportionally better;
- plateau means stop;
- one optimal practice schedule for all learners/tasks.
