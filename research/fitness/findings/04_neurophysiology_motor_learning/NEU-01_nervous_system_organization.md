# NEU-01 — Nervous-system organization

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-18**  
Область: 04 — Neurophysiology / Motor Learning

## 0. Core decision

Human movement is produced by distributed closed-loop sensorimotor control.

Canonical model:

`goal / context / learned policy`
`↔ cortex + basal ganglia + cerebellum`
`→ cortical/brainstem descending systems`
`↔ spinal interneurons + sensory afferents`
`→ alpha motor neurons`
`→ NMJ`
`→ skeletal-muscle activation`.

Feedback continuously modifies future output.

## 1. CNS and PNS

CNS:
- brain;
- spinal cord.

PNS:
- peripheral sensory/motor axons;
- nerves;
- ganglia.

Project rule:

**spinal cord and PNS are active parts of movement control, not passive wiring.**

## 2. Somatic nervous system

Somatic control includes:

- sensory afferents from body tissues;
- motor output to skeletal muscle;
- voluntary actions;
- automatic reflex/postural processing.

Project rule:

**somatic ≠ conscious-only.**

## 3. Autonomic nervous system

Sympathetic and parasympathetic divisions remain useful coarse categories.

But autonomic control is:

- organ specific;
- cell/circuit specific;
- dynamically regulated.

Project rule:

**sympathetic/parasympathetic are not two globally opposed master switches.**

## 4. Afferent and efferent pathways

Afferent:
- toward CNS.

Efferent:
- from CNS toward effectors.

Many peripheral nerves contain both sensory and motor axons.

Project rule:

**motor control is closed-loop: afferent feedback continuously shapes efferent output.**

## 5. Motor cortex

M1 is important for skilled voluntary movement and force control.

It interacts with:
- premotor/SMA networks;
- sensory input;
- basal ganglia;
- cerebellum;
- thalamus;
- brainstem;
- spinal circuits.

Project rule:

**M1 is a major hub, not the sole movement controller.**

## 6. Cerebellum

Useful applied functions:

- prediction;
- timing;
- coordination;
- sensorimotor calibration;
- error-based adaptation.

Project rule:

**cerebellum is a predictive/adaptive coordination network, not merely a balance center or one settled algorithm.**

## 7. Basal ganglia

Training-relevant functions include:

- reinforcement;
- vigor/movement modulation;
- habits/sequences;
- context-sensitive learned action policies;
- action-selection-related processes.

Project rule:

**“action selection” is useful shorthand, not a complete settled mechanism.**

## 8. Corticospinal pathway

CST is a major voluntary descending pathway and is especially important for skilled distal control.

But descending motor output also uses brainstem systems.

Project rule:

**CST ≠ whole descending motor system.**

## 9. Alpha motor neurons

Alpha motor neurons:

- innervate extrafusal skeletal-muscle fibers;
- receive descending, afferent and interneuronal input;
- possess intrinsic electrical properties that shape output.

Project rule:

**alpha motor neurons are final common somatic outputs, not passive relays.**

## 10. Interneurons

Spinal interneurons participate in:

- sensory integration;
- excitation/inhibition of motoneurons;
- reflexes;
- coordination;
- locomotor/postural control.

Project rule:

**interneurons transform motor signals rather than simply passing them through.**

## 11. Neuromuscular junction

NMJ is the cholinergic synapse:

`alpha motor neuron → muscle endplate`.

Project rule:

**NMJ is the neural-to-muscle interface.**

Detailed neurotransmission and excitation-contraction physiology remain PHY-02-owned.

## 12. Canonical project rules

1. Movement is distributed closed-loop control.
2. Spinal cord/PNS are not passive wiring.
3. Somatic ≠ conscious-only.
4. Autonomic control is not a two-switch system.
5. Afferent feedback is part of movement generation.
6. M1 is important but non-exclusive.
7. Cerebellum supports prediction/coordination/adaptation.
8. Basal ganglia are broader than a simple action-selection gate.
9. CST is major but non-exclusive.
10. Alpha motor neurons are active integrators.
11. Interneurons are active circuit elements.
12. NMJ is the neural-muscle handoff.

## 13. Training-relevant implications

1. **Do not explain strength simply as a stronger brain signal.**
2. **Do not ignore sensory/spinal contributions to movement.**
3. **Do not use “sympathetic on / parasympathetic off” as a complete exercise model.**
4. **Treat movement as ongoing feedback control.**
5. **Do not assign one brain region ownership of coordination, learning or force.**
6. **Do not treat CST measures as complete measures of neural drive.**
7. **Keep recruitment/rate coding analysis in NEU-02.**
8. **Keep detailed NMJ/EC-coupling physiology in PHY-02.**

## 14. What NEU-01 does not prescribe

Not owned here:

- motor-unit recruitment;
- rate coding;
- voluntary activation testing;
- cortical stimulation interpretation;
- fatigue testing;
- cueing/feedback strategy;
- motor-learning method;
- personalized neural limitation.

These remain NEU-02…NEU-20, TST, TEC and IND.

## 15. Confidence

### HIGH
- distributed sensorimotor organization;
- continuous sensory-feedback integration;
- CST as major but non-exclusive;
- alpha-motor-neuron final-common-path role;
- spinal-interneuron integration;
- NMJ neural-muscle interface.

### MODERATE-HIGH
- autonomic system is substantially more heterogeneous than binary teaching models;
- cerebellum is best treated as predictive/adaptive coordination network;
- basal-ganglia function extends beyond simple action-selection gating.

### MODERATE / CONTEXT-DEPENDENT
- exact contribution of each network to one resistance exercise;
- exact basal-ganglia computational mechanism in human training;
- one definitive cerebellar computational theory;
- practical magnitude of NMJ plasticity for healthy strength adaptation.
