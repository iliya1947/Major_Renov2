Meta-analysis of monetary delay discounting case-control studies, adult subgroup reported d=.40. This is a group contrast in valuation of delayed money, not prevalence, an individual discount parameter, motor impulsivity, all-domain preference, or direct evidence of unwillingness to pursue long goals. A shorter delay may be chosen because of subjective value, credibility, uncertainty, time perception, immediate needs or competing opportunity; design must distinguish delay aversion (escaping waiting) from temporal discounting (valuation), reward magnitude, probability and effort. Exact adult k and intervals not verified; no total N adopted. Clinical specificity not established by healthy-control comparisons.

## Operational analysis and competing models
1. Specify behavior and level: stated goal/value, anticipated reward, subjective cost/difficulty, choice, implementation, persistence, stopping, accuracy, actual output, functional consequences. Separate wanting, liking and learning as conceptual distinctions; no neurochemical inference. A person may strongly value a distant goal but struggle to translate it into action; alternatively a choice not to act may be rational under competing priorities or insufficient expected value.
2. Define impulsivity: premature motor response, inability to cancel initiated response, choice of shorter delay, decision under uncertainty, spontaneous action, emotion-linked urgency. Separate speed, risk, novelty preference and intentional rapid expert decision. Neither SST nor delay-discounting task is a proxy for all. Examine accuracy, actual consequences, current intention, context and developmental course; no label from one incident.
3. Compare at least two hypotheses: goal valuation vs delay/probability/effort cost vs planning/working-memory/inhibition/attention vs skill/unclear instructions, fatigue, stress, other conditions and environmental barriers. These can coexist. Prefer distinct predictions: changing clarity/feedback and task decomposition may improve execution while leaving valuation unchanged; a different delay/reward offer may change choice without changing execution capacity. These manipulations also affect several processes, so do not infer unique mediation; observe ordinary voluntary contexts, not covert tests or removing supports. No medication advice.
4. Research design: ADHD×IQ factorial contrast and within-person task/context comparison; examine baseline capacity and speed-accuracy, task demands, reward magnitude/delay/probability and effort separately, actual performance and report. Distinguish main effect, interaction, mediation and selection. Do not infer moderation from contrasting significance; use effect difference and uncertainty. Relative discrepancy vs absolute norm vs functional impact independent; part-whole IQ association and referral selection risks. Tests and ratings complement each other; shared method and ecological criterion limitations.
5. Compare multiple-pathway account (executive, motivational/energetic/context, learning) against single-mechanism explanation; empirical dissociations are compatible with partially shared or interacting mechanisms and do not prove discrete biological subtypes. For adult IQ150–160+ use only general hypotheses with marked extrapolation and individual evidence; no normative numbers or diagnostic rule. Developmental and clinical boundaries of 12.1 retained. Carry compensation details to GFT-14 and differential to 13/15.

### Provisional analytical rules
1. Separate value of goal, intention, initiation, execution, maintenance, inhibition of response, stopping/switching, and realized output. High goal importance plus delayed initiation can reflect initiation friction, uncertain next action, conflicting goals, learned avoidance, sleep/state, or cost/value changes; no automatic attribution to ADHD.
2. Compare incentive magnitude, frequency, delay, probability, choice and feedback independently where possible. Increased output can result from changed strategy or speed-accuracy tradeoff rather than restored capacity. Distinguish task-supplied stimulation from subjective interest and opportunity cost.
3. Separate choosing immediate reward from failed motor inhibition. Choosing immediate option may be rational under uncertainty or liquidity constraints; specify incentives and independent criteria before calling it impulsive.
4. High absolute performance can coexist with relative peer weakness and actual support costs; within-person discrepancy alone is not diagnosis or proof of compensation. Do not withdraw needed supports to test an account. Record ordinary comparable observations of initiation, output/errors, actual/subjective effort, prior goal, interruptions and alternatives; revise if predicted relationships fail.
5. Multiple-pathway models are hypotheses with potentially distinct motivational, learning, temporal and executive contributions; no single inferred dopamine level, resource allocation mechanism or high-IQ interaction. Clinical assessment boundary unchanged.
