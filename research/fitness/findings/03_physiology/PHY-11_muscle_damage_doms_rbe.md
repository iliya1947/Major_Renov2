# PHY-11 — Muscle damage / DOMS / repeated-bout effect

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-18**  
Область: 03 — Physiology

## 0. Core decision

Exercise-induced muscle damage (EIMD), DOMS, inflammation, edema and temporary force loss are related but non-equivalent responses.

Canonical model:

`unaccustomed / unusually demanding loading`
`→ tissue/contractile perturbation`
`→ functional impairment + inflammatory/repair response + edema + soreness/biochemical changes`
`→ recovery/remodeling`
`→ repeated-bout protection after similar future exposure`.

Project rule:

**damage can accompany adaptation but is not the adaptation target.**

## 1. Exercise-induced muscle damage

EIMD is a heterogeneous response, especially after novel/high-dose lengthening-biased loading.

Possible affected domains include:

- contractile function;
- sarcomeric/cytoskeletal organization;
- membrane/permeability;
- E–C coupling;
- ECM/connective interfaces;
- inflammatory/repair signaling.

Project rule:

**EIMD is not one binary lesion and has no single universal indirect marker.**

## 2. DOMS

DOMS is delayed soreness/tenderness following unfamiliar or intense loading, commonly emerging across roughly 24–72 h.

It is a perceptual/nociceptive outcome.

Project rule:

**DOMS is not a quantitative damage score.**

## 3. DOMS vs workout quality

A productive RT session does not require:

- soreness;
- high CK;
- edema;
- major strength loss.

Project rule:

**soreness is not a score of training quality.**

## 4. Inflammation

Inflammatory and immune responses can participate in repair/remodeling after unfamiliar damaging loading.

Project rule:

**acute inflammation can be part of repair without being a hypertrophy or workout-quality surrogate.**

## 5. Edema

Damage-related fluid accumulation can transiently increase:

- muscle thickness;
- CSA;
- circumference;
- MRI/ultrasound water-related signal.

Project rule:

**post-damage swelling ≠ retained hypertrophy.**

Chronic morphology testing should avoid recent high-damage sessions.

## 6. Temporary strength loss

EIMD can produce transient force/torque loss through interacting:

- contractile/E–C mechanisms;
- Ca²⁺ handling changes;
- pain-related inhibition;
- neural adjustment;
- swelling/mechanical effects.

Project rule:

**temporary force loss is functionally relevant but not a lesion-specific diagnostic marker.**

## 7. Repeated-bout effect

A prior damaging bout reduces responses to a later similar bout.

Current meta-analysis supports protection for:

- MVC;
- DOMS;
- CK.

Project rule:

**RBE is real and outcome/time specific.**

## 8. RBE duration

Protection can persist for weeks but is not permanent.

A 2026 duration study found broad protection at 3–6 weeks, selected retained effects at 9 weeks and little meaningful protection for most measured outcomes by 12 weeks under that protocol.

Project rule:

**there is no universal RBE expiration date.**

## 9. RBE mechanisms

Likely contributors include:

- peripheral contractile adaptation;
- structural/cytoskeletal remodeling;
- ECM/connective adaptation;
- altered inflammatory/repair response;
- neural/motor-unit strategy changes.

Current evidence most consistently supports peripheral contractile protection; central/motor-unit evidence is less consistent.

Project rule:

**RBE is multi-mechanistic.**

## 10. Eccentric loading

Eccentric loading can provoke high EIMD when novel or unusually demanding.

But repeated exposure reduces damage responses.

Current AEL meta-analysis does not show consistently greater CK, soreness or swelling than constant-load RT despite greater eccentric-phase stimulus.

Project rule:

**eccentric loading ≠ inevitable damage.**

## 11. Damage and hypertrophy

Current human evidence does not establish muscle damage as necessary for hypertrophy.

Hypertrophy can occur with low damage.

Early damage-related MPS can partly reflect repair rather than tissue accretion.

Project rule:

**more damage does not mean more hypertrophy.**

## 12. Why DOMS does not predict hypertrophy

DOMS fails as a hypertrophy surrogate because:

1. it is perceptual;
2. it varies widely between people;
3. it is reduced by repeated-bout protection;
4. edema can mimic size gain;
5. early MPS can reflect repair;
6. productive chronic loading continues after soreness falls.

Project rule:

**greater DOMS ≠ greater growth stimulus.**

## 13. Excessive damage

Excessive EIMD can reduce:

- subsequent force capacity;
- training quality;
- feasible frequency/volume;
- movement quality;
- adherence.

Project rule:

**manage collateral damage rather than maximize it.**

## 14. Marker hierarchy

Use multiple domains when EIMD matters.

### Functional
- MVC/torque;
- ROM;
- movement performance.

### Perceptual
- DOMS/tenderness.

### Biochemical
- CK;
- myoglobin;
- inflammatory markers.

### Morphological
- edema;
- ultrasound/MRI changes.

Project rule:

**one marker does not define EIMD severity.**

## 15. Medical boundary

Typical DOMS can involve:

- delayed soreness;
- stiffness;
- mild/moderate swelling;
- temporary ROM/force loss.

Severe or atypical symptoms require medical differential, including possible:

- strain;
- compartment syndrome;
- rhabdomyolysis;
- inflammatory myopathy;
- other pathology.

Project rule:

**do not normalize severe weakness, disproportionate pain/swelling, dark urine or systemic illness as ordinary DOMS.**

Clinical diagnosis/management → MED.

## 16. Training implications

1. **Do not chase soreness.**
2. **Do not use CK as a workout-quality score.**
3. **Do not measure hypertrophy immediately after damaging sessions.**
4. **Expect more EIMD from novel/high-dose eccentric-biased exposure.**
5. **Expect repeated exposure to reduce damage responses.**
6. **Do not interpret reduced DOMS after familiarization as reduced training effectiveness.**
7. **Use temporary performance loss as more functionally relevant information than soreness alone when recovery matters.**
8. **Progress unfamiliar high-damage loading gradually.**
9. **Treat severe/atypical symptoms as a medical boundary, not as “good soreness.”**

## 17. Canonical project rules

1. EIMD is heterogeneous.
2. DOMS ≠ damage amount.
3. CK ≠ damage amount.
4. Edema ≠ hypertrophy.
5. Soreness ≠ workout quality.
6. Damage is not required for hypertrophy.
7. More damage ≠ more growth.
8. Eccentric loading ≠ inevitable damage.
9. RBE is real and time/outcome specific.
10. RBE is multi-mechanistic.
11. Reduced DOMS after repeated exposure does not mean lost stimulus.
12. Severe/atypical postexercise symptoms belong to medical differential.

## 18. What PHY-11 does not prescribe

Not owned here:

- exact eccentric progression;
- exact DOMS threshold;
- exact recovery days;
- exact readiness marker;
- exact injury diagnosis;
- exact anti-inflammatory strategy;
- personal susceptibility to rhabdomyolysis/injury.

These remain PRO, REC, TST, MED and IND.

## 19. Confidence

### HIGH
- unaccustomed eccentric-biased loading can produce EIMD;
- DOMS is not a quantitative structural-damage measure;
- EIMD can cause edema and transient force loss;
- edema can contaminate hypertrophy measurement;
- RBE reduces MVC loss/DOMS/CK responses;
- damage is not necessary for hypertrophy;
- more DOMS is not more hypertrophy;
- severe/atypical symptoms require medical differential.

### MODERATE-HIGH
- RBE is multi-mechanistic;
- peripheral contractile protection is better supported than single central explanations;
- excessive damage can reduce subsequent training quality.

### MODERATE / CONTEXT-DEPENDENT
- exact tissue lesion represented by an indirect marker;
- exact inflammatory contribution;
- exact neural contribution to RBE;
- exact RBE duration;
- exact damage threshold for a person/protocol.
