# BIO-04 — Lactate metabolism

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-19**  
Область: 05 — Bioenergetics / Exercise Metabolism

## 0. Core decision

Lactate is a recyclable metabolic intermediate and fuel.

Project rule:

**lactate is not a dead-end metabolic waste product, not proof of anoxia, and not a standalone explanation of fatigue or DOMS.**

---

## 1. Lactate production, release and blood concentration are different

Lactate can be formed intracellularly through the reversible LDH reaction.

But the following are distinct:

- gross intracellular lactate production;
- net tissue lactate release;
- blood lactate appearance;
- blood lactate disappearance/disposal;
- blood lactate concentration.

Working muscle can simultaneously:

- release lactate;
- extract circulating lactate.

Project rule:

**net tissue release ≠ gross production.**

---

## 2. Blood lactate is a state variable, not a flux

Blood [lactate] reflects the balance of:

- production;
- tissue release/appearance;
- uptake;
- oxidation;
- gluconeogenic handling;
- transport/distribution;
- blood flow;
- sampling timing and protocol.

Project rule:

**blood [lactate] ≠ lactate-production rate.**

---

## 3. “Clearance” terminology

“Lactate clearance” can be used as shorthand.

When mechanism matters, prefer:

- disappearance;
- disposal;
- uptake/oxidation;
- gluconeogenic/storage handling.

Project rule:

**lactate disappearance is metabolic use/redistribution, not removal of useless waste.**

---

## 4. Lactate oxidation

Lactate-derived carbon can be oxidized and contribute materially to exercise metabolism.

During exercise, oxidation is a major fate of circulating lactate.

Project rule:

**lactate is an oxidizable carbohydrate-derived fuel.**

No universal percentage of exercise ATP from lactate is retained.

---

## 5. Lactate shuttle

The robust lactate-shuttle concept is:

**lactate produced in one cell/tissue can be transported and used in another.**

Relevant producer/consumer relationships include exchange among:

- glycolytic and oxidative muscle regions/fibers;
- working and less-active muscle;
- skeletal muscle and heart;
- skeletal muscle and other organs;
- muscle and gluconeogenic tissues.

Project rule:

**cell-cell/interorgan lactate shuttling is strongly supported.**

---

## 6. Intracellular mitochondrial lactate routing

One exact universal intracellular mitochondrial lactate-shuttle topology is not treated as settled.

Current mechanistic evidence shows:

- mitochondrial lactate pools can exist;
- intracellular routing is biologically real and complex;
- direct lactate fueling of the ETC is not consistently demonstrated across models/conditions.

Project rule:

**retain lactate-derived oxidative use without hard-coding one universal mitochondrial route.**

---

## 7. MCT1 and MCT4

MCT1 and MCT4 are proton-coupled monocarboxylate transporters involved in lactate/H+ transport.

Applied pattern:

- MCT1 often supports uptake in oxidative contexts;
- MCT4 often supports efflux in highly glycolytic contexts.

But transport depends on:

- lactate gradient;
- proton gradient/pH;
- transporter properties;
- metabolic state.

Project rule:

**MCT1 uptake / MCT4 export are physiological tendencies, not irreversible one-way directions.**

---

## 8. Exercise adaptation of MCTs

Exercise/training can increase skeletal-muscle MCT protein.

Human evidence is stronger/more consistent for MCT1 than MCT4.

This is retained as supporting context only.

Detailed transporter adaptation belongs BIO-16.

---

## 9. Lactate threshold is not one universal point

Several method-defined constructs exist:

- LT1;
- LT2;
- lactate turnpoint methods;
- Dmax;
- fixed concentration methods;
- OBLA;
- MLSS.

Project rule:

**always name the threshold method and test protocol.**

---

## 10. Fixed 4 mmol/L is not universal

A 4 mmol/L blood-lactate anchor can be useful as a test convention.

It is not an individual physiological constant.

Current direct data show wide repeatable interindividual blood-lactate values at MLSS.

Project rule:

**4 mmol/L ≠ universal individual lactate threshold.**

---

## 11. MLSS

MLSS is an operationally determined constant-load boundary where blood lactate remains approximately stable according to a stated protocol/criterion.

It depends on:

- bout duration;
- sampling schedule;
- allowed blood-lactate change;
- exercise modality;
- prior state;
- measurement protocol.

Project rule:

**MLSS is a protocol-defined estimate, not a molecular constant.**

---

## 12. Threshold constructs are not automatically interchangeable

LT1/LT2, MLSS, CP and other intensity-domain markers can be related.

They are not guaranteed to produce the same power/speed/intensity.

Project rule:

**related physiological boundaries ≠ numerically interchangeable tests.**

---

## 13. Blood lactate vs intramuscular lactate

Blood and muscle lactate can correlate.

They are not the same measurement.

Their relationship depends on:

- tissue production/uptake;
- transport;
- perfusion;
- sampling timing;
- fiber/recruitment pattern.

Project rule:

**blood [lactate] ≠ intramuscular [lactate].**

---

## 14. Blood lactate vs glycolytic ATP flux

Blood lactate can inform metabolic state.

In validated model-based contexts, lactate accumulation can contribute to estimates of glycolytic/anaerobic-lactic energy contribution.

But:

**one blood lactate concentration is not a universal direct measure of local glycolytic ATP production.**

---

## 15. Lactate and DOMS

Acute lactate accumulation does not cause delayed-onset muscle soreness.

DOMS develops through delayed, multifactorial tissue/neural/inflammatory mechanisms.

Project rule:

**lactate ≠ DOMS cause.**

Detailed DOMS physiology belongs REC/PHY.

---

## 16. Lactate and fatigue

High lactate commonly accompanies intense exercise.

This does not mean lactate itself is the sole cause of fatigue.

Current evidence indicates:

- high lactate alone has little direct detrimental effect on muscle performance;
- severe acidosis can contribute in some conditions;
- fatigue remains multifactorial.

Project rule:

**lactate concentration ≠ causal fatigue diagnosis.**

---

## 17. Lactate as fatigue/readiness marker

Lactate is strongly exercise responsive.

It is not specific enough to stand alone as a fatigue/readiness biomarker.

Project rule:

**use lactate as one contextual metabolic signal, not as a standalone readiness score.**

---

## 18. Lactate and acid-base status

Lactate concentration is not identical to H+ concentration.

The LDH reaction written in the lactate-forming direction consumes H+.

Project rule:

**lactate ≠ “the acid.”**

Detailed proton sources, buffering and pH-related fatigue belong BIO-05.

---

1. Lactate ≠ metabolic waste.
2. Lactate formation ≠ anoxia.
3. Lactate production ≠ net tissue release.
4. Net tissue release ≠ blood appearance.
5. Blood concentration ≠ production flux.
6. Blood concentration ≠ disposal flux.
7. “Clearance” ≠ waste excretion.
8. Lactate oxidation ≠ prior conversion to glucose.
9. Cell-cell lactate shuttle ≠ one settled universal intracellular mitochondrial route.
10. MCT1 ≠ irreversible importer.
11. MCT4 ≠ irreversible exporter.
12. One lactate-threshold method ≠ every other method.
13. 4 mmol/L ≠ universal threshold.
14. MLSS ≠ protocol-independent constant.
15. MLSS ≠ automatically identical to CP.
16. Blood lactate ≠ intramuscular lactate.
17. Blood lactate ≠ direct universal glycolytic ATP flux.
18. Lactate ≠ DOMS cause.
19. Lactate ≠ sole fatigue cause.
20. Lactate ≠ standalone readiness biomarker.
21. Lactate concentration ≠ H+ concentration.
