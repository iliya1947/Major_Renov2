## Source-version note

Current JBI Chapter 8 states that mixed-methods reviews can conceptually use:
- convergent;
- sequential
approaches.

However, because of minimal use of the sequential approach, **current JBI guidance focuses exclusively on convergent MMSR**.

Project consequence:
do not present sequential MMSR as unsupported or nonexistent, but do not claim current JBI provides equally developed operational guidance for it.

---

# Part A — Why mixed methods is a question-design issue, not just “use two evidence types”

## 1. Including quantitative + qualitative studies does not automatically make a coherent MMSR

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

JBI treats mixed-methods review as requiring:
- explicit question(s);
- explicit relationship between evidence types;
- planned integration.

Project consequence:
a review is not methodologically mixed simply because it contains:
- RCTs;
- interviews;
- surveys.

There must be a reason to integrate the different evidence forms.

---

## 2. Integration intent belongs at question-framing stage

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

Current JBI guidance states that the **nature/type of review question(s) dictates the synthesis/integration approach**.

Therefore integration strategy cannot be chosen only after results are seen.

Project rule:
before search, specify:

1. what quantitative evidence is expected to contribute;
2. what qualitative evidence is expected to contribute;
3. whether they answer the **same question** or **different linked questions**;
4. how the final integrated conclusion is intended to be produced.

---

# Part B — Convergent integrated

## 3. Convergent integrated = one phenomenon/question answerable by both evidence types

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

JBI recommends the convergent integrated approach when the review question can be answered by:
- quantitative studies;
- qualitative studies.

Typically:
- one broad review question;
- often structured with PICo;
- another framework such as PICO may be used if better suited.

### Example pattern

> What are the barriers and facilitators to maintaining a home resistance-training program?

Evidence could include:
- quantitative surveys reporting frequency/strength of barriers;
- qualitative interviews describing barriers and facilitators.

Both address the same underlying phenomenon.

---

## 4. Integrated approach requires data transformation

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

In current JBI convergent integrated MMSR:
- quantitative data are generally **qualitized** into textual descriptions;
- these are integrated with qualitative data.

Project consequence:
choosing integrated approach implies willingness to sacrifice some quantitative numerical form in order to answer one shared phenomenon-level question.

This is a synthesis consequence of the question architecture.

---

## 5. Integrated approach is not appropriate merely because two evidence types exist

### Project inference grounded in JBI
**Confidence: HIGH**

If the quantitative evidence asks:
> Does intervention A improve adherence?

and qualitative evidence asks:
> Why do users find intervention A irritating?

these may be related but are not necessarily the same question.

Trying to force both into one transformed synthesis can blur:
- causal effect;
- experience;
- mechanism.

In that situation, segregated approach may be better.

---

# Part C — Convergent segregated

## 6. Convergent segregated = different aspects/dimensions of one broader phenomenon

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

JBI recommends convergent segregated when the review has:
- separate quantitative and qualitative questions;
- focused on different aspects/dimensions of the same phenomenon.

The two evidence streams are:
1. synthesized independently;
2. then integrated/configured into an overall interpretation.

---

## 7. Separate question frameworks are expected

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

JBI specifically recommends:
- PICO for quantitative effectiveness-type question;
- PICo for qualitative experience/perception question

when using convergent segregated design.

Example:

### Quantitative
> Does automated prompting improve workout completion?

### Qualitative
> How do users experience automated prompting during home training?

These are linked but distinct questions.

---

## 8. Segregated approach preserves evidence-type meaning longer

### Project interpretation grounded in JBI
**Confidence: HIGH**

Because quantitative and qualitative streams are synthesized separately before integration, this approach preserves:

- quantitative effect estimates;
- qualitative meanings/experiences

until the final configuration stage.

Project consequence:
prefer segregated approach when:
- preserving numerical causal/effect evidence matters;
- qualitative evidence explains acceptability/context/implementation;
- evidence types answer distinct but complementary questions.

---

# Part D — Current status of sequential approaches

## 9. Sequential MMSR exists conceptually

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

JBI's methodological typology recognizes sequential designs:
one synthesis occurs after or is informed by the other.

Example concept:
qualitative synthesis identifies barriers first, then quantitative evidence is synthesized around those identified dimensions.

## 10. But current JBI operational guidance focuses on convergent approaches

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

Current 2026 JBI guidance explicitly says it focuses exclusively on the convergent approach because sequential approaches have seen minimal use.

Project rule:
if a future question truly requires sequential mixed-methods synthesis:
- do not improvise from convergent guidance;
- perform a dedicated current-methodology check;
- label methodology source/limitations explicitly.

For RES-01 default routing:
- convergent integrated;
- convergent segregated.

---

# Part E — Mixed methods study vs mixed methods review

## 11. A mixed-methods primary study does not determine the review design

### Established methodological distinction
**Confidence: HIGH**

A review may include:
- quantitative studies;
- qualitative studies;
- mixed-methods primary studies.

The review's mixed-methods design depends on:
- review question(s);
- how extracted quantitative and qualitative evidence are synthesized/integrated.

Project consequence:
do not classify the review as mixed-methods merely because one included study used mixed methods.

---

## 12. Components of mixed-methods primary studies can feed different evidence streams

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

In JBI MMSR:
- quantitative components can contribute to quantitative synthesis;
- qualitative components can contribute to qualitative synthesis;
subject to relevant appraisal/extraction rules.

Project rule:
eligibility should specify whether:
- both components are required;
- either component can contribute;
- mixed-methods studies are split by component for synthesis.

Detailed appraisal belongs later.

---

# Part F — Integration question

## 13. A MMSR needs an explicit final integration objective

### Project rule grounded in JBI
**Confidence: HIGH**

Do not stop after:
- quantitative synthesis;
- qualitative synthesis.

Before searching, state what integration is expected to answer.

Examples:

1. **Convergence**
   - do quantitative and qualitative evidence point in the same direction?

2. **Complementarity**
   - does qualitative evidence explain how/why/for whom a quantitative effect occurs?

3. **Expansion**
   - does one evidence type reveal dimensions absent in the other?

4. **Conflict**
   - do evidence streams disagree, and what does that imply?

These labels are project use of common mixed-method logic; they are not a claim that JBI mandates these four exact categories.

---

## 14. Integration must not collapse epistemically different claims

### Project methodological boundary
**Confidence: HIGH**

Example:

Quantitative:
> reminder system increased completion by X.

Qualitative:
> some users found reminders intrusive.

Correct integration:
> reminders may improve completion while creating acceptability costs for some users.

Incorrect collapse:
> qualitative dislike disproves the quantitative effect.

Likewise:
> quantitative effect does not invalidate qualitative burden.

Different evidence types can be simultaneously true.

---

# Part G — When not to use MMSR

## 15. Mixed methods is not automatically better

### Project rule grounded in JBI question-driven design
**Confidence: HIGH**

Do not choose MMSR merely because:
- both evidence types exist;
- it sounds more comprehensive.

Use MMSR only when integrated evidence changes the decision/understanding.

If the decision can be answered adequately by:
- one focused effectiveness review;
- one qualitative review;
then adding another stream may add complexity without decision value.

---

## 16. Separate reviews may be better than one MMSR

Use separate reviews when:
1. questions are only loosely related;
2. populations/interventions differ materially;
3. integration has no clear final objective;
4. evidence streams serve different downstream decisions;
5. one stream requires very different scope and would distort the other.

Project consequence:
mixed-methods review is a design choice, not a completeness badge.

# Part H — Fitness-project examples

## 17. Convergent integrated example

Question:
> What factors help or hinder sustained completion of home resistance training?

Quantitative evidence:
- frequency/prevalence of reported barriers;
- associations between barriers and completion.

Qualitative evidence:
- experiences/meanings of barriers/facilitators.

Both inform the same phenomenon.

Likely route:
**convergent integrated**.

---

## 18. Convergent segregated example

Broader decision:
> Is an adherence support strategy useful and tolerable?

### Quantitative question
> Does reminder strategy A increase session completion vs no reminders?

### Qualitative question
> How do users experience reminder strategy A and what makes it acceptable or irritating?

Then integrate:
- effectiveness;
- acceptability/experience.

Likely route:
**convergent segregated**.

# Part I — Project Mixed-Methods Question Module

Use only when question type = **MIXED METHODS**.

## Core

1. **Why are both quantitative and qualitative evidence needed?**
2. **What decision/phenomenon requires integration?**
3. **Do the evidence types answer the same question or linked distinct questions?**
4. **Chosen approach**
   - convergent integrated;
   - convergent segregated;
   - sequential/other → requires dedicated methodology check.
5. **Rationale for approach**

## If convergent integrated

6. Single overarching question
7. Question framework:
   - PICo default if suitable;
   - other justified framework if better.
8. How quantitative evidence can answer the same phenomenon
9. How qualitative evidence can answer the same phenomenon
10. Planned data-transformation logic
11. What integration is intended to establish

## If convergent segregated

12. Quantitative question + framework
13. Qualitative question + framework
14. Why the two questions are linked
15. Separate eligibility rules for each stream
16. Separate synthesis plans
17. Final integration question

## Both approaches

18. Eligible mixed-methods primary studies
19. How their quantitative/qualitative components contribute
20. Expected integration relation:
   - convergence;
   - complementarity;
   - expansion;
   - conflict/discordance;
   - other explicitly defined relation.
21. What the integrated conclusion may inform
22. What the integrated conclusion may **not** claim

# Part J — Architecture correction

## 19. Mixed Methods must be a router, not a giant combined form

Final QUESTION_CARD should not append:
- all PICO fields;
- all PICo fields;
- all integration fields
to every topic.

Routing:

### Same phenomenon answerable by both evidence types
→ Mixed Methods / Convergent Integrated module.

### Different linked quantitative + qualitative questions
→ Mixed Methods / Convergent Segregated module.

### Only one evidence type needed
→ use its ordinary question module instead.

---

## 20. Practical rules produced by PASS-09C

1. Both evidence types alone do not make a coherent MMSR.
2. Specify integration intent before search.
3. Let question type determine mixed-methods synthesis approach.
4. Use convergent integrated when both quantitative and qualitative evidence answer the same broad phenomenon/question.
5. Use convergent segregated when separate quantitative and qualitative questions address different but linked dimensions.
6. Integrated approach commonly uses one broad question and often PICo.
7. Segregated approach commonly uses separate PICO + PICo questions.
8. Preserve separate evidence claims until appropriate integration.
9. Do not treat qualitative and quantitative findings as mutually invalidating merely because they differ.
10. Current JBI operational guidance focuses on convergent approaches.
11. Sequential MMSR requires a separate current-methodology check if later needed.
12. Mixed-methods primary studies do not determine the review design.
13. State the final integration question/objective explicitly.
14. Do not use MMSR as a completeness badge.
15. Add a conditional Mixed-Methods Question Module to the modular QUESTION_CARD.
