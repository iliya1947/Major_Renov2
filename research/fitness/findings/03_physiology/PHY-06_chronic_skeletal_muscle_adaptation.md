# PHY-06 — Chronic skeletal-muscle adaptation

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-18**  
Область: 03 — Physiology

## 0. Decision

Создать модель retained skeletal-muscle adaptation, достаточную для ответа на вопрос:

> **какие structural/functional adaptations происходят и с какой time course?**

и для downstream hypertrophy mechanisms, strength, power, fatigue, programming, testing and individualization.

## 1. Core model

Chronic skeletal-muscle adaptation is multidimensional:

`repeated training exposure → retained remodeling across size + PCSA/architecture + microvasculature + mitochondria/metabolic enzymes + substrate stores + ECM + damage resistance`

These dimensions:

- do not change in lockstep;
- can differ in direction and magnitude;
- operate on different clocks;
- depend on modality/dose/baseline state;
- require distinct measurements.

Project rule:

**muscle adaptation ≠ hypertrophy alone.**

## 2. Hypertrophy

Resistance training can increase:

- whole-muscle volume;
- anatomical CSA;
- muscle thickness;
- fiber CSA.

Project rule:

**hypertrophy means retained tissue enlargement, not acute pump/swelling.**

Early postexercise morphology can be contaminated by fluid shifts.

Detailed molecular mechanisms → PHY-07.

## 3. Hypertrophy time course

Hypertrophy becomes detectable across repeated weeks-to-months.

Exact onset is not universal because it depends on:

- measurement method;
- error;
- edema control;
- training status;
- muscle;
- program;
- sampling schedule.

Project rule:

**do not assign one universal week when hypertrophy “starts.”**

## 4. Anatomical CSA vs PCSA vs specific tension

These are separate constructs.

### Anatomical CSA
Geometric muscle cross-section.

### PCSA
Contractile tissue effectively arranged in parallel; depends on muscle volume and architecture assumptions.

### Specific tension
Force normalized to contractile cross-sectional area.

Project rule:

**bigger anatomical CSA ≠ identical PCSA ≠ identical force quality.**

## 5. Fascicle length

Fascicle length can change with training, but response is:

- muscle-specific;
- region-specific;
- protocol-specific;
- method-dependent.

Project rule:

**fascicle-length increase is not a mandatory marker of successful training adaptation.**

## 6. Pennation angle

Pennation can remodel with training/hypertrophy, but pooled effects are not uniformly large or present across populations.

Project rule:

**pennation angle is context-dependent architecture, not a universal adaptation-quality score.**

## 7. Muscle quality

“Muscle quality” can refer to:

- strength per muscle size/mass;
- specific tension;
- echo intensity;
- CT/MRI attenuation;
- intramuscular fat/composition metrics.

Project rule:

**never report muscle quality without stating how it was defined.**

## 8. Capillarization

RT can increase skeletal-muscle capillary supply in some populations/protocols.

Relevant metrics include:

- capillary contacts;
- capillary-to-fiber ratio;
- CFPE;
- capillary density per tissue area.

These are not interchangeable.

Project rule:

**capillary supply can improve while density changes differently because hypertrophy changes the denominator.**

Broader endurance/HIT/SIT evidence must not be treated as an RT effect estimate.

## 9. Mitochondrial adaptation

RT can alter:

- respiratory capacity;
- ETC proteins;
- fusion/fission machinery;
- redox handling;
- mitochondrial content/relative volume.

Hypertrophy can reduce relative mitochondrial volume density by dilution even without net mitochondrial loss.

Project rule:

**mitochondrial function, absolute content and relative volume density are different outcomes.**

## 10. Enzymatic profile

Training can alter:

- glycolytic enzymes;
- oxidative enzymes;
- pathway-specific enzyme activities.

Project rule:

**RT does not impose one universal “more glycolytic” or “more oxidative” profile.**

One enzyme cannot define whole-muscle metabolism.

Detailed metabolism → BIO.

## 11. Substrate stores

Chronic RT can alter resting:

- glycogen;
- phosphocreatine/creatine pool;
- high-energy phosphate stores

in some protocols.

Magnitude depends strongly on nutrition, timing and program.

Project rule:

**chronic storage capacity ≠ acute substrate depletion.**

Confidence for generic RT-specific magnitude: **MODERATE**.

## 12. Extracellular matrix

Skeletal-muscle ECM responds to repeated loading through:

- collagen turnover;
- MMP/TIMP remodeling;
- matrix-related gene expression;
- structural/adhesion remodeling.

Project rule:

**ECM is an adaptation compartment, but acute collagen turnover does not prove a specific chronic matrix architecture.**

Exact chronic collagen quantity/crosslinking remains less certain than fiber hypertrophy.

## 13. Damage resistance

Repeated exposure increases resistance to later exercise-induced muscle damage.

Repeated-bout protection includes reduced later:

- MVC loss;
- DOMS;
- CK response.

Project rule:

**lower soreness/damage after familiarization is expected adaptation, not evidence of ineffective training.**

## 14. Regional adaptation

Hypertrophy and architecture can vary:

- proximally/distally;
- between muscle heads;
- across superficial/deep regions;
- between agonists.

Project rule:

**one measurement site cannot automatically represent whole-muscle adaptation.**

Regional targeting effects must be measured, not assumed.

## 15. Multi-clock adaptation model

### Early
Can include:
- repeated-bout protection;
- early capillary/angiogenic remodeling;
- neural adaptation outside primary PHY-06 ownership.

### Weeks
Can include:
- retained hypertrophy becoming detectable;
- some architecture/PCSA change;
- capillary remodeling;
- enzyme/mitochondrial remodeling.

### Months+
Can continue:
- hypertrophy;
- architecture;
- ECM remodeling;
- metabolic/substrate phenotype;
- tissue-specific structural maturation.

Project rule:

**there is no single skeletal-muscle adaptation clock.**

## 16. Measurement safeguards

1. Hypertrophy → measure after acute swelling resolves.
2. Architecture → measure the relevant muscle/region directly.
3. PCSA → state model assumptions.
4. Muscle quality → define the metric.
5. Capillaries → state numerator/denominator.
6. Mitochondria → distinguish function/content/volume density.
7. Enzymes → identify pathway/activity.
8. Substrates → standardize diet/timing.
9. ECM → distinguish turnover biomarkers from chronic structure.
10. Regionality → use multiple sites if whole-muscle inference is intended.

Project rule:

**different adaptation assays are not interchangeable.**

## 17. Training-relevant implications

1. **Adaptation is multi-compartment, not one-dimensional.**
2. **Hypertrophy must be separated from acute swelling.**
3. **Size, PCSA and specific tension answer different questions.**
4. **Architecture can adapt without a universal fascicle/pennation pattern.**
5. **Muscle quality requires an explicit definition.**
6. **RT can adapt capillary supply; density alone can mislead.**
7. **Mitochondrial function can improve despite relative-volume dilution.**
8. **Enzyme/substrate adaptation depends on protocol/nutrition.**
9. **ECM adapts but exact chronic structure is less certain.**
10. **Repeated-bout protection is a real chronic adaptation.**
11. **Regional adaptation means one-site monitoring has limits.**
12. **Different outcomes require different reassessment intervals.**
13. **PHY-05 acute markers do not substitute for measuring retained chronic outcomes.**

1. Exact first-detectable hypertrophy time.
2. Exact PCSA contribution to strength.
3. Exact architecture response by exercise/muscle.
4. Exact RT-specific capillary-density trajectory.
5. Exact mitochondrial content-vs-dilution balance.
6. Exact enzyme/substrate magnitude.
7. Exact chronic ECM phenotype.
8. Exact regional targeting effects.
9. Exact personal adaptation clocks.

Project rule:

**uncertainty is preserved rather than converted into fixed timelines or universal morphology rules.**

## 21. Canonical project rules

1. Chronic adaptation ≠ acute response.
2. Muscle adaptation ≠ hypertrophy alone.
3. Hypertrophy ≠ swelling.
4. Anatomical CSA ≠ PCSA ≠ specific tension.
5. Architecture is plastic but context dependent.
6. Muscle quality must be operationally defined.
7. Capillary supply ≠ capillary density.
8. Mitochondrial function ≠ mitochondrial volume density.
9. One enzyme ≠ whole metabolic phenotype.
10. Chronic substrate storage ≠ acute depletion.
11. ECM remodeling is real but exact chronic structure is less certain.
12. Repeated-bout protection is a chronic adaptation.
13. One site ≠ whole-muscle adaptation.
14. There is no universal adaptation clock.
