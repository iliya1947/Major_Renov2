## 3. Learning vs performance audit

PASS — CORE RULE.

The learning-performance distinction remains essential.

Observable performance during practice can be altered by:

- feedback;
- motivation;
- fatigue;
- warm-up;
- task familiarity;
- temporary strategy;
- attentional state.

Therefore:

`better practice performance`
does not by itself prove
`durable learning`.

The 2026 random-vs-blocked study (PMID 42691817) is a direct contemporary example:

- blocked practice produced better end-of-practice performance;
- random practice produced better 24-h retention;
- no meaningful group difference appeared at 5 min or 6 h.

This demonstrates that both the acquisition-retention dissociation and the choice of retention interval matter.

Canonical rule:

**motor learning is inferred from durable capability, not from acquisition performance alone.**

No blocker.

## 4. Acquisition audit

PASS.

Acquisition is retained as the performance trajectory during practice.

It is useful for describing:

- immediate responsiveness to practice;
- error reduction;
- current speed/accuracy;
- short-term strategy;
- fatigue or guidance effects.

But acquisition does not establish durable learning.

The 2025/2026 faded-vs-constant KR RCT (PMID 41353792) reinforces this boundary:

- both groups improved immediately after acquisition;
- the faded-KR group later outperformed constant-KR at 24 and 48 h retention and transfer.

Canonical rule:

**a practice method can produce similar immediate performance but different delayed learning.**

No blocker.

## 5. Retention audit

PASS.

Retention is persistence of a practiced capability after a delay without substantial re-practice.

Interpretation requires explicit specification of:

- delay;
- test task;
- feedback/instruction status;
- reminder/warm-up trials;
- performance metric.

The 2025 meta-analysis (PMID 40455501) confirms that procedural skill decay is heterogeneous across:

- tasks;
- outcomes;
- retention intervals;
- intermittent use;
- instructions;
- complexity.

Therefore:

- no universal motor-skill half-life is retained;
- one retained dimension does not prove all skill components are retained.

Canonical rule:

**retention is task-, outcome-, delay- and test-condition-specific.**

No blocker.

---

## 6. Transfer audit

PASS.

Transfer requires performance in an untrained target condition.

Examples include changes in:

- load;
- speed;
- direction;
- limb;
- equipment;
- task dynamics;
- environment.

PMID 42607302 shows that practice-dependent execution improvements can be retained and transferred, but transfer was constrained by movement direction.

The 2025 RT meta-analysis (PMID 40314751) provides direct resistance-training specificity evidence:

- trained dynamic strength improved substantially;
- transfer to non-trained isometric strength was smaller.

This evidence cannot isolate motor skill from muscular adaptation, but it strongly supports task specificity of strength expression.

Canonical rule:

**successful retention of the trained task does not guarantee transfer to another strength/motor task.**

No blocker.

---

## 7. Generalization audit

PASS WITH TERMINOLOGY LIMIT.

The project keeps an operational distinction:

- **generalization** = expression of learning across related untrained values/contexts within a task space;
- **transfer** = broader benefit in a different target condition/task.

This is explicitly a project convention because literature usage overlaps.

Closure-critical findings:

- PMID 42799321: generalization depends on inferred goals and psychological/context similarity, not only physical similarity;
- PMID 43003929: extended practice increased long-term retention while narrowing angular generalization.

Therefore:

**retention strength and generalization breadth are separable properties.**

No blocker.

## 8. Automaticity audit

PASS WITH MEASUREMENT LIMIT.

The 2025 multilayer model (PMID 41386165) supports automaticity as an emergent, multilayer, gradual process.

The 2026 sequence-learning study (PMID 41844636) is an important corrective:

- trained sequences became faster;
- dual-task cost did not simply disappear;
- attentional demand could remain or increase for trained sequence execution.

Therefore:

- smooth performance ≠ complete automaticity;
- automaticity ≠ zero attention;
- one dual-task result ≠ universal automaticity score.

Canonical rule:

**automaticity is graded reorganization/reduction of control demand, not a binary autonomous state.**

No blocker.

---

## 9. Relearning / savings audit

PASS.

Savings is faster or more efficient re-acquisition after prior learning.

It is distinct from overt retention.

The 2026 human fMRI study (PMID 41805913) supports:

- persistent neural changes after behavioral effects fade;
- faster relearning associated with reinstatement of prior activity structure.

However exact causal mechanism remains unresolved.

The 2026 MotorNet paper (PMID 42126916) provides a computational demonstration that savings can arise through persistent high-dimensional preparatory activity without explicit contextual cues.

Because it is an RNN model, it is retained as:

- mechanistic plausibility;
- not direct biological proof.

Canonical rule:

**savings demonstrates retained learning history but does not equal full retention or prove one neural memory mechanism.**

No blocker.

---

## 10. Fatigue-robustness audit

PASS.

The 2026 systematic review (PMID 42480681) found fatigue effects on motor learning to be heterogeneous.

Across the reviewed evidence:

- fatigue often impaired acquisition;
- some outcomes were neutral/mixed;
- retention effects varied by fatigue modality and task;
- local neuromuscular fatigue appeared more consistently disruptive than cardiovascular fatigue.

The 2026 fatigue-practice study (PMID 42811472) found:

- both fatigue and control groups learned the tracking task;
- practice after cardiovascular fatigue did not reliably impair learning;
- it also did not produce a clear group-level fatigue-resilience benefit.

Canonical rule:

**fatigue robustness must be demonstrated under the relevant fatigue state; it cannot be inferred from rested retention or assumed from practicing while fatigued.**

No blocker.

---

## 11. Pressure/stress robustness audit

PASS.

The 2026 ESSA position statement (PMID 42271117) explicitly treats pressure as capable of:

- facilitative effects;
- debilitative effects.

The 2026 meta-analysis (PMID 41951087) found pressure-countering interventions improved:

- motor accuracy;
- executional success;
- target deviation,

with stronger/more consistent effects in closed-skill tasks.

The 2024 mild-anxiety training study (PMID 38376935) found:

- anxiety-trained participants maintained putting performance across pressure tests;
- control participants deteriorated under pressure.

This supports pressure-specific practice in some contexts, but not a universal prescription.

Canonical rule:

**pressure robustness is a separate context-specific performance property; calm retention does not prove it.**

No blocker.

## 12. Exercise technique as motor skill audit

PASS.

The project retains technique as an executable sensorimotor capability involving:

- perception;
- coordination;
- prediction;
- feedback;
- force control;
- practice;
- retention;
- task adaptation.

It is not reduced to declarative knowledge of instructions.

Direct squat evidence (PMID 37916599) is closure-critical:

- novices practiced squat with visual, tactile or no augmented feedback;
- several kinematic/COP variables improved during acquisition;
- in the visual-feedback group, specific changes remained at delayed tests up to 3 days or 1 week depending on the variable.

This establishes that at least some squat-technique variables can show:

- acquisition;
- delayed retention;
- feedback-dependent learning.

The broader RT feedback review (PMID 37410360) supports both acute performance effects and chronic technical/adaptation effects, but acute improvements alone are not treated as learning.

Canonical rule:

**exercise technique is a practiced motor skill, not merely verbal-rule knowledge.**

No blocker.

## 13. Technique / universal-template boundary audit

PASS AND CONSISTENT WITH NEU-09.

NEU-09 established:

- motor abundance;
- task-specific coordination;
- multiple valid motor solutions;
- variability ≠ automatic error.

Therefore NEU-11 cannot define learning as convergence toward one universal geometry.

Technique learning is better framed as:

> more reliable control of task-relevant variables within acceptable constraints across relevant conditions.

Exact acceptable constraints remain TEC/MEC.

Canonical rule:

**learned technique can be stable and transferable without every repetition being kinematically identical.**

No blocker.

## 14. Measurement non-equivalence audit

PASS.

The following are explicitly non-interchangeable:

### Acquisition performance
Describes practice-phase output.

### Immediate post-test
Can still contain short-lived performance/guidance effects.

### Delayed retention
Tests durability under specified conditions.

### Transfer
Tests an explicitly untrained target condition.

### Generalization
Tests expression across related task/context dimensions.

### Dual-task cost
Probes attentional demand/automaticity but is not a universal automaticity score.

### Savings
Tests reacquisition advantage, not full retention.

### Fatigued performance
Tests state-dependent robustness.

### Pressure performance
Tests pressure-context robustness.

Project rule:

**motor-learning claims must name the outcome dimension being tested; “learning” cannot be inferred from a single generic performance score.**

No blocker.
