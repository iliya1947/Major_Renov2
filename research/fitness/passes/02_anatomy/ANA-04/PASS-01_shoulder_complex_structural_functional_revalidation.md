## 2. Canonical shoulder-complex model

For this project:

### True synovial joints

1. **Sternoclavicular (SC)**;
2. **Acromioclavicular (AC)**;
3. **Glenohumeral (GH)**.

### Functional articulation

4. **Scapulothoracic (ST)** — movement/gliding of scapula over thoracic wall through intervening soft-tissue layers; not a conventional bone-to-bone synovial joint.

Project rule:

**"shoulder" ≠ GH joint alone.**

Total upper-limb motion is distributed through clavicle, scapula, humerus and thorax.

---

## 3. Structural load / motion chain

Useful map:

`sternum/first rib ↔ SC joint ↔ clavicle ↔ AC/CC complex ↔ scapula ↔ GH joint ↔ humerus`

ST articulation provides the functional scapula–thorax interface.

This means:

1. clavicle is the principal bony axial–appendicular bridge;
2. scapula is a mobile socket/orientation platform;
3. GH motion occurs relative to a glenoid whose position itself changes;
4. thoracic geometry provides the surface over which scapula moves.

---

## 4. Clavicle

Current 2026 anatomy reference describes the clavicle as an S/sigmoid-shaped long bone connecting axial and appendicular skeletons.

Project structural roles:

1. axial–appendicular bony connection through SC;
2. strut maintaining lateral position of shoulder girdle relative to thorax;
3. load-transfer pathway;
4. muscle/ligament attachment platform.

Key landmarks:
- medial/sternal end;
- lateral/acromial end;
- shaft;
- conoid tubercle;
- trapezoid line;
- costal tuberosity;
- subclavian groove.

Relevant attachments include:
- pectoralis major;
- deltoid;
- trapezius;
- sternocleidomastoid;
- subclavius.

Current source also notes normal variation in clavicular length, curvature and side-to-side morphology.

Project rule:
**clavicle shape is not assumed identical between people or sides.**

---

## 5. Scapula

Scapula = flat triangular bone on posterolateral thoracic cage.

Key landmarks:

1. borders — superior, medial, lateral;
2. angles — superior, inferior, lateral;
3. spine;
4. acromion;
5. coracoid;
6. glenoid cavity;
7. supraspinous fossa;
8. infraspinous fossa;
9. subscapular fossa;
10. supraglenoid/infraglenoid tubercles;
11. suprascapular/spinoglenoid notches.

Structural roles:
- glenoid socket base;
- large muscular attachment platform;
- mobile orientation platform for GH joint.

---

## 6. Proximal humerus

Relevant structures:

1. humeral head;
2. anatomical neck;
3. surgical neck;
4. greater tubercle;
5. lesser tubercle;
6. intertubercular/bicipital groove;
7. deltoid tuberosity more distally.

Rotator-cuff tendon attachment pattern:

- supraspinatus → greater tubercle;
- infraspinatus → greater tubercle;
- teres minor → greater tubercle;
- subscapularis → lesser tubercle.

This pattern is structurally sufficient here; footprint microanatomy is not needed for training decisions.

---

## 7. Sternoclavicular joint

SC joint is a synovial saddle-shaped articulation involving:

- medial clavicle;
- manubrium;
- first costal-cartilage region.

It is the primary skeletal connection between upper limb and axial skeleton.

Contains:
- fibrocartilaginous articular disc;
- fibrous capsule.

Main stabilizing structures:

1. anterior sternoclavicular ligament;
2. posterior sternoclavicular ligament;
3. interclavicular ligament;
4. costoclavicular ligament.

Clavicular motion includes combinations of:

- elevation/depression;
- protraction/retraction;
- axial rotation.

Project rule:
exact angles are biomechanical/context-dependent, not anatomical constants.

---

## 8. Acromioclavicular joint

Current 2026 reference describes AC as a planar synovial articulation between:

- lateral clavicle;
- acromion.

It contributes to scapular mobility relative to clavicle.

Relevant structures:

1. fibrous capsule;
2. synovial lining;
3. acromioclavicular ligament;
4. coracoclavicular ligament complex:
   - conoid;
   - trapezoid.

Current source treats the CC complex as the major extrinsic stabilizing connection maintaining clavicle–scapula spatial relation.

Project distinction:

**CC ligaments stabilize the AC region but do not themselves form a separate conventional synovial joint.**

---

## 9. Coracoacromial arch

Coracoacromial ligament spans:
- coracoid;
- acromion.

Together with adjacent osseous structures it forms the superior arch over the cuff/humeral-head region.

It is **not** a separate joint.

Detailed subacromial contact/impingement mechanics → MEC/MED.

---

## 10. Scapulothoracic articulation

ST articulation = functional scapular gliding over posterolateral thoracic wall.

It is not a true synovial joint.

Intervening structures include:

- subscapularis;
- serratus anterior;
- bursae / gliding planes.

Project rule:

**scapula is not pinned to the thorax; it is dynamically positioned on a moving 3D surface.**

ANA-03 already established that thoracic shape itself varies normally.

---

## 11. Scapular motion vocabulary

3D scapular motion includes:

1. upward/downward rotation;
2. internal/external rotation;
3. anterior/posterior tilt;
4. elevation/depression;
5. protraction/retraction as translational/compound descriptions.

Project caution:

"protraction" or "retraction" is not a pure one-axis slide in vivo.

Real scapular motion combines rotations and translations.

---

## 12. Glenohumeral joint

GH joint:
- humeral head ↔ glenoid;
- ball-and-socket;
- diarthrodial;
- multiaxial;
- highly mobile.

Current GH and rotator-cuff references describe the large humeral head / relatively small glenoid relationship and reliance on static + dynamic stabilizers.

Project wording:

**high mobility with limited osseous congruence**, not "fragile by design".

---

## 13. Glenoid labrum

Labrum = fibrocartilaginous rim surrounding glenoid.

Structural roles:

1. deepens/enlarges effective socket;
2. contributes to capsulolabral stability;
3. interfaces with capsule/ligament structures;
4. relates superiorly to long-head biceps attachment complex.

Labrum ≠ meniscus.

Pathology/tears → MED.

---

## 14. GH capsule and ligaments

Fibrous capsule extends around GH articulation from glenoid-region attachment to humeral anatomical-neck region.

Current NCBI reference emphasizes that capsulolabral thickened bands — glenohumeral ligaments — become taut differently depending on:

- arm abduction;
- humeral rotation;
- translation direction.

Core ligament structures:

1. SGHL;
2. MGHL;
3. IGHL complex;
4. coracohumeral ligament.

Project rule:

**GH ligaments are position-dependent restraints, not ropes with one fixed action.**

---

## 15. Rotator interval

Functional anatomical region between:

- supraspinatus superiorly;
- subscapularis inferiorly;

with associated:
- capsule;
- coracohumeral / superior GH ligament structures;
- long-head biceps relationship.

This level is enough for ANA-04.

Detailed pathology → MED.

---

## 16. Shoulder bursae

Relevant bursae:

1. subacromial/subdeltoid;
2. subscapular;
3. subcoracoid.

Function:
reduce friction and permit gliding between moving tissues.

Project rule:

**bursa presence ≠ bursitis.**

---

## 17. Static / dynamic stabilizers

Useful functional classification.

### Static contributors

- bony geometry;
- labrum;
- capsule;
- glenohumeral ligaments;
- intra-articular pressure mechanisms.

### Dynamic contributors

- rotator cuff;
- deltoid;
- long-head biceps in relevant contexts;
- periscapular muscles through glenoid/scapular orientation.

This is a functional categorization, not a rigid anatomical partition.

---

## 18. Rotator cuff

Current 2026 reference confirms four muscles:

1. supraspinatus;
2. infraspinatus;
3. teres minor;
4. subscapularis.

All originate on scapula and insert on proximal humerus.

Their tendons blend with GH capsule, forming a musculotendinous cuff around anterior/superior/posterior joint regions.

Core project concept:

**rotator cuff contributes to both movement and dynamic humeral-head stabilization/centering.**

Detailed angle-specific forces → MEC.

---

## 19. Supraspinatus

Origin:
- supraspinous fossa.

Insertion:
- superior greater-tubercle region.

Innervation:
- suprascapular nerve.

Broad roles:
- contributes to humeral elevation/abduction;
- contributes to GH stabilization.

Project rule:

Do **not** encode "supraspinatus owns exactly first 15° of abduction".

Muscle contributions overlap continuously.

---

## 20. Infraspinatus

Origin:
- infraspinous fossa.

Insertion:
- greater-tubercle region.

Innervation:
- suprascapular nerve.

Broad roles:
- lateral/external rotation;
- dynamic GH stabilization.

---

## 21. Teres minor

Origin:
- posterior/lateral scapular border region.

Insertion:
- inferior greater-tubercle region.

Innervation:
- axillary nerve.

Broad roles:
- lateral rotation;
- dynamic stabilization;
- possible adduction contribution depending position.

Teres minor ≠ teres major.

---

## 22. Subscapularis

Origin:
- subscapular fossa.

Insertion:
- lesser tubercle / adjacent capsular region.

Innervation:
- upper/lower subscapular nerves.

Broad roles:
- medial/internal rotation;
- anterior dynamic stabilization;
- adduction contribution depending position.

---

## 23. Cuff as a coordinated system

Anatomical distribution:
- subscapularis anteriorly;
- supraspinatus superiorly;
- infraspinatus/teres minor posteriorly.

This arrangement supports coordinated compression/centering of humeral head while larger muscles generate arm movement.

Project caution:

Do not turn this into a fixed numerical "force-couple equation" inside anatomy.

Exact force vectors → MEC.

---

## 24. Deltoid

Broad regions:

1. anterior/clavicular;
2. middle/acromial;
3. posterior/spinal.

Origin:
- lateral clavicle;
- acromion;
- scapular spine.

Insertion:
- deltoid tuberosity.

Innervation:
- axillary nerve.

Broad action tendencies:
- anterior → flexion / medial-rotation contribution;
- middle → elevation/abduction contribution;
- posterior → extension / lateral-rotation contribution.

Moment arms vary with arm position → MEC.

---

## 25. Trapezius

Attachments:
- occipital/nuchal/C7–T12 region;
- lateral clavicle, acromion, scapular spine.

Motor innervation:
- spinal accessory nerve;
- cervical contributions for sensory/proprioceptive components.

Regional functions include combinations of:
- elevation;
- depression;
- retraction;
- upward rotation;
- scapular stabilization.

Project rule:

**upper trapezius ≠ "shrug muscle only".**

---

## 26. Serratus anterior

Current anatomy source:
- origin typically ribs 1–8 or 1–9, with variation;
- insertion along anterior medial border / inferior-angle region;
- long thoracic nerve C5–C7.

Core functions:
- protraction;
- holds scapula against thoracic wall;
- contributes strongly to upward rotation.

Anatomical variation in rib digitations is normal.

---

## 27. Rhomboids

Rhomboid major/minor connect axial spine to medial scapular border.

Broad roles:
- retraction;
- downward-rotation contribution;
- stabilization against thorax.

Detailed task-specific activation → PHY/MEC, not anatomy.

---

## 28. Levator scapulae

Origin:
- C1–C4 transverse-process region.

Insertion:
- superior medial scapular border / superior angle.

Broad roles:
- elevation;
- downward-rotation contribution.

Also forms a direct cervical–scapular anatomical link.

---

## 29. Pectoralis minor / subclavius

### Pectoralis minor

Origin:
- ribs 3–5 region.

Insertion:
- coracoid.

Acts on scapula rather than humerus.

Broad contributions:
- anterior/inferior scapular motion;
- protraction/depression patterns depending task.

### Subclavius

Origin:
- first rib/costal cartilage.

Insertion:
- inferior middle clavicle.

Role:
- anchors/depresses clavicle;
- contributes to SC stability.

---

## 30. Axiohumeral muscles

### Pectoralis major

Broad heads:
- clavicular;
- sternocostal.

Insertion:
- lateral intertubercular-groove region.

Broad humeral actions:
- adduction;
- medial rotation;
- horizontal adduction;
- clavicular fibers can contribute to flexion.

### Latissimus dorsi

Broad attachments link humerus to:
- lower thoracic spine;
- thoracolumbar fascia;
- iliac region;
- lower ribs, variably.

Humeral insertion:
- intertubercular-groove region.

Broad actions:
- extension;
- adduction;
- medial rotation.

### Teres major

Scapula → proximal humerus.

Broad actions:
- adduction;
- medial rotation;
- extension contribution.

Project rule:

**teres major is not rotator cuff.**

## 31. Coracobrachialis boundary

Coracoid → medial humerus.

Contributes to GH:
- flexion;
- adduction.

Detailed arm anatomy belongs to ANA-05.

## 32. Long head of biceps at shoulder

Long-head biceps tendon:
- attaches at supraglenoid/superior-labral complex;
- courses through GH region;
- continues through intertubercular groove.

Current rotator-cuff reference includes long-head biceps among dynamic GH stabilizing contributors.

Project rule:

Biceps is not anatomically "elbow only".

Detailed biceps anatomy → ANA-05.

## 33. Scapular upward-rotation coordination

Major muscular contributors include:
- serratus anterior;
- trapezius regions.

Rhomboids/levator and other muscles provide task-dependent opposing/stabilizing components.

Project prohibition:

Do not model scapular upward rotation as one isolated muscle action.

## 34. Scapulohumeral coordination

This is the major update checkpoint.

The 2025 systematic review/meta-regression reports:

- substantial interindividual variability in scapular kinematics;
- no universal "setting phase";
- no constant universal scapulohumeral ratio;
- estimated ratios themselves depend on the movement/analysis framework.

Therefore:

**scapulohumeral rhythm = dynamic coordination pattern, not a fixed gear ratio.**

Project does not replace the old 2:1/3:1 teaching myth with a new single number.

---

## 35. Humerothoracic vs glenohumeral elevation

"Arm elevation" can describe humerus relative to thorax.

Total humerothoracic elevation is distributed across:

1. GH motion;
2. scapular rotation/translation;
3. AC motion;
4. SC/clavicular motion;
5. thoracic contribution depending task.

Project rule:

ROM measurements must specify the reference frame.

---

## 36. Scapular plane

Scapula lies obliquely on thoracic wall.

Therefore natural humeral elevation often occurs in an oblique scapular plane rather than strict frontal plane.

"Scaption" is useful descriptive terminology.

No single universal scapular-plane angle is encoded.

## 38. Scapular kinematics — evidence boundary

The 2025 systematic review found broad variability in asymptomatic shoulder kinematics and very low-certainty group differences for some resting scapular variables in rotator-cuff-related shoulder pain.

Project conclusion:

1. do not diagnose "dyskinesis" from one visual pattern alone;
2. do not use one fixed ratio as normality threshold;
3. preserve individual/contextual variability.

Clinical diagnosis → MED/TST.

## 39. Innervation — minimum map

Key nerves:

1. suprascapular → supraspinatus / infraspinatus;
2. axillary → deltoid / teres minor;
3. upper/lower subscapular → subscapularis; lower also teres major;
4. long thoracic → serratus anterior;
5. dorsal scapular → rhomboids + levator contribution;
6. spinal accessory → trapezius motor;
7. thoracodorsal → latissimus dorsi;
8. medial/lateral pectoral → pectoral muscles;
9. nerve to subclavius → subclavius.

Detailed brachial plexus architecture → later neural-anatomy topic.

## 40. Training-relevant structural implications

Anatomy alone supports these statements:

1. presses/pulls are not GH-only movements;
2. scapula and clavicle are expected to move during many arm movements;
3. rotator cuff stabilizes dynamically while also producing movement;
4. shoulder position changes multi-joint muscle geometry;
5. arm-isolation exercises may still involve shoulder structures;
6. "shoulders pinned back" cannot be derived as a universal anatomical rule;
7. one observed scapular pattern is not automatically abnormal.

No exercise prescription is made here.

- instability;

## 42. What changed versus legacy ANA-04

### Confirmed

Legacy core architecture remains valid:

- SC/AC/GH + functional ST model;
- clavicle as axial–appendicular bridge;
- scapula as mobile socket platform;
- labrum/capsule/ligament architecture;
- cuff composition;
- major periscapular / axiohumeral muscles;
- long-head biceps shoulder relationship;
- distributed arm elevation.

### Strengthened / current

1. 2026 clavicle source explicitly documents normal side/sex-related morphology variation.
2. 2026 AC source confirms planar synovial AC structure and conoid/trapezoid CC complex.
3. 2026 rotator-cuff source confirms four cuff muscles and dynamic GH stabilization role.
4. 2025 scapular meta-analysis rejects both universal setting phase and constant scapulohumeral ratio.
5. Normal scapular kinematic variation is explicitly retained as part of the anatomy-functional baseline.

### Tightened

Legacy phrasing such as "high mobility at expense of stability" is retained only as a broad structural trade-off and must not be converted into:
- fragility claim;
- injury prediction;
- technique prescription.

## 43. Established findings

### HIGH

1. SC, AC and GH are true synovial joints; ST is a functional articulation.
2. Clavicle is the main bony axial–appendicular bridge.
3. Scapula is a mobile glenoid/orientation platform on thoracic wall.
4. GH stability depends on integrated bony, capsulolabral and dynamic muscular contributors.
5. Rotator cuff = supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor, subscapularis.
6. Cuff tendons blend with capsule and contribute dynamic stabilization.
7. Major scapular and axiohumeral muscle anatomy is sufficiently mapped for later mechanics.
8. Total arm elevation is distributed across GH + scapular + clavicular + thoracic motion.

### HIGH / SYNTHESIS-LEVEL

9. A universal constant scapulohumeral ratio and universal setting phase are not supported by the 2025 systematic review.

### MODERATE / INDIVIDUAL-DEPENDENT

10. Exact kinematic pattern, ratio, scapular-plane angle and muscle contribution vary across persons, angles and tasks.
