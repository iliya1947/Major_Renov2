## 3. Core distinction — landmark, proxy, diagnosis

### Anatomical landmark

A reproducible visible/palpable feature used to:
- locate a structure;
- place a tape/marker/goniometer;
- define a segment axis;
- standardize repeated measurement.

### Surface proxy

A skin-level point used as an approximation for:
- an underlying bone point;
- joint axis/center;
- segment coordinate system.

### Diagnosis

A medical inference about:
- pathology;
- injury;
- deformity;
- nerve lesion;
- joint dysfunction.

Project rule:

**landmark observation ≠ diagnosis.**

This is the central ANA-14 boundary.

---

## 4. Surface-landmark reliability is structure-dependent

Landmarks differ in practical accessibility.

Usually easier:
- prominent superficial bony edges;
- malleoli;
- patella;
- tibial tuberosity;
- epicondyles;
- clavicle;
- olecranon.

Often harder / more examiner-dependent:
- PSIS;
- lumbar spinous-process level;
- scapular landmarks under soft tissue;
- deep joint centers;
- landmarks with broad/rounded contours.

Current literature supports:
- variable palpation reliability;
- greater uncertainty for some pelvic/spinal/scapular landmarks;
- meaningful marker-placement error.

Project rule:

**palpable does not mean perfectly localizable.**

## 12. Circumference measurement — landmark principle

A circumference value is meaningful only with a defined protocol.

Required protocol dimensions:

1. exact anatomical level;
2. side;
3. body/joint position;
4. muscle relaxed vs contracted state;
5. tape orientation;
6. tape tension / skin compression;
7. respiration where relevant;
8. repeatability rules;
9. time/context if serial tracking.

Project rule:

**“arm = 35 cm” without site/protocol is incomplete data.**

---

## 13. Upper-arm circumference — concrete reference protocol

NHANES 2021 protocol:
- right upper arm;
- at the marked upper-arm midpoint;
- tape perpendicular to long axis;
- snug without compressing skin;
- recorded to 0.1 cm.

Project use:

This is a strong example of **site-standardized girth measurement**.

For bodybuilding/hypertrophy tracking, a different protocol may be chosen, but:
**the same protocol must be repeated longitudinally.**

Exact TST protocol selection → TST.

---

## 14. Waist circumference — protocol dependence

Different established protocols use different anatomical levels.

NHANES 2021:
- landmarked from the iliac-crest region;
- tape horizontal/parallel to floor;
- measurement at normal expiration.

WHO expert-consultation material historically recommends:
- midpoint between lower margin of last palpable rib and top of iliac crest.

Project conclusion:

**waist circumference is protocol-dependent; values from different anatomical sites should not be treated as perfectly interchangeable.**

For project tracking:
- choose one protocol;
- document it;
- repeat the same site/respiratory condition.

## 15. Hip circumference

NHANES/WHO-style protocols use:
- widest/maximal buttock circumference;
- tape horizontal/parallel to floor.

Project rule:

**hip circumference is a standardized girth, not a direct measure of glute muscle mass.**

It contains contributions from:
- bone/pelvic geometry;
- gluteal muscle;
- subcutaneous fat;
- soft tissue.

Body-composition interpretation → TST/IND.

Surface landmarks can define segment coordinate systems.

Examples:

### Shoulder / thorax
- C7;
- thoracic reference points;
- suprasternal notch;
- scapular/acromial landmarks.

### Elbow
- humeral epicondyles;
- ulnar/radial landmarks.

### Hip/pelvis
- ASIS/PSIS;
- greater-trochanter region.

### Knee
- femoral epicondyles;
- patella/joint-line/tibial landmarks.

### Ankle/foot
- malleoli;
- calcaneus;
- metatarsal landmarks.

ISB standards use defined anatomical landmarks to standardize joint coordinate systems.

Project rule:

**landmark-defined coordinate system is a measurement convention, not direct access to internal joint motion.**

## 18. Joint center vs surface landmark

Internal joint centers/axes may lie:
- deep to the skin;
- away from the palpable surface point;
- dynamically change in some joints.

Examples:
- GH center;
- hip center;
- knee instantaneous/functional axis;
- subtalar/midfoot centers.

Therefore a surface landmark can be:
1. an endpoint/reference;
2. an estimator;
3. part of a regression/functional calibration.

It is not automatically:
- the true anatomical joint center.

---

## 19. Marker-placement error

Current biomechanics literature shows:
- manually placed markers can be offset from intended bony landmarks;
- errors propagate into calculated joint angles;
- transverse/frontal-plane measures can be particularly sensitive in some models.

2026 scapular-landmark work further shows manual landmark uncertainty can materially influence estimated scapular posture.

Project rule:

**small apparent angle differences may be measurement error rather than real anatomical change.**

MDC/reliability handling → TST/MEC.

---

## 20. Soft-tissue artifact — STA

Even if a marker is correctly placed initially, skin/subcutaneous tissue moves relative to underlying bone.

This creates:
- translation error;
- rotation error;
- task-dependent distortion.

Recent lumbar/knee/whole-body motion literature continues to identify STA as a major limitation of skin-marker kinematics.

Project rule:

**skin marker trajectory ≠ bone trajectory.**

This is especially important for:
- spine;
- thigh;
- scapula;
- high-speed/high-amplitude movement.

## 22. “Knee over toe”, “shoulder packed”, “pelvis level” boundary

Surface landmarks may describe:
- relative position;
- direction;
- symmetry.

But anatomy alone cannot convert those observations into:
- injury risk;
- pathology;
- weakness diagnosis;
- universal correction.

Examples:
- knee anterior to toe is not itself pathology;
- scapular position is not itself a diagnosis;
- ASIS/PSIS asymmetry is not itself SIJ dysfunction;
- foot pronation appearance is not itself pathology.

Project rule:

**description ≠ diagnosis ≠ prescription.**

---

## 23. Static asymmetry boundary

Visible/palpable asymmetry may reflect:

- normal anatomy;
- stance;
- soft-tissue distribution;
- measurement error;
- camera perspective;
- temporary motor strategy;
- previous injury/pathology.

Therefore:

**surface asymmetry is an observation requiring context, not a diagnosis.**

This directly protects later IND/TST from posture-screening overreach.

---

## 24. Palpation accuracy boundary

A useful hierarchy:

### Better suited
Prominent superficial landmarks:
- malleoli;
- patella;
- tibial tuberosity;
- olecranon;
- clavicle.

### More uncertain
- PSIS;
- specific lumbar levels;
- scapular points under thicker soft tissue;
- deep joint centers;
- small carpal/tarsal structures.

Project rule:

**landmark choice should reflect expected localization error.**

Where high precision matters:
- use validated imaging/calibration methods;
- do not claim palpation precision that the method cannot support.

---

## 25. Circumference vs muscle hypertrophy boundary

Circumference changes reflect combined changes in:
- muscle;
- subcutaneous fat;
- fluid;
- glycogen-associated water;
- measurement site/technique;
- transient pump;
- limb position.

Therefore:

**girth is a useful repeated field measure but not a pure muscle-mass measurement.**

Interpretation → TST/PHY/NUT.

---

## 26. Visual muscle contour vs activation boundary

A visible/contracting muscle region may not tell us:

- relative force contribution;
- motor-unit recruitment;
- deeper synergist activity;
- tendon force;
- regional hypertrophy cause.

Project rule:

**surface anatomy can identify where a muscle/group lies, not how much it is contributing mechanically.**

Activation/force → NEU/MEC.

## 30. Established findings

### HIGH

1. Surface bony landmarks are useful for standardized measurement and segment reference.
2. Landmark usefulness depends on prominence/accessibility and protocol.
3. Circumference values are site/protocol-dependent.
4. NHANES provides reproducible landmarked procedures for arm/waist/hip circumferences.
5. ISB joint-coordinate standards rely on defined anatomical landmarks.
6. Skin landmarks are proxies for underlying skeletal anatomy, not direct joint centers.
7. Marker placement error and soft-tissue artifact can materially affect kinematic estimates.
8. Visible muscle contours identify approximate regions/groups, not activation or force.
9. Static asymmetry/shape alone does not diagnose pathology.
10. Repeated longitudinal measurement requires consistent protocol.

### MODERATE / METHOD-DEPENDENT

1. Exact palpation accuracy of individual landmarks.
2. Exact marker error under a given movement/model.
3. Scapular/pelvic/spinal landmark precision.
4. Degree to which a given visual landmark reflects internal joint position.
