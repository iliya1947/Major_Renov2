# RES-14 — Acute vs chronic inference

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v1.0**  
Дата: 2026-11-16  
Область: 13 — Методология исследований

## 0. Question Card

### Decision
Когда acute study может информировать долгосрочную тренировочную рекомендацию, а когда chronic outcome evidence обязательно имеет приоритет.

### Question type
Methodological / surrogate / longitudinal inference.

### Critical outcomes
1. Distinguish acute response from chronic adaptation.
2. Define conditions under which acute evidence can support, but not replace, chronic evidence.
3. Prevent time-horizon mismatch.
4. Define hierarchy when acute and chronic evidence conflict.

### Closure criterion
Есть explicit project rule for translating acute mechanisms into chronic recommendations.

---

## 1. Acute and chronic are different outcomes

### Acute response
Measured:
- during session;
- immediately after;
- hours/days after one or few exposures.

Examples:
- EMG;
- lactate;
- MPS;
- velocity loss;
- hormones;
- soreness;
- swelling;
- acute performance.

### Chronic adaptation
Measured after repeated exposure over weeks/months.

Examples:
- hypertrophy;
- maximal strength;
- power;
- tendon adaptation;
- bone adaptation;
- adherence;
- injury/tolerability.

Acute and chronic outcomes can both be valid, but answer different questions.

## 2. Time-horizon mismatch = indirectness

If target question is:
> “Which method builds more muscle over 12 weeks?”

and evidence is:
> “Which method produced more lactate today?”

the evidence is indirect by outcome/time.

GRADE treats surrogate outcomes as a source of indirectness when patient/decision-important outcomes are unavailable.

Exercise-science analogue:
acute proxy evidence is indirect for chronic adaptation unless validated.

## 3. Acute evidence is strongest when acute outcome is itself target

Examples:
- warm-up effect on immediate jump;
- caffeine effect on same-day performance;
- rest interval effect on next set;
- acute safety/hemodynamic response.

Here no proxy jump is needed.

## 4. Acute evidence as mechanism support

Acute study can:
1. identify pathway;
2. explain why chronic effect is plausible;
3. refine dose hypotheses;
4. identify potential harms/fatigue;
5. guide design of longitudinal trial.

But mechanism support should be labeled:
- explanatory;
- hypothesis-generating;
- not efficacy proof.

## 5. Chronic evidence has priority for chronic decisions

If high-quality longitudinal outcome data exist, they dominate acute proxies for practical chronic prescription.

Example:
acute method A causes:
- more GH;
- more lactate;
- higher RPE;

but chronic A and B show similar hypertrophy.

Recommendation should follow chronic hypertrophy + burden, not acute “anabolic” narrative.

## 6. Acute/chronic discordance is informative

A 2026 meta-analysis of accentuated eccentric loading found some acute differences in:
- lactate;
- growth hormone;
- eccentric EMG;
- RPE,

while chronic strength/muscle architecture outcomes were broadly similar to conventional loading.

This directly illustrates:
**larger acute physiological response need not translate into superior chronic adaptation.**

## 7. Repeated acute effects can matter without being valid proxy

A marker may be mechanistically involved in every session but still not rank interventions accurately.

Example:
MPS is required for net protein accretion.
Yet one early acute MPS measurement may not predict which program produces more hypertrophy.

Causal necessity ≠ surrogate validity.

## 8. Longitudinal adaptation includes processes absent in acute study

Over weeks:
1. skill learning;
2. repeated-bout effect;
3. altered fatigue tolerance;
4. tendon adaptation;
5. neural adaptation;
6. adherence decay;
7. progression;
8. recovery;
9. changing MPS/signaling response;
10. behavioral adaptation.

Acute study freezes system before many of these emerge.

## 9. Novelty effect

First exposure often exaggerates:
- soreness;
- damage;
- MPS;
- fatigue;
- coordination demand.

This makes first-session acute data particularly poor predictor of steady-state training response.

## 10. Intervention changes over time

Real training is adaptive:
- load increases;
- technique improves;
- reps change;
- rest changes;
- tolerance improves.

An acute comparison at fixed starting dose may not reflect chronic programs after progression.

## 11. Acute studies may still identify chronic constraints

Examples:
- excessive BP response;
- intolerable pain;
- extreme fatigue;
- unsafe joint position.

A chronic efficacy trial is not required to respect immediate safety signal.

So acute evidence can matter strongly for:
- safety;
- tolerability;
- feasibility.

## 12. Surrogate validation standard

REASSurE 2026 emphasizes that surrogate evaluation must consider treatment effects on both surrogate and real endpoint, uncertainty, generalizability and context rather than relying on one correlation threshold.

Translated to exercise science:
acute marker becomes credible longitudinal surrogate only if:
1. intervention changes marker;
2. marker relates to chronic outcome;
3. across interventions, treatment effects on marker track treatment effects on outcome;
4. validation generalizes to target context;
5. uncertainty is acceptable.

Most popular acute hypertrophy proxies do not meet this bar.

## 13. Correlation across individuals is insufficient

Suppose people with larger acute MPS grow more.

Even if correlation exists, that does not prove:
program that increases MPS more will increase hypertrophy more.

Need treatment-effect/surrogate validation, not only subject-level association.

This is classic surrogate paradox risk.

## 14. Surrogate paradox

Intervention can:
- improve surrogate;
- yet not improve real outcome;
or even worsen it through other pathways.

Exercise example:
method increases:
- pump/metabolic stress;
but also:
- fatigue;
- lower total quality work;
- poorer adherence.

Net chronic outcome can be unchanged/worse.

## 15. Acute vs chronic evidence matrix

### Chronic target + chronic direct evidence available
Use chronic evidence as primary.

### Chronic target + only acute evidence
Use acute evidence as provisional mechanism hypothesis; do not make strong superiority claim.

### Acute target
Use acute evidence directly.

### Safety target
Acute adverse response can be highly relevant even without chronic trial.

### Mechanism target
Acute mechanistic study may be primary evidence, but do not translate beyond mechanism.

## 16. Minimum longitudinal duration

No universal duration fits every adaptation.

Need match biology:
- neural strength changes can emerge quickly;
- hypertrophy needs sufficient weeks and sensitive measurement;
- tendon/bone often slower;
- adherence needs enough time to observe decay/sustainability.

Project rule:
duration adequacy is outcome-specific.

## 17. “No chronic difference” can still be inconclusive

If longitudinal study:
- small;
- short;
- imprecise;
- poorly measured,

absence of significant difference does not establish equivalence.

Therefore chronic evidence priority does not mean blind acceptance.
Still apply RES-04/07/09/11.

## 18. Evidence triangulation

Best chronic recommendation often combines:
1. longitudinal direct outcomes;
2. mechanism/acute evidence;
3. biomechanics;
4. safety/tolerability;
5. adherence;
6. practical constraints.

But weights differ.

Outcome evidence answers efficacy.
Mechanism helps explain/transport.
Behavior/safety constrain implementation.

## 19. Acute-only marketing claims

Common red flags:
1. “boosts testosterone therefore builds more muscle”;
2. “highest EMG activation therefore best exercise”;
3. “more lactate therefore more hypertrophy”;
4. “huge pump proves anabolic stimulus”;
5. “more soreness means effective workout”;
6. “greater calorie burn means better fat loss long-term”.

Project rule:
any “therefore chronic result” requires validation bridge.

## 20. Exercise method example: AEL

Recent systematic evidence comparing accentuated eccentric loading and constant-load resistance training found:
- some acute markers differed;
- chronic maximal strength/muscle architecture outcomes were often similar/imprecise.

Use:
good teaching case for why acute effect ≠ chronic superiority.

## 21. BFR example

2026 review of high-load BFR:
- acute physiological/perceptual effects can differ;
- chronic strength/size superiority over conventional high-load training was not established and certainty was very low.

Again:
acute novelty does not guarantee chronic benefit.

## 22. Project acute→chronic translation checklist

Before chronic claim from acute study:

1. What is target chronic outcome?
2. Is acute marker on plausible pathway?
3. Is marker validated longitudinally?
4. Does individual association exist?
5. Does intervention-level association exist?
6. Are repeated exposures similar to acute session?
7. Does adaptation change marker response over time?
8. Could competing pathways offset marker effect?
9. Are chronic studies available?
10. Is acute evidence only mechanism/support?
11. Is safety/tolerability target instead?
12. What confidence wording is justified?

## 23. Hierarchy when evidence conflicts

1. First check study quality/directness.
2. For chronic performance/hypertrophy decision:
   - high-quality chronic direct evidence > acute proxy.
3. Acute evidence can explain discrepancy or identify subgroup/mechanism hypothesis.
4. If chronic evidence weak/imprecise, keep uncertainty rather than letting acute marker decide.
5. If acute safety signal serious, safety can override efficacy uncertainty.

## 24. Project wording

### Strong
“12-week trials show X produced greater hypertrophy.”

### Moderate
“Acute mechanism is consistent with potential chronic benefit, but longitudinal evidence is limited.”

### Weak/prohibited
“X increased EMG/lactate/MPS, therefore it builds more muscle.”

## 25. Confidence

### HIGH
- acute and chronic outcomes are different evidentiary targets;
- surrogate outcome indirectness must be considered;
- larger acute response does not establish larger chronic adaptation;
- direct longitudinal evidence should dominate chronic recommendations when methodologically adequate;
- treatment-level surrogate validation is stronger than simple individual correlation.

### PRACTICAL_HEURISTIC
- project acute→chronic checklist and evidence hierarchy.
