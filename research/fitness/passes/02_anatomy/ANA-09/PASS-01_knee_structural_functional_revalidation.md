## 2. Canonical knee-complex model

Knee is a compound synovial system comprising:

1. medial tibiofemoral compartment;
2. lateral tibiofemoral compartment;
3. patellofemoral articulation.

Primary motion:
- flexion / extension.

Secondary constrained motions include:
- axial rotation;
- translation;
- small varus/valgus excursion depending position/load.

Project rule:

**knee is hinge-dominant, not a perfect fixed-axis hinge.**

---

## 3. Distal femur

Key landmarks:

1. medial condyle;
2. lateral condyle;
3. medial/lateral epicondyles;
4. intercondylar notch;
5. patellar/trochlear surface;
6. adductor tubercle.

Condylar geometry is asymmetric and interacts with:
- tibial plateaus;
- menisci;
- cruciates;
- patella/trochlea.

Exact axis migration/rollback → MEC.

---

## 4. Proximal tibia

Key structures:

1. medial tibial plateau;
2. lateral tibial plateau;
3. intercondylar eminence/spines;
4. anterior/posterior intercondylar areas;
5. tibial tuberosity;
6. Gerdy tubercle;
7. pes-anserinus region.

Tibia is the main lower-leg weight-bearing bone.

Intercondylar regions provide attachment sites for:
- cruciate ligaments;
- meniscal roots.

---

## 5. Fibula relation

Fibula does not articulate with femur as part of the primary knee joint.

Fibular head is nevertheless important for:
- LCL/FCL;
- biceps femoris;
- proximal tibiofibular joint;
- posterolateral structures.

Project rule:

**fibula is outside the primary tibiofemoral articulation but remains structurally relevant to lateral knee stability and lower-limb force transmission.**

---

## 6. Patella

Patella:
- largest sesamoid bone;
- embedded within quadriceps/extensor mechanism;
- articulates with femoral trochlea.

Structural roles:

1. increases quadriceps extensor leverage;
2. redirects tendon force;
3. separates extensor tendon from distal femur;
4. contributes to anterior joint protection/contact distribution.

Exact moment-arm/contact-pressure changes by angle → MEC.

---

## 7. Tibiofemoral articulation

Femoral condyles articulate with tibial plateaus with menisci interposed.

Because articular congruence is limited relative to the hip, stability depends on integrated:

- menisci;
- cruciates;
- collateral/medial/lateral complexes;
- capsule;
- bony geometry;
- muscles.

Project rule:

**tibiofemoral stability is distributed, not ligament-only.**

---

## 8. Patellofemoral articulation

Posterior patellar facets articulate with femoral trochlea.

Patellar contact/orientation changes through flexion-extension.

Project rule:

**patella does not simply slide in a perfectly fixed straight rail.**

Patellofemoral path depends on:

- trochlear/patellar morphology;
- retinacular/ligamentous restraints;
- quadriceps/extensor mechanism;
- limb/joint position.

Exact 3D tracking → MEC/TST.

---

## 9. Menisci

Medial and lateral menisci are fibrocartilaginous intra-articular structures.

Broad functions:

1. improve tibiofemoral congruence;
2. distribute contact load;
3. reduce focal contact stress;
4. contribute to stability;
5. contribute to lubrication/proprioception.

Project rule:

**menisci are load-sharing/stability structures, not merely “shock absorbers”.**

---

## 10. Medial vs lateral meniscus

### Medial

- broadly C-shaped;
- more closely tethered to medial capsular structures;
- generally less mobile.

### Lateral

- more circular;
- relatively more mobile;
- popliteus hiatus/tendon relationship posterolaterally.

These are broad modal patterns, not absolute geometry for every person.

---

## 11. Meniscal roots

Anterior/posterior roots anchor menisci to tibia.

Root fixation allows meniscal tissue to function as a circumferential load-sharing structure.

Project rule:

**meniscus function cannot be reduced to the free fibrocartilage body while ignoring root attachment.**

Mechanical consequences of root disruption → MEC/MED.

---

## 12. Meniscal-root normal variation — current update

A 2026 high-resolution 7-T MRI study of 57 asymptomatic knees found meaningful root-insertion variation.

Most notable:
- posterior lateral meniscus frequently had more than one root insertion;
- double insertions were most common in that sample;
- single and triple variants also occurred;
- meniscofemoral-ligament morphology/attachment was heterogeneous.

Project conclusion:

**meniscal-root anatomy is variable; one atlas insertion map is not a universal personal requirement.**

No pathology is inferred from variant root number/location alone.

---

## 13. ACL

ACL connects:
- anterior tibial intercondylar region;
- lateral femoral-condyle intercondylar surface.

Broad roles:

- restrains anterior tibial translation relative to femur;
- contributes to rotational control;
- contributes to terminal-extension restraint.

Common bundle description:
- anteromedial;
- posterolateral.

Project rule:

**ACL tension/function is position-dependent, not one fixed vector.**

Detailed strain/loading → MEC/MED.

---

## 14. PCL

PCL connects:
- posterior tibial intercondylar region;
- medial femoral-condyle intercondylar surface.

Broad roles:

- restrains posterior tibial translation;
- contributes to rotational/flexion stability.

Common bundle description:
- anterolateral;
- posteromedial.

Project rule:

**PCL is a position-dependent stabilizer, not merely “the posterior drawer ligament”.**

---

## 15. Cruciate location

ACL and PCL are:

- intracapsular;
- extrasynovial relative to the synovial cavity proper.

This anatomical distinction is retained because simplified diagrams often obscure it.

---

## 16. Medial knee — current complex model

Legacy “MCL = one medial ligament” is insufficient.

Current anatomy/review literature supports a medial stabilizing complex including:

1. superficial MCL (sMCL);
2. deep MCL (dMCL);
3. posteromedial complex / posterior oblique ligament region;
4. capsular/meniscal relationships;
5. dynamic medial structures including semimembranosus, semitendinosus and gracilis.

Project rule:

**medial knee stability ≠ one MCL strap.**

For project-level anatomy:
- sMCL = major valgus restraint;
- dMCL / posteromedial structures contribute importantly to rotational stability;
- deep structures interface with medial meniscus/capsule.

Detailed bundle-specific mechanics → MEC/MED.

---

## 17. LCL / fibular collateral ligament

LCL/FCL:
- lateral femoral epicondylar region → fibular head;
- cord-like;
- extracapsular;
- major varus restraint.

Unlike medial collateral structures, it does not blend directly with lateral meniscus in the same manner because popliteal/popliteus-related structures intervene.

---

## 18. Posterolateral corner — current complex model

Current 2025–2026 literature emphasizes that posterolateral knee is anatomically complex and terminology remains partly inconsistent.

Main repeatedly recognized stabilizers include:

1. fibular collateral ligament / LCL;
2. popliteus myotendinous complex;
3. popliteofibular ligament.

Associated structures include:
- arcuate complex/capsule;
- biceps femoris;
- ITB/lateral structures;
- additional variable ligamentous structures depending nomenclature.

Project rule:

**posterolateral stability ≠ LCL alone.**

The exact taxonomy of smaller PLC structures is not expanded further because it does not block later fitness mechanics.

---

## 19. Posterior / posteromedial reinforcement

Important posterior structures include:

- oblique popliteal ligament;
- posterior capsule;
- semimembranosus expansions;
- posteromedial complex.

Oblique popliteal ligament is related to semimembranosus tendon expansion.

Project rule:

**posterior stability is an integrated capsulotendinous/ligamentous system.**

---

## 20. Extensor mechanism

Canonical force path:

`quadriceps → quadriceps tendon → patella → patellar tendon/ligament → tibial tuberosity`

But this is not one homogeneous cord.

Extensor mechanism also includes:

- medial/lateral retinacular expansions;
- aponeurotic layers;
- patellofemoral soft-tissue restraints.

Project rule:

**patella is integrated into a distributed extensor mechanism.**

---

## 21. Patellar tendon vs ligament terminology

The structure between patella and tibial tuberosity:

- connects bone to bone → “ligament” is anatomically defensible;
- continues the quadriceps extensor-tendon chain → “patellar tendon” is common clinically/training.

Project rule:

**patellar tendon/ligament are acceptable contextual terms; clarify structure when needed.**

---

## 22. Quadriceps femoris

Canonical four muscles:

1. rectus femoris;
2. vastus lateralis;
3. vastus medialis;
4. vastus intermedius.

All contribute to knee extension through the extensor mechanism.

Innervation:
- femoral nerve.

---

## 23. Rectus femoris

Crosses:
- hip;
- knee.

Broad roles:
- hip flexion;
- knee extension.

Therefore hip position changes rectus-femoris muscle-tendon length during knee-extension work.

Exact length-tension/torque effect → MEC/PHY.

---

## 24. Vastus lateralis

Broad attachment:
- proximal/lateral femur;
- lateral linea-aspera region.

Contributes:
- knee extension;
- quadriceps tendon / patellar / retinacular structures.

---

## 25. Vastus medialis

Broad attachment:
- medial femur / linea-aspera / intertrochanteric regions;
- quadriceps tendon;
- medial patellar/retinacular structures.

Distal oblique fibers are often termed **VMO** descriptively.

Project rule:

**VMO is not assumed to be an independently isolatable separate muscle with a unique universal exercise prescription.**

---

## 26. Vastus intermedius / articularis genus

### Vastus intermedius

Deep to rectus femoris.

Primary:
- knee extension.

### Articularis genus

Small deep distal anterior-femoral muscle.

Broad role:
- retracts suprapatellar synovial/capsular tissue during extension.

Low training relevance, retained for complete extensor anatomy.

## 27. Quadriceps tendon — current variability update

A 2025 cadaveric study of 63 quadriceps-tendon specimens from 35 Korean cadavers found marked structural variability.

Among the fully dissected layering subset, observed patterns included:

- classic trilaminar arrangement;
- multilayered merged RF/VM/VL configurations;
- variants with reduced/absent VM contribution at the sampled patellar insertion;
- substantial variation in relative tendon contribution ratios.

Project conclusion:

**quadriceps tendon is a composite, variably layered structure rather than one universal three-layer rope.**

Limit:
- elderly Korean cadaver sample;
- do not generalize exact frequencies to every population.

---

## 28. Patellar retinacula / medial patellofemoral soft tissues

Medial/lateral retinacular expansions transmit force around patella and reinforce capsule.

Medial patellofemoral structures contribute to patellar restraint.

Current 2026 meta-analysis found substantial heterogeneity in the radiographic location of the MPFL femoral attachment across cadaveric studies.

Project conclusion:

**patellofemoral soft-tissue attachment anatomy has real variability; one radiographic point is not a universal personal insertion map.**

This is relevant to anatomy only; reconstruction/surgical targeting → MED.

---

## 29. Hamstrings at knee

### Biceps femoris

Broad roles:
- knee flexion;
- external/lateral rotation of flexed tibia.

### Semitendinosus / semimembranosus

Broad roles:
- knee flexion;
- internal/medial rotation of flexed tibia.

Most hamstring proximal anatomy was handled in ANA-08.

Project rule:

**medial and lateral hamstrings are not interchangeable rotationally.**

## 30. Pes anserinus

Combined insertion region of:

1. sartorius;
2. gracilis;
3. semitendinosus.

These muscles arise from different proximal compartments/nerve systems but converge on medial proximal tibia.

Project implication:

**functional anatomy crosses simple compartment labels.**

---

## 31. Semimembranosus expansions

Semimembranosus distal tendon has multiple expansions related to:

- medial tibia;
- posterior capsule;
- oblique popliteal ligament / posteromedial structures.

Therefore semimembranosus contributes beyond simple knee flexion.

Exact stabilizing force → MEC.

---

## 32. Biceps femoris / lateral knee relation

Biceps-femoris tendon inserts around fibular head with complex expansions.

LCL/FCL also attaches at fibular head.

Therefore lateral knee contains closely interwoven:
- tendon;
- ligament;
- capsule/fascia.

Exact PLC force sharing → MEC/MED.

---

## 33. Popliteus

Popliteus:
- small deep posterior knee muscle;
- femoral/popliteal-tendon origin region;
- inserts on posterior proximal tibia above soleal line;
- tibial nerve.

Broad roles:

- contributes to internal tibial rotation in open chain;
- contributes to external femoral rotation on fixed tibia;
- assists transition from terminal extension toward flexion;
- contributes posterolateral dynamic stability.

Project rule:

**“unlocks the knee” is shorthand for a coupled rotational role, not a literal mechanical latch.**

---

## 34. Popliteus — 2026 architecture update

A 2026 cadaveric/neuroanatomical study of 32 lower extremities found:

- superficial and deep layers separated around a central tendon;
- compartmentalized intramuscular nerve territories;
- differences in fiber-type/pennation characteristics between layers.

Project conclusion:

**popliteus is internally nonuniform rather than a one-vector miniature rotator.**

Limit:
- cadaveric anatomical evidence;
- no exercise-specific prescription follows.

---

## 35. Gastrocnemius cross-joint role

Medial/lateral gastrocnemius heads originate from distal femoral regions and cross:

- knee;
- ankle.

At knee:
- contributes flexion/posterior dynamic control.

At ankle:
- plantarflexion.

Project rule:

**knee angle can change gastrocnemius length and ankle-force conditions; ankle position can reciprocally affect geometry.**

Detailed calf anatomy → ANA-10.

---

## 36. Plantaris boundary

Plantaris:
- small;
- variable;
- crosses knee + ankle.

Low training priority.

It is retained as anatomical variation, not a dedicated programming target.

## 37. Knee rotation / screw-home boundary

Knee permits axial rotation, particularly away from terminal extension.

Terminal extension includes coupled rotational behavior related to:

- condylar geometry;
- ligament tension;
- muscle action.

Popliteus contributes to reversal/transition toward flexion.

Project rule:

**knee rotation is real, but exact screw-home kinematics belong to MEC.**

3. popliteus;

## 40. Patellofemoral stability

Depends on integrated:

- trochlear morphology;
- patellar morphology;
- extensor mechanism;
- medial/lateral retinacular/ligamentous tissues;
- limb position.

Project rule:

**do not reduce patellofemoral behavior or symptoms to “VMO weakness”.**

## 41. Knee capsule / synovial boundary

Fibrous capsule encloses the knee complex and is reinforced by:

- ligaments;
- tendons;
- retinacula.

Synovial membrane lines the internal capsule but reflects around cruciates.

Posterolaterally, popliteus tendon/capsular relationships complicate the simple “closed bag” model.

Normal periarticular bursae include:

### Rectus femoris
Crosses:
- hip;

### Hamstrings
Most hip-crossing hamstrings cross:
- hip;

### Gastrocnemius

## 44. Open vs closed chain boundary

Anatomically the same knee structures participate whether:

- foot is free;
- foot is fixed.

Relative segment motion differs:
- tibia may move relative to femur;
- femur may move relative to tibia.

Project rule:

**open/closed chain is a mechanical/task distinction, not a different anatomy.**

Loading differences → MEC/TEC.

---

## 45. “Knee over toes” boundary

Anatomy alone does not establish that anterior knee translation is pathological.

The knee is structurally designed to flex with:
- tibiofemoral motion;
- patellofemoral motion;
- extensor mechanism excursion.

Whether a given task is appropriate depends on:
- external load;
- moment arms;
- tissue capacity;
- ROM;
- symptoms/context.

Those are MEC/MED/TEC questions.

---

## 46. Valgus / varus boundary

Valgus/varus describe relative frontal-plane alignment/angulation.

Visible dynamic movement does not by itself equal:

- MCL/LCL failure;
- injury diagnosis;
- pathology.

Project rule:

**movement description ≠ tissue diagnosis.**

## 48. What changed versus legacy ANA-09

### Confirmed

Legacy core remains valid:

- knee = tibiofemoral + patellofemoral compound system;
- knee is hinge-dominant but allows rotation/translation;
- menisci distribute load and contribute stability;
- ACL/PCL + collateral/posterior structures provide passive stability;
- patella is integrated into extensor mechanism;
- quadriceps = RF + VL + VM + VI;
- hamstrings have medial/lateral rotational distinctions;
- popliteus contributes to rotational transition/stability;
- gastrocnemius crosses knee + ankle.

### Strengthened / updated

1. **Quadriceps tendon:** 2025 cadaveric data directly demonstrate substantial layering and patellar-insertion variability; fixed trilaminar anatomy is not universal.
2. **Meniscal roots:** 2026 7-T MRI in asymptomatic knees demonstrates meaningful root-number/insertion variability, especially posterior lateral meniscus.
3. **Medial knee:** current 2026 review supports sMCL + dMCL + posteromedial-complex model rather than one MCL strap.
4. **Posterolateral knee:** 2025–2026 literature emphasizes LCL/FCL + popliteus complex + popliteofibular ligament and ongoing terminology variability.
5. **Popliteus:** 2026 anatomical work supports superficial/deep layered compartmentalization and nonuniform innervation.
6. **Patellofemoral soft tissue:** 2026 meta-analysis shows substantial variability in radiographic MPFL femoral-attachment location.

### Tightened

1. No “knee = pure hinge” model.
2. No “VMO isolation fixes patellar tracking” anatomical claim.
3. No universal meniscal-root or MPFL attachment map.
4. No single-MCL or single-LCL stability model.
5. No “popliteus literally unlocks a mechanical latch” wording.
6. No technique prohibition from anatomy alone.

## 49. Established findings

### HIGH

1. Knee is a compound tibiofemoral + patellofemoral synovial system.
2. Menisci are load-sharing/congruence/stability structures with function dependent on root attachment.
3. ACL/PCL are intracapsular-extrasynovial position-dependent stabilizers.
4. Medial stability is distributed across superficial/deep MCL and posteromedial structures.
5. Lateral/posterolateral stability is distributed across FCL/LCL, popliteus-related and popliteofibular/capsular structures.
6. Patella is integrated into a distributed extensor mechanism.
7. Quadriceps comprises RF, VL, VM, VI; RF is biarticular.
8. Medial/lateral hamstrings differ in rotational action at flexed knee.
9. Gastrocnemius is biarticular across knee + ankle.
10. Knee allows limited axial rotation/translation in addition to flexion-extension.
11. Normal knee soft-tissue/bony anatomy shows meaningful variation.

### MODERATE / SAMPLE- OR MODEL-DEPENDENT

1. Exact quadriceps-tendon layering frequencies from the 2025 cadaveric Korean sample.
2. Exact meniscal-root variant prevalence from the 57-knee 7-T MRI sample.
3. Exact taxonomy of smaller PLC structures remains partly inconsistent.
4. Exact functional specialization of popliteus superficial/deep layers remains uncertain.
5. Exact MPFL radiographic attachment coordinates are heterogeneous across studies.
