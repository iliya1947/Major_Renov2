E1. Tager-Shafrir, Szepsenwol, Dvir & Zamir (2024), The gaslighting relationship exposure inventory: Reliability and validity in two cultures. https://doi.org/10.1177/02654075241266942 . Publisher sections directly inspected. Two community samples of people in current romantic relationships (N=509 and 395), online self-report. The 11-item GREI showed a one-factor solution, reliability and metric invariance by gender. Reported exposure was associated with depressive symptoms and lower relationship quality after accounting for other measured IPV forms. Authors explicitly identify cross-sectional design, self-report, sample exclusions/underrepresentation and absence of clinical cutoffs as limitations. Associations do not identify direction or intent; scale validation does not establish diagnostic accuracy for isolated incidents or third-party assessments. Exact coefficients, full invariance results, and tables not audited. A factual disagreement, memory discrepancy or boundary alone is insufficient to establish gaslighting.

E3. Pennycook et al. (2024), Inoculation and accuracy prompting increase accuracy discernment in combination but not alone. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-024-02023-2 ; https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02023-2 . ## 3. Integrated construct map
Separate influence (possible change in judgment/action), information quality, private belief, normative expectation, voluntary endorsement, public compliance, choice constraints, power, threatened consequences and actual harm. A persuasive form is not a truth criterion; an accurate claim can be communicated poorly, and a misleading claim can appear calm and reasonable. Fact verification, evidence of intent, subjective impact and actual freedom to choose require different evidence. A person's refusal is not a diagnostic sign of reactance or a problem to overcome.

Manipulation recognition concerns evidence that a communication or decision process misleads or undermines informed choice. Do not infer a manipulative disposition solely from an unpleasant outcome, conflict, delayed response, different recollection or unmet preference. Distinguish ordinary disagreement, honest error, misleading conduct, psychological aggression and coercive restrictions. A recurring pattern may matter, but one serious threat or violation can warrant safety action. Proof of hidden intent is not necessary to recognize a documented threat, respect a boundary or seek help.

Signal detection provides an analytical distinction between sensitivity to genuine differences and response tendency. Labeling everything suspicious increases false alarms, while dismissing every warning increases misses. No numerical personal sensitivity or specificity estimates without an appropriate validated design and reference criterion. In a personal interaction, exact factual evidence, voluntary statements, actual agreements, concrete consequences and meaningful alternatives matter more than a generalized suspicion score.

Autonomy is compatible with reliance, care and mutual influence. Actual ability to refuse, informed choice and freedom from retaliation matter alongside subjective perceptions. External agreement, internal endorsement and continuing contact do not by themselves establish consent or safety. Protective decisions need not wait for certainty about another person's motive. Clinical screening, clinical safety planning and legal assessment are distinct from ordinary interpersonal reasoning; retain SOC-X02.2 evidence limits and appropriate referral principles.

## 4. Operational procedure
1. Define the actual decision, actors, time, context, source and target claim. Preserve observed conduct, voluntary reports, verified facts and hypotheses separately.
2. Check the truth of material assertions independently when possible, distinguish disagreement from misinformation, and separate recognition of rhetorical form from establishing a factual error or deliberate deception.
3. Compare ordinary preference, misunderstanding, shared norm, resource constraint, independent agreement, misleading conduct and coercive restriction. More than one process may operate; do not assume every negative outcome reflects concealed strategy.
4. Identify actual alternatives and consequences of refusal. Examine relevant legitimately available evidence over time. Respect explicit refusal without trying to identify a way around it. No surveillance, engineered tests, fabricated consensus, threats or pressure.
5. Assess harm and safety separately from inferred motive. Do not dismiss credible reports because a motive or clinical label cannot be established. If danger is credible, prioritize safety and appropriate confidential support; immediate danger warrants emergency help.
6. Evaluate source populations, outcome measures, task validity, false alarms, misses, context, uncertainty and transfer. Separate screen performance, protective intervention effectiveness and individual diagnosis. Evidence about news judgments does not validate identification of hidden intentions.
7. Revise the narrowest supported claim, retain alternatives and specify disconfirming evidence. Do not promise accurate identification of all manipulation, quantify individual susceptibility, or recommend exploiting psychological vulnerabilities.

## 5. Internal synthetic review
Rubric: construct separation, credible alternative explanation, discriminating evidence, inference boundary. Critical errors: unsupported motive or diagnosis, treating rhetoric as truth evidence, causal inflation, disregard of contrary findings or safety, coercion or privacy breach. Six neutral newly authored examples, not independent or held-out.
A. Two collaborators remember a meeting differently. Response: discrepancy and deliberate deception differ; ordinary memory error and different attention compete. Compare legitimately available records. No gaslighting diagnosis from one discrepancy.
B. A headline is emotionally worded but its central claim has independent support. Response: persuasive style and truth differ; accuracy requires checking evidence. No rejection solely on style.
C. A training trial improves recognition of questionable communication but not truth discernment. Response: outcomes differ; criteria and task difficulty matter. Inspect verified true and false items and both error types. No claim of global manipulation immunity.
D. A voluntary group survey indicates agreement while people describe serious consequences for dissent. Response: expressed agreement and freedom to refuse differ; actual alternatives and credible consequences matter. No inference of private endorsement or recommendation to increase pressure.
E. An exposure measure correlates with distress but lacks a validated clinical threshold. Response: association, reported experience and diagnosis differ; shared causes, measurement and selection matter. No individual label or score inferred from selected messages.
F. A person declines another request after a credible threat. Response: refusal, self-protection and any hypothesized reactance differ; the documented threat warrants attention independently of motive. Respect choice and support safety. No attempt to overcome refusal.

Internal self-review: all four criteria addressed in each of six authored examples (24/24 checklist coverage). No independent competence, diagnosis, forecast calibration, professional qualification or model training established.
