# BIO-06 — Oxidative metabolism

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-19**  
Область: 05 — Bioenergetics / Exercise Metabolism

## 0. Core decision

Oxidative ATP resynthesis is a continuously active mitochondrial process coupled to substrate oxidation, electron transport, proton-motive force and ATP synthase.

Project rule:

**oxidative metabolism is not a delayed “aerobic system” that switches on after an anaerobic phase, and oxygen availability alone does not define oxidative ATP flux.**

---

## 1. Pyruvate oxidation

Carbohydrate-derived pyruvate enters mitochondrial oxidation through the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex (PDH), which converts pyruvate to acetyl-CoA.

PDH activity is regulated by exercise/metabolic state.

Project rule:

**pyruvate production ≠ pyruvate oxidation.**

---

## 2. Acetyl-CoA

Acetyl-CoA can derive from:

- pyruvate;
- fatty-acid beta-oxidation;
- selected amino-acid metabolism.

Project rule:

**acetyl-CoA ≠ glucose oxidation.**

Detailed substrate selection belongs BIO-07.

---

## 3. TCA / Krebs cycle

The TCA cycle:

- accepts acetyl-CoA;
- regenerates oxaloacetate;
- produces NADH/FADH2;
- produces CO2;
- yields a small substrate-level GTP/ATP equivalent;
- exchanges intermediates with other pathways.

Project rule:

**TCA is a central oxidative/reducing-equivalent hub, not the main direct ATP-producing step.**

---

## 4. Electron transport chain

The ETC resides in the inner mitochondrial membrane.

Core organization:

- Complex I: NADH-linked electron entry + proton pumping;
- Complex II: succinate/FAD-linked electron entry without proton pumping;
- CoQ: mobile electron carrier;
- Complex III: electron transfer + proton pumping;
- cytochrome c: mobile carrier;
- Complex IV: electron transfer to O2 + proton pumping.

Project rule:

**electron transfer and proton pumping are coupled but distinct; Complex II is not a proton pump.**

---

## 5. Oxidative phosphorylation

OXPHOS couples:

`substrate oxidation → NADH/FADH2 → ETC → proton-motive force → ATP synthase → ATP`.

ATP synthase uses proton-motive force to phosphorylate ADP + Pi.

Project rule:

**ATP synthase is driven by the electrochemical proton gradient, not by oxygen directly.**

---

## 6. Oxygen

O2 is the terminal electron acceptor at Complex IV and is reduced to water.

Adequate O2 is necessary for normal sustained respiratory-chain function.

But oxidative ATP production also depends on:

- ADP;
- Pi;
- free creatine / PCr state;
- carbon substrates;
- reducing equivalents;
- Ca2+;
- mitochondrial respiratory capacity;
- intracellular O2;
- pH/redox state.

Project rule:

**O2 is indispensable but not a universal one-variable rate limiter.**

---

## 7. Concurrent energy systems

Phosphagen, substrate-level and oxidative ATP resynthesis operate concurrently.

Their relative contributions shift continuously with task demand.

Project rule:

**there is no universal “aerobic system starts after X seconds” rule.**

---

## 8. ATP yield / P/O

Theoretical ATP and P/O calculations are useful models.

They depend on:

- substrate entry point;
- proton stoichiometry;
- redox shuttles;
- transport costs;
- proton leak/uncoupling;
- membrane state;
- measurement assumptions.

Project rule:

**no single ATP/glucose, ATP/NADH, ATP/FADH2 or in-vivo P/O value is retained as a universal physiological constant.**

---

## 9. Oxygen consumption vs ATP production

Respiration can support ATP synthesis but also includes coupling losses such as proton leak.

Project rule:

**O2 consumption ≠ ATP production.**

P/O or ATP/O2 interpretation requires explicit method/state.

---

## 10. Mitochondrial content vs function

Keep separate:

### Mitochondrial content / volume density
How much mitochondrial structure is present relative to muscle.

### Mass-specific respiratory capacity
Respiration/OXPHOS per muscle mass.

### Intrinsic mitochondrial function
Respiration normalized to mitochondrial content/structure.

### Cristae-normalized respiration
Respiration normalized to cristae/inner-membrane structural area.

### Coupling efficiency
ATP output relative to oxygen/respiratory flux under defined state.

Project rule:

**more mitochondrial content can increase whole-muscle oxidative capacity without making each unit of mitochondrial structure intrinsically more powerful.**

---

## 11. Citrate synthase

CS activity is useful as:

- an oxidative-enzyme marker;
- a common mitochondrial-content proxy.

It is not automatically:

- mitochondrial number;
- mitochondrial volume;
- OXPHOS capacity;
- intrinsic function;
- coupling efficiency.

Project rule:

**CS is a marker, not a complete mitochondrial-function measurement.**

---

## 12. Mitochondrial biogenesis

Use “mitochondrial biogenesis” for coordinated expansion/remodeling involving:

- nuclear/mitochondrial gene regulation;
- protein synthesis/import;
- mtDNA maintenance/expression;
- membrane/cristae remodeling;
- fusion/fission;
- quality control/turnover.

Project rule:

**biogenesis does not mean simply counting newly born isolated mitochondria.**

---

## 13. PGC-1α

PGC-1α is an important exercise-responsive transcriptional coactivator.

But:

- acute PGC-1α mRNA/protein responses are signaling;
- chronic mitochondrial structure/function can change without one-to-one PGC-1α changes.

Project rule:

**PGC-1α ≠ mitochondrial adaptation itself.**

---

## 14. Training modality

Endurance training, HIT and SIT can all increase mitochondrial content.

Magnitude depends on:

- baseline fitness;
- training load;
- frequency;
- duration;
- modality;
- tolerance.

Project rule:

**no single universally superior modality is retained for all mitochondrial outcomes.**

---

## 15. Oxidative enzyme adaptations

Training can increase:

- citrate synthase;
- respiratory-chain enzyme activities;
- other oxidative enzymes.

Project rule:

**enzyme adaptation is real, but enzyme activity ≠ whole-mitochondrion function ≠ whole-body performance.**

---

## 16. Resistance training

Resistance training can alter mitochondrial respiratory function/content.

Hypertrophy can create a denominator/dilution issue when mitochondrial density is expressed relative to increasing muscle volume.

Project rule:

**resistance training is not metabolically “non-mitochondrial”; interpret mitochondrial outcomes with the denominator and hypertrophy state.**

Detailed RT energetics remains BIO-12.

---

## 17. Mitochondria vs VO2max

VO2max integrates:

- pulmonary gas exchange;
- cardiac output;
- blood O2 carrying;
- muscle perfusion;
- capillarization/O2 diffusion;
- mitochondrial utilization.

Project rule:

**mitochondrial adaptation ≠ VO2max, and VO2max ≠ isolated mitochondrial function.**

---

## 18. Canonical non-equivalences

1. Pyruvate production ≠ pyruvate oxidation.
2. Pyruvate ≠ only acetyl-CoA source.
3. Acetyl-CoA ≠ glucose oxidation.
4. TCA cycle ≠ main direct ATP source.
5. Electron transport ≠ ATP synthesis.
6. Complex II ≠ proton pump.
7. O2 requirement ≠ O2 always being sole limiter.
8. O2 consumption ≠ ATP production.
9. Theoretical ATP yield ≠ actual in-vivo ATP yield.
10. One ATP/glucose value ≠ universal constant.
11. Mitochondrial content ≠ mitochondrial function.
12. Mitochondrial volume density ≠ intrinsic respiration.
13. Mass-specific OXPHOS ≠ intrinsic OXPHOS.
14. Citrate synthase ≠ complete mitochondrial function.
15. PGC-1α expression ≠ mitochondrial biogenesis itself.
16. Acute signaling ≠ chronic adaptation.
17. Mitochondrial adaptation ≠ VO2max.
18. VO2max ≠ isolated mitochondrial capacity.
19. More mitochondria ≠ necessarily greater function per structural unit.
20. Resistance training ≠ no mitochondrial adaptation.
21. “Aerobic system” ≠ delayed-onset pathway.

## 20. Confidence

### HIGH

- PDH is a regulated carbohydrate-oxidation gateway;
- acetyl-CoA has multiple substrate origins;
- TCA is primarily a reducing-equivalent/metabolic hub;
- Complex II does not pump protons;
- O2 is terminal electron acceptor;
- O2 is not the universal sole limiter;
- energy systems operate concurrently;
- fixed ATP/glucose values are not universal in vivo;
- O2 consumption and ATP production are distinct;
- mitochondrial content/function/intrinsic function are distinct;
- CS is a proxy/marker rather than complete function;
- acute PGC-1α signaling is not adaptation itself;
- training increases mitochondrial content/oxidative capacity;
- mitochondrial adaptation and VO2max are non-equivalent.

### MODERATE-HIGH

- ET/HIT/SIT produce broadly comparable content gains after accounting for relevant covariates;
- resistance training can improve mitochondrial respiratory function;
- cristae density materially explains mass-specific OXPHOS differences across training status.

### MODERATE / CONTEXT DEPENDENT

- exact effective in-vivo P/O;
- exact respiratory-control contribution from O2 vs ADP/Pi/Ca2+/substrates;
- exact superiority of one training modality for a specific mitochondrial endpoint;
- exact mapping from signaling markers to chronic structure/function.

### NOT RETAINED AS UNIVERSAL

- 36/38 ATP per glucose as fixed physiological truth;
- 3 ATP/NADH and 2 ATP/FADH2 as fixed in-vivo truth;
- O2 alone controls OXPHOS;
- CS = mitochondrial function;
- PGC-1α = biogenesis;
- more mitochondria = greater intrinsic function;
- aerobic metabolism only starts after initial anaerobic work;
- resistance training has no mitochondrial effect.
