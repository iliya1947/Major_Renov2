# GFT-11.3 — Communication, mutual understanding and cognitive differences

27.11.2026. COMPLETE_FOR_SPECIFIED_SCOPE (bounded general scientific-methodological scope). Source HEAD c58b8040fa1a7af038fe649f04f80a41ff6d0a01. Prospective question card before external search.
1. Question: under what conditions do differences in cognitive ability predict communicative accuracy, coordination or mutual understanding, beyond knowledge, common ground, language, goals, opportunity and interaction partner?
2. Separate comprehension, pragmatic inference, production/audience design, repair, coordination, satisfaction and perceived similarity; distinguish ability level, signed difference, absolute difference and dyadic composition.
3. Populations: general adults, youth, gifted identification, measured high-ability adults, separately measured adult IQ150–160+. No indirect tail claim.
4. Evidence: experimental referential communication and common ground; direct dyadic cognitive ability measures and outcomes; contradictory results; actual performance versus subjective report; targeted extreme-tail and purported communication-gap threshold search.
5. Compare role, prior knowledge, task difficulty, working memory, language, social motives and partner feedback. Observational assortative matching is not causal evidence of mutual understanding.
6. Prediction: shared knowledge or feedback changes understanding without any IQ change; if an IQ-gap hypothesis is supported, seek continuous and nonlinear estimates, both partners measured, prespecified breakpoint and controls. Null evidence does not prove equivalence.
7. Closure: bounded general scientific-methodological findings, documented source access, competing models and counterexamples; three novel synthetic tasks with contradictory updates; six criteria 0–2 each must reach 2, no critical errors; independent assessment if available, otherwise explicit self-review. No claim of universal threshold, diagnosis or qualification gain from self-review.
8. Privacy: only public science, generic method and invented cases. No personal material.

## Evidence
E1. Bovet, Knutsen & Fossard (2024, online 2023), systematic review, https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-023-02359-2. Abstract, inclusion and results sections inspected. 23 publications, searches 2021/2022, oral interactive matching tasks, healthy and clinical adults. Seven linguistic indicators distinguish referential content from discourse shape. Repeated references can become efficient; number of words/turns alone not proof of accurate common ground. Evidence on underlying cognitive/social functions sparse. Clinical results not extrapolated to gifted adults.

E2. Clark & Krych (2004), https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jml.2003.08.004; author abstract in ERIC https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ731310 directly inspected. Directors instructed builders to assemble ten models; visible workspace vs hidden vs recorded instructions. Hidden workspace slowed joint performance; taped instructions produced more errors. Supports feedback and observable repair under a task-specific manipulation, not an estimate of IQ effects. Full sample and effect intervals not verified. Indexed Holler/common-ground studies and https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36795429/ suggest familiarity/local common ground as additional candidates; PubMed blocked, not counted as independently verified estimates.

E3. De la Garza, Dabrowska & Gedik (2026), https://doi.org/10.1017/S014271642610071X. Cambridge HTML abstract, methods, regression and discussion inspected. Spanish-speaking female adults in Monterrey, two SES/education strata; paired picture-difference task; individual nonverbal IQ, education, literacy, ToM. Published model reports IQ beta .452, p<.001; education .356, p=.002; faux-pas .232, p=.037; R² about .68. Reading composite and false belief not retained. Important limitations: abstract says 40 (20+20), methods 44 (20+24); analysis-specific N and pairing/dependence not independently reconstructed. LMG averages sequential sums of squares, so allocations should not be called independent unique causal contributions; reported approximate percentages sum to ~70 rather than ~68, with rounding/model details unresolved. Different computer/paper modes by SES, small selected sample, stepwise selection and potential shared dyad outcome. Do not interpret as effect of IQ gap, generalized ability, causal effect of IQ or tail evidence. Q-11-source-03 open; preserve reported directions only with design caveats.

E4. Sources of variability in human communicative skills (2012), https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2012.00310. Frontiers HTML methods and results inspected. 54 male students 18–27 selected across EQ/SQ-R; one of 27 pairs excluded for exceptionally poor performance. Nonverbal Tacit Communication Game separates existing and newly constructed conventions. New vs old accuracy 49% vs 95% among analyzed pairs. Sender and receiver fixed roles; receiver Raven and sender need for cognition linked to new problem performance; pair mismatch did not predict overall performance or learning rate in reported analyses. Strategy correlations not identical to outcome; only 15 pairs for some mismatch measures, stepwise model, selected population, exclusion of failure. Direct limited counterevidence against simple difference-only model; no equivalence, universal gap claim or extreme-tail result.

E5. Denissen (2005), doctoral dissertation, https://doi.org/10.18452/15283. Institutional abstract directly inspected, dissertation full text not read. Perceived partner intelligence associated with perceived relationship quality; psychometric measures generally did not replicate association except vocabulary. Reported evidence did not support dyadic personality differences constraining communication quality. Selected gifted sample had lower adjustment with indications of oversampling adjustment problems. This is direct topical evidence but abstract-level only, sample sizes, exact gap models and effect precision unverified; non-peer-reviewed dissertation not independent replication of other data without further check. Distinguish perceived IQ from measured ability and satisfaction from communication.

E6. Blanchard et al. (2025), https://doi.org/10.1186/s41235-025-00655-0. Abstract, sample, methods, discussion inspected. 210 Australian undergraduates, 105 randomly formed dyads after exclusions; Raven Advanced Progressive Matrices and well-structured CHC group tasks. Individual intelligence and confidence strongest predictors of dyadic performance; social sensitivity association, original equality-of-turns and gender findings not replicated. Aggregate average predictors explicitly do not distinguish within-pair difference; group task success not communication or closeness. Exploratory latent profiles and role dominance interpretation not causal. Contrasts earlier Woolley et al. (2010), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20929725/ and meta-analysis https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8019454/ (indexed excerpts, PMC blocked), which highlight task, sample and measurement differences. No universal best conversational pattern.

E7. Stålnacke & Smedler (2011), https://doi.org/10.1177/0162353211424988, publisher abstract directly inspected. N302 Swedish adults, abstract-reasoning IQ >130, questionnaire: perceived difference but little need to conceal giftedness for social acceptance; lower SOC-13 than general Swedish comparator, interpretation ambiguous. Self-report, selection, no direct partner measurement, no separately analyzed extreme tail. Qualitative gifted-adult interview candidate https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7102/15/6/677 (indexed abstract; publisher 429) not quantified as objective communication. Gignac & Zajenkowski (2019), https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160289618302150, indexed publisher excerpt; full access 403; reported nonsignificant IQ compatibility–relationship satisfaction association, not proof of equal comprehension; candidate for 11.4.

## Synthesis / reasoning gate
Communication performance depends on goal, both partners, channel, shared knowledge, access to feedback, language, task complexity and situational resources. Distinguish understanding content from agreeing, reproducing procedure, inferring intent, perceived being understood, closeness and efficiency. Comprehension can be high despite disagreement or slow elaboration; brevity can be shared-context efficiency or omitted premises. Consider both individual levels (A and B), directed asymmetry (speaker vs receiver), absolute difference, shared competence, interaction and nonlinear alternatives. Identical gap at different mean levels is not equivalent. Difference scores compound measurement error and hide direction and levels; no post-hoc breakpoint. To estimate gap effects require both measured on comparable reliable scales with sufficient range, appropriately modeled dependence, prespecified outcomes and nonlinear comparison, distinguish expertise and language, adjust confounds justified by causal model. Report uncertainty and contradictory observations, not a universal number. Repeated episodes: compare task and topic changes, ask for paraphrase or application, offer missing premise/example, monitor whether repair improves criterion, compare roles and contexts. A mismatch that resolves after adding missing knowledge weakens a fixed-ability explanation but does not exclude all ability effects. A mismatch that persists under a single repair is not proof of ability deficit; consider disagreement, goal conflict, attention, language, inaccessible representation, mutual unwillingness and time. Avoid assuming extra explanatory cost always comes from the higher-IQ party. No diagnosis or IQ inferred from conversation. Social compatibility and preferences remain distinct for 11.4.

## Internal assessment (new wholly fictional cases)
A. Two fictional museum volunteers with unequal scores repeatedly misunderstand instructions for artifact labels; one knows local naming rules. Analyst claims the difference proves a universal IQ barrier. Compare equal shared vocabulary, role reversal, and correct label application; after a glossary and visible verification both succeed. Update: knowledge/feedback explanation strengthened, no universal cutoff, retain possible task-specific ability effect. Distinguish speed from accuracy.
B. Fictional puzzle dyads A/B have identical absolute score gaps but different mean scores; group scores differ. One analyst attributes all variance to discrepancy. Compare measured levels, signed roles, task demands and model of A/B jointly; after shifting problem domain and exchanging roles, rankings reverse. Revise difference-only claim; conditional competence and coordination plausible, no latent IQ or generalized hierarchy from this outcome. Require independent measured abilities and uncertain estimates.
C. Fictional committee member paraphrases a colleague's proposal correctly but rejects it and reports feeling dismissed. Analyst interprets rejection as comprehension failure. Separate semantic accuracy, agreement, recognition, motive and satisfaction. New: colleague confirms paraphrase while disagreement persists over budget; withdraw semantic deficit, retain substantive preference conflict and possible perceived disrespect; no inference of IQ or intent.
Six criteria: concepts 2 (distinct outcomes), evidence 2 (limited associations, direct mismatch null, source discrepancy), alternatives 2, contradictions 2, transfer 2, revision 2. Internal 12/12 same author/evaluator, no identified critical errors; not independent held-out evaluation or T1. Scope is general methods and selected evidence; no validated qualification gain.
