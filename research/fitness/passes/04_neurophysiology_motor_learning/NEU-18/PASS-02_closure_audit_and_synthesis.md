## 3. Outcome error vs movement error audit

PASS — DISTINCTION CONFIRMED.

For coaching:

### Outcome error
Failure to meet the task-success criterion.

### Movement error
Violation of a justified execution criterion.

These can dissociate.

A technically acceptable attempt may miss the task outcome.

A task-successful attempt may still violate a competition, safety, comfort or transfer-relevant criterion.

The 2026 J Neurosci study PMID 42680590 independently confirms that task-performance error and sensory prediction error are separable signals:

- SPE was held/induced independently;
- task-performance error magnitude was manipulated;
- SPE-mediated learning was insensitive to task-performance error magnitude in those clamp paradigms.

Canonical rule:

**name the failed criterion before naming the error.**

No blocker.

## 4. Stable vs random error audit

PASS WITH TERMINOLOGY LIMIT.

“Stable vs random error” is retained only operationally:

- recurrent/systematic pattern across comparable attempts;
- fluctuating/variable pattern across comparable attempts.

It is not treated as a universal formal motor-learning taxonomy.

Most important boundary:

**repeatability increases confidence that the observed phenomenon is real; it does not establish why it occurs.**

No universal minimum number of repetitions is established.

Exact reliability thresholds belong TST.

No blocker.

---

## 5. Variability audit

PASS — VARIABILITY ≠ ERROR.

The systematic review PMID 39547333 supports a context-dependent relation between initial motor variability and subsequent learning/adaptation.

The 2026 kettlebell-swing study PMID 42617501 provides a direct strength-skill example:

- n = 12;
- one week of kettlebell-swing practice;
- uncontrolled-manifold analysis separated solution variability from error variability;
- higher early solution variability was associated with fewer errors during transfer to a water-filled kettlebell;
- benefit appeared when error variability was low.

This directly supports:

**solution exploration can be useful while task-error variability remains controlled.**

It does not establish an optimal variability dose.

No blocker.

---

## 6. Task misunderstanding / goal representation audit

PASS.

PMID 32997556 shows that instruction can shape the initial representation of a motor sequence and leave persistent temporal traces after 10 days of practice.

PMID 41309101:

- n = 180;
- goal uncertainty was manipulated while controlling relevant visual properties;
- greater goal uncertainty attenuated implicit adaptation.

These studies are not compound-lift trials.

But they support a robust general boundary:

**an unclear or misrepresented goal can itself be a learning problem.**

Therefore “explain the task criterion” can be a more appropriate intervention than adding biomechanical corrections when misunderstanding is plausible.

No blocker.

---

## 7. Strength/capacity audit

PASS WITH CAUSAL LANGUAGE RESTRICTED.

Strength/force capacity can constrain movement mechanics and available solutions.

Current squat evidence supports:

- relationships between relative strength and some squat kinetic/kinematic variables;
- increasing intensity changes joint demand and coordination even in elite lifters.

2026 elite powerlifting study PMID 41826687:

- n = 29;
- 70–90% estimated 1RM;
- increasing load increased hip moments;
- relative joint contribution shifted toward the hip.

PMID 42342242:

- n = 8 elite national powerlifters;
- 90/95/100% 1RM;
- mechanics and torque/velocity changed across near-maximal/maximal loads.

But none of this supports:

> visible pattern X = weak muscle Y.

Canonical rule:

**capacity deficit is a testable hypothesis, not a visual diagnosis.**

No blocker.

---

## 8. Mobility audit

PASS WITH MEASUREMENT/PERTURBATION REQUIREMENT.

PMID 41196246:

- n = 44 physically active adults;
- limited ankle dorsiflexion group showed more pelvis ROM, hip/lumbar flexion and forward trunk lean;
- ankle DF correlated with trunk lean within the limited group.

This supports mobility as a possible constraint.

It does not prove that a given forward lean is caused by ankle limitation.

Stronger individual inference requires:

1. relevant mobility actually measured;
2. task requires that ROM;
3. modifying the constraint changes the movement in the predicted direction.

No blocker.

---

## 9. Load audit

PASS — LOAD CHANGES THE MOTOR PROBLEM.

The systematic review PMID 41085612:

- 22 studies;
- 293 experienced lifters;
- higher intensity consistently reduced bar velocity/power;
- concentric duration increased;
- joint mechanics became more variable, especially around acceleration/sticking regions;
- lifters used different mechanical strategies as intensity increased.

2026 elite studies independently confirm load-dependent redistribution.

Therefore:

**a movement pattern that appears only at high load cannot be interpreted as a pure learning/knowledge error without testing the demand effect.**

No blocker.

---

## 10. Fatigue audit

PASS — STATE CHANGE ≠ LOST LEARNING.

The 2025 systematic review found fatigue/proximity-to-failure associated with:

- lower velocity/power;
- altered/variable joint mechanics.

Important limitation:

- studies often operationalized fatigue indirectly through proximity to failure or performance decline;
- not all directly quantified neuromuscular fatigue.

Therefore:

**fatigue is a plausible state cause when the pattern emerges with accumulated effort and reverses after recovery, but appearance alone does not diagnose fatigue.**

No blocker.

---

## 11. Cue-induced error audit

PASS.

PMID 32997556 shows instruction can impose a poor initial task structure.

PMID 42190132 shows in lifting that:

- simple “activate your core” did not produce the targeted coordination change;
- detailed coaching changed displacement/stiffness;
- both cue conditions reduced maximal force acutely.

PMID 33170116 shows internal vs external squat instructions redistributed muscle excitation.

Therefore:

**a cue can create a new motor solution, including an undesirable one.**

Canonical diagnostic rule:

**if a problem appears after a cue, remove/rephrase that cue before stacking more instructions.**

No blocker.

---

## 12. Inappropriate movement solution audit

PASS — MUST BE TASK-RELATIVE.

A movement solution is not inappropriate because:

- it differs from an expert;
- it differs from the learner's previous repetition;
- it looks unusual.

It becomes inappropriate if it violates a justified criterion such as:

- task failure;
- competition rule;
- defined safety/comfort boundary;
- transfer requirement;
- unnecessary cost relevant to the current goal.

The kettlebell solution-variability evidence directly rejects exact-template thinking.

No blocker.

---

## 13. “Correct the cause, not the symptom” audit

PASS AS DIAGNOSTIC HEURISTIC, NOT DIRECT UNIVERSAL TREATMENT EVIDENCE.

No strong direct resistance-exercise RCT evidence was identified showing a universal advantage of:

`cause-targeted correction`
over
`symptom-targeted correction`

across technical errors.

The defensible interpretation is methodological:

**do not assert a cause from appearance; test plausible explanations.**

Candidate domains:

- task representation;
- load;
- fatigue;
- mobility;
- capacity;
- cue/instruction;
- setup/environment;
- movement-solution choice.

Multiple causes can coexist.

No blocker.

---

## 14. One-constraint perturbation audit

PASS AS PRACTICAL N-OF-1 HEURISTIC.

A useful coaching diagnostic approach is to manipulate one plausible driver while keeping others as stable as practical.

Examples:

- lower load;
- retest after recovery;
- remove/rephrase cue;
- alter heel support/stance/ROM when mechanically justified;
- clarify task criterion;
- compare within-capacity vs near-capacity demand.

Interpretation:

- predicted change supports the hypothesis;
- no change weakens it;
- neither establishes universal causality from one trial.

This is **not** a medical diagnostic protocol.

No blocker.

## 15. Task/environment manipulation audit

PASS — LEGITIMATE TOOL, NOT UNIVERSAL SUPERIORITY.

The 2026 errorless-learning meta-analysis PMID 41953321:

- 29 studies / 31 experiments;
- 1,509 participants;
- no significant overall advantage for movement performance:
  - g ≈ 0.05;
- movement accuracy showed a positive pooled effect;
- benefits were stronger in children/participants with impairments;
- no clear advantage in young adults/participants without impairments for several outcomes;
- bias concerns and substantial heterogeneity;
- GRADE certainty very low to low.

Therefore:

**errorless practice / environmental constraint manipulation cannot be declared universally superior to errorful practice.**

No blocker.

---

## 16. Heel-elevation perturbation audit

PASS AS DIRECT EXAMPLE OF CONSTRAINT EFFECT.

The 2026 heel-elevation meta-analysis PMID 41612762:

- 14 studies;
- n = 177;
- ankle ROM increased by about 4.33°;
- knee ROM increased by about 4.94°;
- no overall significant hip/trunk ROM effect;
- meta-regression suggested greater heel height related to lower hip/trunk ROM.

This confirms:

**changing support geometry changes the movement solution.**

It does not prove:

- better technique;
- corrected learning;
- ankle restriction as the cause in every lifter;
- durable transfer after heel removal.

No blocker.

---

## 17. Errorless vs informative error audit

PASS.

The current evidence rejects both extremes:

`all errors should be eliminated`

and

`errors are always beneficial`.

The defensible synthesis is:

- excessive task-error variability can obscure successful solutions;
- some exploration/solution variability can support adaptation/transfer;
- errorless structures can help some populations/tasks;
- healthy-young-adult evidence does not show a universal errorless advantage.

Canonical rule:

**seek informative, tolerable error rather than zero error or uncontrolled error.**

No blocker.

## 18. “Bad technique” audit

PASS — PHRASE REJECTED AS CAUSAL DIAGNOSIS.

The same visible phenomenon can plausibly reflect:

- misunderstanding;
- load;
- fatigue;
- mobility;
- capacity;
- cue;
- setup/environment;
- harmless individual solution variability;
- measurement/viewpoint error.

Therefore the diagnostic chain is retained:

`observed phenomenon`
→
`task relevance`
→
`recurrence under comparable conditions`
→
`candidate causes`
→
`controlled perturbation / measurement`
→
`response`
→
`correction decision`.

This is the central NEU-18 synthesis.

No blocker.

## 20. Measurement non-equivalence audit

PASS.

Separate:

### Outcome error
Failure to meet task success.

### Movement error
Violation of a justified execution criterion.

### Sensory prediction error
Mismatch between predicted and actual sensory consequence.

### Stable/systematic pattern
Repeated similar deviation under comparable conditions.

### Random/variable pattern
Fluctuating deviation under comparable conditions.

### Solution variability
Different movements preserving task success.

### Error variability
Variation degrading task outcome/constraint.

### Candidate cause
Hypothesis requiring evidence.

### Correction
Intervention intended to change the relevant driver/criterion.

### Compensation
Alternative solution, not automatically pathological or wrong.

Project rule:

**do not collapse observation, criterion failure, mechanism and diagnosis.**

No blocker.
