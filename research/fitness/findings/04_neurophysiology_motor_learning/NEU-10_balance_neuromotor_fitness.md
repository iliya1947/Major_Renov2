# NEU-10 — Balance / neuromotor fitness

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-19**  
Область: 04 — Neurophysiology / Motor Learning

## 0. Core decision

Balance is a task-specific functional outcome of distributed postural-control processes.

Canonical model:

`task + environment + body mechanics`
+
`visual + vestibular + somatosensory information`
→
`context-dependent sensory weighting`
→
`predictive + reactive postural control`
→
`task-specific balance outcome`.

Project rule:

**balance is multidimensional; one test, one sensory condition or one sway metric cannot represent total balance capacity.**

## 1. Sensory contributions

Visual, vestibular and somatosensory information are integrated rather than used with fixed universal weights.

Project rule:

**sensory weighting changes with task, reliability, conflict, support surface and prior exposure.**

Eyes-closed stance does not isolate vestibular function.

Foam/unstable-surface stance does not isolate proprioception.

## 2. Static balance

Static balance is steady-state control with a stationary base of support.

Examples:

- quiet stance;
- tandem stance;
- single-leg stance.

Project rule:

**static balance is one task family, not a global balance score.**

## 3. Dynamic balance

Dynamic balance involves control during:

- locomotion;
- reaching;
- transitions;
- changing COM/base-of-support conditions.

Y-Balance/SEBT-type tests combine balance with mobility, strength, anthropometry and coordination.

Project rule:

**dynamic balance tests are task specific and not interchangeable.**

## 4. Balance-domain taxonomy

Canonical project taxonomy:

1. static steady-state;
2. dynamic steady-state;
3. proactive/anticipatory;
4. reactive.

Project rule:

**balance domains are only weakly coupled and should be matched to the actual downstream demand.**

## 5. Postural sway

Postural sway is commonly quantified using center-of-pressure measures such as:

- path length;
- velocity;
- area;
- amplitude;
- frequency-domain features.

Project rule:

**COP ≠ COM ≠ total balance ability.**

Also:

**less sway is not universally better.**

Lower amplitude can reflect a cautious/stiffened strategy rather than globally improved control.

## 6. Reactive balance

Reactive balance is compensation after an unexpected perturbation.

Relevant tasks include:

- slips;
- trips;
- platform translations;
- waist pulls;
- unexpected support changes.

Project rule:

**reactive balance requires perturbation-matched testing/training; quiet stance is not an adequate substitute.**

Optimal perturbation-training dose remains uncertain.

## 7. Proactive / anticipatory balance

Proactive balance is preparation before a predicted/self-generated challenge.

Anticipatory postural adjustments are one mechanism within proactive control.

Project rule:

**proactive balance and reactive balance are distinct control demands.**

## 8. Training specificity

Balance training improves trained/similar tasks much more than dissimilar untrained tasks.

Project rule:

**balance skill learning is strongly task specific.**

Generic one-leg stance or one unstable-surface drill should not be treated as a universal balance intervention.

## 9. Transfer to strength / power

Balance training can improve balance and some related performance outcomes.

But transfer to maximal strength/power is limited and inconsistent.

Project rule:

**balance training is not a substitute for specific resistance training when maximal strength is the target.**

## 10. Unstable vs stable resistance training

Unstable-surface resistance training can be useful when instability/balance is itself part of the target.

Current evidence does not establish a universal maximal-strength advantage over stable resistance training.

Project rule:

**choose instability for a task-specific reason, not because it is assumed to produce superior strength or “stabilizer activation”.**

## 11. Reverse transfer from resistance training

Resistance training can improve some balance outcomes, particularly in adults/older adults.

Project rule:

**strength can support balance but does not replace sensory integration, proactive/reactive control or task-specific coordination.**

## 12. Clinical boundary

Fitness balance tests describe performance.

They do not diagnose:

- vestibular hypofunction;
- BPPV;
- vestibular migraine;
- cerebellar/brainstem disorders;
- stroke/TIA;
- neuropathy;
- other pathological causes of dizziness/imbalance.

Project rule:

**symptomatic or unexplained pathological imbalance belongs to MED, not fitness classification.**

## 13. Canonical project rules

1. Balance is multidimensional and task specific.
2. Sensory weighting is dynamic.
3. Eyes closed is not a pure vestibular test.
4. Foam is not a pure proprioception test.
5. Static balance is not a proxy for all balance.
6. Dynamic tests are construct specific.
7. COP/sway is not balance itself.
8. Less sway is not always better.
9. Reactive balance follows unexpected perturbation.
10. Proactive balance prepares for predicted perturbation.
11. Balance training is strongly task specific.
12. Transfer to untrained balance tasks is limited.
13. Generic balance training has limited transfer to maximal strength.
14. Unstable RT is not universally superior to stable RT for strength.
15. Resistance training can support some balance outcomes.
16. Fitness balance tests do not diagnose clinical pathology.

## 14. Training-relevant implications

1. **Define the balance domain before testing or training it.**
2. **Do not use one balance test as a global neuromotor score.**
3. **Match reactive-balance goals with perturbation tasks.**
4. **Match proactive-balance goals with predictable/self-generated challenges.**
5. **Interpret sway by exact metric and task.**
6. **Use balance-specific training when balance itself is limiting.**
7. **Use specific resistance training when maximal strength is the target.**
8. **Do not add instability without a defined training purpose.**
9. **Route unexplained dizziness/vertigo/pathological imbalance to MED.**

## 15. What NEU-10 does not prescribe

Not owned here:

- individual balance test battery;
- exact perturbation-training dose;
- exact sensory-reweighting progression;
- exact unstable-surface dose;
- fall-risk diagnosis;
- vestibular diagnosis;
- individual need for dedicated balance training;
- sport-specific balance prescription.

These remain TST, TEC, PRO, IND and MED.

## 16. Confidence

### HIGH
- multisensory integration;
- balance task specificity;
- domain distinction;
- sway/COP interpretation limits;
- proactive/reactive distinction;
- clinical-boundary rule.

### MODERATE-HIGH
- trainable sensory reweighting;
- strong specificity of balance training;
- limited transfer to strength/power;
- lack of universal strength advantage from unstable RT;
- resistance training improving some balance outcomes.

### MODERATE / CONTEXT-DEPENDENT
- exact transfer among balance tasks;
- optimal perturbation dose;
- optimal sensory-challenge progression;
- exact value of dedicated balance training in healthy strength trainees.

### NOT ESTABLISHED AS UNIVERSAL
- one global balance ability;
- one ideal sway value;
- one universal balance test;
- one fixed sensory weighting;
- unstable surfaces as superior strength training.
