## 2. Corrections and substantive extensions

R1 — VSA independent associations versus prediction of change. Direct Ross Methods identify 431 recruited first-married couples, 43 excluded due to missing/incomplete task and/or questionnaires, 388 analyzed; 36.3% entered marriage with children, not exclusively childless newlyweds. Later interviews not completed after separation/divorce; no general conclusion for dissolution. Aim1 main-effects model: wives' initial satisfaction intercept associations with vulnerabilities beta=-.33, chronic stress -.18 and adaptive processes .47; husbands' vulnerabilities -.43 and adaptive processes .32, stress -.16 nonsignificant. Main predictors did not predict slopes; Limitations specify all significant main effects and interactions except moderated wives' vulnerability-slope association concern intercepts. Original REL-X03.1 statement about satisfaction associations is correct but insufficiently specific; do not call this prospective prediction of change. All tested indirect effects nonsignificant (5000 bootstrap simulations); this does not identify absence of every behavioral pathway, since brief coded baseline interactions represent only one aspect of adaptation. The intercept associations largely contemporaneous, not evidence of causal direct effects independent of communication. McNulty pooled ten studies, time-varying stress and specific observed behavior; Ross discusses differences in coding, sampling and tasks as unresolved possibilities. No exact shared dataset audit; distinct study labels not proof of independent samples. Root cause: abstract's broad 'trajectories' language can obscure intercept/slope distinction. Retest direct Results and Limitations; preserve prediction of levels vs change from REL-X01/IND-03. No general intervention efficacy conclusion.

R2 — Role transition, baseline and endpoint. Doss modeled sudden level shift around birth in addition to gradual trajectories; Mitnick contrasts average changes over periods. They are not the same estimand. Distinguish actual discontinuity from counterfactual effect of childbirth: selection into pregnancy, anticipation, relationship duration, concurrent role/health/resource changes and missingness remain possible. Bogdan reporting/heterogeneity and sparse comparators already audited WOM-05 R6, carried forward unchanged. Household labor fairness measures are subjective single items; equality, actual burden, desired allocation and perceived fairness distinct. Null mediation does not establish no effect of fairness or identified direct effect of work redistribution. Conger family stress model distinguishes resources and experienced pressure; observed problem solving moderation not randomized intervention. Stepfamily abstract verifies limited directional cross-spouse associations, not universal mutual effects; separate moderator interaction, configuration and subgroup significance. Root cause: specify comparison and observable constructs before causal transfer. Fresh checks limited to indexed abstracts/excerpts; exact longitudinal coefficients and cohort overlap not verified. No new universal maternal or family trajectory.

R3 — Resolve prior stress/quality sign ambiguity at levels, expose remaining discrepancies. Williams & Parra original published PDF Methods: FFCWS 2341 co-resident couples at child age1; exclude347 missing interview and317 relationship ended by age3, then239 missing interview and199 relationship ended by age5, leaving1239 (769 married,470 cohabiting at baseline), 52.93% of initial risk set. Included people remained romantically involved/cohabiting at all three waves. This conditions on continuation and interview coverage; not applicable unconditionally to couples who separate, no estimate of breakup risk. Maternal parenting stress scale alpha .56/.59/.64, paternal .58/.61/.61; relationship quality partner-behavior frequency items, not a global objective quality measure. Models for mothers and fathers separately, authors explicitly say not dyadic models. RI-CLPM separates stable between-person component and time-specific deviations. Results §3.2: between-person r=-.37 mothers, -.27 fathers (p<.001 both); discussion agrees, while published abstract describes higher quality with higher stress without adequate level specification. Correct use: negative stable between-person correlation. Within-person maternal stress age1 -> own quality age3 beta=.12, positive; reverse not significant. Father stress age3 -> quality age5 beta=.08 described as marginal p<.10, not conventional significant; discussion loosely calls within-person associations statistically significant. Table3 maternal .12 SE .04 marked *p<.05 vs prose p<.01 (not necessarily incompatible threshold but exact p not rederived). Table3 text shows maternal CLPM PS3->RQ5 -.11 and RQ1->PS3 -.03, whereas prose reports reverse RQ1->PS3 -.11 and later PS3->RQ5 -.03. Unresolved table/prose assignment; screenshot attempts failed so possible extraction/layout issue cannot be excluded. Use clearly stated within-person direction and between-person correlations, not disputed exact CLPM path labels. No proof that stress improves relationship quality; contemporaneous/lagged unmeasured confounding, response shifts, attrition and measurement reliability remain. Added primary methodological reference Hamaker et al. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25822208/ original abstract on CLPM conflation of within/between-person differences. Additional preregistered 20-year Finnish study https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41587156/ and directly read Helsinki institutional abstract (https://researchportal.helsinki.fi/en/publications/psychological-distress-and-partnership-quality-in-parents-a-20-ye/): 375 intact couples, RI-CLPM, distinct intimacy/autonomy and their sum/balance. R4 — Power domain, statistical threshold and comparison. Direct Körner Results: men reported higher positional power d=.85, power motive d=.53; experienced power d=-.07 and satisfaction with power d=-.08, reported nonsignificant sex differences, not equivalence. Table2 own experienced power b=.27, bootstrapped99% CI[.16,.39]; selected partner paths differ, female actor->male total quality b=.09 p=.012 not significant by study's conservative criterion, male actor->female quality b=.21 p<.001. No direct sex contrast inferred from this difference in p values. Positional power Table3 small actor associations p=.025/.026 not significant by author threshold; do not equate to zero associations. Table6 balance estimates imprecise and generally null; not proof of equal actual control or harmless inequality. Table5 power motive and sexuality outcome isolated association, not generalized effect on all relationship quality or causal desire. Actual decision freedom, practical constraints, influence domain, satisfaction with influence and control over resources need separate evidence. Further Körner & Schütz 2026 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41511384/ indexed abstract N1109/four studies including LGBTQ couples separates experienced and desired power; no new precise effects or independent cohort count verified. Root cause: original note lacked actual thresholds, selected partner contrasts and desired vs experienced power distinction. Retest direct Tables2–6, preserve no causal effect or manipulation inference. Coercive conduct and ability to refuse remain independent safety/rights issues, not symmetrical outcome variables.

R5 — Autonomy, accommodation and sacrifice. Kluwer original abstract: three studies, autonomy and relatedness interaction in reported accommodation; cannot infer a causal mechanism or assert that closeness always produces accommodation at high autonomy. Relatedness satisfaction not romantic desire toward a particular partner; autonomy not independence or physical distance. Carry WOM-02 R1 psychometric distinction; need satisfaction and frustration scale validity not presumed. Righetti indexed primary abstract reports actual enacted sacrifice has small negative own personal well-being association r=-.07, distinct from willingness, costs and satisfaction with sacrifice. 9547 effects from82 datasets and32053 participants not 9547 independent replications; no individualized cost/benefit coefficient. Different outcome channels and reciprocal appraisals prevent a single overall benefit estimate. Do not use sacrifices, family duties, receipt of help or coordination as evidence of obligation or hidden commitment. Root cause: expand explicit link to measurement and actor/partner outcome; no new pooled effect. Retest primary abstracts, no full moderation or risk-of-bias audit.

R6 — Heterogeneity, family configuration, dependent samples and outcome. Choi, Williams, Kil and residence/romance studies use FFCWS source infrastructure as described by original records; same cohort lineage not necessarily identical analytic rows. Do not count four distinct articles as four independent population replications. Kil Study1 N574 interethnic unions compared matched parents, Study2 N1148 parents from those same unions, not extra independent cohort; do not confuse parent-level profile proportions with couple-level prevalence. 75.5% stable positive reported, other two profile proportions 13.1/11.4 inherited, sum100 arithmetically but full fit/classification probabilities not rechecked (SAGE403). E8 multiperspective four profiles 42/34/13/11 sum100, model-dependent classifications, not verified individual types or proof of deception. E7 residence/romance N1756 selected stable configuration over four years, not causal effect of changing residence or romantic status. E6 Building Strong Families is a different named source, direct participant overlap not audited. Zhang review 34 articles, 16 quantitative; no family-structure causal effect or equivalence from nonsignificant endpoints. Differences among child adjustment, adult relationship quality and parenting stress matter; cultural/social context and selection not reducible to demographic labels. Early child behavior and later adolescent social anxiety/hostility studies differ in outcome and lag, not directly comparable bidirectional causal tests. Root cause: make cohort lineage, stable-configuration selection and unit of profile explicit. Retest indexed original records and arithmetic; no exact cross-study participant linkage, effect or subgroup precision audit.

Correction specifies two Figure4 histograms omitted their tallest bars; online article corrected. No verified change of inferential estimates stated in correction; do not rely on uncorrected visual distribution or claim complete reanalysis. Higher perceived mutual dependence and lower conflicting interests associated with more cooperation; relative power not directly associated in reported main relation, interactions with interest conflict; laboratory experiments in Study2 reported corresponding associations. Exact manipulation/contrast and coefficients not freshly inspected. No causal transfer from laboratory to whole relationship. Situation reports nested in people and dyads; same interaction can be reported twice, 14014 reports not independent interactions or individuals. Inferences about interdependence additional primary source https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37055313/ indexed abstract: distinguish actual payoff structure from subjective inference; no individual motive or effect asserted. Root cause: original note was only design evidence and omitted published correction. Retest complete correction abstract and original sample arithmetic; include corrected figure status, not unverified figures.

## 4. Revised integrated method
Define actual subsystem, actor, outcome, relationship structure, decision domain, time and channel. Separate role duties, freely chosen goals, practical opportunities, perceived/actual influence, power, dedication, constraints, coping, shared exposure, affection and relationship choice. Preserve individual welfare and safety rather than treating family stability as universal objective. For dynamics, distinguish stable between-family/person differences, within-person deviations, baseline intercept, slope, discontinuity, own effects and partner effects. Model dependence and actual context; check survivor selection and observed versus counterfactual transition. A causal pathway needs design and assumptions beyond a fitted APIM or CLPM/RI-CLPM coefficient; distinguish shared event, spillover, crossover and reciprocal feedback. Check measurement reliability, exact estimand, direct moderator test, missingness and evidence lineage. Use naturally available evidence and explicit disconfirming observations; no inference of romantic intent from coparenting, gratitude, dependence, support or family duties. No pressure, covert tests, induced distress, manipulation of children or interference with ability to leave. Respect explicit choices. No numerical individual forecast without validated evidence.
