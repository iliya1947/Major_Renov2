1. Segerstrom, Monroe & Crofford (2022 issue; online 2021-06-15). Pain, Goal Engagement, and Eudemonic Well-Being: Moderation by Autonomous Motivation. https://doi.org/10.1093/geronb/gbab105 . Publisher abstract, design, selected methods, Table 2, results and discussion inspected, not every method paragraph or supplement. 200 women aged 50–75, 10,697 diaries over two years in seven-day bursts every three months. DAHLiA cohort also used in WOM-02.1 E7: not independent cohort replication. Pain-to-engagement hypotheses unsupported. Within-person association between time in goal activities and eudemonic well-being varied with autonomous motivation: slopes at +1 SD .010 (95% CI .005–.015), at -1 SD -.006 (CI -.010 to -.001). Above-usual goal number associated with concurrent well-being (.021, CI .012–.029); prior-day goal number associated with slightly lower well-being in exploratory analysis (-.009, CI -.018 to -.0002). Pain interaction also exploratory. Eudemonic well-being includes need-satisfaction components and differs from momentary emotion. Construct overlap, self-report, observational design, generally mild pain, selected older educated women (99% White), no formal preregistration and unavailable supplement audit constrain interpretation. No identified causal feedback or individual probabilities.
2. Jakubiak & Feeney (2016). Daily goal progress is facilitated by spousal support and promotes psychological, physical, and relational well-being throughout adulthood. https://doi.org/10.1037/pspi0000062 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27560610/ . Indexed primary abstract and excerpts; direct PMC opening blocked. Two diary studies of younger newlywed and older married couples. Above-usual personal goal progress associated with concurrent and later well-being, including positive affect; some associations stronger for women, not uniformly. Exact coefficients, sample counts, multiplicity and dyadic methods unverified. No universal female sensitivity to support or causal attribution; defer detailed relationship mechanisms to REL-X01/03.
3. Uchida et al. (2026 issue). Trait emotion differentiation is associated with more instrumental emotion regulation motives when people feel negative in daily life. https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0001585 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41021493/ . Indexed journal abstract and excerpts, full author-institution preprint abstract (https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/xzu92_v1) and linked peer-reviewed publication metadata; final linked record cache miss. Two datasets N=173/104. No direct association between trait differentiation and instrumental motives; moderation by negative, not positive emotion intensity reported in both studies. Higher differentiation associated with fewer instrumental motives at low negative intensity and more at high intensity. Final coefficients and methods unverified; no verified gender moderator. Preprint and article are one research stream. Distinguish identification of emotions, purpose of regulation, implementation and effectiveness.

## 4. Integrated procedure

Context, history, social roles and resources interact with values and needs; goals differ in endorsement, priority, attainability and appraisal; affect and action tendencies can influence effort and regulatory aims; behavior and outcomes may alter subsequent affect, resources, expectancy and allocation. This is a conceptual feedback map, not an identified causal model. No universal female hierarchy is established.

1. Define actual target goal, observed behavior, emotional proposition, time window and evidence channel. Preserve source dependence and opportunities.
2. Distinguish need satisfaction, value, absolute importance, relative priority, autonomous endorsement, expectancy, resources, effort and progress; mark unknowns. Enacted roles do not uniquely identify motives.
3. Distinguish appraisal, experience, expression, regulatory purpose and action. Mixed affect, alternation and goal conflict are separate. Do not infer hidden emotion from low expression or lost interest from distress.
4. Track separate outcome channels: goal progress, affect, well-being, costs and later actions. Immediate relief is not target attainment; high effort is not necessarily autonomous interest. Consider reciprocal direction, event exposure and timescales.
5. Compare explanations of changes and rival conditional forecasts; identify discriminating natural observations and revision triggers. Group patterns constrain hypotheses but do not establish a woman's motivation.
6. Apply IND-03 and INT-02; no invented probabilities, covert tests, pressure or engineered distress. Respect explicit choices independently of inferred feelings.
