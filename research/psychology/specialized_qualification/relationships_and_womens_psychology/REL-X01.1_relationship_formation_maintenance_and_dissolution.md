# REL-X01.1 — Romantic relationship formation, maintenance and dissolution

Date: 2026-11-26. Status: COMPLETE_FOR_SPECIFIED_SCOPE (internal methodological review only); REL-X01 IN_PROGRESS.

## 1. Question and dependencies
How can initial attraction, emerging mutual relationship choice, maintenance, satisfaction, commitment and eventual persistence or dissolution be distinguished in individual and dyadic inference? Reopened WOM-06.3 in full at the active branch and inspected targeted excerpts from REL-01, WOM-03.3 and IND-03.3; not a full reread of those three dependencies. SPQ-00 protocol and scope remain applicable. Retain distinctions between partner-specific desire and general desire, attachment and commitment, self-report and observable behavior, and between actor, partner and shared contextual influences. Scope: selected evidence on adult romantic relationships; not a diagnosis, universal stage model or prescription for inducing attachment. Claim types: description, association, prospective prediction and limited experimental evidence. Closure requires relevant evidence, counterexamples to construct equivalence, explicit inference rules and synthetic checks.

## 2. Source ledger
Public searches 2026-11-26: speed-dating ideal preferences and prospective attraction; friends-first initiation; investment-model meta-analysis; dedication versus constraint and subsequent stability; relationship maintenance meta-analysis; nonmarital dissolution meta-analysis; longitudinal prediction of relationship quality; long-distance relationship stability. Selected primary studies and syntheses with contrasting outcomes; not systematic or exhaustive. No article read end-to-end. Several publisher and PubMed/PMC opens failed (403, cache miss or CAPTCHA); indexed author extracts used with access limitations. No private information included in searches.

E1. Baxter et al. (2022), Initial impressions of compatibility and mate value predict later dating and romantic interest. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2206925119 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36322750/ . Indexed author abstract and publisher extracts. Three speed-dating samples (559 participants, over 6,600 encounters) with longitudinal follow-up. Social relations modeling partitions initial ratings into actor, partner and relationship-specific effects; consensual desirability and unique liking predicted later interest and initiation behaviors. Unique liking is an operationalized residual, not proof of durable compatibility. Observational prediction, not causal intervention; authors did not reassess how these impressions changed after the event. Speed-dating selection and early follow-up limit transfer to established relationships. Exact coefficients and attrition not verified.

E2. Eastwick et al. (2011), When and why do ideal partner preferences affect the process of initiating and maintaining romantic relationships? https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21707198/ . Indexed author abstract; direct access blocked. Two laboratory experiments and a longitudinal adult study. Profile-based preference matching affected abstract evaluations, but matching had different predictive relevance after live interaction and in established relationships. Counterexample to treating explicit ideals as a universal choice rule. Distinguish trait-pattern matching from mean level and self-reported preferences from actual choice. Exact sample sizes and effect estimates not verified.

E3. Stinson, Cameron & Hoplock (2022 issue), The Friends-to-Lovers Pathway to Romance: Prevalent, Preferred, and Overlooked by Science. https://doi.org/10.1177/19485506211026992 . Directly inspected publisher abstract and Study 3 method/results. Seven convenience samples (1,897 students and crowdsourced adults in Canada/US); retrospective accounts of successful current or prior romances. Random-effects median proportion reporting friendship preceding romance was 0.66 (95% CI 0.59–0.72), with high heterogeneity (I2=86%). Measures and selection differ across samples. Crucial denominator distinction: proportion of romances preceded by friendship is not probability of a friendship becoming romance. No implication that remaining friends creates attraction or that unreciprocated interest will become reciprocal. Earlier friendship can be one of multiple initiation pathways.

E4. Tran, Judge & Kashima (2019), Commitment in relationships: An updated meta-analysis of the Investment Model. https://doi.org/10.1111/pere.12268 ; https://research.rug.nl/en/publications/commitment-in-relationships-an-updated-meta-analysis-of-the-inves/ . Complete author abstract directly inspected on university repository; final version embargoed there. Across 202 samples (50,427 participants), pooled correlations of commitment with satisfaction r=.65, investment r=.53, and perceived quality of alternatives r=-.43. These are aggregate associations, not individual coefficients or an additive causal equation. Moderators reported; exact moderator estimates, confidence intervals and dependence handling not inspected. Alternatives include nonrelationship alternatives; investments and perceived options may themselves change with commitment. Prior meta-analysis Le & Agnew (2003), https://doi.org/10.1111/1475-6811.00035 , indexed only, also reported prospective breakup association; overlapping evidence likely, not independent replication.

E5. Rhoades, Stanley & Markman (2010), Should I stay or should I go? Predicting dating relationship stability from four aspects of commitment. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20954764/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2958669/ . Indexed author abstract and manuscript excerpts; direct PubMed blocked. Dedication, material constraints and perceived constraints were positively associated with staying over eight months, while feeling trapped had a negative association when examined jointly. Staying and wanting to stay are distinct; objective barriers are not identical to perceived entrapment. Observational multivariable prediction does not justify causal claims about changing constraints, and never warrants adding barriers to exit. Sample count, estimates and precision not independently verified.

E6. Ogolsky & Bowers (2013 issue), A meta-analytic review of relationship maintenance and its correlates. https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407512463338 . Direct publisher abstract (full text restricted). 35 studies, 12,273 participants. Five self-reported maintenance categories (positive interaction, openness, assurances, network involvement, shared tasks) were positively correlated with several quality indicators, but relationship duration showed negative associations with positivity/assurances and null associations with the other categories. Reporter and instrument moderated estimates; partner-perceived maintenance generally showed stronger correlations. No demonstrated causal increase in love from any maintenance behavior; duration is a distinct endpoint. Common-method overlap, reverse causation and selection remain relevant. No exact pooled coefficients beyond abstract verified.

E7. Le et al. (2010), Predicting nonmarital romantic relationship dissolution: A meta-analytic synthesis. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1475-6811.2010.01285.x . Indexed publisher/author abstract, direct publisher returned 403. 137 studies, 37,761 participants. Commitment, love, inclusion of other in self and dependence were among stronger correlates/predictors of subsequent dissolution. No unique causal mechanism or individual breakup probability established; relationship definitions, observation windows and effect precision not independently checked. Likely overlap with investment-model literature. Nonmarital samples do not automatically generalize to marriage or unsafe relationships.

E8. Joel et al. (2020), Machine learning uncovers the most robust self-report predictors of relationship quality across 43 longitudinal couples studies. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1917036117 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32719123/ . Indexed author abstract and manuscript extracts; direct publisher 403 and PubMed CAPTCHA. Across 43 dyadic datasets, 11,196 couples, 29 laboratories, prominent relationship-specific predictors included perceived partner commitment, appreciation, sexual satisfaction, perceived partner satisfaction and conflict. Baseline relationship perceptions accounted for up to 45% of concurrent quality variance and up to 18% of later quality variance in the reported analyses; changes in quality were largely unpredictable from baseline self-report combinations. Distinguish predicting a future level from predicting change beyond baseline. Perception of a partner's commitment is not an independent measurement of that partner's actual commitment. Exact model-specific performance, missingness and external validity not fully checked. No person-specific forecast calibration.

E9. Kelmer et al. (2013), Relationship quality, commitment, and stability in long-distance relationships. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1545-5300.2012.01418.x ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23763685/ . Indexed author abstract. Long-distance participants reported several higher quality indicators and lower perceived breakup risk initially, but observed breakup likelihood did not differ significantly at follow-up. This is counterevidence to treating perceived stability or quality as interchangeable with actual persistence. Null is not equivalence; sample size, follow-up and uncertainty not verified here. No general causal claim about distance.

## 3. Construct map and inference framework
Relationship formation is a transition jointly defined or enacted, not an automatic consequence of attraction. Separate each actor's target-specific attraction, desired relationship type, intention to act, available opportunity, actual choice and mutual agreement. Friendship, emotional closeness, romantic desire and sexual desire can coexist or diverge. Do not infer an unexpressed preference from persistence of contact.

Maintenance refers to actions or processes that sustain some aspect of the relationship; observed enactment and partner perception can diverge. Satisfaction is an evaluation, not synonymous with love or safety. Commitment can denote intention/dedication to continue, or operational measures combining dependence and constraints; define the instrument and target construct. Persistence is an observed status over a specified interval and can occur with low satisfaction or low desire. Dissolution is a transition, potentially unilateral; later contact does not by itself reestablish the prior romantic relationship.

Candidate model (hypothesis map, not identified causal DAG): actor characteristics and goals + partner-specific evaluations + context and opportunity -> initiation choices; reciprocal experiences, appraisal and external demands -> quality and dedication; dedication and structural constraints may influence persistence and maintenance; persistence also changes investments, alternatives and evaluations. Feedback and selection among persisting couples complicate interpretation. Do not treat the map as a fixed progression or assume greater contact implies a later romantic stage.

Operational rules:
1. Define the relationship and actor, time window, outcome and evidence channel. Distinguish forming a mutually desired romance from maintaining another kind of bond.
2. Separate observable initiative, responses, freely stated preferences, actual feasible opportunities, satisfaction, dedication and costs of leaving. Record discrepancies rather than combining them into a single interest score.
3. For the same observed persistence compare desire to maintain the bond, habit, external obligations, mutual enjoyment, lack of alternatives or constraint; allow coexistence. Do not assign a hidden motive without discriminating evidence.
4. Keep predictions at their actual endpoint: subsequent messaging, dates, reported quality, change in quality, mutual relationship transition and breakup are different. Consider base rates, selection, actor/partner/shared effects, measurement overlap and uncertainty. No reverse-conditional inference from friends-first prevalence.
5. Evaluate both sides of mutuality; one actor's interest cannot establish the other actor's intent. A clear stated choice takes precedence for action over speculation about latent states. Respect consent and freedom to leave; no engineered scarcity, dependence, distress or constraints.
6. In individual inference use comparable naturally occurring observations and voluntary statements. Specify which evidence would strengthen or weaken each explanation before updating. Lack of data leaves competing hypotheses unresolved. Do not equate observed continued relationship with a healthy, safe or desired relationship.
7. Keep separate questions of interpersonal quality, attachment regulation, family interdependence and sexual intimacy for subsequent blocks; no premature closure of all REL-X01 directions.

## 4. Internal transfer checks
Rubric before evaluating examples: construct separation, competing explanation, distinguishing evidence, inference boundary. Critical failures: group-to-individual determinism, causal inflation, unsupported diagnosis/motive, ignoring contrary evidence, coercion, privacy breach. Self-authored synthetic examples unrelated to private cases.

A. In a study most established couples were friends first. A reader estimates the same proportion of friendships will become romantic. Response: reversed conditional probability; successful romances are selected denominator. Need prospective friendship cohort with transition outcomes and mutual preferences. No individual probability. Four criteria addressed.
B. A dating participant's initial high rating predicts later messages but no partnership is recorded. Response: attraction, pursuit and mutual formation differ; opportunity and other person's preferences are alternatives. Follow actual mutual choices; cannot infer partnership. Four criteria addressed.
C. Two people remain cohabiting, one reports high satisfaction and another substantial costs of moving out. Response: persistence, satisfaction, dedication and barriers differ; both relational desire and constraints may contribute. Voluntarily expressed future preferences and feasible alternatives clarify, not prove motives. No recommendation to increase dependence. Four criteria addressed.
D. A survey finds perceived maintenance and love scores correlated, but duration unassociated. Response: behavior/perception, quality and persistence differ; reverse causation or shared reporting may explain correlation. Compare repeated reports from both actors and actual outcomes. No causal maintenance tactic inferred. Four criteria addressed.
E. A model predicts high quality scores a year later but performs poorly for changes. Response: stable baseline can support level prediction without predicting slope; time-varying exposures and measurement error compete. Compare persistence baseline and held-out change forecasts. No confident individual trajectory. Four criteria addressed.
F. One group predicts fewer breakups, but observed follow-up differences are nonsignificant. Response: forecast perceptions and actual event differ; sample selection, precision and missing outcomes may matter. Compare calibrated predictions with observed outcomes and uncertainty. No equivalence or causal claim. Four criteria addressed.

Internal review: 24/24 criteria addressed in six written responses; no critical error identified in this limited self-review. This is not independent assessment, prospective forecast validation or model retraining.
