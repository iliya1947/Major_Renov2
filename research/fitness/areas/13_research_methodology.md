## 32. Evidence hierarchy by question

Do not use one universal ladder.

1. Clinical safety → guidelines, surveillance, appropriate clinical evidence.
2. Long-term hypertrophy → longitudinal controlled training studies + syntheses.
3. Mechanism → mechanistic/lab evidence.
4. Exercise experience/adherence barriers → qualitative/behavioral evidence can be highly relevant.
5. Measurement validity → validation studies.
6. Rare harms → observational/registry/case evidence may be necessary.
7. Practical implementation → combine evidence with feasibility and individual data.

## Результат аудита 2026-11-16

Исходная версия contained core study types, bias-relevant study features, descriptive statistics, effect size, heterogeneity, responder variability and external validity. The audit added the missing methodological framework:

1. PICOT and question-type matching;
2. randomization/allocation/blinding;
3. formal bias domains;
4. confounding/causal inference;
5. power and multiplicity;
6. missing-data handling;
7. detailed validity/reliability;
8. exercise-specific measurement issues;
9. acute proxy vs chronic outcome logic;
10. systematic-review/meta-analysis methods;
11. heterogeneity and publication bias;
12. GRADE-style certainty;
13. guideline appraisal;
14. network meta-analysis caveats;
15. individual-response methodology;
16. evidence hierarchy by question;
17. evidence-to-decision framework;
18. explicit updating/version-control logic.

NSCA's current CSCS Detailed Content Outline explicitly includes scientific research process, basic statistics, PICOT, reliability/validity and practical application. Cochrane's current Handbook requires risk-of-bias assessment, appropriate synthesis, heterogeneity analysis and consideration of missing evidence; GRADE separates certainty into domains such as risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision and publication bias.
