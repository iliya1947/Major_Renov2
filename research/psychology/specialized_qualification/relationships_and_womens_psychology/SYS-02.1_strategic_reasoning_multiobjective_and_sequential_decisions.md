## 3. Construct map
Separate fundamental objective, intermediate objective, indicator, decision, feasible alternatives, uncertain world state, consequence, cost, value judgment and decision rule. A proximal gain does not establish attainment of an ultimate goal. Multiple goals need not be reduced to a single score; Pareto dominance, hard constraints and explicit tradeoffs can be more informative. Value judgments belong to the decision-maker. Safety, rights and others' autonomous choices are constraints, not disposable items in an optimization. Alternatives depending on another person's voluntary action are conditional outcomes, not actions the decision-maker can guarantee.

Risk with defensible probabilities differs from ambiguity about probabilities, model structure or outcomes. Expected utility is a normative comparison conditional on specified probabilities and utility function; it does not justify invented probabilities or arbitrary weights. Where uncertainty is deep, compare plausible scenarios and vulnerabilities without treating scenario counts as frequencies. Decision quality concerns available evidence, goals and alternatives at decision time; outcomes provide feedback but do not alone determine quality. Distinguish descriptive biases from an analyst's normative judgment.

Opportunity cost is value forgone by the feasible alternative displaced. Sunk costs are unrecoverable past expenditures that cannot be changed by the current choice. Past investment may affect future opportunities and continuing costs; separate that state dependence from escalation based merely on previous expenditure. The value of delay includes possible information and retained flexibility, offset by time, opportunity loss, continued costs and uncertainty. Reversibility varies by actual costs, feasibility and lasting consequences; legal or ethical freedom to change one's decision is not equivalent to undoing every consequence. In sequential decisions, evaluate current action, resulting state, available next actions and contingent policy. Commitment, provisional choice, wait, and stopping are alternatives where genuinely feasible, not universally ranked. A plan depends on actual implementation and subsequent freely made choices.

## 5. Operational procedure
1. Define the actual decision and horizon, distinguish ultimate outcomes from intermediate signals, and elicit relevant objectives and legitimate constraints without substituting the analyst's goals.
2. Generate feasible alternatives, including status quo, delayed choice and revised plans where relevant. Do not treat another person's assent or future change of preference as a controllable action.
3. Compare consequences by objective and time; identify tradeoffs, opportunity costs, side effects, irreversibility, conditional dependencies and material uncertainty. Separate previous unrecoverable cost from future costs/benefits.
4. Remove infeasible or dominated options where justified. Use qualitative comparison unless probabilities, scales and preference weights are defensible. Check whether rankings change with plausible assumptions.
5. Evaluate information value, costs of waiting, potential future choices and revision conditions. A plan is not superior merely because it has more steps or is more flexible. Preserve alternatives without delaying necessary protective action.
6. Assess decisions against information available ex ante, while updating subsequent plans using outcomes and contrary evidence. Recognize that more alternatives may either help or burden choice depending on context.
7. Respect autonomy, privacy and safety. No covert tests, strategic pressure, forced commitment, or exploiting inferred vulnerabilities. If another person's choice is unknown, keep it uncertain; distinguish decisions about one's own conduct from attempts to control others.

## 6. Internal synthetic transfer review
Rubric: C1 exact construct/decision distinction; C2 plausible alternative or competing account; C3 relevant comparison/criterion; C4 inference boundary. Critical errors: invented personal probabilities/weights, outcome bias, coercion, conflating intermediate and final goals, ignoring alternative costs, unsupported causal inference or privacy breach. Six authored neutral examples, not independent or held-out.

A. A design team can optimize reliability or flexibility; neither feasible design dominates the other. C1 competing objectives; C2 different weights/hard constraints; C3 compare consequences and sensitivity as in the worked exercise; C4 no universal best design from arbitrary analyst weights. 4/4.
B. An organization has already paid for a tool, but a substitute would be better from today onward. C1 sunk expenditure versus future consequences; C2 continued use may be justified by compatibility or switching costs; C3 compare remaining benefits/costs and feasible alternative; C4 no automatic persistence or cancellation recommendation based on past spending. 4/4.
C. A team can wait for a test result, but delay consumes resources. C1 information value versus total value of waiting; C2 act now or choose reversible interim action; C3 determine whether result changes feasible choices enough to offset waiting costs; C4 no assumed probability or automatic value of waiting. 4/4.
D. A reasonable decision under known conditions has an unfavorable chance outcome. C1 decision quality versus outcome; C2 bad luck versus flawed assumptions; C3 inspect prior evidence and use outcome for later updating; C4 no automatic hindsight verdict. 4/4.
E. A task offers undo; performance improves but postdecision satisfaction may not. C1 accuracy/performance versus satisfaction; C2 option value versus continued deliberation costs; C3 compare actual reversal cost and both endpoints; C4 no general superiority of reversibility or removal of autonomy. 4/4.
F. A joint voluntary project requires two participants' agreement. C1 own decision versus another person's independent choice; C2 alternative project or conditional collaboration; C3 evaluate actual consent, feasible options and one's own priorities; C4 no optimized method to force agreement or reinterpret refusal. 4/4.
