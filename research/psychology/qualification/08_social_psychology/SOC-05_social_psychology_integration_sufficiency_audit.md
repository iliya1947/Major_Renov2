## 1. Purpose and inputs

This block integrates and audits:

1. **SOC-01** — person perception, attribution, mentalizing, social inference, first impressions, context, culture, and measurement;
2. **SOC-02** — empathy-related constructs, thought–feeling accuracy, compassion, contagion, self–other distinction, motivation, and measurement;
3. **SOC-03** — social categorization, stereotypes, prejudice, indirect/explicit attitudes, individuation, discrimination, and intervention measurement;
4. **SOC-04** — social identity, norms, conformity, influence, contact, cooperation/competition, and group-process measurement.

The purpose is not to claim that social psychology is complete.

The purpose is to determine whether the current foundation is sufficient to prevent the main social-inference errors that would otherwise contaminate person-level psychological reasoning.

## 2. Integrated social-reasoning scaffold

A generic social inference can be organized as:

\`observable cues/behavior -> situational and role context -> category/identity/norm context -> candidate mental-state/goal/trait inferences -> interpersonal/group influence -> observable judgment/decision/behavior -> feedback and updating\`

This is a reasoning scaffold, not a universal causal sequence.

Different social processes can enter at different points:
- mentalizing can precede explicit attribution;
- category priors can bias cue interpretation;
- norms can change overt behavior without private belief;
- interaction can change the target being observed;
- contact can update category-level beliefs;
- identity can change which norms are treated as relevant.

## 3. Observation remains distinct from explanation

Across SOC-01…04, the most important invariant is:

**what is observed is not the same thing as why it happened.**

Observed:
- words;
- facial/vocal behavior;
- choices;
- group membership;
- public agreement;
- helping;
- avoidance;
- cooperation.

Inferred:
- belief;
- intention;
- trait;
- prejudice;
- empathy;
- conformity motive;
- private norm endorsement.

Each inference requires alternatives and criterion evidence.

## 4. Behavior is multiply determined

Behavior can reflect:
- stable dispositions;
- current goals;
- social roles;
- incentives;
- norms;
- group identity;
- fear of sanction;
- informational learning;
- habit;
- impression management;
- resource constraints;
- strategic interaction.

Therefore no single behavior supports a high-confidence personality or motive inference by itself.

## 5. State, goal, trait, role, and category explanations are distinct

A behavior may be explained by:
- current state;
- local goal;
- stable trait;
- assigned role;
- category-linked expectation;
- group norm.

These levels can coexist.

The correct task is not to select a "person" or "situation" explanation prematurely, but to compare viable explanations at each level.

## 6. Person perception is probabilistic

SOC-01 established that:
- first impressions are structured;
- some cues contain modest information;
- context strongly affects judgment;
- observer agreement is not equivalent to truth.

A social judgment should therefore be expressed as:
**probabilistic evidence under specific cue/context conditions**.

Not:
**direct readout of character or internal state**.

## 7. Consensus is not accuracy

This boundary recurs across:
- facial impressions;
- trait ratings;
- stereotype content;
- majority opinion;
- norm perception.

High agreement can reflect:
- valid common evidence;
- shared stereotype;
- common task demands;
- cultural convention;
- common misinformation.

Accuracy requires an independent criterion.

## 8. Attribution biases are conditional, not universal laws

SOC-01 retained:
- correspondence/dispositional inference as a meaningful phenomenon;
while rejecting:
- universal "people always ignore situations";
- actor–observer asymmetry as a pervasive general law.

The 173-study actor–observer meta-analysis remains a key calibration example: the average effect was approximately zero overall and depended on conditions.

Use bias labels only after demonstrating the relevant comparison.

## 9. Mentalizing is not one global ability

SOC-01 showed major heterogeneity in:
- ToM tasks;
- language demands;
- executive/memory demands;
- reliability;
- ceiling effects;
- ecological validity.

Current 2026 psychometric meta-analysis further reinforces that social-cognition tasks have population-specific validation profiles and heterogeneous ecological validity.

Therefore:
**one mentalizing task ≠ global ability to understand people**.

## 10. Empathy is an umbrella, not a unitary trait

SOC-02 separated:
- perspective taking;
- accurate inference;
- vicarious affect;
- emotional contagion;
- compassion/sympathy;
- personal distress;
- mimicry;
- synchrony.

The 2026 common-lexicon paper makes this distinction explicit.

Any empirical use of "empathy" should identify the lower-order target.

## 11. Accuracy and care are different dimensions

Thought–feeling accuracy concerns whether a perceiver correctly infers another person's mental content.

Compassion concerns other-oriented concern.

Helping concerns behavior.

These can dissociate.

Therefore:
- accurate inference does not prove kindness;
- concern does not prove accuracy;
- helping does not prove empathic concern.

## 12. Self-report and performance are not substitutes

Across ToM, empathy, prejudice, norms, and social identity:

Self-report can validly measure:
- beliefs;
- attitudes;
- perceived tendencies;
- perceived identification;
- perceived norms.

Performance/behavioral measures can assess:
- task accuracy;
- reaction-time association;
- cooperation;
- discrimination;
- conformity.

A weak correlation between methods can reflect different constructs rather than failure of one method.

## 13. Social categorization is not prejudice

SOC-03 established the chain:

**category representation ≠ stereotype ≠ prejudice ≠ indirect association ≠ discrimination**.

A category can be cognitively salient without negative evaluation.

A stereotype can be known without endorsement.

A prejudiced attitude can fail to produce discrimination.

Discrimination can also be produced by rules/institutions without strong individual prejudice.

## 14. Group-level information is not person-level truth

A group-level average or stereotype cannot establish an individual's attribute because:
- within-group variance may be large;
- distributions overlap;
- the criterion may not transport;
- the person may be atypical;
- direct individuating evidence may be stronger.

Canonical rule:

**group information can modify a prior; it does not determine an individual posterior.**

## 15. Implicit measures do not reveal a hidden true self

SOC-03 integrated:
- moderate IAT test–retest reliability;
- small predictive associations;
- differences among indirect measures;
- distinct implicit and explicit behavioral associations.

The 2025 meta-analysis of 1,108 studies found both implicit and explicit attitudes had unique behavioral associations, with larger explicit effects on average.

Therefore:
**implicit ≠ more authentic**.

## 16. A bias measure is not an individual diagnosis

The 2026 systematic review of prejudice/stereotype instruments included 263 studies and found:
- only ~4% of instruments reached high-quality evidence;
- predictive validity and temporal stability were often poorly tested;
- conceptual definitions were frequently weak.

This is incompatible with high-confidence individual moral labeling from one score.

## 17. Individuation and categorization coexist

SOC-03 established that person-specific evidence can modify category-based judgment, but:
- category effects can persist;
- explicit and indirect judgments may weight information differently;
- weak individuating information need not override a strong prior.

The practical rule is:
**compare diagnosticity, reliability, and relevance of category versus person-specific evidence.**

## 18. Social identity is not conformity

SOC-04 separated:
- membership;
- identification;
- salience;
- identity content.

High identification can increase:
- conformity to a norm;
- dissent in defense of an alternative group prototype;
- norm change.

Therefore:
**identity strength has no invariant behavioral direction without norm content.**

## 19. Norm content and referent are necessary

A social norm must specify:
- descriptive versus injunctive;
- perceived versus actual;
- relevant referent group.

The 2026 meta-analysis of 99 studies (N=80,722) found descriptive and injunctive norms correlated only moderately and related differently to attitudes/intentions versus behavior.

A generic "norm effect" without norm type/referent is underspecified.

## 20. Public compliance is not private acceptance

Conformity can reflect:
- approval seeking;
- sanction avoidance;
- informational learning;
- identity-based alignment;
- genuine private belief change.

A public response alone cannot distinguish these.

Need:
- private response;
- delayed response;
- persistence after group removal;
where internalization is claimed.

## 21. Majority consensus is not objective truth

The 2025 review of aggregated opinions highlights that group opinion can be useful evidence, but its value depends on:
- source independence;
- information quality;
- aggregation process;
- causal history of the opinions.

Online counts can be particularly misleading when algorithmic amplification or copied information violates independence.

## 22. Social influence is not irrationality by definition

Normative and informational influence can be adaptive when:
- others know more;
- norms coordinate behavior;
- social acceptance has real consequences.

Influence becomes epistemically problematic when:
- sources are dependent;
- norms are harmful;
- conformity substitutes for evidence;
- perceived consensus is manipulated.

The mechanism and decision environment matter.

## 23. Cooperation is not altruism

Cooperation can reflect:
- altruistic concern;
- reciprocity;
- reputation;
- group norm;
- identity;
- strategic self-interest;
- punishment avoidance.

A cooperative choice in a game does not identify one moral motive.

## 24. Competition is not hostility

Competition concerns incompatible payoffs or comparative incentives.

The 2026 meta-analysis of experimental social dilemmas showed that intergroup competition can increase intragroup cooperation.

Thus:
**competition can strengthen internal cooperation without implying generalized prosociality or hatred**.

## 25. Ingroup prosociality and intergroup prosociality differ

A group can become:
- more cooperative internally;
while
- more competitive externally.

Therefore outcome level must be specified:
- within-group cooperation;
- between-group cooperation;
- outgroup helping;
- aggregate welfare.

Do not generalize one to another.

## 26. Intergroup contact is valence- and selection-dependent

SOC-04 established:
- positive contact relates to lower prejudice on average;
- negative contact relates to higher prejudice;
- negative contact has stronger effects in the 2024 meta-analysis;
- contact frequency is not contact quality.

Selection into contact matters.

People with favorable attitudes may seek contact; people with negative attitudes may avoid it.

## 27. Observational longitudinal contact is not automatically causal

The 2026 re-analysis of:
- 31 datasets;
- 21 studies;
- N=33,348

found traditional longitudinal models produced contact effects, whereas models with stronger control for stable confounding did not show comparable within-person evidence.

This does not invalidate experimental contact effects.

It establishes:
**causal conclusions depend on design and model.**

## 28. Social processes are dynamic and reciprocal

Examples:
- identification can increase norm adherence;
- norm adherence can reinforce identification;
- contact can alter stereotypes/norms;
- expectations can alter target behavior;
- public conformity can make a norm appear more consensual;
- behavior can reinforce stereotypes.

Linear one-direction explanations are often incomplete.

## 29. Social context can create self-fulfilling evidence

If an observer expects:
- hostility;
- incompetence;
- rejection;

they can behave in ways that change the target's response.

Later behavior may then appear to confirm the initial belief.

Distinguish:
- prediction accuracy;
- elicitation;
- selective attention;
- causal interaction.

## 30. Motivation is a cross-domain moderator

MOT interfaces with SOC because people vary in motivation to:
- mentalize;
- empathize;
- individuate;
- conform;
- resist influence;
- seek intergroup contact;
- cooperate.

An observed failure can reflect:
- inability;
- low motivation;
- high cost;
- competing goal.

Ability and deployment must be separated.

## 31. Emotion and stress are cross-domain moderators

AFF and STR interface with SOC because:
- affect changes social attention;
- threat changes categorization and vigilance;
- stress can alter social approach/avoidance;
- regulation changes empathic distress;
- social support changes stress appraisal.

But:
**social behavior should not be relabeled as emotion/stress without direct evidence**.

## 32. Learning is a cross-domain mechanism

The 2025 learning-and-memory account of impression formation emphasizes:
- episodic;
- semantic;
- Pavlovian;
- instrumental learning systems

in person impression formation and updating.

This creates a LEA–SOC bridge.

However:
**a learning framework does not collapse social representation into one associative mechanism**.

## 33. Culture is a construct-validity issue, not a nuisance covariate

SOC-01 and SOC-03 showed:
- social-cognition tasks;
- stereotype/prejudice measures

often have weak cross-cultural validation.

The 2025 systematic review of social-cognition tests in non-Western/minoritized populations found variable psychometric quality and called for culturally sensitive validation.

Thus:
**translation alone ≠ construct equivalence**.

## 34. Measurement invariance is required for group comparison

Cross-group score comparisons require evidence that:
- items/tasks function similarly;
- latent structure is comparable;
- scoring has comparable meaning.

Without invariance:
**score difference ≠ construct difference**.

This applies to:
- empathy;
- social cognition;
- prejudice;
- identity;
- norms.

## 35. Ecological validity is construct-specific

A task can be:
- psychometrically reliable but artificial;
- naturalistic but poorly standardized;
- behaviorally realistic but weakly criterion-validated.

"Realistic" is not itself a validity coefficient.

The relevant question:
**Does the task predict or represent the intended real-world social process?**

## 36. Social classifiers and AI require construct validation

Algorithms can classify:
- emotion labels;
- personality impressions;
- prejudice-related text;
- social behavior.

High predictive accuracy does not establish:
- latent psychological state;
- motive;
- fairness;
- individual certainty.

Dataset artifacts and demographic/context proxies remain viable alternatives.

## 37. Clinical social-cognition findings do not transfer automatically

A task may discriminate:
- schizophrenia;
- autism;
- neurological conditions

at a group level.

This does not imply:
- diagnostic validity for an individual;
- general social-cognitive deficit;
- transport to another population.

The 2026 social-cognition psychometric meta-analysis explicitly shows population-specific validation.

Clinical inference belongs to CLN/NDEV.

## 38. Trait inference is the next unresolved dependency

SOC can establish:
- social-context effects;
- situational constraints;
- measurement limits.

It does not yet provide a full framework for when repeated behavior justifies:
- stable trait inference;
- cross-situational personality claims;
- individual-difference interpretation.

That is the next core dependency: **PER**.

## 40. Red-team inference cases

**Case A — person interrupts repeatedly.**
Possible explanations: dominance trait, urgency, role authority, anxiety, conversational norm, poor turn-taking skill. One behavior does not establish personality.

**Case B — observer accurately identifies someone's distress.**
This establishes accuracy for that target/state under the criterion; it does not establish compassion or helping motivation.

**Case C — high empathy questionnaire score.**
May index self-perceived concern, perspective-taking tendency, contagion, or mixed content. It does not prove interpersonal accuracy.

**Case D — high IAT score.**
Supports an indirectly measured association under that task. It does not establish hidden moral belief or future discriminatory act.

**Case E — employee publicly agrees with group.**
Could reflect private belief, normative compliance, informational reliance, role pressure, or strategic behavior.

**Case F — highly identified member rejects group policy.**
Could reflect dissent motivated by perceived core identity values rather than weak identification.

**Case G — team cooperates strongly during competition.**
Shows intragroup cooperation; it does not establish generalized altruism toward outsiders.

**Case H — frequent positive contact correlates with low prejudice.**
Could reflect contact effect, self-selection, or both. Stronger causal design is needed.

**Case I — many observers agree on a trait impression.**
Shows consensus; independent criterion is required for accuracy.

## 43. Evidence confidence map

### High-confidence practical boundaries
- social cognition is probabilistic inference, not direct mind reading;
- behavior is multiply determined;
- mentalizing/empathy measures are construct-specific;
- empathy-related accuracy, concern, contagion, and helping are distinct;
- categorization, prejudice, association, and discrimination are distinct;
- indirect measures are not individual moral diagnostics;
- group membership, identity, norm, and conformity are distinct;
- descriptive/injunctive and perceived/actual norms differ;
- public compliance is not private acceptance;
- cooperation is not altruism;
- observational contact effects require causal caution;
- cultural/psychometric validation is necessary.

### Supported but context-sensitive
- first impressions can contain modest valid information;
- perspective taking can support understanding;
- individuating information can reduce category effects;
- identification can increase norm sensitivity;
- norms can powerfully shape behavior;
- shared identity can facilitate cooperation;
- positive contact can reduce prejudice;
- negative contact can have stronger adverse effects;
- social processes can be reciprocal/self-fulfilling.

### Not established as universal
- one social-cognition ability;
- one empathy factor;
- one universal attribution bias;
- one hidden implicit attitude;
- one stereotype taxonomy;
- one conformity rate;
- one identity effect direction;
- one cooperation motive;
- one contact-effect magnitude across contexts;
- one culture-neutral social-cognition measure.

## Canonical operating rule

**Observe the social evidence -> identify situation, role, category, identity, norm, incentives, and interaction context -> specify the exact inferred state/goal/trait/social process -> generate competing explanations -> require an explicit accuracy/behavior criterion -> separate self-report, indirect, performance, and behavioral measures -> test cultural/psychometric validity -> preserve cross-level and causal boundaries -> state only the narrowest supported social inference.**

Never:
**behavior = character; consensus = truth; empathy = one score; category = prejudice; IAT = hidden self; identity = conformity; compliance = belief; cooperation = altruism; contact = causal attitude change; group effect = person mechanism.**
