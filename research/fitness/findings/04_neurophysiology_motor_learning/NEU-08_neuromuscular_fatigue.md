# NEU-08 — Neuromuscular fatigue

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-19**  
Область: 04 — Neurophysiology / Motor Learning

## 0. Core decision

Neuromuscular fatigue is a task-specific reduction in force/performance capacity produced by interacting central, peripheral and sensorimotor constraints.

Canonical model:

`descending voluntary drive`
↔
`afferent feedback + spinal/motoneuron output`
↔
`peripheral contractile state`
+
`coordination + sensory state + task mechanics`
→
`momentary force / velocity / power / steadiness / technique`.

Project rule:

**no single neural, EMG, VA or performance marker universally explains fatigue.**

## 1. Central fatigue

Central fatigue is an exercise-induced reduction in effective voluntary neural drive/activation capacity upstream of the neuromuscular junction.

Project rule:

**central fatigue ≠ mental fatigue ≠ subjective tiredness ≠ one VA/EMG value.**

## 2. Peripheral fatigue

Peripheral fatigue reflects impairment distal to central voluntary drive.

Detailed mechanisms remain PHY-10:

- membrane/T-tubule excitability;
- E-C coupling;
- Ca²⁺ handling;
- ionic/metabolic state;
- cross-bridge/contractile force.

Project rule:

**large performance loss can occur without large measurable central-activation loss.**

## 3. Voluntary activation

VA can decrease with fatigue, but not universally.

Project rule:

**VA is method-/muscle-/protocol-dependent and is not a literal percent of total CNS capacity.**

Unchanged VA does not exclude every central-fatigue process.

## 4. Motor-unit firing

Fatigue does not force all motor-unit firing rates in one direction.

Possible simultaneous responses include:

- reduced firing in some lower-threshold units;
- increased firing in some higher-threshold units;
- increased variability;
- threshold shifts.

Project rule:

**motor-unit firing under fatigue is a population-control response, not a universal downward curve.**

## 5. Recruitment

During many submaximal fatiguing tasks, reduced force per active fiber increases recruitment/excitation demand.

Project rule:

**recruitment often rises during fatigue, but depends on initial force level, remaining motor-unit reserve, contraction mode and duration.**

## 6. Coordination

Fatigue can alter:

- muscle activation;
- intermuscular coordination;
- corticomuscular coherence;
- agonist-antagonist strategy.

Project rule:

**there is no universal coordination/coherence signature of fatigue.**

## 7. Proprioception

Fatigue commonly worsens active joint-position accuracy in some lower-limb tasks.

Project rule:

**fatigue-related proprioceptive impairment is joint-, task-, submodality- and measurement-specific.**

Do not infer global sensory failure from one position-sense or balance outcome.

## 8. Technique / movement strategy

Fatigue commonly changes:

- bar velocity;
- power;
- concentric duration;
- joint kinematics/kinetics;
- movement variability.

Project rule:

**fatigue-related biomechanical change ≠ automatically technical failure ≠ established injury mechanism.**

Exact technical-failure criteria remain TEC.

## 9. Task dependency

Fatigue expression depends on:

- intensity;
- duration;
- contraction mode;
- muscle group/mass;
- exercise complexity;
- duty cycle/rest;
- blood flow;
- proximity to failure;
- training status;
- measurement method;
- measurement time point.

Project rule:

**interpret fatigue only in the context of a defined task and measurement.**

## 10. Recovery

Different outcomes recover on different time courses:

- voluntary force;
- velocity;
- RFD;
- VA;
- twitch/contractile function;
- proprioception;
- coordination;
- soreness;
- perceived fatigue.

Project rule:

**there is no single neuromuscular recovery clock.**

Normalization of one marker does not prove full recovery.

## 11. One-marker error

Rejected inferences:

- normal VA = fully recovered;
- normal EMG = no neural fatigue;
- reduced CMJ = central fatigue;
- normal CMJ = every subsystem recovered;
- soreness = readiness;
- velocity loss = one identified mechanism.

Project rule:

**performance tests answer performance-state questions; mechanistic tests answer only the mechanisms they validly probe.**

## 12. Canonical project rules

1. Neuromuscular fatigue is multidimensional and task specific.
2. Central and peripheral fatigue can coexist.
3. Central fatigue is not mental fatigue.
4. Peripheral/contractile impairment can dominate despite relatively preserved VA.
5. VA is not a direct CNS-capacity percentage.
6. Surface EMG is not a direct neural-drive/fatigue score.
7. Motor-unit firing changes are non-uniform.
8. Recruitment compensation depends on task and remaining reserve.
9. Coordination changes are heterogeneous.
10. Proprioceptive impairment is submodality specific.
11. Fatigue changes movement strategy without one universal technique-failure pattern.
12. Recovery occurs on multiple outcome-specific clocks.
13. One normalized marker does not prove full recovery.

## 13. Training-relevant implications

1. **Do not diagnose central fatigue from surface EMG alone.**
2. **Do not assume central fatigue is the main limiter near failure.**
3. **Do not interpret VA as a literal percent of CNS use.**
4. **Do not assume all firing rates fall under fatigue.**
5. **Expect recruitment behavior to depend on force level and remaining reserve.**
6. **Treat coordination changes as task specific.**
7. **Expect some active position-sense error to worsen under fatigue without assuming global proprioceptive loss.**
8. **Define technical failure separately from any kinematic change.**
9. **Near-failure/high-volume/high-density work generally increases acute fatigue burden.**
10. **Define recovery against the function needed in the next session.**

## 14. What NEU-08 does not prescribe

Not owned here:

- exact RIR/failure prescription;
- exact velocity-loss threshold;
- exact rest interval;
- exact recovery days;
- readiness cutoff;
- technical-failure threshold;
- individual central/peripheral fatigue profile.

These remain TST, TEC, PRO/GPP, REC and IND.

## 15. Confidence

### HIGH
- central/peripheral coexistence;
- central fatigue ≠ mental fatigue;
- VA measurement dependence;
- non-uniform motor-unit behavior;
- task dependency;
- outcome-specific recovery;
- fatigue-related movement change.

### MODERATE-HIGH
- active position-sense error worsening after fatigue;
- increased acute fatigue closer to failure/higher volume/density;
- coordination changes under fatigue;
- preserved central activation despite substantial peripheral impairment in some protocols.

### MODERATE / CONTEXT-DEPENDENT
- exact central/peripheral share;
- exact motor-unit strategy in dynamic multijoint lifting;
- exact interpretation of one coherence change;
- exact sensory mechanism;
- exact neural vs contractile recovery time.

### LOW / NOT ESTABLISHED AS UNIVERSAL
- one central-fatigue biomarker;
- one firing-rate direction;
- one coordination signature;
- one universal fatigued-technique pattern;
- direct causal injury inference from fatigue-related kinematic change;
- one universal recovery duration.
