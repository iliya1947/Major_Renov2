# 3. Defining feature: integration

The strongest cross-source conclusion is:

> **Mixed methods is not defined merely by collecting qualitative and quantitative data. It is defined by intentional interrelation/integration of the qualitative and quantitative components to answer a shared or linked research problem.**

Current PCORI 2026 explicitly requires researchers to specify:

- why mixed methods are appropriate;
- what data are integrated;
- when integration occurs;
- how integration occurs;
- how divergent results influence interpretation.

MMAT similarly treats effective integration and interpretation of integrated outputs as core mixed-methods quality criteria.

### Canonical project definition

**Mixed-methods research = deliberate quantitative + qualitative inquiry in which the components are methodologically linked and integrated to produce an inference/understanding that depends on their combination.**

---

# 4. Qualitative + quantitative coexistence is insufficient

Examples that are **not automatically mixed methods**:

- survey + interviews reported in separate papers with no integration;
- RCT + unrelated satisfaction interviews;
- quantitative outcomes followed by qualitative quotations that merely decorate the Discussion;
- interview study plus descriptive demographics;
- survey with one open-ended comment box if the qualitative material is not systematically analyzed/integrated;
- qualitative and quantitative analyses that remain independent and answer unrelated questions.

### Critical rule

`QUAN present + QUAL present`

does not equal:

`MIXED METHODS`.

The missing component may be:

**integration**.

---

# 5. Mixed methods vs multimethod

Terminology varies across fields, but a practical distinction is stable.

## Mixed methods

Usually means:

- quantitative + qualitative approaches;
- intentionally integrated.

## Multimethod / multiple-method research

Can involve:

- multiple qualitative methods;
- multiple quantitative methods;
- qualitative + quantitative components that remain largely independent;
- several methods under one methodology.

### Project rule

Store two separate flags:

1. **multiple methods used?**
2. **qual–quant integration sufficient for mixed-methods classification?**

### Critical rule

Multiple data sources do not establish mixed methods.

---

# 6. Rationale for mixing must be explicit

Mixed methods should solve a problem that one strand alone cannot solve as well.

Common purposes include:

1. **triangulation / confirmation**;
2. **complementarity**;
3. **explanation**;
4. **development/building**;
5. **expansion**;
6. **initiation** through paradox/discordance;
7. instrument/model development;
8. contextualization;
9. implementation/process interpretation;
10. intervention development/adaptation.

### Critical rule

“Mixed methods provides a more complete picture” is acceptable only if the study shows **what specific gap between strands is being solved**.

---

# 7. Parent quantitative and qualitative identities must be retained

A mixed-methods study is not one methodological blob.

Store separately:

## Quantitative strand

- parent design;
- sample;
- measurement;
- outcome/estimand;
- analysis;
- inferential limits.

## Qualitative strand

- parent methodology;
- sample/adequacy;
- data generation;
- analysis;
- reflexivity;
- inferential limits.

## Mixed-methods layer

- rationale;
- timing;
- priority;
- dependence;
- integration points;
- integration method;
- meta-inference.

### Critical rule

The quality of mixing cannot repair a weak component.

---

# 8. Mixed-methods architecture is multi-dimensional

A mixed-methods label should be decomposed along at least these axes:

1. purpose of mixing;
2. sequence/timing;
3. relative priority;
4. dependency between strands;
5. sampling relationship;
6. integration point(s);
7. integration technique(s);
8. final meta-inference.

### Critical rule

A label such as:

> “sequential mixed methods”

is under-specified.

---

# 9. Timing

## Concurrent / parallel

QUAL and QUAN occur at roughly the same stage.

Typical:

- convergent designs.

## Sequential

One strand precedes and informs the next.

Typical:

- explanatory sequential;
- exploratory sequential.

## Iterative / multistage

Multiple cycles or phases occur across a program.

### Critical rule

Timing alone does not define the design.

A QUAN→QUAL sequence is not explanatory sequential unless the qualitative phase is **linked to/explains** the quantitative results.

---

# 10. Priority / weighting

Studies may give:

- equal priority;
- quantitative priority;
- qualitative priority.

Priority can be inferred from:

- research aims;
- resource allocation;
- phase dominance;
- publication emphasis;
- integration logic.

### Project rule

Store author-declared priority where available.

If not declared:

- do not infer confidently from sample size alone.

---

# 11. Dependence / interaction between strands

Strands can be:

## Independent until merging

Common in convergent designs.

## Dependent

Earlier results shape:

- sampling;
- questions;
- instruments;
- later analysis.

Common in sequential designs.

## Embedded

One strand is nested within a dominant parent design/program.

### Critical rule

Dependency is a stronger classifier than chronology alone.

---

# 12. Core architecture 1 — Convergent design

Canonical structure:

`QUAL || QUAN → merge/integrate → meta-inference`

Usually:

- concurrent/parallel collection;
- separate initial analyses;
- integration at analysis/interpretation.

Scientific purpose often includes:

- compare;
- complement;
- expand;
- reconcile different perspectives.

### Critical rule

Convergent does not mean:

> results must agree.

Discordance can be a legitimate integrated finding.

---

# 13. Convergent design — same phenomenon requirement

The strands need not measure identical constructs.

But they should address sufficiently related aspects of one overarching research problem that integration is meaningful.

### Weak design

Quantitative:

- strength gains.

Qualitative:

- unrelated views about nutrition policy.

No explicit linkage.

### Stronger design

Quantitative:

- adherence rates / performance outcomes.

Qualitative:

- participant experience explaining contextual variation in adherence/performance.

---

# 14. Convergent design — current direct exercise example

The 2026 community-based high-intensity multimodal training study explicitly used:

- a two-arm parallel RCT;
- mixed-method convergent evaluation;
- quantitative outcomes;
- qualitative/perceptual data.

### Project lesson

Store separately:

`quantitative parent = parallel RCT`

and:

`mixed-method integration architecture = convergent`.

The word “convergent” does not replace the RCT design.

---

# 15. Core architecture 2 — Explanatory sequential

Canonical structure:

`QUAN → QUAL → integrated explanation`

Purpose:

- explain;
- contextualize;
- elaborate;
- investigate unexpected/heterogeneous quantitative results.

### Constitutive link

The quantitative phase should inform at least one of:

- qualitative sampling;
- interview prompts;
- cases selected;
- phenomena explored;
- final explanation.

### Critical rule

Survey first + interviews second is not enough.

The later QUAL phase must be analytically dependent on the QUAN findings.

---

# 16. Explanatory sequential — connecting

A common integration mechanism is **connecting**.

Example:

QUAN results identify:

- high adherence;
- low adherence;
- unexpected responders.

These categories guide purposive sampling for QUAL interviews.

### Project fields

- what quantitative result triggered QUAL sampling?;
- what sampling rule was used?;
- was maximum variation / extreme-case / discrepant-case sampling used?;
- are qualitative participants linked to their quantitative records?

---

# 17. Explanatory sequential — building

The earlier quantitative findings may also build:

- interview guide;
- probes;
- case-selection logic.

### Critical rule

The qualitative phase should not simply repeat the same questions in words.

Its role is explanatory/interpretive.

---

# 18. Direct rehabilitation example — stroke adherence

A 2026 home-based stroke exercise rehabilitation study used:

- QUAN survey: n=289;
- purposive/max-variation QUAL subsample: n=16;
- interviews specifically to explain/supplement quantitative findings.

This is a clear current explanatory sequential rehabilitation example.

### Claim boundary

The qualitative explanation can support:

- perceived drivers;
- contextual interpretation;
- hypothesis generation.

It does not prove a quantitative mediation mechanism solely through interview concordance.

---

# 19. Direct PA education example — rehabilitation students

Current 2026 research on Chinese rehabilitation students used:

- quantitative survey first;
- qualitative interviews second;
- interview design focused on contradictions/complex patterns from quantitative results.

### Taxonomy implication

This demonstrates genuine strand dependence rather than simple sequential coexistence.

---

# 20. Core architecture 3 — Exploratory sequential

Canonical structure:

`QUAL → QUAN → integrated development/testing/generalization`

Purposes include:

- identify concepts;
- generate items;
- build instrument;
- develop taxonomy/model;
- construct intervention;
- define determinants;
- test/quantify qualitative findings.

### Constitutive link

QUAL results must materially build the later QUAN phase.

---

# 21. Exploratory sequential — building

Typical sequence:

QUAL exploration  
→ concepts/themes  
→ items/variables/intervention components  
→ quantitative assessment/testing.

### Critical rule

QUAL followed by an unrelated survey is not exploratory sequential mixed methods.

The quantitative phase should have a traceable origin in qualitative findings.

---

# 22. Direct rehabilitation example — MS web exercise

The 2026 multiple-sclerosis tele-exercise study used:

- focus groups to identify needs/preferences;
- those findings to inform development;
- subsequent pilot testing with quantitative feasibility/safety/mobility outcomes and further user feedback.

### Classification

`exploratory sequential mixed methods`

with:

- intervention-development/co-innovation overlay.

### Claim boundary

Pilot outcomes do not establish definitive effectiveness.

---

# 23. Direct PA example — adolescents with intellectual disability

A 2026 exploratory sequential study on PA determinants used:

- participatory/visual qualitative generation;
- later structured quantitative rating of identified determinants.

### Taxonomy lesson

QUAL findings can build the later quantification structure without converting the qualitative phase into “preliminary anecdote.”

---

# 24. Embedded / nested mixed methods

Embedded designs place one strand within a larger dominant design or process.

Examples:

- RCT + embedded qualitative process study;
- feasibility intervention + qualitative implementation interview;
- quantitative experiment + embedded qualitative experience component.

### Key issue

The embedded strand usually addresses a secondary/complementary question but is linked to the dominant design.

### Critical rule

`embedded qualitative component`

is not automatically:

`embedded mixed methods`.

Integration must still occur.

---

# 25. Embedded nomenclature is heterogeneous

Important synthesis finding:

- Fetters et al. 2013 describe **embedding** as a methods-level integration approach and intervention framework;
- other current health-methods literature treats **embedded design** as one of the main mixed-methods design structures.

### Project decision for E1

Do not force one universal nomenclature yet.

Store:

1. embedded/nested strand structure;
2. dominant parent design;
3. exact integration points.

A dedicated embedded/intervention pass is required.

# 27. Advanced / multistage frameworks

Fetters/Curry/Creswell identify advanced frameworks such as:

- multistage;
- intervention;
- case study;
- participatory.

These are often:

> basic integration designs embedded within a larger research program or framework.

### Project rule

Do not automatically create four new atomic designs.

Store:

- basic integration pattern;
- advanced parent framework.

---

# 28. Multistage / multiphase mixed methods

A multistage program can combine:

- exploratory sequential development;
- pilot intervention;
- convergent process evaluation;
- definitive trial;
- post-trial explanatory interviews.

### Critical rule

“Multiphase” should not erase the design of each phase.

### Future need

A dedicated later pass should determine:

- when multistage/multiphase becomes a distinct study architecture;
- program vs single-study boundary;
- integration across phases.

# 29. Intervention mixed-methods framework

Mixed methods can be linked to an intervention:

## Pre-intervention

- needs;
- development;
- adaptation;
- measure selection.

## During intervention

- implementation;
- fidelity;
- context;
- participant/provider response.

## Post-intervention

- explanation;
- sustainment;
- unexpected outcomes.

### Critical rule

The intervention’s causal effect is established by the quantitative causal design, not by the mixed-methods label.

---

# 30. Case-study mixed-methods framework

A bounded case may contain:

- quantitative measures;
- qualitative interviews/observations;
- integration within/cross cases.

### Important

Case Study remains a cross-paradigm bounded-case architecture.

### Canonical record

`Case Study parent`  
+ quantitative strand  
+ qualitative strand  
+ mixed integration architecture.

---

# 31. Participatory / transformative mixed-methods framework

Participatory/community governance can shape:

- question;
- sampling;
- method selection;
- interpretation;
- dissemination.

Quantitative and qualitative strands may be integrated under:

- PAR/CBPR;
- Indigenous governance;
- transformative/social-justice orientation.

### Critical rule

Participatory governance is not itself proof that the study is mixed methods.

Both qual–quant components and integration are still required.

---

# 32. Design-level integration

Integration can be planned through:

- sequential dependency;
- concurrent convergence;
- embedding;
- linked sampling;
- multistage program design.

### Strong design

Integration is visible in the research question and protocol.

### Weak design

Integration appears only after both strands are completed and authors search for similarities post hoc.

---

# 33. Methods-level integration — connecting

**Connecting**:

one strand determines or informs sampling for another.

Example:

QUAN adherence distribution  
→ select high/low adherence cases for QUAL interview.

### Key field

Store exact linking identifier where participants/cases are connected across strands.

---

# 34. Methods-level integration — building

**Building**:

results from one strand shape later:

- questions;
- measures;
- instrument;
- intervention components;
- data-collection procedures.

Common in:

- exploratory sequential;
- explanatory sequential.

---

# 35. Methods-level integration — merging

**Merging**:

QUAL and QUAN data/results are brought together for comparison/synthesis.

Common in:

- convergent design.

Techniques include:

- joint display;
- matrix;
- weaving narrative;
- data transformation;
- case-by-case comparison.

---

# 36. Methods-level integration — embedding

**Embedding**:

one form of data is nested within another design and linked at one or more points.

Examples:

- interviews nested in RCT;
- observation inside implementation trial;
- qualitative component inside cohort.

### Critical rule

Embedding must add an explicit linkage/integration function.

---

# 37. Integration at analysis and interpretation

Mixed-methods integration may occur through:

- merging datasets;
- merging results;
- building later analyses;
- data transformation;
- joint analysis;
- following a thread;
- cross-case integrated matrix;
- narrative weaving;
- joint display.

### Critical rule

“Discussed together in Discussion” is the weakest form and may not demonstrate adequate integration by itself.

---

# 38. Joint displays

A joint display is a visual integration structure that juxtaposes or links:

- quantitative results;
- qualitative findings;
- integrated interpretation/meta-inference.

It can be:

- table;
- matrix;
- figure;
- case-by-case display;
- statistics-by-theme display;
- explanatory sequential display;
- exploratory-development display.

### Critical rule

A table containing two result columns is not automatically a meaningful joint display.

The display should support a new integrated interpretation.

---

# 39. Joint displays are not mandatory

Integration can be rigorous without a formal joint display.

Alternative strategies include:

- narrative weaving;
- following a thread;
- data transformation;
- integrated case analysis;
- iterative cross-strand analysis.

### Project rule

Store joint display as one integration technique, not a validity requirement.

---

# 40. Data transformation

Possible forms:

## Quantitizing

Qualitative material transformed into numerical/categorical variables.

## Qualitizing

Quantitative patterns transformed/interpreted through qualitative representation.

### Critical rule

Transformation can enable integration but can also lose meaning/uncertainty.

Record:

- transformation rule;
- rationale;
- traceability.

---

# 41. Following a thread

One finding is traced across datasets.

Example:

QUAN:

- low adherence subgroup.

Then:

- QUAL interviews;
- observational/contextual data;
- further quantitative contrasts.

### Value

It creates a linked analytic question rather than parallel separate results.

---

# 42. Meta-inference

A **meta-inference** is the overall interpretation generated from the integrated strands.

It should answer:

> What do we know because the strands were combined that we would not know from either alone?

### Examples

- explanation of heterogeneity;
- confirmation with richer context;
- contradiction requiring revised theory;
- intervention-design decision;
- refined determinant model.

### Critical rule

A summary of separate QUAL and QUAN conclusions is not necessarily a meta-inference.

---

# 43. Fit of integration

Integrated findings may show:

- convergence/confirmation;
- complementarity;
- expansion;
- discordance/dissonance.

### Critical rule

Discordance is not automatically a study failure.

It can reveal:

- measurement mismatch;
- subgroup heterogeneity;
- temporal mismatch;
- construct difference;
- social desirability;
- model misspecification;
- unexpected contextual mechanism.

---

# 44. Divergent findings must be investigated

Current PCORI standards explicitly require conflicting/divergent results to be identified and incorporated into the overall interpretation.

### Weak response

> “Qualitative and quantitative findings differed.”

### Stronger response

Investigate:

- different populations/samples?;
- different time windows?;
- different constructs?;
- method bias?;
- outliers/subgroups?;
- participant meaning vs scale content?;
- statistical uncertainty?;
- qualitative disconfirming cases?

---

# 45. Sampling is a mixed-methods design issue

Mixed-methods sampling includes:

- quantitative sample design;
- qualitative sample design;
- relationship between samples.

Possible relationships:

- identical;
- nested;
- overlapping;
- parallel/independent;
- sequentially connected.

### Critical rule

The quantitative sample-size rationale does not determine qualitative adequacy.

The qualitative sample-size rationale does not determine quantitative power.

---

# 46. Connecting samples

Examples:

QUAN cohort  
→ purposive QUAL subsample.

Qualitative categories  
→ build quantitative sampling strata.

### Project fields

- which sample came first?;
- same participants?;
- linkage mechanism?;
- selection based on outcomes?;
- selection based on qualitative category?;
- missing linkage?;
- selection bias introduced?

---

# 47. Separate strand quality

Each component must meet its own standards.

## Quantitative

Depending on design:

- randomization/confounding;
- measurement;
- missingness;
- statistical analysis;
- sample size.

## Qualitative

Depending on methodology:

- methodological coherence;
- sampling/adequacy;
- reflexivity;
- analysis;
- trustworthiness.

### Critical rule

An excellent joint display cannot rescue:

- invalid quantitative measurement;
- methodologically incoherent qualitative work.

---

# 48. Mixed-methods quality layer

Current PCORI + MMAT imply a mixed-methods-specific quality architecture.

At minimum:

1. clear rationale for mixing;
2. appropriate strand designs;
3. planned timing/linkage;
4. appropriate sampling relationship;
5. effective integration;
6. interpretation of integrated output;
7. attention to divergence;
8. quality of both component traditions;
9. transparent meta-inference.

---

# 49. Reporting standards are not quality scores

JARS-Mixed and GRAMMS help report:

- rationale;
- purpose;
- priority;
- sequence;
- component methods;
- integration;
- mixed-method insights.

### Critical rule

Complete reporting does not guarantee low bias or good design.

Incomplete reporting can make appraisal impossible.

---

# 50. Mixed methods and causal inference

Mixed methods may sit on top of:

- randomized trial;
- quasi-experiment;
- cohort;
- cross-sectional survey;
- diagnostic study;
- prediction study.

### Critical rule

The causal/inferential strength comes from the **quantitative parent design and assumptions**, not from mixing itself.

QUAL integration may:

- explain;
- contextualize;
- challenge;
- expand;
- generate mechanism hypotheses.

It does not by itself:

- randomize;
- remove confounding;
- identify mediation;
- produce causal effects.

---

# 51. Mixed methods and prevalence

A survey strand may estimate prevalence if:

- sampling/measurement support it.

Qualitative findings may explain:

- why;
- how;
- contextual meaning.

### Critical rule

The qualitative sample does not inherit survey representativeness.

---

# 52. Mixed methods and mechanisms

Mixed methods can generate:

- perceived mechanisms;
- implementation mechanisms;
- program theory;
- explanatory hypotheses.

### Critical rule

Participant accounts of mechanism are not causal mediation estimates.

Mechanism claims must be routed to the mechanistic/causal design layer when stronger causal language is used.

---

# 53. Mixed methods and intervention development

Exploratory sequential designs are especially useful for:

- intervention component generation;
- cultural adaptation;
- measure development;
- feasibility planning.

### Critical rule

Development evidence ≠ effectiveness evidence.

A later evaluation design is required.

---

# 54. Mixed methods and process evaluation

Process evaluation frequently mixes:

- quantitative fidelity/reach/dose;
- qualitative context/implementation/experience.
