## 3. Observational learning audit

PASS — USEFUL BUT NOT EQUIVALENT TO PHYSICAL PRACTICE.

The systematic review PMID 36011744:

- included 18 studies;
- used best-evidence synthesis;
- found strong evidence that observational learning improved motor-skill learning relative to no observational-learning condition;
- found moderate evidence of no significant expert-vs-self model difference;
- found conflicting evidence for verbal cues added to observation.

This supports demonstration as a useful instructional component.

It does not prove:

- equal magnitude to physical practice;
- equal learning mechanism;
- equal generalization to adult compound lifts.

The reaching/adaptation review further supports an observation-vs-physical-practice distinction.

Canonical rule:

**use observation to establish/refine task representation, but combine it with physical practice.**

No blocker.

---

## 4. Expert model audit

PASS — USEFUL REFERENCE, NOT UNIVERSAL WINNER.

PMID 30872661:

- n = 60;
- expert observation interspersed with practice;
- expert observation produced rapid movement-planning improvements;
- benefits persisted at 24 h;
- observation condensed acquisition in that task.

This supports expert modeling.

But:

- systematic review evidence does not establish expert > self universally;
- expert > novice is not consistent across all tasks.

Canonical rule:

**an expert model is valuable when the learner needs a clear example of a successful task relation; model status alone does not guarantee superiority.**

No blocker.

## 5. Peer / novice model audit

PASS — NOT INHERENTLY INFERIOR.

PMID 1574658:

- skilled-model and learning-model observation both improved learning relative to no observation;
- no significant difference between the two observational-model groups.

PMID 21986667:

- novice-only observation was weaker than expert/mixed in one timing task;
- mixed expert+novice modeling produced more stable timing/generalization on some outcomes.

Thus:

**peer/novice modeling can be useful because it exposes error progression and correction, but usefulness depends on what information the learner extracts.**

No blocker.

---

## 6. Mixed expert + novice modeling audit

PASS AS TASK-SPECIFIC STRATEGY.

PMID 21986667 supports:

- expert model → accurate task template;
- novice model → error contrast;
- mixed schedule → better stability/generalization on some outcomes.

This is mechanistically plausible and directly demonstrated in one sequential-timing paradigm.

It is not a general model hierarchy.

Canonical rule:

**mixed modeling is useful when contrast between successful and unsuccessful execution teaches a specific relation.**

No blocker.

## 7. Self-modeling meta-analysis audit

PASS WITH STRONG DURABILITY LIMIT.

The 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis PMID 42688263 found:

### Post-test
- SMD ≈ 0.81;
- 95% CI ≈ 0.28 to 1.33;
- p ≈ 0.0025;
- I² ≈ 73%.

### One-week follow-up
- SMD ≈ 0.59;
- 95% CI ≈ −0.09 to 1.27;
- p ≈ 0.09;
- I² ≈ 70%.

Sensitivity analysis did not make the one-week effect robustly significant.

Thus:

**self-modeling has evidence for post-test improvement but not a robust demonstrated one-week motor benefit.**

No blocker.

## 8. Self-modeling methodological audit

PASS — CURRENT EVIDENCE QUALITY REQUIRES CONSERVATIVE INTERPRETATION.

The critical review published 2026-11-09 (DOI 10.3390/reports9040384):

- 24 core sources;
- 18 primary empirical studies;
- 17 intervention studies appraised;
- only four primary studies used movement actually belonging to the participant;
- three used hybrid identity/movement stimuli;
- one used generative AI;
- 16/17 intervention studies were below sample size needed for d = 0.50 at 80% power;
- no between-subjects study reached that threshold;
- assessor blinding reported in only two studies;
- delayed retention/transfer in seven.

The review concludes the evidence does not currently support strong practice recommendations.

This does not invalidate all self-modeling evidence.

It lowers certainty.

Canonical rule:

**self-modeling is promising but not established as a durable universally superior modeling strategy.**

No blocker.

## 9. Self-modeling ≠ video feedback audit

PASS.

### Self-modeling
Own performance is used as the model/reference.

### Video feedback
Own recent performance is viewed to obtain performance/error information.

The same video can serve both functions, but the instructional purpose differs.

Canonical rule:

**classify by function, not merely by modality.**

No blocker.

## 10. Video feedback audit

PASS — DIRECT SQUAT EVIDENCE EXISTS BUT IS NARROW.

PMID 37916599:

- n = 30 novices;
- video visual feedback vs tactile feedback vs control;
- feedback after each set;
- video group improved multiple squat variables;
- selected retention:
  - 3 d for neutral knee position;
  - 1 wk for trunk lean, anterior knee displacement and AP COP;
- tactile group did not show comparable retention.

This is genuine direct squat acquisition/retention evidence.

But:

- n = 10/group;
- one exercise;
- selected outcomes;
- video plus corrective information;
- not a factorial demonstration/modeling study.

Canonical rule:

**video can make hidden movement information observable and can improve novice squat learning under some protocols; universal video superiority is not established.**

No blocker.

## 11. Slow-motion audit

PASS — UNIVERSAL SUPERIORITY REJECTED.

PMID 33421652:

- n = 51;
- slow-motion, real-time and mixed demonstration groups;
- pirouette learning;
- all groups improved;
- no significant between-group learning differences.

Therefore:

**slow motion is a visibility tool, not a universally superior learning speed.**

Use slow motion when it reveals a fast relation otherwise difficult to inspect.

Real-time viewing remains necessary when actual temporal structure matters.

No blocker.

---

## 12. Interactive replay audit

PASS — DISTINCT INTERVENTION.

PMID 42413171:

- n = 64 novices;
- dart throwing;
- learner control over timing crossed with interactive playback functions;
- full-control group had strongest/durable gains at 24/72 h;
- yoked interactive group performed worse despite same pause/slow-motion/rewind availability.

Therefore:

**interactive replay benefit cannot be reduced to slow motion itself.**

The intervention includes:

- timing autonomy;
- pause;
- rewind;
- slow motion;
- learner decision about information access.

No blocker.

## 13. Observation before execution audit

PASS.

Pre-practice observation can establish:

- task goal;
- broad sequence;
- relevant spatial/temporal structure.

PMID 10900708 shows pre-practice demonstrations were useful.

But all-pre-practice observation was not the best condition in that experiment.

Canonical rule:

**demonstrate unfamiliar task structure before first attempts, but do not assume one initial demonstration block is sufficient for all learning.**

No blocker.

---

## 14. Observation between attempts audit

PASS — USEFUL BUT NO UNIVERSAL SCHEDULE.

PMID 10900708 compared:

- 10 pre-practice demonstrations;
- 10 interspersed demonstrations;
- combined 5 pre + 5 early interspersed demonstrations.

The combined schedule produced the highest form scores during acquisition and retention.

PMID 30872661 independently shows step-wise movement-planning improvement after interspersed expert observation.

Thus:

**re-observation can be especially useful once the learner has their own attempt to compare against the model.**

No universal watch-after-every-attempt frequency is established.

No blocker.

---

## 15. What to observe audit

PASS — OBSERVATION TARGET MATTERS.

PMID 14620026:

- soccer pass;
- video + visual/verbal cueing;
- combined cueing improved error/form across acquisition and retention.

PMID 27224057:

- n = 21 novice golfers;
- visual guidance highlighted task-relevant regions;
- guided learners looked more at those regions;
- improved swing execution persisted at one-week retention.

PMID 36435171:

- n = 40;
- basketball free throw;
- external-focus / gaze-instruction manipulations improved task-specific accuracy/quiet-eye outcomes.

These studies show that observation is not merely exposure.

Canonical rule:

**direct attention to the relation/event that the model is intended to teach; do not instruct the learner to monitor everything simultaneously.**

NEU-14 remains binding: these task-specific studies do not reinstate universal external-focus superiority.

No blocker.

## 16. Error-detection audit

PASS.

PMID 10994232 found:

- prior observation allowed error estimation comparable to physical-practice participants in one task;
- observation of a model receiving biased KR produced correspondingly biased reference/error-detection processes.

Therefore:

**observation can build error-detection/reference mechanisms, but a misleading model or misleading feedback can train a misleading reference.**

No blocker.

---

## 17. Camera-angle audit

PASS AS VISIBILITY VARIABLE, NOT UNIVERSAL LEARNING FACTOR.

PMID 37964620:

- n = 42;
- front vs rear video self-modeling vs control;
- rear-angle condition produced faster acquisition in cup stacking;
- no retention benefit attributable to camera angle.

Thus:

**viewpoint can affect acquisition/visibility without producing durable learning.**

For resistance exercise, camera angle should be selected by the relation to be inspected.

No universal best angle is established.

No blocker.

## 18. Anthropometry / morphology audit

PASS — EXACT GEOMETRIC COPYING REJECTED, DETERMINISM ALSO REJECTED.

### Positive geometry-modifier evidence

PMID 29997725:

- n = 32;
- stance width altered squat joint ROM;
- trunk/thigh and thigh/shank ratios related to some ankle/knee angles.

PMID 41196246:

- n = 44;
- limited ankle dorsiflexion associated with greater pelvis ROM, hip/lumbar flexion and trunk forward lean.

These support individual geometry differences.

### Counterweight

PMID 34712337:

- n = 53;
- squat biomechanics related more clearly to strength/flexibility than leg-length ratio in that sample.

PMID 37123458:

- n = 11 trained men;
- arm, torso, thigh and shank lengths did not significantly predict most conventional-deadlift outcomes;
- one thigh-length/ankle-work relationship appeared.

Thus:

**anthropometry can constrain/shift a movement solution, but body proportions do not determine one unique technique.**

Canonical rule:

**copy task constraints, success criteria and relevant coordination relationships—not another lifter's exact joint geometry.**

No blocker.

## 19. Direct compound-lift evidence audit

PASS WITH STRONG INDIRECTNESS WARNING.

Direct long-term modeling evidence in compound resistance exercise remains sparse.

Strongest checked direct learning evidence:

- own-video novice squat feedback, PMID 37916599.

Most broader observational-learning evidence comes from:

- PE tasks;
- sport skills;
- reaching/adaptation;
- sequencing;
- dance;
- darts;
- golf.

Therefore:

**general modeling principles can guide RT coaching, but exact expert/self/peer model mix, dose, timing, slow-motion schedule and camera angle for compound lifts remain unestablished.**

No blocker because those exact exercise-specific prescriptions belong TEC/COA/IND.

## 20. Measurement non-equivalence audit

PASS.

Separate:

### Demonstration
Viewing another model to establish task representation.

### Self-modeling
Viewing one's own performance as the model/reference.

### Video feedback
Viewing own recent attempt for error/performance information.

### Feedforward
Viewing a modified self-representation of not-yet-achieved performance.

### Model identity
Expert / peer / novice / self.

### Model quality
Successful / error-containing / mixed.

### Playback speed
Real time / slow motion.

### Timing
Pre-practice / interspersed / learner-controlled.

### Observation target
Specific relation/event being inspected.

### Acquisition
Practice-period improvement.

### Retention
Delayed independent performance.

### Transfer
Changed condition/load/context.

Project rule:

**do not collapse these variables into “video works” or “modeling works.”**

No blocker.
