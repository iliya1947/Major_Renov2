# BIO-12 — Resistance training bioenergetics

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-19**  
Область: 05 — Bioenergetics / Exercise Metabolism

## 0. Core decision

Resistance training is a **metabolically mixed, intermittent, non-steady-state task**.

Phosphagen, glycolytic and oxidative ATP-resynthesis pathways can all contribute, but their relative contribution changes:

- within a set;
- across repeated sets;
- across rest intervals;
- with load, reps, tempo, effort, exercise and starting state.

Project rule:

**resistance training is not “anaerobic-only,” and no fixed pathway percentage applies to every set.**

---

## 1. Energy demand within a set

Set energetic demand depends jointly on:

- external load;
- repetitions;
- repetition duration/velocity;
- set duration;
- contraction type;
- ROM/mechanics;
- active muscle mass;
- proximity to failure;
- prior-set state;
- rest interval.

Project rule:

**no single prescription variable is a complete metabolic dose.**

---

## 2. Energy expenditure measurement

Resistance-exercise energy expenditure is difficult to quantify because non-oxidative energy contribution is substantial.

Indirect calorimetry is useful for oxidative cost but does not directly capture total anaerobic ATP turnover.

Project rule:

**energy-cost estimates are method conditioned.**

---

## 3. Total energy cost vs pathway flux

Whole-set/session energy expenditure is not identical to:

- instantaneous phosphagen ATP flux;
- instantaneous glycolytic ATP flux;
- local muscle ATP turnover.

Project rule:

**integrated energy cost ≠ instantaneous pathway contribution.**

---

## 4. Repetition tempo

Tempo changes:

- TUT;
- external work/volume;
- impulse;
- local oxygenation;
- HR;
- lactate/metabolic responses.

Current evidence shows tempo effects depend strongly on what is matched.

Project rule:

**tempo cannot be interpreted without specifying whether TUT, effort, load, reps, work or failure endpoint was matched.**

---

## 5. Slow tempo boundary

Slow repetitions can increase TUT and some local deoxygenation/metabolic proxy responses.

They do not universally increase total energy expenditure or hypertrophy.

Project rule:

**slow tempo ≠ universally greater metabolic dose or hypertrophy stimulus.**

---

## 6. Set duration

Longer continuous sets sustain ATP demand for longer and often increase metabolic disturbance.

But set duration covaries with:

- load;
- reps;
- tempo;
- proximity to failure.

Project rule:

**set duration matters, but is not an independent standalone dose.**

---

## 7. Load

%1RM changes force demand and attainable repetition number.

It does not uniquely determine:

- lactate;
- set duration;
- glycolytic contribution;
- fatigue;
- total metabolic disturbance.

Project rule:

**%1RM is a mechanical constraint, not a standalone metabolic dose.**

---

## 8. Mechanical intensity vs metabolic stress

A heavy low-rep set may have high force demand but limited duration.

A lighter set taken close to failure may have:

- more reps;
- longer duration;
- greater metabolite accumulation.

Project rule:

**mechanical intensity ≠ metabolic stress.**

---

## 9. Repetition number

More repetitions in one continuous set usually increase:

- set duration;
- cumulative ATP turnover;
- metabolite accumulation;
- fatigue.

But effects depend on load, tempo, rest and effort.

Project rule:

**rep count alone is metabolically incomplete.**

---

## 10. No energy-system rep zones

Reject:

- 1–5 reps = phosphagen only;
- 6–12 reps = glycolytic;
- high reps = oxidative/glycolytic only.

Project rule:

**rep ranges do not own exclusive energy systems.**

---

## 11. Proximity to failure

Closer proximity to failure generally increases acute:

- repetitions performed;
- velocity loss;
- RPE;
- metabolic disturbance;
- neuromuscular fatigue.

Project rule:

**closer-to-failure work generally creates greater acute internal strain.**

---

## 12. Failure boundary

Momentary failure is not required for hypertrophy, strength, endurance or power adaptation.

Project rule:

**failure is not a mandatory metabolic or adaptation target.**

---

## 13. Proximity to failure vs hypertrophy mechanism

Closer proximity to failure can matter for hypertrophy.

This does not prove that:

- lactate;
- H+;
- Pi;
- cell swelling;
- pump

are the causal mediators.

Project rule:

**effective hard training ≠ proof that metabolic stress causes hypertrophy.**

---

## 14. Rest interval

Rest interval changes the physiological state before the next set.

Longer rest generally permits more:

- PCr restoration;
- metabolite/ion recovery;
- force/velocity recovery;
- work maintenance.

Shorter rest generally increases:

- density;
- residual disturbance;
- acute internal strain.

Project rule:

**rest is part of the metabolic state of the next set.**

---

## 15. Rest ≠ fixed PCr restoration

PCr resynthesis is oxidative and variable.

Recovery depends on:

- prior depletion;
- pH;
- O2 availability;
- muscle;
- individual state;
- measurement method.

Project rule:

**no universal “X minutes = Y% PCr restored” rule is retained.**

---

## 16. Repeated-set PCr state

Later sets can begin before full PCr restoration.

Project rule:

**same external set prescription ≠ same starting phosphagen state across sets.**

PCr restoration supports performance but does not certify complete recovery.

---

## 17. Glycolytic contribution

Longer/moderate-high-repetition continuous sets can have substantial glycolytic contribution.

But:

- blood lactate ≠ direct glycolytic ATP flux;
- muscle lactate ≠ complete glycolytic ATP accounting;
- no fixed rep threshold defines glycolytic dominance.

Project rule:

**glycolytic contribution is task dependent and method dependent.**

---

## 18. Oxidative contribution

Oxidative metabolism contributes:

- during resistance work;
- across repeated sets;
- during between-set recovery;
- after exercise.

It is especially important for:

- PCr resynthesis;
- ATP-dependent ion/homeostatic recovery.

Project rule:

**resistance exercise is not anaerobic-only; recovery is strongly oxidative.**

---

## 19. Training density

Density describes how much work/exposure is compressed into time.

Higher density can increase:

- VO2;
- HR;
- lactate;
- RPE;
- metabolic stress.

It can also reduce:

- repetition quality;
- velocity/power;
- maintainable load/work.

Project rule:

**density is a trade-off variable, not “more stimulus” by definition.**

---

## 20. Volume load

Equal/similar volume load can coexist with different:

- metabolic disturbance;
- lactate;
- PCr/ATP changes;
- velocity loss;
- perceived effort.

Project rule:

**volume load ≠ internal metabolic stress.**

---

## 21. Rest redistribution / clusters

Redistributing rest into shorter, more frequent intervals can:

- reduce continuous-set lactate accumulation;
- preserve velocity/power in some protocols;
- reduce RPE/internal load in some populations.

Effects are not uniform across all tasks/populations/outcomes.

Project rule:

**rest redistribution changes the internal response without automatically changing total work.**

---

## 22. Supersets

Supersets are generally:

- more time efficient;
- higher in acute blood lactate;
- higher in estimated energy cost;
- higher in RPE.

But effects depend strongly on superset type.

Project rule:

**“superset” is not one physiological intervention class.**

---

## 23. Superset types

Distinguish:

- agonist–antagonist;
- similar-biomechanical;
- alternate-peripheral.

Similar-biomechanical supersets can compromise volume more than agonist–antagonist configurations.

Project rule:

**superset configuration matters.**

---

## 24. Superset chronic boundary

Pooled chronic outcomes can be broadly similar to traditional sets.

But exercise-specific strength trade-offs can occur.

Project rule:

**average chronic similarity ≠ equivalence for every exercise/outcome.**

---

## 25. Circuits

High-density resistance circuits can substantially increase:

- VO2;
- HR;
- lactate;
- session energy cost.

Exact effects depend on:

- exercise sequence;
- local muscle overlap;
- load;
- reps;
- work:rest;
- active muscle mass.

Project rule:

**circuit RT can be strongly cardiorespiratory/metabolic without having one fixed “circuit metabolism.”**

---

## 26. Metabolic stress and hypertrophy

Acute resistance exercise can produce:

- lactate;
- H+/pH changes;
- Pi accumulation;
- cell swelling;
- pump;
- local hypoxia/deoxygenation.

These responses are not established as necessary or independently sufficient hypertrophy doses.

Project rule:

**metabolic stress is an acute exercise response, not a validated standalone causal hypertrophy dose.**

---

## 27. Pump / cell swelling

Pump and acute swelling can accompany hard training.

Project rule:

**pump ≠ retained hypertrophy, and acute swelling ≠ muscle growth.**

---

## 28. Acute metabolic cost vs chronic adaptation

Higher:

- lactate;
- VO2;
- energy cost;
- EPOC;
- RPE

does not automatically mean greater:

- hypertrophy;
- strength;
- long-term fat loss.

Project rule:

**acute metabolic cost is not a chronic adaptation score.**

---

## 29. Measurement boundaries

### Indirect calorimetry
Oxidative whole-body cost; incomplete for total anaerobic turnover.

### Blood lactate
Systemic concentration; not direct glycolytic ATP flux.

### Muscle lactate
Local metabolite concentration; not complete glycolytic accounting.

### 31P-MRS
Local PCr/Pi/pH; protocol/model dependent.

### Biopsy
Local sampled tissue only.

### NIRS / SmO2
Local oxygenation/hemodynamic proxy; not ATP flux.

### nVCO2
Metabolic proxy; not direct glycolytic ATP measurement.

### Velocity loss
Mechanical-fatigue proxy; not metabolite measure.

### RPE
Perceptual response; not biochemical assay.

Project rule:

**no routine single measure directly quantifies total resistance-set bioenergetics.**

---

---

## 31. Trainer-level implications

1. Describe sets by load + reps + tempo + effort + rest + exercise, not one variable.
2. Expect later sets to begin from altered PCr/metabolite state.
3. Treat longer continuous sets as more likely to create glycolytic/metabolite disturbance, not as an automatic hypertrophy advantage.
4. Recognize that lighter loads near failure can be metabolically demanding.
5. Recognize that heavy low-rep sets can be mechanically demanding without maximizing lactate.
6. Do not program toward blood lactate, burn or pump as primary targets.
7. Use rest redistribution/clusters when preserving mechanical quality matters, without assuming universal superiority.
8. Use supersets primarily as a time-efficiency tool with configuration-specific trade-offs.
9. Treat circuits as combined resistance + cardiorespiratory/metabolic exposure when density is high.
10. Exact programming remains PRO.

---

## 32. Confidence

### HIGH

- resistance exercise is metabolically mixed;
- set internal demand is multi-variable;
- rest changes later-set metabolic state;
- PCr recovery is oxidative and variable;
- blood lactate is not direct glycolytic ATP flux;
- oxidative metabolism contributes during RT and recovery;
- density changes internal strain;
- volume load does not uniquely determine metabolic stress;
- supersets generally raise acute internal load and improve time efficiency;
- metabolic stress is not established as a standalone hypertrophy dose;
- acute metabolic cost does not rank chronic adaptation.

### MODERATE-HIGH

- tempo effects depend strongly on matching constraints;
- lighter/moderate loads near failure can match/exceed heavier-load metabolic strain in selected protocols;
- rest redistribution can preserve mechanical output/reduce lactate;
- pooled superset chronic outcomes can be similar to traditional sets.

### MODERATE / CONTEXT DEPENDENT

- exact pathway percentages in any set;
- exact PCr recovery at a given rest;
- exact metabolic consequence of a tempo;
- exact RIR where metabolic disturbance changes;
- exact density at which cardiorespiratory strain dominates;
- exact superset/circuit metabolic cost.
