# BIO-08 — Interaction of energy systems

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-19**  
Область: 05 — Bioenergetics / Exercise Metabolism

## 0. Core decision

Exercise ATP resynthesis is an integrated process involving overlapping:

- phosphagen / ATP-PCr pathways;
- glycolytic substrate-level phosphorylation;
- oxidative phosphorylation.

Project rule:

**energy systems are interacting biochemical pathways, not sequential engines that wait for one another to switch off.**

---

## 1. Simultaneous / overlapping contribution

At whole-muscle/task level, major ATP-resynthesis pathways can contribute over the same exercise interval.

This does not imply:

- equal flux;
- identical contribution in every fibre;
- identical contribution at every instant.

Project rule:

**overlap ≠ equal contribution.**

---

## 2. Dominance

A dominant system is the largest relative contributor over a defined interval/task.

Project rule:

**dominant system ≠ only active system.**

---

## 3. Intensity

As ATP demand rises:

- PCr use increases rapidly;
- glycolytic ATP supply can rise;
- oxidative ATP supply also rises;
- relative contribution depends on how quickly demand exceeds oxidative supply.

Project rule:

**higher intensity changes relative flux; it does not turn oxidative metabolism off.**

---

## 4. Duration

Short maximal exercise is generally more anaerobic dominated.

As maximal exercise duration increases, oxidative contribution rises.

The 2026 pooled systematic-review model estimated equal anaerobic/aerobic contribution at ~78.6 s.

Project rule:

**~78.6 s is an aggregate model estimate, not a universal individual switch point.**

---

## 5. Transition

Exercise onset involves overlapping changes in:

- ATP/PCr buffering;
- glycolytic ATP production;
- oxidative ATP production.

Project rule:

**transition = redistribution of flux, not discrete handoff.**

---

## 6. Stored ATP vs PCr

Stored ATP and PCr are distinct high-energy-phosphate pools.

They may be grouped educationally within the immediate/phosphagen response, but quantitative accounting must separate them.

Project rule:

**stored ATP ≠ PCr.**

---

## 7. Measurement of energy-system contribution

Common approaches include:

- MAOD;
- PCr-La-O2;
- critical power;
- gross efficiency;
- bioenergetic models.

Current evidence:

- MAOD has the strongest reliability/validity evidence for total aerobic-vs-anaerobic estimation;
- MAOD does not directly partition phosphagen vs glycolytic contribution;
- PCr-La-O2 can estimate those components separately but has a smaller validation base;
- no universally accepted gold standard directly measures all pathway ATP flux in vivo.

Project rule:

**energy-system percentages are method-conditioned estimates.**

---

## 8. Lactate and glycolytic contribution

Blood lactate depends on:

- production;
- transport;
- oxidation;
- redistribution;
- sampling timing.

Project rule:

**blood lactate ≠ direct glycolytic ATP flux.**

---

## 9. VO2 and oxidative contribution

Pulmonary VO2 is useful for estimating whole-body oxidative metabolism.

But it is not a direct measurement of:

- total ATP production;
- local muscle ATP flux;
- non-oxidative ATP production.

Project rule:

**VO2 ≠ total ATP production.**

---

## 10. Repeated sets

Later sets can begin with:

- incompletely restored PCr;
- altered Pi/pH;
- elevated VO2;
- altered glycolytic state;
- reduced power/work.

Project rule:

**same external set prescription ≠ same internal metabolic state across sets.**

The direction and size of pathway redistribution remain task dependent.

---

## 11. Repeated sprints

Repeated sprints do not reproduce the first sprint's metabolic profile.

Evidence supports:

- incomplete PCr recovery;
- declining glycolytic contribution in later sprints;
- increasing relative oxidative contribution in many protocols;
- performance decrement.

Project rule:

**maximal intent ≠ constant pathway flux.**

Exact percentages remain protocol/method dependent.

---

## 12. Long low/moderate-intensity activity

Oxidative phosphorylation supplies most ATP during prolonged steady-state low/moderate-intensity exercise.

But:

- glycolysis continues to process carbohydrate;
- CK/PCr remains involved in ATP buffering;
- transient intensity changes can increase non-oxidative contribution.

Project rule:

**oxidative-dominant ≠ oxidative-only.**

---

## 13. Resistance exercise

Resistance exercise is intermittent and non-steady-state.

Contribution depends on:

- load;
- repetitions;
- set duration;
- cadence;
- proximity to failure;
- active muscle mass;
- rest interval;
- session density.

Project rule:

**resistance exercise is metabolically mixed; no universal phosphagen/glycolytic/oxidative percentage split is retained.**

---

## 14. Recovery intervals

Recovery is metabolically active.

Oxidative ATP production supports:

- PCr resynthesis;
- oxygen-store restoration;
- ion/homeostatic restoration;
- other ATP-dependent recovery processes.

Project rule:

**rest interval is part of the metabolic state of the next bout.**

---

## 15. PCr recovery

PCr resynthesis is primarily supported by oxidative ATP production.

Its kinetics depend on:

- oxidative capacity;
- O2 availability/perfusion;
- end-exercise pH;
- degree of PCr depletion;
- protocol/method;
- training/health state.

Project rule:

**PCr recovery is oxidative but not universally O2-limited and not governed by one fixed recovery-time law.**

---

## 16. Recovery vs EPOC

PCr resynthesis contributes to recovery metabolism but is not equivalent to the complete EPOC construct.

Project rule:

**PCr resynthesis ≠ EPOC.**

Detailed EPOC remains BIO-09.

---

## 17. Integrated percentages vs instantaneous flux

A reported energy-system percentage usually integrates a whole interval/bout.

It does not describe pathway ATP-production rate at every instant.

Project rule:

**integrated contribution ≠ instantaneous ATP flux.**

---

## 18. Canonical non-equivalences

1. overlapping systems ≠ equal flux.
2. dominant system ≠ only active system.
3. anaerobic-dominant ≠ zero oxidative ATP.
4. oxidative-dominant ≠ zero glycolysis/phosphagen flux.
5. stored ATP ≠ PCr.
6. activation ≠ dominance.
7. dominance ≠ fixed percentage.
8. duration ≠ sole determinant of contribution.
9. ~78.6 s pooled estimate ≠ universal switch time.
10. maximal intent ≠ constant maximal glycolytic flux.
11. first sprint ≠ later sprint metabolic profile.
12. first set ≠ later-set metabolic profile.
13. blood lactate ≠ glycolytic ATP flux.
14. VO2 ≠ total ATP production.
15. MAOD ≠ direct phosphagen/glycolytic split.
16. PCr-La-O2 ≠ gold-standard direct ATP measurement.
17. PCr depletion ≠ complete fatigue model.
18. PCr recovery ≠ complete metabolic recovery.
19. O2 affects PCr recovery ≠ O2 is sole limiter.
20. 3 min rest ≠ guaranteed full PCr restoration.
21. recovery interval ≠ metabolically inactive.
22. PCr resynthesis ≠ EPOC.
23. integrated percentage ≠ instantaneous ATP flux.
24. resistance exercise ≠ purely anaerobic.
25. prolonged low-intensity exercise ≠ purely aerobic.
26. “energy system” ≠ independent engine.

---

## 19. Confidence

### HIGH

- phosphagen, glycolytic and oxidative pathways overlap/interact;
- dominance is relative;
- contribution changes with intensity and duration;
- oxidative ATP contributes during brief maximal work;
- no universal switch time exists;
- repeated bouts alter metabolic starting state;
- maximal intent does not guarantee constant glycolytic flux;
- resistance exercise is metabolically mixed;
- recovery intervals are metabolically active;
- PCr resynthesis depends on oxidative ATP production;
- energy-system percentages are method dependent;
- integrated contribution differs from instantaneous ATP flux.

### MODERATE-HIGH

- the pooled maximal-exercise anaerobic/aerobic crossover is around 75–80 s in the 2026 model;
- relative oxidative contribution commonly rises across repeated sprints with incomplete recovery;
- training/oxidative capacity can influence repeated-bout recovery and pathway redistribution.

### MODERATE / CONTEXT DEPENDENT

- exact contribution percentages in any specific task;
- exact crossover duration for an individual;
- exact PCr recovery fraction after a specified rest;
- exact resistance-exercise energy split;
- exact sequence-specific pathway redistribution across repeated sets/sprints.

### NOT RETAINED AS UNIVERSAL

- 0–10 s = 100% ATP-PCr;
- glycolysis starts only after PCr exhaustion;
- aerobic metabolism starts after 2 min;
- 78.6 s is a physiological switch;
- long endurance work is 100% aerobic;
- resistance exercise is purely anaerobic;
- repeated sprints have the same pathway split;
- maximal effort maintains maximal glycolytic ATP production;
- three minutes always fully restores PCr;
- blood lactate directly equals glycolytic ATP production;
- VO2 directly equals total ATP production;
- one percentage chart applies to every athlete/task.
