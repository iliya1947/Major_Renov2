E1. Leaper & Ayres (2007), A Meta-Analytic Review of Gender Variations in Adults' Language Use: Talkativeness, Affiliative Speech, and Assertive Speech. https://doi.org/10.1177/1088868307302221 ; https://escholarship.org/uc/item/5wh3p8cw .
Indexed publisher and university repository abstract/excerpts; direct PubMed and repository opening blocked. Three meta-analyses: men more talkative (absolute d=.14), more assertive speech (d=.09), women more affiliative speech (d=.12). Seventeen moderators examined, including familiarity, composition, activity and measurement. Small average differences; no universal individual prediction. Speech amount, affiliative function and assertive function distinct. Historical datasets and operational variation limit comparison; full moderator audit not performed.

E2. Tidwell et al. (2025), Are Women Really (Not) More Talkative Than Men? A Registered Report of Binary Gender Similarities/Differences in Daily Word Use. https://doi.org/10.1037/pspp0000534 .
Complete published abstract inspected at author institution https://researchconnect.stonybrook.edu/en/publications/are-women-really-not-more-talkative-than-men-a-registered-report-/ ; indexed primary text excerpts, PMC direct opening blocked. N=2,197, 22 samples, 631,030 ambient audio recordings. Model-estimated difference 1,073 words/day favoring women (d=.13, 95% credible interval 316–1,824); early/middle adulthood estimate 3,275 (d=.32). Other age strata smaller or reversed, wide uncertainty; preregistered equivalence criterion ±1,000 words/day usually inconclusive. Descriptive averages are not identical to modeled contrast. Individual variation large. Not a longitudinal within-person age trajectory. No clear evidence self-rated gap systematically exceeds observed gap; stress not a meaningful moderator in reported analyses. E1 opposite average direction is compatible with different historical samples and definitions, but reconciliation not identified. Word counts do not establish desire for conversation with a particular person, closeness, emotionality or motive.

Indexed published abstract only; direct PubMed opening blocked. Moderator comparisons do not establish a unique psychological cause. Economic outcome, request amount, bargaining behavior, social consequences and competence are separate targets. ## 3. Operational procedure and practice

1. Define target communicative behavior, content, function, context, opportunity, audience and actual outcome; distinguish quantity from reciprocity, assertion from aggression, affiliation from agreement, and self-advocacy from negotiation success.
2. Separate stable tendencies and current goals from situational constraints, role expectations, actual social consequences and anticipated evaluation. Public compliance does not establish private endorsement.
3. For relational communication, consider both actors' desires for change, topic, reciprocal sequence, actual opportunity and competing explanations. Do not infer power, intent or stable attachment pattern from one interaction.
4. Compare observed behavior, voluntary self-report and observer interpretation. Group averages and exploratory profiles do not identify an individual's motive or vulnerability. Respect explicit choices; no pressure, exploitative testing or manipulation.
5. Preserve time, actor/partner levels, uncertainty and revision conditions; distinguish direct moderator tests from separate subgroup significance and observed mediation from identified mechanism.

Generic invented checks: A. A fictional woman speaks little in a large formal meeting but extensively in a small workshop: compare roles, opportunities, audience and goals rather than infer a global trait. B. A negotiator requests different terms in different contexts: fear of evaluation, information, actual incentives and preferences compete; E4 replication blocks an automatic advocacy-based explanation. C. Two women with similar communal trait reports differ in evaluative concern: E5 supports heterogeneous hypotheses, not assigning a latent type from observed behavior. D. In an invented couple discussion one partner wants a change more strongly; either may initiate or withdraw depending on topic. E7 supports examining relative desire for change without presuming sex determines roles.
Retrospective external challenges E1/E2, E4, E5/E6 and E7; results known before checks. Internal reasoning only; no independently scored, held-out or prospective validation.
