# PHY-08 — Strength physiology

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-18**  
Область: 03 — Physiology

## 0. Core decision

Strength is a **task-defined system output**, not a direct synonym for muscle size.

Canonical organization model:

`strength in a given test`
`← contractile capacity (size/PCSA/specific tension)`
`+ neural activation/coordination`
`+ architecture/MTU mechanics`
`+ task skill/specificity`
`+ test conditions`.

This is not a literal additive formula.

## 1. Why strength and hypertrophy dissociate

### Strength can rise faster than hypertrophy
Because of:

- neural adaptation;
- improved skill/technique;
- intermuscular coordination;
- joint-angle/velocity/mode specificity;
- architecture/MTU adaptation;
- familiarization with maximal effort.

### Hypertrophy can rise more than measured strength
Because:

- training/test tasks differ;
- neural/skill transfer is limited;
- regional hypertrophy may not align with test demands;
- relative rather than absolute strength is used;
- the selected test captures a different construct.

Project rule:

**hypertrophy increases force capacity; task-specific neuromuscular/mechanical factors determine expression.**

## 2. Neural contribution

Resistance training can improve:

- voluntary activation;
- motor-unit behavior;
- agonist/intermuscular coordination;
- antagonist control;
- task-specific neural organization.

Project rule:

**neural adaptation contributes to strength, but surface EMG amplitude is not a complete neural-strength measure.**

Detailed mechanisms → NEU.

## 3. Hypertrophy contribution

Larger muscle generally has greater force potential.

Within-person human data show a strong association between muscle growth and strength gain.

But force per unit size and task-specific expression can also change.

Project rule:

**muscle size is a major determinant, not the sole determinant, of strength.**

## 4. Architecture

Relevant variables include:

- PCSA;
- pennation;
- fascicle length;
- muscle-joint geometry.

Project rule:

**architecture modifies force expression but no single architectural variable universally explains strength gain.**

## 5. Tendon stiffness

Tendon stiffness can increase with chronic loading.

Potential relevance:

- force transmission;
- fascicle operating behavior;
- electromechanical delay;
- rapid force expression.

Project rule:

**tendon stiffness is relevant, but its change is not a fixed proportional cause of maximal-strength gain.**

Detailed tendon adaptation → PHY-12.

## 6. Intermuscular coordination

Multi-joint strength depends on coordinated contribution of multiple muscles.

Project rule:

**coordination is part of strength expression, especially in task-specific lifts.**

Detailed control → NEU.

## 7. Skill / technique

Dynamic 1RM performance includes motor skill:

- setup;
- bar/device path;
- bracing;
- sequencing;
- timing;
- use of advantageous joint positions;
- maximal-effort familiarity.

Project rule:

**1RM is not an isolated assay of muscle contractile capacity.**

## 8. Task specificity

Dynamic RT produces larger improvements in trained dynamic strength than in non-trained isometric strength.

Project rule:

**transfer exists but decreases as task similarity falls.**

## 9. Joint-angle specificity

Isometric strength adaptation is often largest near the trained joint angle, with transfer to nearby angles.

Project rule:

**joint angle is part of the strength task, but adaptation is not confined to one exact angle.**

## 10. Velocity specificity

Training adaptations depend partly on the practiced force–velocity region and intent.

- heavy/maximal-strength work → stronger high-force specificity;
- explosive/ballistic work → stronger high-velocity specificity;
- cross-velocity transfer exists.

Project rule:

**no one velocity develops every strength expression maximally.**

## 11. Contraction-mode specificity

- eccentric-only training preferentially improves eccentric strength;
- isometric training preferentially improves isometric strength;
- cross-mode transfer still occurs.

Project rule:

**contraction-mode specificity is substantial, not absolute.**

## 12. Rate of force development

RFD = rate at which force rises after contraction onset.

It differs from maximal force.

- early RFD → especially rapid neural activation;
- later RFD → larger contribution from maximal force and muscle/MTU properties.

Project rule:

**RFD requires separate measurement/training specificity.**

Detailed synthesis → PHY-09.

## 13. MVC / MVF

MVC/MVF is maximal voluntary force or torque under a defined:

- contraction mode;
- joint angle;
- setup.

Project rule:

**MVC is not interchangeable with 1RM.**

## 14. Dynamic 1RM

1RM is the maximum external load completed in a dynamic task.

It combines:

- force capacity;
- technique;
- coordination;
- ROM;
- external mechanics;
- confidence/familiarization.

Project rule:

**always state the strength test before interpreting “strength gain.”**

## 15. Absolute strength

Absolute strength = unnormalized force/torque/load.

Examples:

- kg in a 1RM lift;
- N·m in MVC torque.

## 16. Relative strength

Relative strength normalizes strength to body size or tissue quantity.

Possible denominators:

- body mass;
- lean mass;
- PCSA;
- allometric body-size scaling.

Project rule:

**relative strength is metric-dependent; state the denominator/scaling method.**

## 17. Strength endurance

Strength endurance = ability to sustain or repeat force production.

It depends on:

- maximal-strength reserve;
- metabolic capacity;
- fatigue resistance;
- task/load;
- pacing.

Project rule:

**strength endurance is not simply maximal strength repeated.**

## 18. Heavy loading

Current synthesis supports heavier loads for maximal dynamic-strength development.

ACSM 2026 identifies ≥80% 1RM as a prescription characteristic favoring voluntary strength in the synthesized review literature.

Project rule:

**heavy loading has strong maximal-strength specificity, not universal superiority for every strength-related outcome.**

## 19. Dose-response boundary

Strength gains show non-linear relationships with training dosage in pooled data.

Project rule:

**group-level modeled plateaus are not personal ceilings and must not be converted directly into individual limits.**

Detailed prescription → PRO.

## 20. Transfer hierarchy

Transfer depends on similarity in:

- movement;
- joint angles/ROM;
- contraction mode;
- velocity;
- muscles;
- external mechanics;
- coordination.

Project rule:

**general force capacity transfers more broadly; task-specific skill/coordination transfers less broadly.**

## 21. Canonical rules

1. Strength is test/task specific.
2. Strength ≠ hypertrophy.
3. Hypertrophy raises capacity; neural/skill/mechanical factors determine expression.
4. EMG ≠ complete neural adaptation.
5. Architecture contributes but is not a universal proxy.
6. Tendon stiffness adapts but does not explain a fixed share of strength gain.
7. 1RM includes skill and mechanics.
8. Joint-angle specificity exists.
9. Velocity specificity exists.
10. Contraction-mode specificity exists.
11. RFD ≠ maximal force.
12. MVC ≠ 1RM.
13. Absolute strength ≠ relative strength.
14. Relative strength requires an explicit scaling method.
15. Strength endurance ≠ maximal strength.
16. Transfer is graded by task similarity.
17. Heavy loading is especially specific to maximal dynamic strength.
18. Population dose plateaus ≠ personal ceilings.

## 22. Training-relevant implications

1. **Choose the strength outcome before judging a program.**
2. **Use test-specific strength measures when transfer matters.**
3. **Do not infer muscle gain from strength alone.**
4. **Do not infer strength capacity from muscle size alone.**
5. **Expect more transfer when training/testing tasks are similar.**
6. **Treat heavy loading as especially relevant to maximal dynamic strength.**
7. **Use explosive intent/velocity when rapid force is the target.**
8. **Measure RFD separately if rapid force matters.**
9. **Interpret relative strength with the chosen scaling denominator.**
10. **Measure endurance separately from maximal strength.**

## 23. What PHY-08 does not prescribe

## 24. Confidence

### HIGH
- task/test specificity;
- muscle-size contribution but non-equivalence;
- dynamic→isometric transfer being smaller than trained dynamic gain;
- contraction-mode specificity;
- MVC/1RM distinction;
- RFD/maximal-force distinction;
- absolute/relative-strength distinction;
- strength-endurance distinction;
- heavy-load maximal-strength specificity.

### MODERATE-HIGH
- neural/coordination contribution;
- architecture contribution;
- joint-angle specificity;
- velocity/intent specificity.

### MODERATE / CONTEXT-DEPENDENT
- exact causal share of hypertrophy vs neural adaptation;
- exact tendon-stiffness contribution;
- exact transfer gradient between arbitrary tasks;
- optimal relative-strength scaling;
- individual dosage plateau.
