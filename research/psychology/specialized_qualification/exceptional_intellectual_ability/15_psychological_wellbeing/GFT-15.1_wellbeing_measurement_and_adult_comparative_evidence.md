## Synthesis and evidence limits
E02 provides substantial counterevidence to blanket increased disorder prevalence in high measured g in the observed cohort, especially for anxiety/PTSD, not proof of universal protection. E03 reports elevated selected-group outcomes but cannot estimate all high-IQ adults' risk. E04 and E05 show different subjective outcomes under different recruitment and measurement, not a single pooled effect. E06/07 distinguish general association from nonlinear trajectory and childhood from adult exposure. Clinical prevalence, subjective well-being and stress exposure are different; a person may have positive meaning and symptoms, good performance and high cost, or low satisfaction without disorder. Lower trauma exposure does not establish lower stress reactivity at the same exposure. No verified direct separately measured adult IQ150–160+ prevalence or well-being estimate in this bounded search; absence of evidence, not evidence of absence. No claim causal IQ effect. Q-15-tail OPEN; GFT-14 dependency for compensation/resource cost remains to be verified before final closure. Existing GFT-08/10/11/12/13/14 and 19 guardrails retained. No estimate of specific clinical disorder risk from general intelligence.
## Practical analytic rules (PRACTICAL_HEURISTIC)
1. Specify target: life satisfaction vs momentary affect vs meaning/autonomy vs stressor exposure, appraisal, response/recovery vs actual functional impairment vs clinically assessed syndrome. Record context, duration and time window; no composite of incommensurable outcomes, no diagnosis by scale/score or IQ. Positive experiences and distress may coexist. Current, period and approximate lifetime prevalence not interchangeable.
2. Verify cognitive measure (age, norms, test version, precision and selection); distinguish high performance, measured g, formal test-defined threshold, club membership and self-identification. Verify denominator/outcome ascertainment, comparator recruitment, missingness, time window, age/sex/culture, access to assessment and uncertainty. No odds ratio as absolute probability or incidence; null not equivalence. Selection can create or attenuate association; don't assume one bias direction.
3. Consider alternatives: adversity and material resources, sleep/health, social/work context, task demand/support, 2e, developmental history and care access. These are hypotheses, not inferred causes. Separate group-level plausibility from individual observation and prospective discriminators; do not dismiss distress because group averages favorable or pathologize intellect based on a selected group's reports. Don't infer extremal phenotype or compensatory cost without actual evidence.
4. Clinical boundaries: persistent or severe distress, deterioration in everyday function or safety concern merits appropriate qualified evaluation regardless of IQ or achievement; no diagnostic or treatment recommendation based solely on this block. Use ordinary voluntary observations and meaningful comparison, not induced stress or removal of supports. If no additional evidence necessary for self-understanding, stop. Use INT-02/03 and GFT-19 revision for contradictions.
