## 3. Analytic gate — identity and fit (PRACTICAL_HEURISTIC)
Identity = subjective self-meaning, continuity and social positioning; gifted identification = specific assignment of a label under a criterion; actual measured ability = separate evidence. Neither identification nor self-identification diagnoses a mechanism. Lived experience is evidence of experience, not automatically proof of its causal interpretation; disconfirmation of a causal attribution does not invalidate experience. No requirement to identify with or disclose a label, achieve high occupational status, find intellectually similar friends, or adapt to harmful expectations. A gifted person may find identity central, incidental, ambivalent, or irrelevant; no normative sequence. 
| Dimension | Independent evidence | Rival explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Demands–abilities | task requirements and demonstrated skills/performance | training, experience, role clarity, health, tools |
| Needs–supplies | valued challenge, autonomy, resources, feedback and what is available | goals changed, workload, economic constraints |
| Values–environment | stated values and observed norms/practices | local conflict, injustice, incentives, communication |
| Belonging | felt inclusion plus concrete interaction/participation | interest, opportunities, reciprocity, discrimination, chosen solitude |
| Identity | self-description, meaning, identification history and changes | broader life transition, social expectations, retrospective reinterpretation |
| Adaptation | goal-relevant functioning, wellbeing, sustained effort/costs | surface compliance, unsustainable compensation, unrelated stress |
These dimensions need not covary. High performance and low satisfaction can coexist; strong belonging can coexist with different cognitive abilities; low performance can reflect mismatch or skill gap. Avoid treating comfort as sufficient adaptation or effort as proof of impairment. Fit is context-specific and may change over time. Compare subjective and observed match without treating either as infallible. 
Illustrative counterexample: two workplaces each have demands equal to abilities on a hypothetical 0–10 scale (P=2,E=2 vs P=8,E=8); same difference zero does not establish equal outcomes. P=8,E=4 and P=4,E=8 both absolute gap4 but distinct surplus/shortfall, different predictions and supports. A fit interaction requires comparison to main-effect alternatives (e.g. outcome = b0+b1P+b2E+b3P²+b4PE+b5E²), sufficient data, commensurate scales and appropriate model assumptions; do not estimate from anecdotes or demand symmetric optimum. 
Selection illustration: among people with high measured ability, joining a support group may depend on distress or identity salience. P(distress | high ability, member) need not equal P(distress | high ability); even observing high distress in such a sample is consistent with no population association. Similarly recruitment by awards cannot estimate underachievement. No personal probabilities from these examples.
Practical sequence: define desired outcome and voluntary goal; record observed task, relationship/context, subjective meaning, resources, role and chronology; distinguish independent ability/identity/fit evidence; compare competing models (skill/training, demands/supplies, values, discrimination, social opportunities, independent difficulties, selection/recollection); state distinguishable predictions before new data; choose low-risk context change or observation with consent, compare across settings/occasions, record tradeoffs and alternative causes; revise affected claim only. No forced disclosure, risky life change or diagnostic labeling based on these heuristics. GFT-07 output/recognition separation, GFT-08.1 age/cohort and GFT-08.2 profile vs deficit retained. Need for support does not depend on verifying exceptional IQ.
