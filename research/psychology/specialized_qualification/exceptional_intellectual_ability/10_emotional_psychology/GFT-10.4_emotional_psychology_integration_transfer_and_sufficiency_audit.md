## Integrated reasoning gate
A practical heuristic, not identified causal law: elicitor/exposure -> detection and appraisal -> felt/expressed/physiological response over time -> differentiation/knowledge/interpretation -> regulatory goal, options, action and feedback -> outcome/cost. Feedback may change any step, and emotion generation/regulation overlap; no obligatory sequence. Separate within-person changes from between-person differences, and skill from preference, knowledge, opportunity and implementation. 
1. Establish measured component, baseline, exposure, intensity, duration, recovery, valence and functioning; do not conflate sensory detection with distress or subjective intensity with impairment.
2. Distinguish emotion vocabulary, own ratings/granularity, recognition of others, scenario knowledge, confidence and actual behavior. A single EI score or OE label not diagnostic, moral or ability inference.
3. Ask what outcome is being regulated: feeling, expression, environment, task or relationship. Check choices, enactment, contextual fit, costs and alternatives. Avoid universal strategy rule; maintenance of useful strategy can be flexible. 
4. Compare plausible simpler explanations: exposure, learning, fatigue, resources, role, appraisals, measurement and selection. Add more complex interactions only if distinct predictions supported. No fixed gifted latent mechanism; no causal inference from single observation or between-person correlation; no brain-region shortcut.
5. Use voluntary low-risk comparisons of ordinary contexts, measured separately across channels and repeated time points. Do not intentionally provoke distress or lower required safety standards. Revise with contradictory observations, preserve supported findings. Consider functional support regardless of IQ. Clinical symptoms and differential diagnosis require separate qualified assessment.
6. Evidence boundary: general adult laboratory tasks support specific associations, not universal real-life skill or extreme-tail specificity; youth and selected membership do not define population. Treat shared data and repeated reviews as dependent until checked.

## Internal integration tasks (new wholly invented cases)
A. Fictional aquarium guide rapidly detects pump hum, reports intense annoyance, uses precise emotion words and stays composed on tours. Supervisor attributes all to exceptional intelligence and assumes effective regulation. Compare technical listening experience, measured detection vs exposure burden, subjective experience vs expression, actual goal and recovery. Competing predictions: detection advantage should generalize if sensory threshold is lower; equipment-specific expertise predicts domain-local detection; suppression of expression need not reduce feeling. New: guide detects familiar pump changes but not unfamiliar tones better; noise reduction reduces annoyance and recovery time, public composure unchanged. Withdraw global gifted sensitivity and regulation-capacity claims; preserve local skill and context burden, no diagnosis or IQ estimate.
B. Fictional writing workshop participant has high scores on emotion scenarios, describes distinct emotions, and struggles to finish drafts after public criticism. Observer infers low EI or impaired prefrontal regulation despite knowledge. Compare actual feedback severity, appraisal, evaluation pressure, goal and task resources; predict response to clear revision criteria vs more conceptual instruction. New: private concrete feedback leads to completion despite strong brief disappointment; vocabulary and scenario scores unchanged. Revise from global regulatory deficit to context-specific performance and resource hypothesis; separate intensity, duration, outcome and knowledge, no unique neural mechanism.
C. Fictional university study samples high-score volunteers and comparison group; reports higher emotion vocabulary, similar trait regulation ratings and larger average neural contrast. Investigator claims universal superiority of emotional regulation. Separate group task contrast from correlation with individual success; compare selection, verbal knowledge, scoring, baseline and task difficulty. New: within each group neural contrast unrelated to repeated task success, and novel social tasks produce mixed outcomes. Withdraw common latent gifted advantage, retain observed score and contrast differences, no equivalence or absence of all neural relations; require cross-validated prediction of relevant outcome and independent comparison.
