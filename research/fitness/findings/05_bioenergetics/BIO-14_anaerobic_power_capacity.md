# BIO-14 — Anaerobic power and capacity

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-20**  
Область: 05 — Bioenergetics / Exercise Metabolism

## 0. Core decision

Anaerobic power, anaerobic capacity and repeated-bout ability are related but distinct performance/bioenergetic constructs.

Project rules:

- **anaerobic ≠ oxygen absent**;
- **mechanical power ≠ ATP-production rate**;
- **power ≠ capacity**;
- **single-bout output ≠ repeated-bout ability**;
- **test output ≠ direct pathway flux**.

---

## 1. Anaerobic power

Trainer-level definition:

**anaerobic power = ability to produce very high external power in a task where non-oxidative ATP provision is a major contributor.**

It is:

- task specific;
- mode specific;
- mechanically and neuromuscularly influenced;
- metabolically mixed.

It is not:

- direct PCr concentration;
- direct phosphagen ATP flux;
- a universal mode-independent trait.

---

## 2. Mechanical power vs metabolic power

External mechanical power depends on:

- force;
- velocity;
- acceleration;
- technique;
- body size;
- mechanical efficiency;
- test mechanics.

Metabolic ATP-production rate is internal biochemical flux.

Project rule:

**mechanical power ≠ metabolic ATP-production rate.**

---

## 3. Anaerobic capacity

Trainer-level definition:

**anaerobic capacity = task- and method-defined ability to sustain or accumulate high-intensity work while non-oxidative ATP provision is substantial.**

It is not one literal directly measurable biochemical tank.

Why:

- mechanical work ≠ metabolic energy;
- oxidative contribution remains non-zero;
- measurement is protocol/method dependent;
- performance depends on mechanics and fatigue tolerance.

Project rule:

**anaerobic capacity is an integrated task-defined capability, not a directly visible single reservoir.**

---

## 4. Power vs capacity

Power emphasizes:

- rate/output.

Capacity emphasizes:

- amount/sustainability across the defined task.

A person can show:

- high peak power with poor maintenance;
- lower peak power with better maintenance;
- similar mean power with different peak/decrement profiles.

Project rule:

**power and capacity are non-equivalent.**

---

## 5. Peak power

Peak power is:

- the highest measured external power over a defined sampling/averaging window.

It depends on:

- mode;
- test resistance/load;
- cadence/velocity;
- acceleration;
- sampling;
- technique;
- familiarization;
- body size.

Project rule:

**peak power is an operational performance output, not direct phosphagen biochemistry.**

---

## 6. Mean power

Mean power is:

- average external power over the specified test interval.

It reflects:

- initial output;
- output maintenance;
- decrement;
- pacing;
- load;
- duration;
- technique.

Project rule:

**mean power is not a direct biochemical measurement of anaerobic capacity or glycolytic ATP flux.**

---

## 7. Peak ≠ phosphagen / mean ≠ glycolytic

Reject:

- peak power = “alactic/phosphagen system”;
- mean power = “lactic/glycolytic system”;
- anaerobic capacity = glycolysis.

Reason:

- phosphagen, glycolytic and oxidative pathways overlap;
- contribution changes continuously;
- external power does not directly measure pathway flux.

Project rule:

**performance constructs and metabolic pathways are related but not interchangeable taxonomies.**

---

## 8. Fatigue / decrement

Power decline across a test can reflect:

- high initial power;
- PCr/metabolite/ion state;
- neuromuscular fatigue;
- E–C changes;
- pacing;
- technique;
- recovery state.

Project rule:

**fatigue/decrement is descriptive performance information, not a single physiological mechanism.**

A low percentage decrement does not automatically mean superior fitness because a modest first-bout output can reduce the apparent decrement.

---

## 9. Repeated-bout performance

Repeated-bout ability means reproducing high output across multiple efforts.

It depends on:

- first-bout performance;
- work duration;
- recovery duration/mode;
- PCr resynthesis;
- residual Pi/pH/ion state;
- oxidative recovery;
- glycolytic redistribution;
- neuromuscular fatigue;
- mode and technique.

Project rule:

**repeated-bout ability ≠ single-bout anaerobic power repeated.**

---

## 10. Repeated high-intensity work is mixed-metabolic

Across repeated sprints:

- PCr can remain incompletely restored;
- glycolytic contribution changes;
- oxidative contribution supports ongoing work and recovery;
- performance can decline despite maximal intent.

Project rule:

**repeated-sprint ability is not purely anaerobic capacity.**

---

## 11. Recovery between efforts

Recovery is metabolically active.

Oxidative ATP supports:

- PCr resynthesis;
- ATP-dependent homeostasis;
- restoration processes.

Project rule:

**recovery is part of repeated-bout performance, not dead time.**

---

## 12. PCr recovery

PCr recovery is predominantly oxidative and depends on:

- prior depletion;
- pH;
- O2 availability/perfusion;
- muscle;
- protocol;
- individual state.

Project rule:

**no fixed rest duration maps to one universal %PCr restoration.**

---

## 13. PCr recovery ≠ complete readiness

Even when PCr has substantially recovered, later performance can still be limited by:

- Pi;
- ions;
- pH;
- E–C function;
- neural fatigue;
- mechanics;
- motivation.

Project rule:

**PCr recovery contributes to repeated-bout recovery but does not certify complete readiness.**

---

## 14. Longer rest

Longer rest generally permits:

- more PCr restoration;
- greater metabolite/ion recovery;
- better opportunity to reproduce high output.

But there is no universal “full recovery” time.

Project rule:

**longer rest generally supports output quality, not guaranteed complete recovery.**

---

## 15. Recovery mode

Recovery mode is part of the protocol and can change the internal state before the next effort.

BIO-14 does not retain one universal active-vs-passive recovery advantage.

**Информация не проверена** for current 2025–2026 protocol-specific active-vs-passive recovery review evidence in this PASS.

Exact recovery-mode evidence/prescription remains TST/PRO.

---

## 16. Phosphagen relationship

Phosphagen contribution is especially important for:

- abrupt high ATP demand;
- brief maximal output;
- early high-rate work.

But:

- glycolysis is active early;
- oxidative ATP is active early;
- no phosphagen-only time window is retained.

Project rule:

**brief high-power task with substantial phosphagen contribution ≠ phosphagen-only task.**

---

## 17. Glycolytic relationship

Glycolytic contribution can rise during:

- sustained high ATP turnover;
- longer intense bouts;
- incomplete-recovery repeated work.

But:

- blood lactate ≠ glycolytic ATP flux;
- high lactate ≠ anaerobic capacity;
- glycolytic contribution ≠ glycolysis-only exercise.

Project rule:

**glycolytic contribution does not map one-to-one to mean power or capacity.**

---

## 18. Oxidative support

Oxidative ATP production remains active during high-intensity work and is important during recovery.

Project rule:

**aerobic support can improve repeated high-intensity performance without making the task aerobic-only.**

---

## 19. High power and lactate

A brief high-power effort can occur without large systemic lactate accumulation.

Project rule:

**high anaerobic power does not require high blood lactate.**

Likewise:

**high blood lactate does not prove high anaerobic power or capacity.**

---

## 20. Anaerobic fitness is multidimensional

Relevant attributes can include:

- peak power;
- acceleration;
- short-bout mean power;
- total work;
- repeatability;
- fatigue resistance;
- recovery between efforts.

Project rule:

**anaerobic fitness is not one scalar trait.**

## 22. Wingate-type tests

Wingate-type all-out cycle tests can produce operational outputs such as:

- peak power;
- mean power;
- total work;
- decrement.

They do not directly measure:

- PCr concentration;
- phosphagen ATP flux;
- glycolytic ATP flux;
- exact pathway split;
- universal anaerobic capacity.

Project rule:

**Wingate-type performance ≠ direct biochemical anaerobic-capacity measurement.**

MAOD/oxygen-deficit approaches are retained from BIO-08 as indirect/model-derived estimates of anaerobic contribution.

Project rule:

**MAOD-type estimate ≠ direct measurement of a literal anaerobic-energy tank.**

Protocol-specific validity/reliability remains TST-owned.

---

## 24. PCr–La–O2 models

PCr–La–O2 approaches can estimate pathway components under model assumptions.

Project rule:

**model-derived pathway partition ≠ direct simultaneous in-vivo ATP-flux measurement.**

Blood lactate remains an indirect input rather than direct glycolytic ATP measurement.

---

## 25. Mechanical work vs metabolic energy

Mechanical work is external task output.

Metabolic energy is internal biochemical turnover.

Project rule:

**mechanical work ≠ metabolic energy.**

---

## 26. Mode specificity

Cycling, running, rowing, jumping and resisted sprinting differ in:

- mechanics;
- muscle recruitment;
- skill;
- inertia/load;
- contraction pattern.

Project rule:

**test mode is part of the construct.**

A cycle score does not automatically equal running/sport power.

---

## 27. Test duration

Changing test duration changes what the test emphasizes.

Very short tests emphasize:

- peak rate/output.

Longer all-out tests increasingly include:

- output maintenance;
- fatigue;
- glycolytic contribution;
- oxidative contribution.

Project rule:

**test duration changes construct emphasis.**

---

## 28. Test load / cadence / sampling

Peak and mean power can change with:

- resistance/load;
- cadence;
- acceleration;
- sampling/averaging.

Project rule:

**test mechanics are not neutral details.**

Exact protocol optimization remains TST.

---

## 29. Familiarization and pacing

Test scores can improve through:

- familiarization;
- technique;
- pacing;
- acceleration strategy.

Project rule:

**test score improvement ≠ automatically metabolic adaptation.**

---

## 30. Scaling

Absolute W and normalized/scaled outputs answer different questions.

Project rule:

**absolute power ≠ relative/scaled power.**

No universal scaling method is retained in BIO-14.

Formal scaling remains RES/TST.

## 32. Trainer-level implications

1. Separate peak output, sustained output and repeatability.
2. Treat peak power as performance, not direct PCr measurement.
3. Treat mean power as interval performance, not direct glycolytic capacity.
4. Do not use blood lactate as an anaerobic-capacity score.
5. Do not diagnose one fatigue mechanism from decrement.
6. Evaluate repeated-bout profiles with both absolute output and maintenance.
7. Recognize oxidative recovery as part of repeated high-intensity performance.
8. Use mode-specific outputs when transfer matters.
9. Interpret test duration/load/familiarization as part of the result.
10. Leave formal test validity/reliability/standardization to TST.
11. Exact training prescription remains PRO.

### HIGH

- anaerobic power and capacity are distinct;
- mechanical power is not ATP-production rate;
- peak and mean power are distinct;
- peak power is not direct phosphagen measurement;
- mean power is not direct glycolytic-capacity measurement;
- high-intensity work remains metabolically mixed;
