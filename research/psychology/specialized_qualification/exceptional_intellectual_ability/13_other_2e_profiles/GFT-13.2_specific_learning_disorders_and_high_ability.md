## Practical inference
1. Separate measured general ability/index/talent, age-standardized skill, and functional impact. Norms, precision, language/orthography, instruction, aids, speed and effort. Large gap warrants investigating needs, not diagnosis. Average composite can conceal specific difficulty or documented prior impairment, not presumed from IQ. Low score alone also insufficient.
2. Separate accuracy/rate/comprehension, spelling/composition/transcription, number facts/calculation/reasoning. Check developmental history, opportunities, multilingual exposure, ADHD, anxiety, sleep, sensory/motor factors and acquired loss. Oral strength and weak spelling may coexist without causal relation. Preserve aids.
3. Compare developmental skill difficulty, practice/instruction, language, attention, motor/anxiety, curriculum mismatch and combinations. Difficulties confined to unfamiliar written language plus established native-language skill and learning response weaken generalized developmental account. Persistent age-relative decoding/fluency difficulty and childhood history warrant qualified assessment, not diagnosis here.
4. Separate alternate-route compensation, environmental accommodation, underlying skill improvement, task selection and lower severity. Higher output with higher IQ is not causal compensation; compare severity, longitudinal design and actual strategies. Never withdraw aids to test mechanism. Support needs independent of diagnosis. No universal profile.
5. Direct measured high-general-IQ adult evidence insufficient; high-domain adult study indirect for general giftedness; general adults and pediatric data explicitly indirect. Do not equate PIQ/VIQ/GAI with FSIQ, transfer reading mechanisms to math/writing or derive prevalence from selected samples.
