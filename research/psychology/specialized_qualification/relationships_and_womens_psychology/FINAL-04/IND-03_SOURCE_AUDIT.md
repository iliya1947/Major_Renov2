## 2. Corrections and substantive extensions

R1 — Conditional transfer and mathematical terminology. Original IND-03.1 avoids saying group evidence is worthless but does not explicitly adjudicate the published dispute on ergodicity. Adolf & Fried 2019 https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1818675116 (original indexed letter) argue sufficiency is not necessity for particular cross-level generalizations; Fisher et al. reply https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1820221116 (indexed original reply/institutional record) acknowledges graded transfer and conditional equivalence, retains strong requirement for claims of full group-to-individual equivalence. Subsequent Fisher et al. 2024 What ergodicity means for you https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcn.2024.101406 (indexed original text) distinguishes mathematical ergodicity from equality of selected empirical statistics; homogeneity alone is insufficient and equality of moments after conditioning does not demonstrate mathematical ergodicity. Do not infer the process is ergodic solely from approximate group/person correlations; do not require strict ergodicity to use a group estimate as a qualified prior for one defined target. Transfer is estimand-, measure-, population-, time- and context-specific; assess plausible moderators, measurement equivalence and individual evidence. Distinguish partial conditional generalization from full process equivalence. Root cause: insufficient explicit treatment of the competing mathematical interpretation. Retest: preserves earlier prohibition on automatic transfer without excluding useful group evidence. Applies to later WOM/SOC/REL/SYS sections as a rule, not their completed source audit.

R2 — Outcome and selection limits in temporal prediction. Beck/Jackson use self-reported event labels, not independent verified behavior; 104/208 retained, based in part on outcome variability. AUC/accuracy therefore apply to outcome-eligible students and the defined label/horizon, not all enrolled adults. Accurate prediction of a report is not accurate identification of its unique cause or hidden intention. Six feature-set configurations, three model families and additional post-preregistration outcomes require separating tuned training decisions from exploratory comparisons of test performance. Published text does not establish that all reported best test configurations were frozen before inspection; no assertion of confirmed leakage. Compare to training-only base rate and last available outcome, test fixed final pipeline on untouched time window if selection used test outcomes. Explicitly register shared data across earlier publications. Root cause: original note records exclusions and deviations but omits sample flow, dependence and precise outcome boundary. Retest source methods: 208-9-85-10=104; first75/last25 and outcome lag confirmed; no general forecast claim added.

R3 — Dense samples vs resampled data: dependence, potential leakage and metric interpretation. Precision experience sampling article methods distinguish (i) class balancing with SMOTE within training folds, which is appropriate in principle, and (ii) sevenfold augmentation of sparse dataset before repeating the classification approach; the stated order raises risk of training/test leakage through synthetic neighbors derived from real test observations or synthetic labels in evaluation. Code not inspected, so actual leakage is unconfirmed. Primary 100 random partitions reuse the same participants and observations; Wilcoxon comparison across iterations is not independent population replication, uncertainty about the significance test remains. Choice of k=25 by trial and error is reported, nested tuning not established. Standard unadjusted balanced accuracy is mean class recall, range [0,1], not [-1,1] as described in article (adjusted variant differs; actual implementation not verified). Cross-participant vs person-specific models evaluated against concurrent reported thought dimensions. Require genuine, untouched held-out observations, augmentation and feature selection exclusively inside each training split, session/person separation matched to target, cluster-aware uncertainty and transparent model-selection procedure. Root cause: original IND-03.1 identifies repeated-split dependence but misses distinction between two oversampling stages and metric ambiguity. Retest: both groups have 1715 real probes, augmented records cannot increase independent real observations. Retain descriptive heterogeneous results; withhold strong causal superiority, universal classifier validity and prospective forecasts.

R4 — Denominators, coverage, baseline and inconsistencies in reports. Phanord confirms negative fold-level R2 even with positive dataset-level R2; fold means, mean participant scores and pooled scores are separate estimands. Source terminology about pooling in section3.2 differs from section3.4/figure4 (mean fold vs pooled); use explicit Table4 and section3.4 definitions; do not label these values individual mean R2 without verifying calculation. Negative test R2 relative to test-set mean is not itself performance against a deployable training-mean predictor; authors also give actual intercept-only benchmarks. Model3 intercept-only fold R2 -.081 to -.038 vs feature model -.163 to -.061; aggregate ranges alone do not give exact paired differences. Report actual comparator, same folds and same coverage. In Langener Table4, only 45 of 150 simulated participants had nonconstant held-out binary outcomes permitting within-person AUC in moving window; 30% coverage. Real Qwantify example includes 19/20 selected participants in moving-window AUC, while overall sample already selected on >=150 days; 95% conditional coverage, not coverage of all app users. Table7 reports within-person AUC .54 vs baseline .58, but adjacent text says .47 vs .58. Exact model value in that comparison unresolved; do not silently reconcile. Table4 independently lists .54; both .47 and .54 below .58. Report AUC only when defined, number eligible/all target units and exclusions; for constant outcomes use other appropriate scoring (e.g., Brier/log loss for probabilities) without treating undefined AUC as zero or successful prediction. Compare same prediction target, availability and time horizon. Distinguish time-varying baseline from one constant score per individual; baseline moving across windows can have AUC other than .5. AUC is discrimination, not calibration or percentage correct. Root cause: original synthesis correctly states baseline principle but does not include explicit coverage/denominator and discrepancy rules. Retest quantitative values in section3; baseline finding robust to unresolved .47/.54. Retain actual quantitative comparator, not general preference for simple models.

R5 — Separate explanatory hypotheses, predictive performance and process recovery. IND-03.3 says complexity requires evidence. Clarify that explanatory complexity may be useful for organizing distinct observations or generating discriminating hypotheses even without a fitted predictor; it earns no numerical predictive credit merely for coherence. Distinct timescales, insufficient sampling and wrong model class may make the underlying mechanisms unidentifiable even with a good forecast. Fechtelpeter independent evaluation supports specific nonlinear model advantages for measured outcomes, but simulated intervention effects remain hypotheses; no claim that changing a central node causes improved real outcomes. Frumkin's discrepancy between reported expected relations and fitted networks does not decide which one represents causal truth. Zhang's BIC result supports distinguishing selection for process recovery from performance in a deployment regime. Operational update: choose estimand first; test measurement, alternative models and causal identifiability separately. No automatic preference for either simpler or more complex models. Prior FINAL-04 R3 terminology continues to apply: replace 'calibrated verbal uncertainty' with evidence-bounded verbal confidence unless calibration actually measured. No duplicate correction counted. Retest: consistent with IND-01 structural-vs-level change and IND-02 perceived-vs-observed outcome distinction.

## 4. Operational addendum
For quantitative individual forecast require: defined observable target and data channel; timestamp of availability for every predictor; intended population and known-vs-new-person distinction; horizon and eligible opportunities; fixed preprocessing/tuning/augmentation inside training; comparator computed exclusively using information available at forecast time; disjoint test data respecting episode/person/time dependencies; coverage including undefined metrics and missing/unresolved outcomes; per-person and pooled metrics reported separately; calibration for probabilistic forecasts; uncertainty and drift checks. Where causal inference is intended, require an appropriate identification argument, not only model prediction or temporal precedence. For qualitative work, use typed and versioned propositions, evidence dependencies, alternative explanations, disconfirming observations and bounded confidence; freeze observable forecasts before outcome, retain errors and unknowns, propagate verified corrections, preserve explicit choice and boundary. Do not force a statistical model from insufficient observations or use unnecessary complexity as a proxy for insight.
