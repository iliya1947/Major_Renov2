# 2. Canonical scientific target

Standalone hermeneutic inquiry asks:

**How can a deeper, contextually and historically situated understanding of meaning emerge through interpretive dialogue between researcher, participants/texts and the researcher's prior horizon?**

Its target is **understanding through interpretation**, not automatically:

- phenomenological essence/lifeworld structure;
- a list of perceptions;
- a narrative plot;
- discourse-as-social-action;
- a causal process model.

### Canonical project definition

> Explicit philosophical hermeneutic inquiry is a distinct interpretive route when the research question and analysis are organized around Gadamerian/Ricoeurian hermeneutic principles and the study does not claim phenomenological methodology as its parent design.

---

# 3. Critical caveat: Gadamer did not prescribe a research recipe

Gadamer's philosophical hermeneutics is fundamentally a philosophy of understanding, not a cookbook method.

Walsh and Austgard both emphasize this distinction.

Fleming, Gaidys and Robb therefore proposed a **Gadamerian-based research method** for empirical health research rather than claiming that Gadamer himself wrote a five-step research procedure.

### Project rule

Never write:

> “Gadamer's five-step method”

as if those five steps were authored by Gadamer.

Correct:

> “Fleming et al.'s five-step Gadamerian-based empirical research adaptation.”

# 4. Standalone hermeneutics vs hermeneutic phenomenology

This is the key taxonomy boundary.

## Hermeneutic phenomenology

Primary target:
- lived experience;
- lifeworld;
- existential/embodied meaning;
- phenomenological interpretation.

Examples:
- Heideggerian phenomenology;
- van Manen;
- other explicit hermeneutic-phenomenological lineages.

## Standalone philosophical/Gadamerian hermeneutic inquiry

Primary target:
- interpretive understanding;
- dialogue;
- historically situated meaning;
- practice/text/action;
- changing pre-understanding;
- horizon transformation.

It may study:
- clinical decisions;
- professional practice;
- interaction;
- texts;
- experience;

without claiming that the phenomenological structure of lived experience is the research object.

### Critical rule

“Interpretive” + “Gadamer” does not automatically mean hermeneutic phenomenology.

Check the actual scientific target and declared methodology.

---

# 5. Hermeneutics is broader than Gadamer

Hermeneutic traditions include, among others:

- Heidegger;
- Gadamer;
- Ricoeur;
- critical hermeneutics.

This PASS focuses primarily on **Gadamerian standalone inquiry** because it is the clearest blocker identified by PASS-06D14.

### Project rule

Record the exact hermeneutic lineage.

Do not collapse:

- Gadamerian;
- Ricoeurian;
- Heideggerian;
- hermeneutic phenomenological;

approaches into one generic label.

# 6. Pre-understanding / prejudice

In Gadamerian hermeneutics, the interpreter never begins from a view from nowhere.

Understanding begins from historically situated:

- assumptions;
- traditions;
- professional knowledge;
- prior experience;
- language;
- expectations.

Gadamerian **prejudice** is not simply a negative bias.

It means a pre-judgment or pre-understanding that makes interpretation possible while also requiring examination.

### Project rule

Researcher prior knowledge is not to be “eliminated.”

It should be:

1. made visible where relevant;
2. put at risk;
3. tested through dialogue/text;
4. allowed to change.

---

# 7. Bracketing boundary

Standalone Gadamerian inquiry does not share the Husserlian goal of suspending all pre-understanding.

A researcher can deliberately reflect on assumptions, but complete neutrality is neither achievable nor the epistemic aim.

### Better wording

> The researcher identified and critically examined pre-understandings.

Avoid:

> The researcher bracketed away all bias.

---

# 8. Horizon

A **horizon** is the historically/culturally situated range of understanding available from one's position.

Relevant researcher horizon can include:

- disciplinary training;
- professional practice;
- cultural context;
- theoretical commitments;
- personal history.

Participant/text also comes from its own horizon.

### Implication

Understanding is relational and situated.

---

# 9. Fusion of horizons

A central Gadamerian concept is **fusion of horizons**.

Interpretation involves an encounter between:

- the interpreter's current horizon;
- the participant/text/tradition's horizon.

Through genuine engagement, the researcher's prior understanding may be:

- challenged;
- expanded;
- reconfigured.

### Critical rule

Fusion of horizons is not:

- simple agreement;
- consensus;
- adoption of the participant's view;
- averaging two perspectives.

It is a transformed understanding emerging through interpretive encounter.

---

# 10. Fusion is not necessarily a final endpoint

Current 2026 Gadamerian caring-science work cautions against treating fusion of horizons as a finalized endpoint.

Interpretation remains:

- open;
- historically situated;
- revisable.

### Project rule

Use:

> provisional/enlarged understanding

rather than:

> final true interpretation.

---

# 11. Hermeneutic circle

The hermeneutic circle is the iterative movement between:

- part ↔ whole;
- prior understanding ↔ new material;
- question ↔ answer;
- interpretation ↔ reconsideration.

### Example

Researcher reads one physiotherapist statement about exercise adherence.

Interpretation changes when it is considered in relation to:

- the whole interview;
- observation of actual practice;
- the study context;
- other participants;
- prior assumptions.

Then the whole may be re-read in light of the part.

### Critical rule

Hermeneutic circle ≠ mechanical coding loop.

It is an epistemic movement of understanding.

---

# 12. Dialogue as condition of understanding

Gadamerian inquiry treats understanding as dialogical.

Dialogue requires openness to the possibility that:

> the other/text may be right in a way that changes my prior understanding.

This differs from an interview used merely to extract fixed information.

### Project implication

A hermeneutic interview should allow:

- follow-up;
- clarification;
- challenge;
- revisiting assumptions;
- genuine responsiveness.

---

# 13. Question–answer structure

Gadamer places importance on the structure of question and answer.

Understanding a text/account involves asking:

- What question is this statement answering?
- What issue makes this answer meaningful?
- What assumptions are embedded in my question?
- Does the response force the question itself to change?

### Rule

Research questions and interview prompts can evolve interpretively rather than functioning only as fixed survey items.

---

# 14. Hermeneutic interviewing

Geanellos developed hermeneutic interviewing around:

- tradition;
- dialectics of interpretation;
- question–answer dialogue.

### Critical boundary

Hermeneutic interviewing is a data-generation approach.

It does not by itself establish a complete hermeneutic study unless the analytic/epistemic architecture is also hermeneutic.

---

# 15. Dialogue with participants vs dialogue with text

Fleming et al.'s empirical adaptation distinguishes:

1. understanding through dialogue with participants;
2. understanding through dialogue with the resulting text.

These are not identical stages.

### Participant dialogue

Interactive meaning formation.

### Text dialogue

Repeated interpretive engagement with the transcript/material after the encounter.

### Rule

A transcript is not treated as a neutral container of facts.

It is interpreted in relation to:

- context;
- whole;
- parts;
- researcher pre-understanding.

---

# 16. Fleming et al. five-step empirical adaptation

Fleming et al. propose:

1. decide upon a question;
2. identify pre-understandings;
3. gain understanding through dialogue with participants;
4. gain understanding through dialogue with text;
5. establish trustworthiness.

### Critical caveat

These are a practical empirical translation of Gadamerian principles.

They are **not universal requirements for every philosophical-hermeneutic study**.

---

# 17. Austgard four-step methodological adaptation

Austgard proposed another methodological organization of Gadamerian hermeneutics for text interpretation.

The existence of multiple empirical adaptations reinforces:

> philosophical hermeneutics does not supply one universal procedural algorithm.

### Project rule

Extract the specific empirical hermeneutic procedure used.

Do not infer one standardized method from the word “Gadamerian.”

---

# 18. Understanding, interpretation and application

Gadamerian tradition treats:

- understanding;
- interpretation;
- application;

as deeply interrelated rather than fully separable technical steps.

### Research implication

A new understanding matters partly because it is applied to the interpreter's current question/context.

### Fitness/clinical example

Understanding exercise prescription is not merely describing what clinicians say.

It can change how:

- partnership;
- adherence;
- patient autonomy;
- evidence;
- professional judgment;

are understood in practice.

---

# 19. Language

Language is not simply a transport channel carrying pre-existing meaning.

In hermeneutic philosophy, understanding occurs through language.

### Implication

Meaning is:

- articulated;
- negotiated;
- reconfigured;

through dialogue and interpretation.

### Boundary

This does not make hermeneutic inquiry the same as discourse analysis.

Hermeneutics asks about **understanding/meaning**.

DP/CDA/FDA ask different questions about:

- discourse action;
- repertoires;
- subject positions;
- ideology;
- power.

---

# 20. Tradition and historical situatedness

Understanding occurs within traditions.

Researchers and participants inherit:

- professional norms;
- cultural assumptions;
- historical practices;
- conceptual language.

### Exercise example

What counts as:

- compliance;
- adherence;
- “good exercise”;
- patient participation;

is partly shaped by professional traditions in physiotherapy.

### Rule

Hermeneutic interpretation should make relevant historical/professional horizons explicit rather than treating meaning as context-free.

---

# 21. Sampling

There is no single Gadamerian sample-size formula.

Sampling should ensure sufficient access to:

- the interpretive problem;
- relevant experiences/practices/texts;
- contrasting horizons where useful.

Possible units:

- participants;
- practitioners;
- patient–clinician encounters;
- observations;
- documents;
- texts.

### Project rule

Sample adequacy is tied to:

- interpretive depth;
- question;
- dialogical richness;
- contextual coverage.

Not to universal N or automatic saturation.

---

# 22. Saturation boundary

Generic “no new themes” saturation is not a defining Gadamerian criterion.

Because interpretation is historically open and revisable, the aim is not to exhaust all possible meaning.

### Better adequacy language

- interpretive depth;
- sufficient dialogue;
- contextual resonance;
- reasonable interpretive closure for the present question.

### Rule

If a study reports saturation, record its operational meaning rather than assuming it is philosophically required.

---

# 23. Data sources

Standalone hermeneutic inquiry may use:

- interviews;
- observations;
- field notes;
- documents;
- professional texts;
- reflective journals;
- clinical encounters;
- multiple textual sources.

### Critical rule

Interview-only data are possible.

But interview = collection method, not methodology.

---

# 24. Observation + interview hermeneutic architecture

A strong practice-oriented design can combine:

- observation of what people do;
- brief post-event dialogue;
- later in-depth interview.

This creates multiple interpretive materials around the same practice.

### Direct physiotherapy example

Stenner et al.:

- 8 physiotherapists;
- 24 practice observations;
- post-observation brief interviews;
- later in-depth interviews;
- iterative hermeneutic interpretation.

---

# 25. Thematic analysis can sit inside hermeneutic inquiry

Stenner's exercise-prescription research used thematic analysis **guided by Gadamerian hermeneutics**.

### Critical rule

Thematic analysis is the analysis technique.

Philosophical hermeneutics is the epistemic/methodological orientation.

Correct extraction:

> philosophical hermeneutic qualitative study + thematic analytic procedure.

Not:

> thematic analysis = methodology.

---

# 26. Interpretation is not unrestricted subjectivity

Hermeneutics rejects neutral interpretation, but it does not permit arbitrary interpretation.

Interpretive claims should be justified through:

- textual/data evidence;
- whole–part coherence;
- contextual plausibility;
- openness to challenge;
- reflexive examination;
- alternative readings.

### Debesay principle

Hermeneutic understanding can be **reasonable and defensible** without claiming absolute certainty.

---

# 27. Trustworthiness: classic empirical approach

Fleming et al. included “establishing trustworthiness” as a fifth empirical step.

Possible supports:

- transparent research decisions;
- reflexive pre-understanding;
- evidence excerpts;
- return to participants where coherent;
- repeated whole–part analysis;
- explanation of interpretive changes.

### Critical rule

Trustworthiness should not be imported as a positivist reliability exercise.

---

# 28. DORR framework, 2026

Estman's 2026 framework proposes four hermeneutically aligned quality dimensions:

1. **Depth**;
2. **Openness**;
3. **Resonance**;
4. **Reflexivity**.

## Depth

Interpretation goes beyond superficial description while remaining grounded.

## Openness

Researcher remains open to otherness, challenge and changed understanding.

## Resonance

Interpretation is contextually meaningful and recognizable without pretending universal replication.

## Reflexivity

Researcher demonstrates accountability for interpretive positioning and change.

### Project rule

DORR is a useful current framework, not a universal mandatory checklist.

---

# 29. Member checking boundary

Hermeneutic work can involve returning interpretations to participants.

But this should not be treated as:

> participant certifies the one correct meaning.

Participant response can:

- challenge;
- deepen;
- redirect;
- expand;

the interpretive dialogue.

### Rule

This aligns better with **member reflection/dialogue** than simple validation.

Consistent with PASS-06D12.

---

# 30. Consensus boundary

Different researchers can bring different pre-understandings and therefore reach different defensible interpretations.

Stenner's physiotherapy work explicitly notes that independent analysis and consensus were not treated as philosophically necessary.

### Critical rule

Intercoder consensus is not a universal quality requirement for philosophical hermeneutics.

---

# 31. Hermeneutic inquiry vs Qualitative Description

## QD

Target:
- direct comprehensive description.

## Hermeneutic inquiry

Target:
- deeper transformed understanding through contextual interpretation.

### Example

QD:
> What barriers do patients report when prescribed exercise?

Hermeneutic:
> How should we understand the meaning of participation, professional authority and patient partnership in exercise-prescription encounters?

---

# 32. Hermeneutic inquiry vs narrative inquiry

Narrative inquiry asks:

- how stories are organized;
- plot;
- temporality;
- identity;
- tellability.

Hermeneutic inquiry may interpret a narrative as text, but story form need not be its scientific object.

### Rule

Narrative material ≠ narrative methodology.

---

# 33. Hermeneutic inquiry vs discourse analysis

## Hermeneutic inquiry

Seeks deep understanding of meaning in historically/contextually situated dialogue/text.

## Discursive psychology

Asks what talk accomplishes in situated action.

## FDA/CDA

Ask how discourses construct subjects/objects and power relations.

### Critical rule

Hermeneutic interpretation should not be relabelled discourse analysis merely because it analyzes language.

---

# 34. Hermeneutic inquiry vs case study

A bounded clinical service could be studied with case-study architecture and hermeneutic analysis.

Possible combined record:

case study
+
Gadamerian hermeneutic interpretation.

### Rule

Bounded case architecture and hermeneutic interpretive orientation are separate layers.

---

# 35. Hermeneutic inquiry vs grounded theory

Hermeneutic inquiry does not require:

- theoretical sampling;
- constant comparison as formal architecture;
- core category;
- theory generation;
- theoretical saturation.

### Rule

Deep interpretation ≠ grounded theory.

---

# 36. Hermeneutic inquiry vs IPA

IPA is:

- phenomenological;
- hermeneutic;
- idiographic.

Standalone philosophical hermeneutic inquiry need not be:

- phenomenological;
- IPA;
- strongly idiographic.

### Critical rule

“Double interpretation” language does not automatically make a study IPA.

---

# 37. Claims hermeneutic inquiry can support

Hermeneutic inquiry can strongly support claims about:

- contextual meaning;
- professional/practical understanding;
- how assumptions shape interpretation;
- relational understanding;
- meaning of actions/practices/texts;
- tensions between inherited tradition and current practice;
- transformed understanding of a phenomenon.

It can produce:

- interpretive themes;
- conceptual understanding;
- practice insight;
- critique of assumptions.

---

# 38. Claims it cannot establish by itself

Standalone hermeneutic inquiry does not by itself establish:

- population prevalence;
- causal treatment effect;
- incidence;
- diagnostic accuracy;
- prediction performance;
- biological mechanism;
- causal mediation.

### Additional boundary

Interpretive plausibility ≠ empirical causal proof.

A meaningful explanation of why exercise prescription becomes compliance-oriented does not estimate the causal effect of clinician communication style on adherence.

---

# 39. Physiotherapy exercise-prescription exemplar

Stenner et al. studied exercise prescription for nonspecific chronic low-back pain using a philosophical hermeneutic approach.

Research target:

> how shared decision making and patient partnership were addressed in exercise prescription.

Data included:

- direct observations;
- brief post-observation interviews;
- later in-depth interviews.

Findings showed:

- unequal opportunities for patient participation;
- professional assumptions shaping decisions;
- tension between evidence and everyday practice;
- compliance orientation rather than concordance.

### Methodological lesson

This is a direct fitness/rehabilitation example of philosophical hermeneutic inquiry that is not best reduced to:

> “phenomenology because interviews discussed experience.”

---

# 40. Patient exercise-prescription exemplar

A companion study interviewed eight patients offered exercise for chronic nonspecific low-back pain.

Target:

- treatment decision-making experiences;
- information/support needs;
- participation in exercise-prescription decisions.

Findings included:

- expectations ≠ needs;
- information necessary but insufficient;
- not every decision needed to be shared;
- desire for individual treatment.

### Project lesson

Hermeneutics can examine the meaning of:

- autonomy;
- participation;
- clinician authority;
- exercise prescription;

in rehabilitation practice.

---

# 41. Cardiorespiratory physiotherapy exemplar

A philosophical-hermeneutic study of 14 cardiorespiratory physiotherapists combined:

- observation of usual care;
- subsequent interviews;
- iterative interpretation.

It found clinical decision making to be:

- cyclic;
- evolving;
- flexible;
- social;
- collaborative.

### Fitness relevance

This supports use of hermeneutic inquiry for questions about:

- coach/practitioner judgment;
- exercise decision-making;
- professional reasoning;
- athlete/client partnership.

---

# 42. Sport boundary

Hermeneutic scholarship in philosophy of sport argues that sporting actions are meaningful phenomena embedded in:

- rules;
- culture;
- history;
- human interpretation.

### Project caution

Philosophical essays about sport are not automatically empirical qualitative research.

For the fitness evidence classifier:

> empirical hermeneutic study requires actual research material and an explicit interpretive methodology.

# 43. Potential fitness questions suited to standalone hermeneutic inquiry

Examples:

- How should we understand coaches' conceptions of athlete autonomy when prescribing training?
- How do practitioner pre-understandings shape interpretations of “good adherence”?
- What does “shared decision making” mean in individualized strength-program prescription?
- How are professional traditions brought into judgments about pain, effort and progression?
- How should conflicting athlete/coach meanings around recovery be interpreted?

### Boundary

If the target is:

> what does recovery feel like?

→ phenomenology.

If:

> how is “recovery” understood and negotiated within professional/practical horizons?

→ hermeneutic inquiry candidate.

# 45. Do-not-confuse matrix

| Do not confuse | Why |
|---|---|
| Gadamer's philosophy vs Fleming five-step method | empirical method was developed later by researchers |
| hermeneutics vs hermeneutic phenomenology | interpretation/understanding target need not be phenomenological |
| Gadamerian inquiry vs IPA | IPA is specifically phenomenological + idiographic |
| pre-understanding vs bad bias | pre-understanding makes interpretation possible |
| reflexivity vs elimination of pre-understanding | assumptions are examined, not erased |
| fusion of horizons vs agreement | transformed understanding ≠ consensus |
| fusion of horizons vs final truth | understanding remains historically open |
| hermeneutic circle vs coding loop | it is an epistemic part–whole interpretive movement |
| dialogue vs simple interview extraction | genuine openness may transform the question/interpreter |
| interview vs hermeneutic methodology | interview is a collection method |
| text vs neutral data container | text is interpreted in context/whole–part relation |
| thematic analysis vs parent hermeneutic orientation | analysis technique and methodology are separate |
| deep interpretation vs arbitrary subjectivity | claims require reasoned evidence/coherence |
| multiple analysts vs mandatory agreement | different defensible interpretations may remain |
| member feedback vs validation certificate | feedback can reopen interpretation |
| DORR vs universal checklist | current hermeneutic quality framework, not mandatory ritual |
| philosophy of sport vs empirical hermeneutic study | philosophical analysis alone is not primary qualitative research |
| interpreted meaning vs prevalence | qualitative interpretation does not estimate frequency |
| interpretive explanation vs causal effect | understanding ≠ causal identification |
