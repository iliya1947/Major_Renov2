# MOT-04 — Motivation integration and sufficiency audit: needs, incentives, value, effort, goals, state and measurement

Research scope: public scientific evidence and generic analytical methodology only. No individualized records.

## 1. Purpose and inputs

Synthesize and audit:
1. MOT-01 — motivational architecture: psychological needs, self-determination, incentive salience, expected value of control, opportunity cost and goals;
2. MOT-02 — reward probability, delay, risk, cognitive/physical effort, opportunity cost, task validity and parameter psychometrics;
3. MOT-03 — goal hierarchy, autonomous/controlled regulation, intention–action gap, implementation planning, persistence/disengagement and measurement.

The purpose is **sufficiency for reliable generic motivational reasoning**, not completeness of motivational science or a claim that every article cited in predecessors has undergone independent replication.

## 2. Central invariant: motivation is not a single intensity

Motivation is a family of interacting processes influencing action direction, energy, initiation, maintenance, and reallocation, in context.

Separate:
- need or deprived state;
- cue-triggered wanting;
- hedonic liking;
- subjective valuation;
- outcome probability and causal controllability;
- motivational regulation quality and autonomy;
- expected physical/cognitive effort;
- opportunity costs and competing options;
- goal intention/commitment;
- opportunity, skill and action control;
- observed behavior and performance.

**No observed action rate, effort score, questionnaire or single neural signal uniquely quantifies an individual's global motivation.**

## 3. The causal-level map

Proposed generic analytic sequence, not a unique neural circuit:

\`needs, goals, current state, context -> outcome/value and expectancy -> effort/alternatives -> goal selection and regulation quality -> implementation opportunity and control -> observed choice/action -> feedback -> reprioritization or learning\`

Dependencies are bidirectional: action changes beliefs, fatigue, feedback and future valuation. A plan may precede a choice in some settings. No universal fixed one-way stage sequence is asserted.

## 4. Learned value versus current motivational expression

LEA examines learned contingencies, representations and policies. MOT examines why available learned actions are currently worth selecting.

A response may be well learned but not currently valuable; a valuable goal may not be executable.

A change in willingness can reflect goal value, opportunity, internal state or motor/cognitive ability rather than new reward learning.

**LEA and MOT complement each other without collapsing into one mechanism.**

## 5. Physiological needs versus psychological need theories

Physiological homeostatic needs, subjective urges and SDT psychological needs are related but not interchangeable constructs.

SDT proposes autonomy, competence and relatedness as important psychosocial conditions; this does not imply three exclusively biological motivational substances, nor that all motives are reducible to those three.

Need satisfaction differs from active need frustration. Individual variation, contexts, age and culture constrain transfer.

## 6. Autonomous and controlled regulation

SDT regulation continuum distinguishes external, introjected, identified, integrated and intrinsic reasons. Autonomy means endorsed volition rather than absence of obligations, independence or mere number of options.

A boring instrumental action can be autonomously endorsed; intrinsic interest is a narrower construct.

Autonomous and controlled reasons can coexist. A difference score can conceal two simultaneously high dimensions. Do not replace full motive profiles with global labels without psychometric validation.

## 7. Wanting, liking, reward learning and reward valuation

Cue-triggered incentive wanting, experienced pleasure and learned outcome expectancies may dissociate. Current valuation of a reward includes outcome identity, goals, probability, risk, delay and efforts.

Dopamine participates in more than one relevant computation and cannot be equated one-to-one with pleasure, wanting, reward learning or total motivation.

MOT-01's incentive salience and MOT-02's valuation are compatible but operate at different explanatory levels.

## 8. Why a single utility formula is not the psychological mechanism

Illustrative decision utility can be expressed as:

\`SV(a|s,g) = anticipated benefit - perceived cost - opportunity cost\`.

This is a bookkeeping/decision hypothesis, not established identity with human cognition. Probability weighting, uncertainty, feature-valued outcomes, state representation, multiple goals and nonadditive interactions can change the formal model.

MOT-01's EVC and MOT-02's discounting functions are conditional tools. Parameter estimation must include competing models, recovery, robustness and task validity.

## 9. Probability, delay, effort and opportunity cost

MOT-02 treats four separable questions:
1. What is the believed likelihood of success or reward?
2. When is work required and when is reward delivered?
3. What is the physical/cognitive effort, and how capable is the actor of meeting the demand?
4. What alternatives are forgone?

Conflation produces false motivational traits. E.g. low choice of a demanding option can stem from lower expected success rather than greater aversion to effort.

NIMH RDoC's current Reward Valuation construct includes probability, delay and effort subconstructs. This is a useful research scaffold, not a complete theory of all human motivation.

## 10. Opportunity-cost theory is conditional

MOT-01 presents an opportunity-cost account; MOT-02 adds 2025 direct experimental counterevidence suggesting on-task errors can influence effort avoidance more than alternatives in the tested context.

These are **not contradictions**: a model can explain some effects without being a dominant universal mechanism. Alternatives, error exposure, subjective difficulty and boredom must be considered jointly.

## 11. Effort is a cost and may be positively valued

Effort can be taxing before a task and meaningful, engaging or competence-signaling during or after it. The evidence does not imply all people enjoy work or all high-demand choices are beneficial.

A difficult task may be selected to avoid boredom, gain information, signal identity or attain valued growth. A simple “law of least effort” does not uniquely determine human choice.

## 12. Goal value, attainment probability and execution resources

Separate desirability, anticipated success, belief that available action causes the desired result, skill, implementation resources, and alternative commitments.

A strongly endorsed goal can remain unacted upon because of low causal expectancy or lack of opportunity.

**Nonaction is an observation, not proof of weak desire, weak character or low willpower.**

## 13. Intention, implementation and action

MOT-03 documents that intention, commitment and actual behavior are distinct dependent variables.

A 2023 physical activity meta-analysis reported 47.6% unsuccessful intenders under its classification; no universal human intention–action failure rate follows.

If–then plans can help link cues to response initiation, but cannot ensure success or establish S–R habit. Feasibility, barriers, competing goals and contextual changes remain relevant.

## 14. Goal hierarchy and multiple goals

Goals operate at levels: end values, proximal outcomes, strategies, concrete actions. Abandoning a failing strategy is not necessarily abandoning the higher-order goal.

Allocation to one goal competes with others and can be reoptimized with new information. Goal shielding can enhance focus but reduce responsiveness when priorities change.

Persistence and flexibility should be assessed relative to feasibility, cost and retained higher-order aims.

## 15. Disengagement, reengagement and flexibility

MOT-03 distinguished:
- disengagement from a prior goal;
- reengagement with a new one;
- flexibility in modifying pursuit.

The 2025-online/2026-issue meta-analysis (235 studies, 1,421 effect sizes) reports low-to-moderate overall evidence strength from cross-sectional predominance, heterogeneity and publication-bias concerns.

Thus "quitting is good", "never quit", and "flexible people are always better" are all unsupported global prescriptions.

## 16. State and time-scale dependence

Motivational estimates may differ across:
- deprivation/satiety and fatigue;
- anticipated time of effort;
- expected reward delay;
- momentary emotional state;
- available opportunity/alternative options;
- stage of goal pursuit;
- ongoing conflict and feedback.

Momentary shifts should not automatically be labeled enduring personality or clinical changes. Study-specific state and temporal granularity matter.

## 17. Observation and explanation are separate

| Observable event | Candidate mechanisms | Invalid unique inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nonaction despite endorsed goal | unavailable cue, opportunity barrier, high cost, uncertainty, conflicting aim, low control | "No motivation" |
| Fast repeated action | habitual preparation, cue planning, practiced skill, strong value | "Global drive" |
| Avoids high-load task | effort cost, low expected accuracy, delayed reward, boring alternatives, fatigue | "Lazy" |
| Chooses difficult activity | competence/interest, boredom avoidance, instrumental value, social meaning | "Always seeks effort" |
| Chooses near-term reward | present utility, uncertainty, delay cost, task interpretation | "Impulsive trait" |
| Abandons strategy | updated evidence, goal hierarchy, costs, constraints | "Gives up on goal" |
| Continues under obstacles | progress expectancy, commitment, obligation, sunk cost | "Healthy persistence" |
| Follows externally requested task | coercion, identified value, integrated purpose, social commitment | "No autonomy" |

The table is a competing-explanations map, not retrospective diagnosis.

## 19. Empirical tension versus logical contradiction

Some theories make competing predictions:
- opportunity cost dominates effort avoidance vs on-task errors dominate;
- autonomous regulation predicts goal adherence vs constrained opportunity dominates;
- goal-disengagement scores predict wellbeing vs pre-existing wellbeing predicts disengagement capacity.

These are genuine empirical alternatives or reciprocal pathways, **not errors to eliminate by redefining all motives as the same thing**. Record uncertainty and require discriminating tests.

## 20. Measurement and identifiability

Never treat a single measure as an omnibus motivation variable.

Choose measurement by construct:
- intention → time-specific intention measure;
- action → objectively observed behavior if feasible;
- reward preference → controlled choice tasks;
- effort valuation → effort task with skill/success/delay controls;
- regulation quality → separable SDT subscales;
- goal adjustment → distinguish disengagement, reengagement and flexibility;
- motivation across time → repeated within-person context-sensitive measures.

Check construct structure, retest reliability, measurement invariance, parameter/model recovery, alternative explanations and generalization. Strong correlation with one outcome is not sufficient for mechanism identification.

## 21. Clinical and neuroscientific boundary

Low activity, weak persistence, avoidance or changed reward preference cannot independently establish apathy, anhedonia, depression, ADHD or a neural dopamine deficit.

Clinical explanations require independently valid assessment; neural claims require neural/causal evidence rather than behavior-to-brain reverse inference.

This scientific repository does not contain any individualized psychological records.

## 23. Red-team inference cases

**Case A — declared high intention, no action.** Probe opportunity, implementation cue, goals, workload, resource constraints and changed values before proposing weak motivation.

**Case B — high reward offered, refusal.** Could reflect high effort cost, insufficient probability of success, timing or moral/social concerns. Do not infer lack of reward sensitivity.

**Case C — prefers a mentally challenging option.** Investigate boredom, competence, intrinsic interest, reward identity, goal meaning, and task skill. Do not infer stable effort-loving personality.

**Case D — externally required activity persists.** The activity may be coercively controlled, autonomously endorsed or mixed. Reported goal content alone does not distinguish.

**Case E — abandon a subgoal after new data.** May indicate successful hierarchical persistence, not loss of core goal commitment.

**Case F — long delay discounting coefficient.** Check model/parameter recovery, reward scale, time horizon and cross-task validity before describing stable impulsivity.

**Case G — reporting low autonomy.** Distinguish active pressure/frustration, perceived choice, socially useful structure, and validity of the instrument.

**Case H — disengagement correlates with well-being.** Reverse causation, selection, measurement and goal type remain plausible; no therapy mechanism is identified.

**Case I — initial willingness but later decline.** Could reflect learning about task accuracy/cost, newly valuable alternatives, fatigue or goal reprioritization, not disappearance of motivation.

## 26. Evidence strength

### High-confidence practical distinctions

- Motivation cannot be inferred uniquely from nonaction;
- value, expectancy, effort, opportunity and implementation are separable;
- reward learning, incentive wanting and liking are not synonyms;
- intentional goal endorsement and actual action are not identical;
- goal disengagement, reengagement and flexibility are different constructs;
- score reliability and construct validity must be separately established.

### Conditional/task-specific evidence

- autonomy support can help some motivational outcomes;
- if–then plans can improve execution, with contextual moderators;
- alternative-task value or boredom can influence willingness to work;
- effort can be positively valued as well as costly;
- goal revision can be adaptive under genuine feasibility changes.

### No universal inference supported

- one formula captures all motivational states;
- every external incentive reduces intrinsic motivation;
- every hard task reveals a fixed effort-discount trait;
- all cognitive effort is opportunity cost;
- persistent goal striving always benefits well-being;
- goal abandonment necessarily reflects weak commitment;
- a questionnaire or laboratory score yields a clinical or neurological diagnosis.

## 27. Sufficiency decision

**MOT-01…04 are sufficient for current generic motivational inference.**

Why:
1. Fundamental constructs and their scope limits are operationalized.
2. Value and effort mechanisms include ability, delay, task-error and alternative-context confounds.
3. Goal pursuit includes motive quality, intentions, implementation and adjustment.
4. No high-risk mechanism leap requires an omitted independent foundation block.
5. Genuine open questions are routed to the appropriate later domain.

Close as **COMPLETE_FOR_CURRENT_SCOPE**, not "all motivation science completely solved."

**Next research dependency: AFF-01 — Emotion foundations: affect, emotion, mood, appraisal, dimensions, and measurement boundaries.**

The aim is to distinguish affective experience/appraisal from motivational valuation and action without confusing emotion with mood, arousal or a single neural circuit.
