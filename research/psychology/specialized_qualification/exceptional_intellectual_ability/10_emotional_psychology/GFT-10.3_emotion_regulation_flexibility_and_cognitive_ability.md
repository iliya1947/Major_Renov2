## Operational gate (generic heuristic)
For an emotional episode distinguish elicitor/exposure, appraisal, baseline, intensity/time, regulatory goal, identification of need, options, actual choice, implementation, monitoring, outcome and costs. Identify what the person is trying to change: subjective affect, expression, environment, interpersonal outcome or task behavior. No requirement to reduce every emotion. Reappraisal changes interpretation; distraction shifts attention; acceptance changes relationship to experience; suppression usually concerns expression, not removal of feeling. Labels alone do not establish enactment. Compare actual performance with stated knowledge and habits; repeated low-risk observations across comparable intensity, feasibility, goals, timing and resources; distinguish within-person effects from group correlations. Greater distress may prompt a particular strategy rather than be caused by it. Strategy switching can reflect failed attempts, new demands or productive adaptation; keeping a successful strategy is also flexibility. Prefer appropriate goal and measurable results over number of strategies. Consider ordinary context, fatigue, opportunity, competing goals and health factors; no IQ or clinical label from success/failure. No universal ranking or automatic prescription; clinical interventions require separate evidence and appropriate professional assessment.

## Internal assessment — new synthetic cases
A. Fictional community orchestra musician can explain reappraisal accurately but reports persistent anxiety before unfamiliar performances, and often changes strategies. Competing explanations: high initial intensity, limited rehearsal, performance evaluation, strategy implementation and monitoring. Compare comparable rehearsed/unrehearsed events, goals and time course, not just immediate distress. New: stable rehearsal routine improves performance and recovery without increased reappraisal; frequent switches occur mainly when access to practice is limited. Revise global cognitive deficit and flexibility-as-switching claims; retain emotion knowledge and conditional burden. No diagnosis.
B. Fictional repair cooperative member suppresses outward irritation during a customer interaction but subsequently raises a valid safety concern with colleagues. Observer calls suppression invariably maladaptive. Separate expression, feeling, immediate social aim, later resolution and costs; rival professional role, fear of criticism, actual unresolved issue. New: concern is addressed promptly and irritation resolves; a different context with prolonged concealment has ongoing costs. Retain contextual effect, withdraw global strategy label; no causal or IQ inference.
C. Fictional training club high reasoning-score group chooses distraction more often during difficult tasks, and reports worse subsequent affect than peers who choose reappraisal. Analyst infers distraction is harmful or high IQ causes emotion dysregulation. Compare baseline intensity, choice vs efficacy, matched tasks and outcome timing. New: group received more difficult tasks; when matched on observed intensity both strategy patterns vary by person and no reliable group contrast is detected. Revise giftedness-based inference to conditional task/selection explanation; null not equivalence, no unique causal mechanism.
