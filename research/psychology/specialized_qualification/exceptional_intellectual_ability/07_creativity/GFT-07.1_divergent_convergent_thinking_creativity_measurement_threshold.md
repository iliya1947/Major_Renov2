## 3. Integrated measurement and model gate (PRACTICAL_HEURISTIC)
| Construct | Operationalization | Common inferential error |
|---|---|---|
| Fluency | Count relevant distinct responses under same instructions/time | More responses = more creative |
| Flexibility | Distinct category/strategy shifts, explicit coding | Category count independent of fluency |
| Originality | Rarity or independent ratings relative to reference context | Unusual = effective; rarity = novelty everywhere |
| Appropriateness | Meets actual constraints/use/contextual criteria | Feasibility = originality |
| DT | Open-ended production, e.g. AUT | Pure generative process or all creative potential |
| CT | Targeted solution/evaluation, e.g. RAT, criterion-based selection | RAT = all evaluation ability; creativity necessarily single right answer |
| Achievement | Verified products, recognition, contributions by domain/time | Pure ability without access, motivation, training, evaluators |
Generation and evaluation may recur/overlap; do not claim obligatory serial stages or distinct neural modules. Creativity as product criterion includes novelty and contextual appropriateness; divergent task performance is proxy not diagnosis. Distinguish ability, task knowledge, strategy, speed/typing/language, motivation, opportunities and outcome. 
Scoring illustration: person A 12 valid ideas with 2 rated highly; B 4 valid ideas with 2 rated highly. Sum high counts equal, proportion 2/12 vs 2/4; neither alone establishes overall creativity, selection quality or productivity. More attempts mechanically increase opportunities for extreme scores; average may penalize exploration; top-k can impose selection and minimum-response restrictions. Use prespecified scoring, multiple tasks/domains, blind independent ratings with interrater reliability, relevance rules and appropriate reference samples. Do not manufacture individual standardized creativity IQ from exercise. Automated scoring needs domain/language validity, appropriateness and reproducibility; embedding distance is not objective total creativity. 

Threshold analysis: let x be normed intelligence measure (or clearly labeled raw/z measure), y specified creativity score. M0: y=a+b*x+error. M1: y=a+b*x+c*max(0,x-k)+error, slopes b and b+c; test slope difference c, uncertainty of k, comparison with continuous/smooth nonlinear models and out-of-sample predictive gain, not simply whether upper correlation p>.05. Fixed prespecified k=120 distinct from searching many k. Account for uncertainty in IQ measurement, floor/ceiling, heteroscedasticity, selection/range restriction, subgroup mixture, scoring and sample sizes on both sides. If estimating k under null with unidentified parameter, use appropriate change-point procedure (e.g. Davies/bootstrap per design), not naive t-test of chosen knot. Boundary/quantile or necessary-condition hypothesis concerns attainable outcomes, not same as conditional mean slope. Even if upper slope near 0 for tested outcome, not proof intelligence causally irrelevant, nor rule for person above threshold. 
Illustrative range restriction: in model Y=X+noise with independent equal noise variance, corr(X,Y)=sqrt(Var(X)/(Var(X)+Var(noise))); Var(X)=1 and noise variance1 yields .707; restricted Var(X)=.1 with same conditional model yields .302. A smaller correlation can arise with unchanged slope. This is mathematical illustration under specified assumptions, not empirical correction or proof of artifact for any study. Do not apply crude subgroup comparison. 
Distinguish observed score from latent model and true conditional mean/variance. Pooled association not tail model. For psychological analysis individual evidence: specific task and context, independent rubric, prior knowledge and alternative explanation, counterexample and revision. No inference about IQ from creative product or vice versa.

## 4. Practical protocol
1. Define whether claim is about number/quality of ideas, selection/revision, actual product, repeated achievement or transfer. Fix domain, reference class, constraints, time, and rubric before judging.
2. Separate generation and evaluation evidence: relevant alternatives, novelty, usefulness, quality of selection, implementation and achieved outcome. Identify at least two rival explanations (knowledge/experience, strategy, motivation, instructions, opportunity, rating/scoring, measurement limits).
3. If comparing ability, require adequate measurement, matched tasks, independence/reliability and range; avoid overinterpreting one timed task or one remarkable result. For threshold claims compare models on same outcome and measure, check local sample and uncertainty, not significant/non-significant split.
4. Update dependent claims when novel evidence contradicts: preserve actual strengths, narrow inference to demonstrated conditions, explain how to test transfer with new tasks and independent criteria. Stop if inference would require inaccessible measurement or tail data. No person-specific IQ estimate or diagnosis.
5. For 07.2 distinguish scientific model construction, explanatory adequacy, predictive tests, critical evaluation and implementation; 07.3 productivity/achievement and contextual factors if warranted; integration after content coverage. 

## 5. Internal transfer tasks
A. Fictional design of a rainwater collector: A generates 20 ideas, B generates 6; blinded reviewers find two workable novel proposals in each. Initial claim A more creative from fluency alone unsupported. Alternatives: exploration vs quality, prior expertise, task interpretation, number of opportunities, ratings. New: B selects an elegant idea but it fails a fixed pressure constraint; A revises a less novel idea to meet all constraints. Revision: distinguish initial originality, evaluation and outcome; A now shows successful revision on this problem, not global creative superiority. Check new matched tasks and blinded novelty/fit ratings; no IQ inference.
B. Fictional study of two IQ-selected adult samples: upper range has nonsignificant correlation between a timed word-idea count and IQ, lower range significant. Claim universal threshold 120 rejected as untested. Alternatives: range restriction, low N, typing, ceiling, continuous slope, subgroup differences. New: same slope fits both in pooled model, CI for change includes zero; untimed novelty ratings show different pattern. Revision: no support for this fixed threshold on this outcome; not proof all creativity linear, original correlations still facts. Need model comparison, measurement invariance, range and independent replication. Do not infer adult extreme profile.
C. Fictional workshop on ceramic glazes: expert has impressive exhibition record and low AUT score; novice has many unusual proposals but no opportunities to exhibit. Rival explanations: relevant knowledge, opportunities, production capacity, language/tempo, narrow task. New: under equivalent domain constraints novice proposes workable novel formulations, expert predicts durability more accurately and implements a superior test; both outperform earlier on a shared critique task. Revision: distinguish domain creative potential, evaluation, expertise and realization; no global ranking or IQ estimate. Test repeated products/transfer and opportunities, not force one creativity trait.
