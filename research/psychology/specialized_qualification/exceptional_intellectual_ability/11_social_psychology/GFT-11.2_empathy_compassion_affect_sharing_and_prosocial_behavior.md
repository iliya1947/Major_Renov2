## Practical analytical gate
Specify whether evidence concerns understanding or accuracy, sharing another person's affect, feeling one's own discomfort, concern for another's welfare, wanting to help, choosing an action, enacting it or actual recipient benefit. These can overlap or diverge. No hierarchy of moral worth from emotion intensity or IQ; measured reasoning may support evaluating alternatives without ensuring a particular value or action. Consider noticing need, information accuracy, self-other distinction, resource constraints, expected efficacy, incentives, role and consent. Absence of visibly shared distress does not prove lack of concern; distress alone does not prove concern, and nonhelp may reflect legitimate limits or recipient preference. Compare repeated ordinary contexts, opportunity and cost, requested vs offered help, outcomes and uncertainty. Do not infer private motives from a single action or try to test another person covertly. Cooperation in repeated games can be strategic or mutually beneficial; helpful action need not be pure altruism. Distinguish a group's mean from an individual. Preserve competing explanations and update if information contradicts prediction; no diagnosis or IQ estimate from social behavior.

## Internal assessment — wholly invented cases
A. Fictional community seed bank member responds calmly to a colleague's disappointment and offers replacement seeds, while another shares intense distress but cannot help. Analyst judges calm member less empathic and upset member more caring. Separate affect intensity, concern, resources, choice, actual benefit; compare requests and available actions. New: calm member reports concern and colleague confirms useful help; upset member has no available seeds but later arranges access. Withdraw comparison of moral character, retain differing expressions/resources; no inference that either has high or low IQ.
B. Fictional cooperative board game group has high reasoning scores and learns reciprocating strategies over rounds; observer claims universally stronger altruistic motivation. Compare payoff incentives, learning, understanding of rules, first-round choice and behavior outside game. New: group advantage vanishes in a one-shot task, and disclosure of relative scores disrupts cooperation. Retain local conditional cooperation association; withdraw universal altruism claim. Alternative task understanding and partner expectations remain; null not equivalence or definitive mechanism.
C. Fictional museum fundraiser reports high concern and a preference for effective donations but makes fewer contributions when income falls. Analyst treats self-report as proof of altruism, or lower contributions as reduced empathy. Compare reported motive, noticing opportunity, available funds, donation choice and recipient benefit; distinguish perceived and verified effectiveness. New: volunteer provides useful nonmonetary support requested by museum while saving funds; stated motive unchanged. Revise global motive deficit; preserve actual donation reduction and evidence of alternative action. No claim of pure altruistic motive, diagnosis or intelligence.
