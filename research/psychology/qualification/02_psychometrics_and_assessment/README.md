# Psychometrics and psychological assessment

## Purpose

This directory stores the applied psychometrics and psychological-assessment qualification sequence for Kira.

The domain translates generic measurement methodology into defensible interpretation of psychological scores, individual precision, norms, classifications, change, and model-based measurement.

### PSYCHOMETRICS-01 — Applied measurement foundations

Status: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v1.0 — 18/18 PASS**

Canonical block:

`PSYCHOMETRICS-01_applied_measurement_score_meaning_precision_validity_invariance_norms_change_individual_interpretation.md`

Established:

1. score, construct, scoring model, and interpretation are distinct;
2. validity is interpretation/use specific;
3. reliability is population/procedure dependent and is not validity;
4. group reliability does not guarantee precision of an individual score;
5. SEM/conditional SEM and score-region precision matter for person-level decisions;
6. total scores and subscores require structural and score-specific support;
7. measurement invariance/DIF constrain group, language, mode, and longitudinal comparisons;
8. target, reference, and normative populations are distinct;
9. percentiles and standardized scores are reference-dependent rather than absolute trait units;
10. cut scores are decision rules; decision consistency/accuracy are separate from score reliability;
11. raw change, reliable change, responsiveness, and meaningful change are distinct;
12. RCI operationalization must be specified because methods can produce materially different thresholds;
13. digital/automated assessment retains ordinary validity, fairness, and precision requirements;
14. a 72-question Applied Psychometrics Inference Gate and 18/18 closure audit are canonical.

### PSYCHOMETRICS-02 — Latent-variable and item-response measurement

Status: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v1.0 — 18/18 PASS**

Canonical block:

`PSYCHOMETRICS-02_latent_variable_irt_rasch_information_local_dependence_dif_factor_scores_test_construction.md`

Established:

1. CTT, factor analysis, IRT, and Rasch are frameworks rather than a hierarchy of truth;
2. latent variables require explicit identification/scaling conventions;
3. theta and factor scores are estimates rather than direct observations;
4. ordinal factor analysis and IRT can represent closely related latent structures while differing in estimation;
5. unidimensionality is a response-structure claim rather than psychological ontology;
6. local dependence can inflate information/discrimination and understate person-score uncertainty;
7. Rasch/1PL/2PL/3PL and major polytomous models require claim-matched selection;
8. Rasch invariance/specific-objectivity claims are conditional on model/calibration rather than automatic;
9. item discrimination and item information are not substitutes for validity/content quality;
10. MLE/MAP/EAP/WLE and factor-score estimators can produce materially different person estimates;
11. factor-score estimates remain error-prone even with large samples;
12. model fit requires global/item/person/residual diagnostics and should not be reduced to one fixed cutoff;
13. DIF is conditional item-functioning difference, not automatically item bias;
14. anchor choice and item-level vs test-level DIF impact matter;
15. item deletion changes construct operationalization and must preserve content coverage;
16. calibration, linking, transport, and item drift constrain score comparability;
17. an 84-question Latent Measurement / IRT Inference Gate and 18/18 closure audit are canonical.

### PSYCHOMETRICS-03 — Integration and sufficiency audit

Status: **COMPLETE_FOR_CURRENT_SCOPE — 20/20 PASS**

Canonical block:

`PSYCHOMETRICS-03_psychometrics_integration_sufficiency_audit.md`

Established:

1. score/use-level and item/model-level psychometrics are internally consistent;
2. CTT reliability, SEM/cSEM, and IRT information are separated but integrated;
3. simple and latent scores are evaluated by inferential value rather than sophistication;
4. model fit remains one validity-evidence component rather than full validity;
5. factor-score fit and individual factor-score precision are distinct;
6. measurement invariance and DIF are complementary rather than interchangeable;
7. fairness is broader than invariance/DIF;
8. longitudinal change requires both precision and measurement comparability;
9. one canonical uncertainty stack now separates response, model, parameter, person-score, transport, norm, decision, and validity uncertainty;
10. a 64-question Integrated Psychometrics Inference Gate and 20/20 closure audit are canonical;
11. no scientifically necessary PSYCHOMETRICS-04 dependency is identified.
