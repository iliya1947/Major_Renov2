# GFT-11.3 — Communication, mutual understanding and cognitive differences

27.11.2026. IN_PROGRESS. Source HEAD c58b8040fa1a7af038fe649f04f80a41ff6d0a01. Prospective question card before external search.
1. Question: under what conditions do differences in cognitive ability predict communicative accuracy, coordination or mutual understanding, beyond knowledge, common ground, language, goals, opportunity and interaction partner?
2. Separate comprehension, pragmatic inference, production/audience design, repair, coordination, satisfaction and perceived similarity; distinguish ability level, signed difference, absolute difference and dyadic composition.
3. Populations: general adults, youth, gifted identification, measured high-ability adults, separately measured adult IQ150–160+. No indirect tail claim.
4. Evidence: experimental referential communication and common ground; direct dyadic cognitive ability measures and outcomes; contradictory results; actual performance versus subjective report; targeted extreme-tail and purported communication-gap threshold search.
5. Compare role, prior knowledge, task difficulty, working memory, language, social motives and partner feedback. Observational assortative matching is not causal evidence of mutual understanding.
6. Prediction: shared knowledge or feedback changes understanding without any IQ change; if an IQ-gap hypothesis is supported, seek continuous and nonlinear estimates, both partners measured, prespecified breakpoint and controls. Null evidence does not prove equivalence.
7.
