# Safety boundaries, crisis recognition, and referral thresholds

## Purpose

This directory is the canonical storage location for the **SAF** Phase C qualification domain.

SAF covers generic psychological safety reasoning, including:

- recognition of acute safety concerns;
- crisis and escalation boundaries;
- self-harm/suicide-risk communication boundaries;
- violence/coercion and immediate-danger boundaries;
- severe impairment/confusion/psychosis/mania/intoxication or medical-red-flag escalation;
- emergency versus urgent versus routine referral thresholds;
- limits of remote/AI-assisted risk inference;
- safety planning principles at a generic methodological level;
- false reassurance and false-positive trade-offs;
- documentation/uncertainty boundaries.

### SAF-01 — Safety and crisis foundations

Status: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v1.0 — 18/18 PASS**

Canonical block:

`SAF-01_safety_crisis_foundations_acute_risk_self_harm_suicide_triage_medical_psychiatric_remote_referral.md`

Established:

1. screening, assessment, risk formulation, prediction, and disposition are separate;
2. validated screening can identify who needs further assessment but does not yield exact future-suicide prediction;
3. NICE low/medium/high global risk stratification is not used to predict future suicide/self-harm or determine treatment/discharge;
4. direct suicide questioning is appropriate and does not create suicidal behavior;
5. self-harm is not synonymous with suicidal intent;
6. intrusive/ego-dystonic harm thoughts are not automatically intent;
7. current suicidal thoughts, intent, plan/access, past behavior, recent escalation, and environment are separate evidence channels;
8. emergency/urgent/routine are action thresholds rather than pseudo-precise probability categories;
9. physical and psychosocial care should proceed concurrently after self-harm;
10. acute confusion/delirium is a medical-assessment red flag;
11. suspected mania/severe depression and possible psychosis have specialist escalation boundaries;
12. severe alcohol withdrawal with seizure/delirium risk is a medical/hospital-level problem;
13. safety plans are distinct from safety contracts; no-suicide contracts are not treated as protection;
14. remote/telehealth crisis management requires local emergency planning and has structural limits;
15. AI conversation is not a formal clinical risk assessment or emergency service;
16. a 112-question Safety / Crisis Foundations Inference Gate and 18/18 closure audit are canonical.
