## 1. Forearm bones

Предплечье образовано:

1. **radius** — lateral в anatomical position, thumb side;
2. **ulna** — medial, little-finger side.

Обе кости связаны:
- proximal radioulnar joint;
- interosseous membrane;
- distal radioulnar joint.

В pronation radius crosses over ulna; ulna остаётся сравнительно более стабильной осью. citeturn480144search0turn480144search6

## 2. Interosseous membrane

Interosseous membrane (IOM):
- fibrous syndesmosis между radius и ulna;
- разделяет/организует compartments;
- даёт muscle attachment;
- участвует в force transfer между костями.

Это не просто перегородка.

Механическое распределение нагрузки через IOM → MEC.

## 3. Forearm ring concept

PRUJ + radius + ulna + IOM + DRUJ образуют функциональный ring, обеспечивающий стабильную pronation/supination.

Повреждение одного звена может менять движение всей системы, но pathology → MED.

## 4. Distal radioulnar joint

DRUJ:
- distal radius sigmoid notch ↔ ulnar head;
- synovial pivot joint;
- ключевой для pronation/supination;
- работает вместе с PRUJ. citeturn480144search0

При forearm rotation distal radius перемещается вокруг ulna.

## 5. TFCC

Triangular fibrocartilage complex (TFCC) — сложный ulnar-sided stabilizing complex запястья/DRUJ.

Включает структуры, связанные с:
- articular disc;
- radioulnar ligaments;
- ulnocarpal ligaments;
- tendon sheath/adjacent structures depending anatomical definition.

Функции:
1. stabilizes DRUJ;
2. transmits/distributes ulnar-sided load;
3. supports ulnocarpal region.

Не сводить TFCC к одной «треугольной связке».

## 6. Forearm compartments

Удобная anatomical organization:

### Anterior
flexor-pronator dominant.

### Posterior
extensor-supinator dominant.

Дополнительно некоторые источники выделяют lateral/mobile wad:
- brachioradialis;
- extensor carpi radialis longus;
- extensor carpi radialis brevis. citeturn480144search1

Compartments разделяются fascia, septa, IOM.

## 7. Anterior superficial flexor-pronator layer

Классически включает:

1. pronator teres;
2. flexor carpi radialis (FCR);
3. palmaris longus;
4. flexor carpi ulnaris (FCU).

Большинство имеют common flexor origin near medial epicondyle.

FDS часто выделяется intermediate layer.

## 8. Pronator teres

Origin:
- humeral head from medial epicondyle region;
- ulnar head from coronoid process.

Insertion:
- lateral mid-radius.

Innervation:
- median nerve.

Primary:
- pronation;
- secondary elbow flexion contribution.

Because it crosses elbow and radius, action depends on joint configuration.

## 9. Pronator quadratus

Deep distal forearm.

Origin:
- distal ulna.

Insertion:
- distal radius.

Innervation:
- anterior interosseous branch median nerve.

Primary:
- pronation;
- dynamic stabilization of distal radius-ulna relationship.

Its distal location makes it anatomically distinct from pronator teres.

## 10. Flexor carpi radialis

Origin:
- medial epicondyle/common flexor origin.

Insertion:
- bases of second/third metacarpal region depending description.

Actions:
- wrist flexion;
- radial deviation.

Innervation:
- median nerve.

## 11. Flexor carpi ulnaris

Origin:
- medial epicondyle + ulna/olecranon heads.

Insertion:
- pisiform with continuation through pisohamate/pisometacarpal structures toward fifth metacarpal.

Actions:
- wrist flexion;
- ulnar deviation.

Innervation:
- ulnar nerve.

Important exception:
FCU is a major superficial anterior forearm muscle not median-nerve innervated.

## 12. Palmaris longus

Origin:
- common flexor region.

Insertion:
- palmar aponeurosis/flexor retinaculum.

Action:
- weak wrist flexion;
- tenses palmar aponeurosis.

Highly variable and congenitally absent in many people.

Project rule:
its absence is a normal anatomical variant, not deficit.

## 13. Flexor digitorum superficialis (FDS)

Intermediate layer.

Origins:
- humeroulnar and radial components.

Tendons:
- digits 2–5.

Insertions:
- sides of middle phalanges.

Primary:
- PIP flexion;
- also MCP/wrist flexion contribution.

At each finger, FDS tendon splits to allow FDP tendon passage.

## 14. Flexor digitorum profundus (FDP)

Deep anterior forearm.

Origin:
- ulna + IOM.

Tendons:
- digits 2–5.

Insertion:
- distal phalanges.

Primary:
- DIP flexion;
- contributes grip force.

Innervation is split:
- lateral part/index-middle largely anterior interosseous/median;
- medial ring/little part ulnar nerve.

This mixed innervation matters in nerve anatomy.

## 15. Flexor pollicis longus (FPL)

Origin:
- anterior radius + IOM.

Insertion:
- distal phalanx thumb.

Primary:
- thumb IP flexion;
- supports strong thumb opposition/grip patterns.

Innervation:
- anterior interosseous nerve.

## 16. Posterior superficial group

Main muscles:
1. extensor carpi radialis longus (ECRL);
2. extensor carpi radialis brevis (ECRB);
3. extensor digitorum;
4. extensor digiti minimi;
5. extensor carpi ulnaris (ECU).

Brachioradialis and anconeus are often grouped with lateral/posterior region but have distinct primary roles. citeturn480144search5turn480144search10

## 17. ECRL/ECRB

Actions:
- wrist extension;
- radial deviation contribution.

Innervation:
- ECRL radial nerve;
- ECRB deep radial/posterior interosseous pathway depending level.

These muscles are important for maintaining wrist extension during grip.

## 18. Extensor carpi ulnaris

Actions:
- wrist extension;
- ulnar deviation.

Insertion:
- fifth metacarpal base.

Innervation:
- posterior interosseous nerve.

Works with radial extensors to create extension without large deviation when coactivated.

## 19. Extensor digitorum

Common extrinsic finger extensor for digits 2–5.

Origin:
- lateral epicondyle/common extensor origin.

Distally:
- enters extensor expansions rather than simple single-bone terminal insertion.

Actions:
- MCP extension;
- through extensor mechanism assists IP extension;
- contributes wrist extension.

Innervation:
- posterior interosseous nerve.

## 20. Extensor digiti minimi / extensor indicis

Provide more independent extension to:
- little finger;
- index finger respectively.

This helps fine finger control beyond common extensor digitorum.

## 21. Deep posterior thumb muscles

1. abductor pollicis longus (APL);
2. extensor pollicis brevis (EPB);
3. extensor pollicis longus (EPL).

Functions:
- thumb abduction/extension at different joints.

Their tendons cross wrist in defined extensor compartments.

## 22. Supinator

Origin complex:
- lateral epicondyle;
- radial collateral/annular ligament region;
- supinator crest/fossa ulna.

Insertion:
- proximal radius.

Innervation:
- deep branch radial nerve/posterior interosseous pathway.

Primary:
- supination.

Works with biceps, but biceps contribution is more dependent on elbow angle/load.

## 23. Wrist is a complex, not one simple joint

“Wrist motion” is distributed across:

1. radiocarpal joint;
2. midcarpal/intercarpal joints;
3. related ulnocarpal soft-tissue interface;
while DRUJ handles forearm rotation rather than wrist flexion/extension proper. citeturn480144search2turn480144search19

## 24. Carpal bones

Eight carpal bones.

### Proximal row
1. scaphoid;
2. lunate;
3. triquetrum;
4. pisiform.

### Distal row
5. trapezium;
6. trapezoid;
7. capitate;
8. hamate.

Pisiform is sesamoid within FCU tendon.

## 25. Radiocarpal joint

Articulation between:
- distal radius + articular disc/TFCC side;
and mainly:
- scaphoid;
- lunate;
- triquetrum depending position.

Type:
- condyloid/ellipsoid synovial joint.

Movements:
1. flexion;
2. extension;
3. radial deviation;
4. ulnar deviation;
5. circumduction.

Ulna does not directly articulate with proximal carpal row like radius does; TFCC separates/contributes interface.

## 26. Midcarpal joint

Between proximal and distal carpal rows.

Significant contributor to:
- wrist flexion/extension;
- deviation.

Total wrist ROM is combination radiocarpal + midcarpal/intercarpal motions.

Therefore “radiocarpal angle” ≠ entire wrist motion in every context.

## 27. Flexor retinaculum

Dense connective-tissue band spanning carpal arch to form roof of carpal tunnel.

Carpal tunnel contains:
- median nerve;
- 4 FDS tendons;
- 4 FDP tendons;
- 1 FPL tendon. citeturn480144search1

FCR tendon travels in its own compartment rather than ordinary contents of tunnel proper.

## 28. Extensor retinaculum

Posterior wrist retinaculum holds extensor tendons close to skeleton and prevents bowstringing.

Creates six dorsal extensor compartments for specific tendons.

Training relevance:
wrist/thumb tendon lines of pull are constrained by these tunnels.

No need to memorize all six compartments for basic arm program, but structure matters for anatomy/pathology.

## 29. Tendon sheaths

Long finger/thumb flexor and extensor tendons pass through synovial sheaths in high-friction constrained regions.

Functions:
- reduce friction;
- permit gliding.

Tendon sheath pathology is medical, not normal anatomy.

## 30. Extrinsic vs intrinsic hand muscles

### Extrinsic
Muscle bellies in forearm; tendons enter hand.

Examples:
- FDS/FDP/FPL;
- extensor digitorum;
- thumb extensors.

### Intrinsic
Origin and insertion contained within hand.

Groups:
1. thenar;
2. hypothenar;
3. lumbricals;
4. interossei;
5. adductor pollicis often grouped with intrinsic thumb muscles. citeturn480144search9

## 31. Thenar group

Commonly:
1. abductor pollicis brevis;
2. flexor pollicis brevis;
3. opponens pollicis.

Adductor pollicis is deep intrinsic thumb muscle, often discussed separately anatomically.

Innervation:
- recurrent median nerve for major thenar components;
- ulnar contribution to deep head FPB/adductor pollicis.

Thumb anatomy has more innervation nuance than mnemonic “thenar = median”.

## 32. Hypothenar group

1. abductor digiti minimi;
2. flexor digiti minimi brevis;
3. opponens digiti minimi;
4. palmaris brevis associated with hypothenar/palmar fascia.

Primarily ulnar nerve.

Functions:
- little-finger positioning;
- cup/contour palm;
- support grip.

## 33. Lumbricals

Four lumbricals.

Origin:
- FDP tendons.

Insertion:
- radial/lateral side of extensor expansions.

Functional pattern:
- flex MCP joints;
- extend IP joints through extensor mechanism.

Common innervation:
- lateral two median;
- medial two ulnar,
but variation exists. citeturn480144search17

## 34. Interossei

### Dorsal interossei
Finger abduction.

### Palmar interossei
Finger adduction.

Both:
- flex MCP;
- assist IP extension via extensor expansions.

Innervation:
- deep branch ulnar nerve. citeturn480144search13

Mnemonic DAB/PAD is useful but does not capture their MCP/IP role.

## 35. Extensor expansion

Dorsal digital extensor mechanism integrates:

1. extensor digitorum tendon;
2. lumbricals;
3. interossei.

This distributes force to PIP/DIP extension.

Therefore finger extension is not simply “extensor digitorum pulls distal phalanx”.

## 36. Thumb is structurally special

Thumb:
- first carpometacarpal joint at trapezium;
- MCP;
- IP.

CMC joint permits:
- flex/extend;
- abduct/adduct;
- axial rotation component;
- opposition/reposition.

Opposition is composite 3D motion, central to precision/power grip.

## 37. Finger joints

Digits 2–5:
1. MCP;
2. PIP;
3. DIP.

MCP:
- flex/extend;
- abduct/adduct especially in extension.

IP:
- primarily hinge flex/extend.

## 38. Power grip anatomy

Power grip typically requires:
1. finger flexors (FDP/FDS);
2. thumb flexion/adduction/opposition;
3. intrinsic hand stabilization;
4. wrist extensors to position wrist;
5. forearm pronation/supination depending object.

Critical point:
**strong grip is not produced by finger flexors alone.**

## 39. Why wrist extensors matter for grip

Long finger flexors cross wrist.

If wrist collapses into excessive flexion:
- finger flexors shorten at wrist;
- their length/force relationship changes.

Wrist extensors stabilize wrist in moderate extension during many strong grip tasks.

This is anatomical basis; exact optimal angle → MEC/PHY.

## 40. Tenodesis effect

Because extrinsic finger flexor/extensor tendons cross wrist:

- wrist extension passively tends to flex fingers;
- wrist flexion passively tends to extend fingers.

This is called tenodesis effect.

It demonstrates mechanical coupling between wrist position and finger posture.

## 41. Active insufficiency preview

FDP/FDS/FPL cross multiple joints.

Extreme wrist flexion + finger flexion shortens them across several joints.

Extreme wrist extension + finger extension lengthens opposing tissues.

This matters for grip, but quantitative force-length → PHY/MEC.

## 42. Forearm rotation and grip

Pronation/supination changes:
- radius-ulna relation;
- orientation of hand/object;
- moment arms of some muscles.

But finger flexor anatomy remains capable of grip across orientations.

No one grip position anatomically “turns off” all other muscles.

## 43. Grip types

Functional categories:
1. power grip;
2. precision grip;
3. pinch:
   - tip;
   - key/lateral;
   - tripod variants;
4. hook grip.

Each uses different balance of:
- extrinsic flexors;
- thumb muscles;
- interossei/lumbricals;
- wrist stabilizers.

For dumbbells, power/cylindrical grip is primary.

## 44. Handle thickness

Anatomically, thicker handle changes:
- finger flexion angles;
- thumb opposition/adduction demand;
- muscle-tendon length conditions.

Whether thicker handle improves forearm hypertrophy/strength is not anatomical fact → TEC/MEC.

## 45. Forearm musculature is not synonymous with grip

Forearm includes:
- wrist flexors/extensors;
- pronators/supinator;
- finger flexors/extensors;
- thumb muscles.

Grip training may strongly load some, weakly load others.

Therefore a “forearm workout” requires defining target:
- grip;
- wrist flexion;
- extension;
- pronation/supination;
- finger extension;
etc.

## 46. Wrist flexion/extension vs deviation

FCR + FCU together:
- wrist flexion with reduced net deviation.

ECRL/B + ECU together:
- wrist extension with reduced net deviation.

Radial-side muscles together:
- radial deviation.

Ulnar-side muscles:
- ulnar deviation.

This is classical force-couple functional anatomy; exact torque → MEC.

## 47. Pronation/supination muscles

### Major pronators
1. pronator teres;
2. pronator quadratus.

### Major supinators
1. supinator;
2. biceps brachii.

Brachioradialis helps return toward neutral from extreme positions.

No single muscle owns forearm rotation.

## 48. Neural map — minimal preview

### Median nerve / anterior interosseous
Most anterior flexor-pronator muscles and thenar/lateral lumbrical functions.

### Ulnar nerve
FCU, medial FDP, most intrinsic hand muscles.

### Radial/posterior interosseous
Posterior extensor-supinator compartment.

Detailed pathways/clinical signs → ANA-12.

## 49. Carpal tunnel anatomical boundary

Contents:
- median nerve;
- nine long flexor tendons.

Not contained:
- ulnar nerve/artery;
- palmaris longus tendon superficial;
- FCR in separate compartment.

This distinction later matters for symptoms but pathology → MED.

## 50. Guyon canal preview

Ulnar nerve/artery enter hand through ulnar-sided Guyon canal region, separate from carpal tunnel.

Detailed landmarks/pathology → ANA-12/MED.

## 51. Grip and “forearm pump”

A forearm pump reflects temporary blood/fluid/metabolic changes in loaded muscles.

It does not tell:
- which exact forearm muscle hypertrophied;
- chronic adaptation.

Acute proxy rule → RES-13.

## 52. Surface anatomy

Palpable/visible landmarks:
- medial/lateral epicondyles;
- olecranon;
- radial head;
- radial/ulnar styloid;
- pisiform;
- thenar/hypothenar eminences;
- dorsal extensor tendons.

Useful for:
- measurement;
- technique;
- symptom localization,
but not diagnosis.

## 53. Anatomical variation

Common:
- palmaris longus absence;
- variable muscle/tendon slips;
- lumbrical innervation patterns;
- accessory hand muscles;
- carpal morphology.

Modern imaging literature shows numerous intrinsic-hand muscle variants. citeturn480144search12

Project rule:
atlas anatomy is modal, not identical in everyone.

## 54. Training relevance — structural only

Anatomy now supports later investigation of:

1. neutral/supinated/pronated curls;
2. wrist-curl variants;
3. reverse curls;
4. pronation/supination loading;
5. grip strength;
6. wrist position during pulling/pressing;
7. handle thickness;
8. finger extensors as antagonist/balance system.

No exercise superiority is inferred yet.

## 55. Project canonical hand/forearm model

1. Radius and ulna form a rotational forearm ring with PRUJ/DRUJ/IOM.
2. Forearm anterior compartment = flexor-pronator dominant.
3. Posterior = extensor-supinator dominant.
4. Wrist motion is distributed across radiocarpal/midcarpal structures.
5. Finger force depends on extrinsic flexors/extensors + intrinsic hand muscles.
6. Grip requires wrist stabilization and thumb opposition/adduction, not finger flexors alone.
7. Wrist position changes long finger-muscle length conditions.
8. Intrinsic hand muscles are mostly ulnar-nerve dominated; key median exceptions exist.
9. “Forearm” is multiple functions, not one muscle group.
10. Grip/pump ≠ complete forearm development.

## 56. Confidence

### HIGH
