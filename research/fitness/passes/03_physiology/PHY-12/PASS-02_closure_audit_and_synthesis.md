## 3. Stiffness / modulus / CSA audit

PASS.

These remain different properties.

### Tendon stiffness
Whole-tendon force–elongation behavior.

### Young's modulus
Material property normalized to geometry.

### CSA
Morphological size variable.

The 2022 meta-analysis found mechanical loading produced:

- moderate stiffness increase;
- large modulus increase;
- small CSA increase.

Meta-regression identified modulus as the main moderator of stiffness increase.

Current 2026 evidence reinforces that morphology and mechanics can dissociate:

- HIRT increased patellar-tendon stiffness while BFRT did not, although CSA increased in both groups;
- high- and low-load elbow-flexion training produced distinct muscle growth while tendon-thickness changes were small/localized and similar across training exposures.

Canonical rule:

**tendon size, material quality and whole-tendon stiffness are not interchangeable outcomes.**

## 4. Tendon adaptation vs strength audit

PASS WITH CAUSAL BOUNDARY.

The 2026 multilevel meta-analysis found both strength and tendon stiffness increased under chronic mechanical loading, with a larger average within-group stiffness response.

But:

- the primary analysis was paired within-group change;
- it was not a control-adjusted treatment effect;
- controlled-design restriction attenuated the strength–stiffness contrast;
- functional significance and causal interpretation remained uncertain.

Canonical rule:

**parallel adaptation does not prove a fixed causal or proportional strength↔stiffness relationship.**

## 5. Collagen synthesis acute→chronic audit

PASS.

Human tendon protein-turnover evidence is method- and time-window dependent.

Classic tracer/biopsy work reported postexercise increases in tendon collagen synthesis.

The 2026 D₂O study, integrating synthesis over 8 days and separating soluble-matrix from insoluble-collagen fractions, found:

- much higher synthesis in soluble matrix than insoluble collagen;
- no significant effect of three unilateral heavy-RT bouts on either fraction.

These findings use different estimands and are not treated as a direct contradiction.

Canonical rule:

**acute/short-term tendon protein synthesis is biological-response evidence, not a validated surrogate for chronic stiffness, modulus, CSA or load tolerance.**

## 6. Collagen / ECM remodeling audit

PASS.

Tendon adaptation is retained as matrix remodeling involving:

- collagen turnover;
- non-collagenous matrix proteins;
- MMP/TIMP systems;
- proteoglycans;
- tenocyte mechanotransduction;
- matrix organization/material properties.

Canonical rule:

**tendon remodeling is not reducible to “more collagen.”**

No blocker.

## 7. Viscoelasticity audit

PASS WITH PRESERVED UNCERTAINTY.

Tendon is viscoelastic and shows:

- hysteresis;
- creep;
- stress relaxation;
- rate dependence.

Human in-vivo data establish these properties.

Acute loading can alter measured viscoelastic/mechanical behavior.

However, chronic RT effects on hysteresis/viscous behavior are less consistently characterized than stiffness/modulus.

Canonical rule:

**viscoelasticity is real, but generic chronic RT direction remains lower-confidence than stiffness/material adaptation.**

No blocker.

## 8. Time-course audit

PASS.

The Achilles 12-week high-load study showed:

- stiffness and Young's modulus changes detectable by week 4;
- CSA increase detectable by week 8.

This demonstrates that material/mechanical adaptation can precede measurable morphology in at least some human protocols.

It does **not** establish a universal tendon clock.

Other studies show different adaptation patterns or little morphology change.

Canonical rule:

**tendon material, mechanical and morphological outcomes follow partially independent time courses.**

## 9. Load-tolerance audit

PASS.

“Load tolerance” is not one direct tissue measurement.

For project use it is an operational longitudinal construct involving:

- task demand;
- symptoms/function;
- tissue loading/stress/strain;
- recovery;
- repeated exposure response.

Canonical rule:

**tendon stiffness alone does not prove load tolerance, injury protection or readiness.**

Clinical/pathological tolerance questions remain MED.

## 10. Tendon RT audit

PASS.

Direct human longitudinal evidence supports tendon adaptation to resistance/mechanical loading.

Most consistently:

- stiffness increases;
- modulus increases;
- CSA can increase but more variably.

The 2026 Achilles NMA suggests favorable rankings for progressive higher-dose loading, including isometric plantar-flexion and plyometric approaches for selected outcomes.

But:

- some networks were sparse;
- estimates were heterogeneous/imprecise;
- mode rankings cannot be converted into one universal best tendon program.

Canonical rule:

**tendon adapts to RT, but the target property, tendon, local mechanics and protocol matter.**

## 11. Local strain / load-magnitude audit

PASS.

The lower-limb tendon meta-analysis found higher-localized-strain RT produced larger stiffness and modulus adaptations than lower-strain protocols.

Important boundary:

- external %1RM is only an indirect proxy;
- local tendon force/strain depends on muscle force, moment arms, joint position and task mechanics;
- “high load” and “high tendon strain” are not synonymous.

Canonical rule:

**local tendon mechanical stimulus is more direct than the external-load label.**

Detailed mechanics → MEC/TEC/PRO.

## 12. Ligament-evidence audit

PASS WITH EVIDENCE LIMITS.

The 2026 ACL review supports ligament mechanoresponsiveness through:

- ECM/collagen remodeling;
- load-history effects;
- animal exercise evidence for stiffness/strength/CSA changes;
- human observational morphology differences associated with long-term/high-load activity, especially when exposure begins during growth.

But direct adult-human longitudinal resistance-training dose-response evidence remains limited.

Canonical rule:

**ligament adaptation is biologically supported, but tendon prescription rules must not be transferred automatically to ligament.**

No blocker because the area explicitly requests ligament adaptation “within the evidence base.”

## 13. Fascia audit

PASS WITH ANTI-SPECULATION BOUNDARY.

Human fascia has established:

- anatomy;
- ECM;
- innervation;
- vascularization;
- mechanobiology.

But anatomy/mechanotransduction alone does not validate special chronic “fascia training.”

The 2025 six-week RCT found no significant intervention-related change in measured fascia thickness or echo intensity after stretching/eccentric interventions despite flexibility improvements.

Canonical rule:

**do not infer special chronic fascia remodeling prescriptions from acute mechanical responses or general fascial biology.**

No blocker.

## 14. Enthesis audit

PASS WITH LIMITED DIRECT HUMAN TRAINING EVIDENCE.

Mechanobiology supports load sensitivity of tendon/enthesis development, maintenance and repair signaling.

The 2025 review identifies multiple mechanotransduction pathways in tendon/enthesis biology.

But it does not provide a controlled adult-human RT dose-response for enthesis morphology/material adaptation.

Canonical rule:

**enthesis is mechanoresponsive; adult-human training prescription remains insufficiently defined.**

No blocker.

## 15. Muscle–tendon mismatch audit

PASS.

Human adolescent-athlete evidence demonstrates that muscle and tendon adaptation can become imbalanced.

But current evidence does not justify:

**muscle always adapts faster than tendon.**

The 2026 paired meta-analysis found average tendon-stiffness changes were not obviously smaller than strength changes, although causal interpretation was limited.

Canonical rule:

**muscle and tendon are separate adaptive compartments with different stimulus sensitivity and outcome clocks.**

Potential mismatch is real; direction and magnitude are context specific.

## 16. Why time course matters audit

PASS.

Performance/strength may improve before every connective-tissue dimension has adapted.

Conversely, tendon material properties can change rapidly in some protocols.

Therefore performance gain is not a universal readiness surrogate for:

- tendon morphology;
- ligament adaptation;
- enthesis adaptation;
- fascia remodeling.

Canonical rule:

**progression must not assume that one improving performance metric proves synchronized adaptation across all tissues.**

Exact progression rules remain PRO/MED/IND.

Result: **PASS.**

Canonical synthesis:

`repeated local mechanical loading`
`→ tissue-specific mechanotransduction / ECM turnover`
`→ material + mechanical + morphological adaptation on partly independent clocks`
`→ altered force transmission / strain behavior / task tolerance`.

For tendon, direct human longitudinal evidence is sufficient for practical physiology.

For ligament, fascia and enthesis, direct adult-human chronic-training evidence is substantially thinner, so uncertainty is preserved and tendon evidence is not generalized automatically.
