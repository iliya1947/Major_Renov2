# 2. Canonical scientific target

Dimensional Analysis asks, in Schatzman's characteristic logic:

**What all is involved in this phenomenon, what dimensions and properties make it intelligible, from which perspective is it best explained, and how do context, conditions, processes and consequences relate around that perspective?**

The target is a **grounded explanatory account / situation-specific theory** of a socially constructed phenomenon.

### Canonical project representation

empirical data
→ dimensionalization
→ dimensions + properties
→ comparison/differentiation
→ perspective selection
→ explanatory matrix
→ context / conditions / processes / consequences
→ integration/reintegration
→ substantive/situation-specific theory.

### Critical rule

Dimensional Analysis is not merely:

> finding several dimensions of a topic.

Full DA uses those dimensions to construct an explanatory theoretical account.

---

# 3. Genealogy

Leonard Schatzman developed DA in the grounded-theory tradition.

Schatzman was closely connected to Anselm Strauss and shared roots in:

- symbolic interactionism;
- pragmatism;
- naturalistic inquiry;
- constant comparative analysis.

Kools et al. explicitly describe DA as:

> an alternate method for the generation of grounded theory.

### Critical rule

For taxonomy, classify DA as:

**GT-derived methodology / grounded-theory variant**

rather than as generic qualitative content analysis.

---

# 4. Why Schatzman developed DA

A core methodological problem for Schatzman was:

> What actually happens analytically between raw qualitative data and the final explanatory theory?

He considered parts of conventional GT analysis insufficiently explicit for teaching and tracing the researcher's reasoning.

DA therefore tries to make the analytic discovery process more visible.

### Project implication

One strength of DA is **analytic traceability**:

- what dimensions were considered;
- how their properties were expanded;
- which perspective was selected;
- why some dimensions became conditions/context/process/consequences.

---

# 5. Natural analysis

Schatzman described a process of **natural analysis**.

The underlying idea:

People routinely make sense of complex situations by:

- noticing aspects;
- naming them;
- comparing them;
- assessing importance;
- viewing them from different perspectives;
- deciding how parts fit together.

DA systematizes this everyday analytic capacity for research.

### Critical rule

Natural analysis does not mean:

> intuition alone is sufficient.

Research DA makes this process explicit, comparative, iterative and evidence-grounded.

---

# 6. Dimensionality

**Dimensionality** refers to the researcher's capacity to recognize the complexity of a phenomenon through:

- dimensions;
- attributes;
- properties;
- relationships;
- context;
- different possible perspectives.

### Example

Phenomenon:
> exercise adherence after rehabilitation.

Possible dimensions might include:

- pain;
- confidence;
- clinician support;
- access;
- identity;
- schedule;
- family;
- beliefs about exercise.

Dimensionality means not prematurely reducing the phenomenon to one explanation such as:

> motivation.

---

# 7. Dimensionalization

Dimensionalization is the early analytic expansion of data into multiple possible aspects and properties.

Whitney & Evered 2025 define dimensionalizing as an analytic technique that expands a code/concept into its:

- attributes;
- context;
- meaning;
- other dimensions/properties.

### Canonical question

> What all is involved here?

### Critical rule

Dimensionalization is broader than simply assigning one code per passage.

It deliberately expands conceptual possibilities before deciding which are explanatory.

---

# 8. Dimensionalization is now portable beyond full DA

Current 2025 methodology explicitly argues that **dimensionalization can be borrowed as an analytic technique** across qualitative and mixed-method traditions.

Whitney & Evered describe five modes:

1. in vivo;
2. collaborative;
3. proliferative;
4. metaphorical;
5. graphic dimensionalization.

### Major taxonomy rule

**dimensionalization technique ≠ Dimensional Analysis methodology.**

A thematic-analysis study can dimensionalize a concept without becoming a full Schatzman DA study.

---

# 9. In vivo dimensionalization

The analyst expands dimensions close to participants' own language/data.

Useful for:

- preserving meanings;
- avoiding premature abstraction;
- generating candidate properties.

### Rule

In vivo dimensionalization is one technique.

It is not equivalent to Glaserian in-vivo coding and does not by itself create grounded theory.

---

# 10. Collaborative dimensionalization

Multiple researchers/stakeholders can jointly expand the possible dimensions of a concept.

Potential value:

- exposes disciplinary blind spots;
- increases conceptual breadth;
- makes analytic assumptions visible.

### Critical boundary

Collaborative dimensionalization does not require coder consensus.

Its aim may be **proliferating possible dimensions**, not proving one correct interpretation.

---

# 11. Proliferative dimensionalization

The analytic goal is deliberately to generate many plausible dimensions/properties before narrowing.

### Value

Reduces premature closure.

### Rule

A long list is not the final product.

Later differentiation/integration must determine explanatory relevance.

---

# 12. Metaphorical / graphic dimensionalization

Metaphors and graphics can help researchers:

- see relations;
- reorganize dimensions;
- uncover implicit properties;
- challenge habitual framing.

### Critical rule

The metaphor/map is an analytic prompt.

It is not evidence by itself.

---

# 13. Designation

In classic DA, early analysis involves **designation**:

> naming data bits / aspects as dimensions.

A dimension is a conceptual component of the phenomenon.

Dimensions can then be expanded through their properties.

### Example

Data:
> “I stopped walking because I was waiting for physio to tell me it was safe.”

Potential dimensions:

- responsibility;
- safety;
- professional authority;
- waiting;
- mobility;
- interprofessional dependency.

### Rule

Designation is provisional.

Initial naming does not determine the final explanatory role.

---

# 14. Dimensions and properties

A dimension can be differentiated through **properties/attributes**.

Example:

Dimension:
> clinician support.

Possible properties:
- frequency;
- accessibility;
- emotional tone;
- technical specificity;
- autonomy support;
- continuity.

### Scientific value

This helps transform broad topics into analytically discriminating concepts.

---

# 15. Differentiation

As data accumulate, dimensions are compared and differentiated.

The researcher asks:

- Which dimensions are similar/different?
- Which are central/peripheral?
- Under what circumstances do they matter?
- What properties distinguish variants?
- Which dimensions explain other dimensions?

### Critical rule

Differentiation is not just splitting codes into subcodes.

Its purpose is to clarify **explanatory relevance and relationships**.

---

# 16. Constant comparison

DA retains the GT constant-comparative engine.

Researchers compare:

- incident ↔ incident;
- incident ↔ dimension;
- dimension ↔ dimension;
- case ↔ case;
- emerging explanation ↔ new data.

### Rule

DA should remain iterative.

A one-time post-hoc sorting of already finalized themes into a matrix is weaker evidence of full DA.

---

# 17. Perspective

A hallmark of DA is selection of a **perspective**.

Among the developed dimensions, one perspective is selected because it has strong explanatory power for organizing the phenomenon.

The perspective functions as a vantage point from which other dimensions become interpretable.

### Critical rule

Perspective ≠ participant demographic viewpoint automatically.

It is an **analytic perspective/dimension with explanatory power**.

---

# 18. Perspective is not simply the most frequent dimension

A dimension becomes perspective because of its ability to organize/explain relationships, not because it appears most often.

### Project rule

Do not infer:

> highest code frequency = perspective.

Conceptual centrality and frequency are different.

---

# 19. Multiple possible perspectives

An important Schatzman feature is that dimensions can initially be **rotated** through the perspective position.

Researchers can ask:

> What happens to the explanation if dimension A is central?

Then:

> What if dimension B is the perspective instead?

### Scientific value

This makes the perspectival nature of qualitative explanation explicit.

### Rule

The final perspective should be selected because it produces the most coherent/productive grounded explanation, not because it was assumed before analysis.

---

# 20. Researcher perspective

DA explicitly recognizes that researchers bring:

- disciplinary;
- biographical;
- professional;

perspectives to analysis.

This differs from any naive idea that theory simply “emerges” independently of the analyst.

### Critical rule

Researcher perspective should be examined reflexively.

DA does not require pretending the analyst is perspective-free.

---

# 21. Explanatory matrix

The **explanatory matrix** is DA's signature integrating architecture.

Its major components are:

- perspective;
- dimensions/properties;
- context;
- conditions;
- processes/actions/interactions;
- consequences.

### Scientific function

The matrix moves analysis from:

> inventory/description of dimensions

toward:

> explanation of how those dimensions relate.

---

# 22. Context

**Context** specifies the environment/situation/background in which the phenomenon and relationships occur.

It helps define:

- boundaries;
- relevant setting;
- actors;
- circumstances.

### Example

Exercise adherence context:
- home vs clinic;
- post-discharge;
- limited equipment;
- chronic pain;
- older age.

### Critical rule

Context should not become a generic dumping category for everything that does not fit elsewhere.

Its role is relative to the selected perspective.

---

# 23. Conditions

**Conditions** are dimensions that:

- facilitate;
- block;
- shape;

actions/interactions/processes.

### Example

Possible conditions:
- pain flare;
- transport;
- social support;
- therapist clearance;
- fear;
- staffing.

### Critical rule

“Condition” in DA is an explanatory role in the matrix.

It is not automatically a formally identified causal exposure.

---

# 24. Processes

**Processes** are actions/interactions that occur under the identified conditions.

They can be:

- intended;
- unintended;
- interpersonal;
- organizational;
- adaptive.

### Example

- seeking reassurance;
- avoiding loading;
- modifying exercise;
- negotiating responsibility;
- resuming activity.

### Rule

Process in DA is conceptually explanatory, not necessarily a time-series causal mechanism.

---

# 25. Consequences

**Consequences** are outcomes of the processes within the explanatory account.

Possible consequences:

- continued exercise;
- disengagement;
- waiting;
- confidence;
- dependency;
- functional activity.

### Critical rule

A DA consequence is not automatically a quantitatively demonstrated causal effect.

It represents the consequence **within the grounded explanatory theory**.

---

# 26. Roles are perspective-dependent

The same dimension can change role depending on the chosen perspective.

A concept can function as:

- context in one explanatory frame;
- condition in another;
- consequence in another.

### Example

Pain can be:

- context;
- condition shaping activity;
- consequence of an action;

depending on the analytic perspective.

### Critical rule

The matrix is not an ontology declaring one permanent role for each concept.

---

# 27. Integration / reintegration

After perspective and matrix roles are developed, the researcher integrates or reintegrates dimensions into a coherent explanatory account.

The final work asks:

- How do these dimensions fit together?
- Which relations best explain the phenomenon?
- What configuration is most grounded/productive?

### Output

A:

- substantive theory;
- situation-specific theory;
- conceptual explanatory model.

### Rule

Full DA should move beyond a taxonomy of dimensions into integrated explanation.

---

# 28. Explanatory matrix as tool and product

Applied DA literature describes the explanatory matrix as both:

- an analytic tool;
- a final organizing theoretical product.

Researchers may rotate candidate dimensions through the perspective position before selecting the most fruitful configuration.

### Critical rule

A matrix drawn after analysis only for presentation is not evidence that DA drove the analytic process.

---

# 29. Memoing

Like GT, DA benefits from analytic memoing.

Memos can document:

- dimension emergence;
- properties;
- alternative perspectives;
- why roles changed;
- matrix configurations;
- contradictory evidence;
- theoretical questions.

### Project rule

Memoing strengthens the traceability of how perspective selection and integration occurred.

---

# 30. Theoretical sampling

Full grounded DA can use theoretical sampling.

New data are sought to:

- develop dimensions;
- differentiate properties;
- test relationships;
- clarify the perspective;
- examine contradictions.

### Critical rule

Theoretical sampling must be driven by the emerging explanation.

Predefined purposive sampling alone does not satisfy this function.

---

# 31. Saturation / adequacy

DA's stopping logic is closer to grounded-theory conceptual sufficiency than to generic thematic repetition.

Adequacy concerns whether:

- important dimensions are developed;
- properties are differentiated;
- key relationships are supported;
- perspective is explanatory;
- matrix is coherent;
- further sampling materially changes theory.

### Rule

“No new interview topics” is not enough to establish DA adequacy.

---

# 32. Designation → differentiation → integration

For project taxonomy, a useful simplified DA progression is:

1. **Designation**
   - identify/name dimensions.
2. **Differentiation**
   - expand properties, compare dimensions, determine relevance and relationships.
3. **Integration / reintegration**
   - organize dimensions around an explanatory perspective/matrix to construct theory.

### Caveat

Applied papers use somewhat varying phase labels and coding procedures.

This sequence is a robust conceptual summary, not one universal mechanical protocol.

---

# 33. DA vs Straussian GT

Both share:

- symbolic-interactionist roots;
- constant comparison;
- conditions/actions/consequences language;
- theory-generation intent;
- iterative analysis.

## Straussian GT

Typical architecture:
- open coding;
- axial coding;
- selective coding;
- categories and relationships;
- central/core category.

## DA

Typical architecture:
- dimensionalization;
- many dimensions/properties;
- explicit perspective;
- explanatory matrix;
- rotation/differentiation/integration.

### Critical rule

The explanatory matrix should not simply be relabelled as Strauss/Corbin axial coding.

They overlap historically but are not identical methodological structures.

---

# 34. DA vs Glaserian GT

Glaserian GT typically emphasizes:

- emergence;
- substantive/theoretical coding;
- avoidance of forcing data into predefined paradigms.

DA more explicitly foregrounds:

- perspective;
- systematic dimensional expansion;
- explanatory matrix.

### Rule

A Glaserian study using dimensional language is not automatically DA.

---

# 35. DA vs constructivist GT

Both can acknowledge the role of researcher interpretation.

Constructivist GT foregrounds:

- co-construction;
- reflexivity;
- participant/researcher meanings.

DA foregrounds:

- dimensionality;
- multiple perspectives;
- explanatory organization.

### Rule

They can be philosophically adjacent but should remain distinct routes when the authors explicitly use Schatzman DA.

---

# 36. DA vs Situational Analysis

Both emerge from GT-related traditions and value complexity.

## Dimensional Analysis

Core product:
- perspective-centered explanatory matrix.

## Situational Analysis

Core product:
- situational maps;
- social worlds/arenas;
- positional maps;
- heterogeneous relational ecology.

### Critical rule

DA seeks an integrated explanatory perspective.

SA deliberately preserves wider positional/material/discursive complexity and does not require one central perspective.

---

# 37. Full Dimensional Analysis vs dimensionalization technique

This is the most important modern boundary.

## Full DA

Requires a methodology-level theory-building architecture such as:

- explicit Schatzman/Kools lineage;
- iterative dimensionalizing;
- dimensions/properties;
- perspective;
- explanatory matrix;
- integration toward theory.

## Dimensionalization technique

Can simply expand:

- properties;
- contexts;
- meanings;

inside another methodology.

### Project rule

Never classify a study as full DA merely because it says:

> “we examined dimensions of the data.”

---

# 38. DA vs dimensional concept analysis

Caron & Bowers adapted dimensional analysis ideas for **concept analysis**, often using published literature rather than primary participant data.

Examples:

- value of nursing care;
- patient-safety culture;
- informed consent;
- neglect.

### Critical boundary

Dimensional concept analysis of literature is not automatically a **primary qualitative DA study**.

Correct taxonomy:

- source = literature/documents;
- purpose = concept analysis;
- dimensional-analysis framework.

### Project rule

Do not mix these studies into the primary qualitative design family without preserving the concept-analysis layer.

---

# 39. DA vs thematic analysis

Thematic analysis can identify patterns/themes.

DA requires more:

- dimensional properties;
- perspectival reasoning;
- explanatory matrix;
- theoretical integration.

### Rule

Themes → matrix is not enough if perspective and explanatory relationships were not actually developed.

---

# 40. DA vs framework analysis

Framework analysis often organizes data in a matrix across:

- cases;
- codes/themes.

DA's explanatory matrix is conceptually different.

It organizes **theoretical roles and relationships**:

- perspective;
- context;
- conditions;
- processes;
- consequences.

### Critical rule

Both use matrices, but they are not the same analysis method.

---

# 41. DA vs case study

A case study may use DA to develop theory within a bounded case.

Direct physiotherapy example:

- parent design = single theory-seeking case study;
- analysis = Dimensional Analysis;
- outcome = substantive theory of learning transition.

### Rule

Store both layers:

case-study architecture
+
DA analytic/theory-building methodology.

---

# 42. DA vs narrative analysis

Recovery narratives can be analyzed using DA, as Jacobson did.

But the narrative itself is then treated as data for dimensional/process analysis.

If:

- plot;
- story structure;
- identity storytelling;

is the main object, narrative inquiry is a better parent route.

### Rule

Narrative data ≠ narrative methodology.

---

# 43. Quality architecture

Strong full DA should demonstrate:

1. explicit Schatzman/Kools lineage;
2. theory/explanation-oriented question;
3. simultaneous/iterative data collection-analysis where possible;
4. dimensionalization;
5. dimensions and properties;
6. constant comparison;
7. reflexivity about researcher perspective;
8. perspective selection;
9. explanatory matrix;
10. context/conditions/processes/consequences distinguished relative to perspective;
11. theoretical sampling or other adequate dimension development;
12. integration/reintegration;
13. traceability from data to theory;
14. negative/contradictory data;
15. claims calibrated to substantive/situation-specific theory.

---

# 44. Perspective selection is a quality bottleneck

Because the perspective organizes the entire matrix, weak justification can make DA arbitrary.

Appraisal should ask:

- What candidate perspectives were considered?
- Why was this one selected?
- What explanatory work did it perform?
- Did contradictory data fit?
- Were alternatives explored?

### Rule

If the perspective appears imposed from prior theory without empirical interrogation, groundedness is weaker.

---

# 45. Researcher reflexivity

DA explicitly makes perspective important.

Therefore researchers should examine:

- disciplinary background;
- professional experience;
- prior conceptual commitments;
- assumptions shaping what dimensions they can see.

### Critical rule

Expertise is not simply bias to remove.

But unexplored expertise can narrow dimensionality.

---

# 46. Empirical grounding vs exhaustive dimensionality

No qualitative analysis can identify every possible dimension of a phenomenon.

The target is a sufficiently rich and explanatory dimensional account.

### Rule

Avoid claims such as:

> “all dimensions of exercise adherence were identified.”

Prefer:

> “the analysis developed an explanatory set of dimensions grounded in this sample/context.”

---

# 47. Claims full DA can support

DA can support contextual qualitative claims about:

- dimensions/properties of a phenomenon;
- perspectives;
- social processes;
- conditions shaping action;
- consequences within the grounded explanation;
- relationships among dimensions;
- substantive/situation-specific theory;
- plausible explanatory pathways.

---

# 48. Claims DA cannot establish by itself

DA alone does not establish:

- population prevalence;
- incidence;
- average treatment effect;
- diagnostic accuracy;
- prediction performance;
- biological mechanism;
- formal causal mediation;
- quantitative probability that condition X causes consequence Y.

### Critical rule

Terms such as:

- condition;
- consequence;
- explanatory;

in the matrix are **qualitative theoretical roles**, not automatically causal estimands.

---

# 49. Physiotherapy exemplar — learning transition toward clinical expertise

A physiotherapy study used:

- naturalistic inquiry;
- single theory-seeking case study;
- 26 semi-structured interviews;
- 11 alumni of an MACP-accredited MSc;
- dimensional analysis.

The goal was to explain how physiotherapists' practice developed after advanced Master's-level education.

The resulting substantive theory described transition from:

- uncritical/routine therapist-centered practice

toward:

- critical understanding of practice knowledge;
- patient-centered practice;
- capability to learn in/from practice.

### Methodological lesson

This is a strong direct rehabilitation example of:

case study
+
DA theory-building analysis.

---

# 50. Mobility/ambulation exemplar

A grounded dimensional analysis of hospital nurses examined responsibility for ambulating older patients.

Two differing perspectives organized action:

- claiming responsibility;
- attributing responsibility elsewhere.

These perspectives were linked to different processes such as:

- collaborating with physical therapy;
- assessing activity orders/risk/resources;
- waiting for PT/medicine clearance.

### Fitness/rehabilitation relevance

The phenomenon is directly related to:

- mobility;
- physical function;
- interprofessional exercise/activity decisions.

### Critical boundary

The qualitative finding explains action patterns.

It does not estimate the causal effect of nurse responsibility beliefs on mobility outcomes.

---

# 51. Rehabilitation-recovery exemplar

Jacobson applied dimensional analysis to recovery narratives in psychiatric rehabilitation.

Four central dimensions were reported:

- self;
- others;
- system;
- problem.

Associated processes included:

- recognizing the problem;
- transforming the self;
- reconciling the system;
- reaching out to others.

### Project lesson

DA can use narrative material to construct an explanatory dimensional model without becoming narrative inquiry.

---

# 52. Serious-illness exemplar

A dimensional analysis of inner strength in ageing with serious illness explicitly used:

- symbolic interactionism;
- dimensionalizing;
- coding;
- explanatory matrix.

The final matrix organized:

- perspective;
- context;
- conditions;
- processes;
- consequences.

### Methodological value

This contemporary open-access example provides unusually clear documentation of full DA structure.

# 53. Direct sport evidence status

A targeted search found a coach moral-reasoning dissertation using dimensional-analysis/grounded-theory logic, but this is weaker evidence than peer-reviewed methodology and rehabilitation studies.

Therefore:

**peer-reviewed sport-specific full-DA evidence was not established strongly enough in this pass to use as a canonical exemplar.**

Fitness relevance is instead supported directly through:

- physiotherapy expertise development;
- hospital mobility/ambulation;
- rehabilitation/recovery.

### Project statement

Sport-specific applicability is conceptually plausible, but **Информация не проверена** for a strong current peer-reviewed full-DA sport exemplar.

# 54. Candidate fitness questions suited to DA

Examples:

- What dimensions organize how lifters decide whether pain is acceptable during training?
- From which perspective can exercise adherence after rehabilitation best be explained?
- What conditions/processes/consequences organize a coach's decision to reduce training load?

The study should seek an **integrated explanatory theory**, not merely list themes/dimensions.

# 56. Do-not-confuse matrix

| Do not confuse | Why |
|---|---|
| dimensionalization vs full Dimensional Analysis | portable technique ≠ methodology |
| dimension vs ordinary theme | DA dimensions are developed through properties/relations |
| designation vs final category | early naming is provisional |
| differentiation vs subcoding only | explanatory relevance/relations matter |
| perspective vs participant demographic viewpoint | analytic organizing vantage point |
| perspective vs most frequent code | explanatory power ≠ frequency |
| perspective vs preselected theory | should be grounded/interrogated |
| explanatory matrix vs Framework Method matrix | theoretical roles vs case-by-code display |
| explanatory matrix vs Straussian axial paradigm | related history but distinct methodology |
| context vs miscellaneous background | relative role in explanatory configuration |
