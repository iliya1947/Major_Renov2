# ANA-12 — Нервная анатомия движения

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-18**  
Область: 02 — Анатомия

## 0. Decision

Создать structural nerve map, достаточную для последующего различения muscle-level, root-level, plexus-level and named-peripheral-nerve explanations без:

- root/ramus conflation;
- one-root/one-muscle simplification;
- dermatome = peripheral cutaneous territory simplification;
- anatomy → diagnosis overclaim;
- premature expansion into detailed neurophysiology.

## 1. Canonical neural chain

`spinal cord segment → dorsal/ventral roots → mixed spinal nerve → dorsal/ventral rami → plexus → named peripheral nerve → terminal branch → muscle/skin territory`

Project rule:

**each level answers a different anatomical question.**

## 2. Spinal cord — minimum structure

Movement-relevant structure:

- gray matter within white matter;
- ventral/anterior horn → somatic lower-motor-neuron cell bodies;
- dorsal/posterior horn → sensory-processing region;
- paired spinal roots arise segmentally.

Detailed central motor/sensory tracts → NEU.

## 3. Dorsal vs ventral roots

### Dorsal root
Primarily sensory/afferent.

Dorsal-root ganglion contains sensory-neuron cell bodies.

### Ventral root
Primarily motor/efferent, with autonomic efferents at relevant levels.

Dorsal + ventral roots unite into:
- short mixed spinal nerve.

Project rule:

**root ≠ ramus.**

## 4. Mixed spinal nerve and rami

After roots unite, the mixed spinal nerve divides into:

### Dorsal ramus
Mixed sensory + motor.

Broad supply:
- intrinsic back muscles;
- posterior skin;
- spinal-joint branches.

### Ventral ramus
Mixed sensory + motor.

Broad supply:
- anterolateral trunk;
- limbs;
- major somatic plexuses.

Project rule:

**roots are pre-spinal-nerve structures; rami are post-spinal-nerve branches.**

## 5. Cervical exit convention

- C1–C7 nerves exit above correspondingly numbered vertebrae;
- C8 exits between C7 and T1;
- from T1 downward, nerves exit below correspondingly numbered vertebrae.

Lower roots descend before exiting as cauda-equina rootlets.

## 6. Plexus concept

Limb plexuses form mainly from **ventral rami**.

Consequences:

1. one peripheral nerve usually contains axons from multiple spinal levels;
2. one spinal root contributes to multiple peripheral nerves;
3. root-level and peripheral-nerve maps are not interchangeable.

Project rule:

**plexus recombination prevents one-root/one-nerve thinking.**

## 7. Brachial plexus

Typical organization:

`C5–T1 ventral rami → trunks → divisions → cords → terminal nerves`

### Trunks
- superior C5–C6;
- middle C7;
- inferior C8–T1.

### Cords
- lateral;
- posterior;
- medial.

### Main terminal nerves
- musculocutaneous;
- axillary;
- radial;
- median;
- ulnar.

Critical terminology:

**brachial-plexus “roots” are ventral rami, not dorsal/ventral spinal roots.**

## 8. Axillary nerve

Typical:
- posterior cord;
- mainly C5–C6.

Motor:
- deltoid;
- teres minor.

Sensory:
- superior/lateral shoulder region.

## 9. Musculocutaneous nerve

Typical:
- lateral cord;
- C5–C7.

Motor:
- coracobrachialis;
- biceps brachii;
- brachialis.

Sensory continuation:
- lateral cutaneous nerve of forearm.

Project rule:

**not a “biceps-only” nerve.**

## 10. Radial nerve

Typical:
- posterior cord;
- broad C5–T1 contribution.

Motor:
- posterior arm;
- posterior forearm/extensor-supinator system;
- brachioradialis.

Distal:
- superficial branch → mainly sensory;
- deep/posterior interosseous pathway → mainly motor.

Broad sensory:
- posterior arm/forearm;
- dorsolateral hand.

## 11. Median nerve

Typical:
- lateral + medial cord contributions;
- broad C5–T1 contribution.

Motor:
- most forearm flexor-pronator muscles;
- thenar motor system;
- lateral lumbricals.

Sensory:
- lateral palm/digits.

Important branches include:
- anterior interosseous;
- palmar cutaneous;
- recurrent motor branch.

Project rule:

**median nerve territory is broader than carpal tunnel.**

## 12. Ulnar nerve

Typical:
- medial cord;
- mainly C8–T1.

Motor:
- FCU;
- medial FDP;
- most intrinsic hand muscles.

Sensory:
- medial hand;
- little finger and ulnar side of ring finger.

Project rule:

**ulnar nerve is not intrinsic-hand-only.**

## 13. Upper-limb nerve variation

Textbook brachial-plexus/peripheral-nerve maps are modal.

Relevant normal variation includes:
- prefixed/postfixed plexus;
- terminal-branch variation;
- median–ulnar communications;
- other nerve anastomoses.

Project rule:

**variant wiring cannot be inferred from symptoms or atlas mismatch alone.**

## 14. Lumbosacral plexus

### Lumbar plexus
Typical:
- L1–L4 ventral rami;
- variable T12 contribution.

Major lower-limb nerves:
- femoral;
- obturator;
- lateral femoral cutaneous.

### Lumbosacral trunk
- mainly L4–L5.

### Sacral plexus
Broadly:
- lumbosacral trunk + sacral ventral rami.

Important branches:
- sciatic;
- superior/inferior gluteal;
- other pelvic branches.

Project rule:

**lumbosacral plexus is a recombining network, not a one-root/one-nerve cable.**

## 15. Femoral nerve

Typical:
- posterior divisions of L2–L4.

Motor:
- quadriceps;
- sartorius;
- iliacus;
- variable pectineus contribution.

Sensory:
- anterior thigh;
- medial leg/foot through saphenous nerve.

Project rule:

**femoral nerve territory extends beyond quadriceps.**

## 16. Obturator nerve

Typical:
- anterior divisions L2–L4.

Motor:
- major adductor compartment.

Sensory/articular:
- small/variable medial-thigh cutaneous territory;
- hip/knee articular contribution.

Project rule:

**obturator sensory territory is variable and not a large fixed dermatome-like field.**

## 17. Sciatic nerve

Typical:
- L4–S3.

Structurally contains:
- tibial component;
- common fibular component.

Course:
- pelvis → posterior thigh;
- commonly inferior to piriformis;
- divides variably into tibial/common-fibular branches.

Project rule:

**division level and piriformis relationship vary normally.**

## 18. Tibial nerve

Typical:
- L4–S3.

Motor:
- posterior leg;
- plantar intrinsic-foot system via plantar branches.

Broad actions:
- plantarflexion;
- inversion contribution;
- toe flexion;
- intrinsic plantar-foot actions.

Sensory:
- plantar foot;
- heel branches;
- sural contribution.

## 19. Common fibular nerve

Typical:
- L4–S2.

Course:
- lateral popliteal region;
- around fibular head/neck.

### Superficial fibular
Motor:
- fibularis longus/brevis.

Sensory:
- most dorsum of foot and anterolateral distal leg.

### Deep fibular
Motor:
- anterior compartment;
- toe extensors;
- dorsal intrinsic extensor system.

Sensory:
- first dorsal web space.

## 20. Root map vs peripheral nerve map

A movement is usually produced by:
- several muscles;
- multiple root levels;
- sometimes several peripheral nerves.

Project rule:

**movement localization requires keeping root, plexus, nerve and muscle maps separate.**

## 21. Myotome

A myotome is an overlapping segmental motor map.

Critical caveats:
- most muscles receive multiple root contributions;
- one root contributes to multiple muscles/nerves;
- published tables differ;
- “key muscle” is a testing convention, not exclusive ownership.

Project rule:

**myotome ≠ one root = one muscle.**

## 22. Dermatome

A dermatome is a skin territory supplied predominantly by one spinal segment/root.

Critical caveats:
- adjacent dermatomes overlap;
- maps differ;
- individual variation exists;
- dermatomes are not identical to named peripheral cutaneous territories.

Project rule:

**dermatome maps are localization aids, not exact personal borders.**

## 23. Peripheral cutaneous territory

Peripheral cutaneous nerve territory is defined after plexus recombination.

Therefore:
- median-hand territory ≠ one dermatome;
- radial first-web-space territory ≠ one root map.

Project rule:

**never substitute a named peripheral sensory territory for a dermatome map.**

## 24. Motor unit

Canonical structural definition:

**one α motor neuron + all extrafusal skeletal-muscle fibers it innervates.**

Path:
`ventral-horn α motor neuron → ventral root → peripheral nerve → intramuscular branches → NMJ → extrafusal fibers`

Recruitment/rate coding/synchronization → NEU.

## 25. Neuromuscular junction

NMJ:
- motor-axon terminal;
- skeletal-muscle fiber motor end plate.

Canonical transmitter:
- acetylcholine.

Transmission physiology → PHY/NEU.

## 26. Muscle spindle

Muscle spindle:
- encapsulated proprioceptive receptor within skeletal muscle;
- contains intrafusal fibers;
- has sensory afferent endings;
- receives γ-motor innervation.

Classical sensory groups:
- Ia;
- II.

Project rule:

**spindle is not an ordinary extrafusal force-producing motor unit.**

Detailed reflex/firing physiology → NEU.

## 27. Golgi tendon organ

GTO:
- mechanosensory/proprioceptive structure associated with tendon/musculotendinous connective tissue;
- classical Ib afferent organization.

Project rule:

**GTO is not a simple “safety switch that shuts off muscle”.**

Central effects belong to NEU.

## 28. Spindle vs GTO

### Spindle
- intramuscular;
- intrafusal fibers;
- length/velocity-related sensing;
- Ia/II afferents;
- γ-motor control.

### GTO
- tendon/musculotendinous;
- force/tension-related sensing;
- Ib afferent organization.

Both are proprioceptive structures; their neural effects are more complex than simple training myths.

## 29. Normal variation

Relevant variation includes:
- brachial-plexus root pattern;
- terminal-branch communications;
- median–ulnar anastomoses;
- lumbar/lumbosacral plexus branching;
- sciatic division/course;
- myotome/dermatome borders;
- cutaneous territories.

Project rule:

**textbook maps are modal, not identical in every individual.**

Variation alone ≠ pathology.

## 30. Neural-symptom boundary

Anatomy can support localization hypotheses among:
- muscle/tendon;
- peripheral nerve;
- plexus;
- spinal root;
- central level.

But:

**ANA-12 does not diagnose radiculopathy, plexopathy, entrapment or central disease.**

Diagnostic/red-flag decisions → MED/TST.

## 31. Canonical project model

1. Root ≠ spinal nerve ≠ ramus.
2. Plexuses are formed from ventral rami.
3. Peripheral nerves combine multiple root levels.
4. Brachial-plexus “roots” are ventral rami.
5. Upper-limb terminal nerves have broad motor/sensory territories with normal communications/variation.
6. Lumbosacral plexus similarly recombines root levels into lower-limb nerves.
7. Myotome and dermatome are overlapping segmental maps.
8. Segmental maps ≠ named peripheral-nerve maps.
9. Motor unit = α motor neuron + its extrafusal fibers.
10. Muscle spindle = intrafusal proprioceptor.
11. GTO = tendon/musculotendinous proprioceptor.
12. Structural maps guide localization but do not establish diagnosis.

## 32. Evidence / confidence

### HIGH
- root/spinal-nerve/ramus organization;
- ventral-ramus plexus formation;
- major brachial/lumbosacral plexus organization;
- broad major peripheral-nerve territories;
- myotome/dermatome distinction;
- motor-unit structural definition;
- spindle/GTO structural definitions.

### MODERATE / MAP-DEPENDENT
- exact individual root contribution;
- exact dermatome/myotome borders;
- exact cutaneous territory borders;
- frequency of variant communications;
- exact individual sciatic course/division.
