# NEU-05 — Neural force production / voluntary activation / RFD

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-19**  
Область: 04 — Neurophysiology / Motor Learning

## 0. Core decision

Human force production is a neuromuscular/mechanical outcome.

Canonical model:

`motor-unit recruitment + rate coding`
`→ ensemble neural drive`
`→ task-specific muscle coordination`
interacts with
`muscle contractile capacity + tendon/joint mechanics + technique`
`→ external force / steadiness / RFD / power`.

## 1. Recruitment and rate coding

Force control uses both:
- recruitment of additional motor units;
- discharge-rate modulation of active units.

Project rule:

**neither recruitment nor rate coding alone explains force production.**

## 2. Neural drive

Neural drive is:

`ensemble discharge of active spinal motor neurons transmitted to muscle fibers`.

Project rule:

**neural drive ≠ surface EMG amplitude.**

EMG is an indirect proxy affected by neural, membrane, geometric and processing factors.

## 3. Voluntary activation

VA estimates incomplete voluntary activation using external stimulation.

Project rule:

**VA ≠ neural drive ≠ cortical activation ≠ percent of the nervous system used.**

VA is muscle/method/protocol dependent.

## 4. Agonist activation

Agonist activation contributes to target torque.

Project rule:

**agonist EMG ≠ agonist force.**

Muscle force also depends on length, velocity, moment arm, contractile state and muscle-tendon mechanics.

## 5. Antagonist coactivation

Coactivation can:
- reduce net agonist torque;
- increase stiffness/stability;
- support movement control.

Project rule:

**less antagonist activity is not universally better.**

## 6. Synergist / intermuscular coordination

Muscle contributions and timing are task dependent and trainable.

Project rule:

**coordination adaptation may alter timing/coupling/weighting without replacing the whole movement strategy.**

## 7. Intramuscular coordination

Intramuscular coordination refers to motor-unit population control:

- recruitment;
- rate coding;
- common input;
- variability;
- timing.

Project rule:

**intramuscular coordination is not a mystical “mind-muscle” mechanism.**

## 8. Force steadiness

Force steadiness reflects fluctuations around a target force.

Project rule:

**force steadiness ≠ maximal strength.**

It is strongly influenced by common-input and motor-unit-discharge variability.

## 9. Rate of force development

RFD:

`Δforce / Δtime`.

Early RFD:
- strongly constrained by rapid neural activation.

Later RFD:
- increasingly influenced by maximal-force and muscle-tendon/contractile properties.

Project rule:

**RFD must always be reported/interpreted with a time window.**

## 10. Explosive voluntary activation

Explosive activation is the rapid rise in voluntary neural activation after contraction onset.

Project rule:

**explosive activation ≠ plateau VA during MVC.**

## 11. Maximal strength

Maximal strength depends on:
- neural output/coordination;
- muscle size/architecture;
- contractile properties;
- tendon/joint mechanics;
- technique.

Project rule:

**maximal strength is not a pure neural trait.**

## 12. Power

Mechanical power:

`P = F × v`.

Neural contribution affects rapid force and activation strategy, but power also depends on:
- strength;
- force–velocity properties;
- mechanics;
- technique;
- load.

Project rule:

**power is not a pure neural-performance measure.**

## 13. Canonical project rules

1. Recruitment + rate coding jointly regulate neural output.
2. Neural drive = ensemble motoneuron discharge.
3. EMG is an indirect proxy, not neural drive itself.
4. VA is a protocol-specific activation estimate, not universal neural capacity.
5. Agonist EMG is not direct muscle force.
6. Antagonist coactivation has torque costs and control/stability functions.
7. Synergist/intermuscular coordination is task specific and adaptable.
8. Intramuscular coordination is motor-unit population control.
9. Force steadiness is distinct from maximal strength.
10. RFD is time-window specific.
11. Early RFD is more neural-drive constrained than later RFD.
12. Explosive activation is distinct from plateau VA.
13. Maximal strength is neural + muscular + mechanical.
14. Power is neural + force–velocity + mechanical/technical.

## 14. Training-relevant implications

1. **Do not treat normalized EMG as a universal neural-drive score.**
2. **Do not interpret VA as percent CNS activation.**
3. **Do not assume less coactivation is automatically better.**
4. **Do not infer force directly from agonist EMG.**
5. **Do not compare RFD without naming time window and test protocol.**
6. **Do not equate early explosive activation with MVC plateau activation.**
7. **Do not call all early strength gains “purely neural.”**
8. **Do not treat power improvement as evidence of neural adaptation alone.**
9. **Keep chronic neural adaptation in NEU-06.**

## 15. What NEU-05 does not prescribe

Not owned here:

- individual neural bottleneck;
- optimal load/velocity;
- RFD training protocol;
- power-training prescription;
- personalized coactivation target;
- EMG/VA diagnostic thresholds;
- chronic neural adaptation magnitude.

These remain NEU-06/08/09, TST, TEC, PRO, GPP and IND.
