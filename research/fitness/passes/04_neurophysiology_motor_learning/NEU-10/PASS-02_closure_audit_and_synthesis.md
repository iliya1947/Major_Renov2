## 3. Multisensory balance audit

PASS.

Current integrative evidence supports balance as an emergent output of distributed postural-control processes using:

- visual information;
- vestibular information;
- somatosensory/proprioceptive information;
- sensory integration/reweighting;
- anticipatory and reactive motor responses;
- neuromuscular and biomechanical constraints.

The 2025/2026 sensory-afference meta-analysis confirms that degrading visual or proprioceptive/somatosensory information changes posturographic behavior.

The light-touch study (PMID 39625307) shows that reliable tactile input changes vestibular-evoked balance responses.

Therefore:

**visual, vestibular and somatosensory contributions are dynamically reweighted; they are not three independent fixed-percentage subsystems.**

No blocker.

---

## 4. Eyes-closed / foam interpretation audit

PASS.

Rejected simplifications:

- eyes closed = vestibular function;
- foam = proprioception;
- poor performance in one sensory condition = one specific sensory-organ deficit.

Eyes-closed stance removes/reduces visual information, but performance still depends on:

- vestibular input;
- somatosensory input;
- central integration;
- strength/coordination;
- task mechanics.

Foam or unstable support changes somatosensory reliability and mechanics, forcing multisensory reweighting.

Canonical rule:

**sensory-condition balance tests challenge the whole control system; they do not isolate a single sensory organ or pathway.**

No blocker.

---

## 5. Balance-domain taxonomy audit

PASS.

The 2018 systematic review/meta-analysis (PMID 30323769) found only small correlations among static/dynamic steady-state, proactive and reactive balance types.

The dynamic/functional test review (PMID 38699672) also found low correlations among different dynamic balance tests, indicating distinct constructs even within “dynamic balance”.

Project taxonomy remains:

1. static steady-state;
2. dynamic steady-state;
3. proactive/anticipatory;
4. reactive.

Canonical rule:

**balance is a family of task-specific capabilities, not one latent ability adequately measured by one test.**

No blocker.

---

## 6. Static-balance audit

PASS.

Static balance tests such as quiet stance or single-leg stance can validly describe performance in that task.

They do not establish:

- dynamic balance;
- perturbation recovery;
- proactive balance;
- sport-specific balance;
- total “neuromotor quality”.

Canonical rule:

**a static test is a static-task measure, not a global balance score.**

No blocker.

---

## 7. Dynamic-balance audit

PASS.

Dynamic balance includes control during:

- movement;
- reaching;
- locomotion;
- changes in base of support;
- task transitions.

Y-Balance/SEBT and similar tests can be useful, but they combine:

- balance;
- mobility;
- strength;
- anthropometry;
- coordination.

They are not pure neural measures and are not interchangeable with every other dynamic-balance task.

Canonical rule:

**dynamic-balance interpretation remains test specific.**

No blocker.

---

## 8. Postural-sway / COP audit

PASS.

Postural sway is a measurement family, commonly operationalized via center-of-pressure variables.

Closure-critical boundary:

**COP ≠ COM ≠ total balance capacity.**

The height-threat meta-analysis (PMID 41458879) showed:

- reduced sway amplitude;
- increased sway frequency;
- reduced gait speed

under threat, consistent with a stiffened/protective strategy.

Therefore:

**lower sway amplitude cannot be interpreted universally as better balance.**

Different COP variables answer different questions and require exact metric/task context.

No blocker.

---

## 9. Reactive-balance audit

PASS WITH EVIDENCE-CERTAINTY LIMIT.

Reactive balance refers to control after an unexpected disturbance.

Perturbation-based balance training is the closest matched training paradigm.

The 2026 systematic review/meta-regression (PMID 42102008) found:

- heterogeneous protocols;
- low certainty for reactive-balance outcomes;
- very low certainty for real-world falls;
- no robust general optimal dose/profile except limited parameter findings.

The 2026 PBT scoping review (PMID 42594763) likewise found large methodological heterogeneity and limited standardized follow-up.

Canonical rule:

**reactive balance requires perturbation-matched testing/training; optimal dosing remains unresolved.**

No blocker.

---

## 10. Proactive / anticipatory-balance audit

PASS.

Proactive balance is preparation before a predicted challenge.

It can involve:

- posture change;
- COM shift;
- step adjustment;
- altered activation/coactivation;
- anticipatory postural adjustments.

APA is one mechanism inside proactive control, not the entire construct.

Reactive balance begins after unexpected disturbance and is feedback-dominant.

Canonical rule:

**proactive and reactive balance are distinct control demands and should not be substituted for one another.**

No blocker.

---

## 11. Balance-training specificity audit

PASS — CORE RULE.

The 2016 meta-analysis (PMID 26993132) found:

- large improvement in trained balance tasks;
- essentially no effect in non-trained balance categories.

Later evidence remains consistent.

The 2023/2024 systematic review/meta-analysis of neural correlates (PMID 38185708) found that trained balance skills improve substantially more than non-trained transfer tasks and that even long training does not guarantee broad transfer.

Canonical rule:

**balance skill learning is strongly task specific.**

This means generic one-leg stance or one unstable-surface task has limited value as a universal balance intervention or global outcome.

No blocker.

---

## 12. Transfer to strength / power audit

PASS WITH LIMITED-TRANSFER BOUNDARY.

The balance-training performance systematic review (PMID 20617915) found:

- clear postural-control improvement;
- conflicting/no superiority for strength and sprint outcomes versus more specific strength/plyometric training.

The 2025 athlete instability-training meta-analysis (PMID 41229270) found:

- clear balance improvements;
- limited jump transfer;
- no significant CMJ improvement.

Therefore:

**balance training can support balance-specific performance but is not a substitute for resistance training when maximal strength is the target.**

No blocker.

---

## 13. Unstable-vs-stable resistance-training audit

PASS.

The 2015 meta-analysis (PMID 26359066) found limited additional benefit of unstable versus stable strength training and inconsistent between-condition results.

The 2026 older-adult meta-analysis (PMID 42468010) found no significant short-term lower-limb-strength advantage of unstable over stable RT, though some mobility/fear-of-falling outcomes may favor unstable training in specific contexts.

Canonical rule:

**unstable-surface RT is not established as universally superior for strength; instability should be selected when instability/balance is part of the actual goal.**

No blocker.

---

## 14. Reverse-transfer audit — resistance training to balance

PASS.

The resistance-exercise meta-analysis (PMID 33203156) found improvements in several balance outcomes, especially in adults/older adults.

This supports:

- strength and force capacity can contribute to balance performance.

It does not support:

- balance = strength;
- sensory integration and reactive/proactive control are unnecessary;
- all balance domains improve equally.

Canonical rule:

**strength can support balance, but balance remains a distinct sensorimotor task family.**

No blocker.

---

## 15. Clinical-boundary audit

PASS.

Fitness balance testing cannot diagnose the medical cause of:

- vertigo;
- persistent dizziness;
- unexplained gait unsteadiness;
- recurrent unexplained falls;
- vestibular dysfunction;
- neurologic balance disorders.

Current NICE NG127 retains acute vestibular syndrome with gait unsteadiness as a potential posterior-circulation stroke presentation requiring trained HINTS assessment or immediate stroke-pathway referral when benign explanations do not account for the presentation.

AAO-HNS continues to classify persistent dizziness/postural instability from vestibular or CNS pathology as a clinical vestibular-rehabilitation domain.

Canonical rule:

**fitness balance tests describe performance; clinical diagnosis of symptomatic/pathological imbalance remains MED.**

No blocker.
