## 2. Corrections, counterevidence and rechecks
R1 — Separate theoretical constructs from operational validity. Existing WOM-02 correctly distinguishes need valuation, desire, satisfaction/frustration and goal priority, but relies primarily on general need-support evidence rather than direct testing of need strength and measurement. Additional Chen et al. 2015 https://doi.org/10.1007/s11031-014-9450-1 indexed original abstract explicitly studies need valuation and desire as indicators of strength alongside satisfaction and frustration across four cultures. No sex-specific moderator or exact effect estimate verified. More direct gender evidence: Arabic BPNSFS validation https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-020-00506-1, direct results/discussion, reports scalar invariance under fitted model across gender and five countries; women in undergraduate samples have higher measured autonomy, competence and relatedness satisfaction, lower relatedness and competence frustration, no significant autonomy-frustration contrast (p=.432). This is context-specific satisfaction, not stronger needs; weak items and untested structural invariance noted; nonsignificance not equivalence. Change history reports author-name/affiliation corrections, correction link https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-021-00540-7 direct cache miss. Counterevidence Murphy 2023 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36442044/ and Payne & Schimmack 2025 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40471799/ indexed original abstracts/excerpts: BPNSFS apparent satisfaction/frustration distinction may reflect item keying/response style. Contrary evidence from Canadian military sample N807 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39760556/ supports six content factors and differential external associations. Systematic mapping review https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1427478 indexed text distinguishes low satisfaction, active thwarting and item wording. Therefore neither universal validity of two separate measured dimensions nor universal bipolarity established. The theoretical distinction can be retained as hypothesis; require independent evidence of active thwarting, not a low satisfaction score alone. Additional early-access unedited study Józefacka et al. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-55223-3 direct abstract (N383,54.5% women, age18–75) reports five estimated profiles and loneliness–unfulfillment association stronger in women; not female types, not causal or independent proof of scale dimensionality. Full models not inspected. Root cause: prior method did not sufficiently separate theoretical construct from contested instrument. Recheck: consistent with WOM-01 measurement and IND-03 inference boundaries, adds direct gender evidence without inventing hierarchy.

R2 — Correct precision of household outcome and interpretation of odds. ESS parenthood measure includes currently or ever living with own, step, adopted, foster or partner's children. Thus no inference about fertility, childbirth, wish, deliberate childlessness, or strength of family values from household classification alone. AUC=.62 is gender discrimination using value index, not predictive accuracy for parenthood. Table2 male typicality coefficient .15 and gender interaction -.17, with gender coded -.5 women/+.5 men: exp(.15+.085)=1.2649 for women, exp(.15-.085)=1.0672 men. Rounding reproduces reported ORs. Hypothetical baseline probability .2 gives odds .25; applying 1.26 yields p=.2395, not +26 percentage points; baseline .5 yields p=.5575. These are illustrative transformations, not predicted personal probabilities. The reported equivalence interval for men is a prespecified practical threshold and not exact zero. Repeated cross-sectional ESS rounds do not identify within-person changes. New same authors value/equality paper https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43019481/ indexed abstract uses ESS 2002–2023, N439389/34 countries, overlap in underlying survey source and years; no independent replication of parenthood association or exact same record matching asserted. Root cause: source outcome only loosely described and no independent numerical check. Retest against footnote3, Table2 and arithmetic.

R3 — Goal profile stability is not stability of every goal or action. Wehner methods use distinct rank-order and ipsative measures (within-person correlations across six goal ratings). A positive ipsative correlation can persist despite uniform shifts in importance; rank stability can coexist with mean change; neither measures effort allocation. Table1 reports mother's communion raw means 3.93 to 4.03 (d=.22), nonmother 4.02 to 3.99 (d=-.06); descriptive change is not causal contrast. No blanket claim of no postbirth change. Baseline nonmother count approx151 vs table149; mother 132 baseline to96 at T2 (72.7% of table baseline), nonmother149 to107 (71.8%). Table-specific sample ratios not universal retention rates. Authors compare rank correlations by falling outside comparison-group CI and also age-controlled models; do not assume CI-overlap heuristic proves contrast. Baseline already pregnant, differing age, selective attrition and unmeasured effort remain alternatives. Root cause: summary 'high stability' can conceal different stability estimands. Retest: existing selection/anticipation distinction remains valid, stronger restrictions carried to WOM-05.

R4 — Emotion frequency, dispersion and temporal dynamics. Nature 2021 paper uses volatility, inertia and cyclicity over 75 days; average BF01 3.3 (sex) and 2.7 (contraceptive comparisons), not posterior probability, not multiplied Bayes factors for independent tests. Prior odds and model specification needed for posterior odds. Source explicitly discusses differing mechanisms with similar overall dispersion, and some contraceptive comparisons favor higher inertia/cyclicity (reduced variability). 2026 community study compares individual SD across two days; indexed methods explicitly target unadjusted group differences. Do not report adjusted association or infer one paper refutes another on a common estimand. In Trampe report-level numbers any-emotion gender difference .01 percentage points; women minus men negative-only +2.81pp and positive-only -6.34pp; neither same-time intensity, variability nor clinical symptoms. Different sampling intervals, mean affect, scale bounds, temporal ordering and reporting can affect dispersion estimates. New methodological coordinated variance study https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6408986/ indexed abstract N2103/45065 observations, seven studies, explicitly decomposes person/day/moment variance. Supports matching timescale rather than combining SD, instability and autocorrelation. No new effect estimate of female variability. Root cause: operational differences noted but explicit raw-vs-adjusted and direction/metric controls incomplete. Retest: all checked studies compatible with limited measure-dependent claims, not proof of exact equality.

R5 — Correct rumination subtype denominator and distinguish signal measurement. Johnson/Whisman indexed article Results explicitly k23 brooding, k21 reflection; WOM-02.2 aggregate subtype statement k23 is correct for brooding only, amend reflection denominator. d=.19/.17 remain unchanged; these are self-report mean differences not frequency counts, unique motive, or evidence of individual's rumination. McDuff methods distinguish facial action presence from duration among action-positive observations; 1862 participants x10 videos, 11.8% video exclusions, not independent participants. Reported mixed-model equations list ad/country effects but not participant random effect. Repeated videos from same viewer raise clustering concern; implementation and sensitivity not inspected, so cannot claim definite incorrect standard errors. Separate coder agreement on action labels from validity for subjective emotion: κ values describe coding agreement, not accuracy of inferring internal state. Sample includes ages16–17; no general adult-only claim. Root cause: subtype denominator error and inadequate specificity of measurement/dependence limits. Retest source results and counts (879+983=1862, 1862*10=18620). Do not export a face-based emotion detector or infer concealed experience.

R6 — Outcome overlap and within-person moderation. Segerstrom et al. Results explicitly report Bonferroni-adjusted post-hoc analyses of EWB components: composite daily time x autonomy interaction was the exception to other significant composite results also showing significance on relatedness and/or competence. This weakens robustness to overlapping autonomy content; does not prove the interaction is entirely artifactual. The slopes are conditional within-person associations, not personal causal dose-response, and one should not conflate time in goal activities with number of goals. Person-level mean associations (Table2) include autonomy–EWB r=.48, hours–EWB r=-.03; these do not contradict within-person moderation. Exploratory prior-day results not confirmatory; original shared DAHLiA cohort warning confirmed. Jakubiak gender moderation concerns observed relationship support and particular outcomes, not innate need strength. Uchida null main association rejects a universal differentiation-to-instrumental-motive rule; no transfer to gender without actual moderation evidence. Root cause: prior composite-overlap warning omitted component-level robustness exception. Retest: direct Results, preserves core distinction of engagement, motivation, goal progress and affect. No causal feedback established.

## 4. Revised operational method
For a woman's motivational/emotional proposition identify actual goal or relationship, level (general need, need valuation/desire, satisfaction, possible active thwarting, value, goal content, priority, expectation, effort, objective/subjective progress), timing and evidence channel. Separate situational opportunities and constraints, role entry, anticipation, adaptation and change. Treat SDT needs as theory-specific constructs; require evidence of measurement validity and avoid assuming questionnaires distinguish low satisfaction from active frustration. Identify target affect, frequency, intensity, duration, dispersion, temporal dynamics, expression, regulation aim/attempt and actual outcome. Compare like timescales, measurement and population; preserve dependent observations, distinguish raw and adjusted estimates, same- vs next-day outcomes and exploratory analyses. Use group findings only as qualified hypothesis priors, not an individual's emotional experience, intent, relationship preference or consent. Respect actual choices. No assumption of universal female need hierarchy or mechanism. This is generic scientific guidance, not validated personal prediction or clinical advice.
