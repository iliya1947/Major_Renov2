# SAF-02 — Safety management and continuity: post-crisis transitions, follow-up, caring contacts, lethal-means safety, repeat self-harm, safeguarding, capacity/confidentiality, handoff, remote/AI boundaries, and SAF sufficiency audit

Status: **COMPLETE_FOR_CURRENT_SCOPE — 20/20 PASS**

Evidence cutoff: **2026-11-19**

## Purpose

Close the remaining SAF qualification dependency after SAF-01.

SAF-01 established:

- screening != assessment != prediction != disposition;
- self-harm != suicidal intent;
- intrusive thoughts != intent;
- current suicidal thoughts, intent, plan, access, past behavior, recent change, medical/psychiatric state, and environment are distinct evidence channels;
- emergency/urgent/routine are action thresholds, not pseudo-precise risk tiers;
- delirium, mania, psychosis, and severe withdrawal have escalation boundaries;
- safety plans != no-suicide contracts;
- remote/AI interaction != formal clinical risk assessment.

SAF-02 adds the continuity and management layer:

1. post-crisis transition risk;
2. discharge and handoff quality;
3. early aftercare;
4. caring contacts / brief interventions;
5. lethal-means safety;
6. safety-planning evidence limits;
7. repeat self-harm;
8. safeguarding and violence escalation;
9. refusal and capacity;
10. confidentiality/information-sharing boundaries;
11. documentation and handoff;
12. remote/AI fail-safe behavior;
13. final SAF sufficiency audit.

Canonical rule:

`stabilize -> define unresolved safety needs -> transition/handoff -> timely follow-up -> collaborative safety/means plan -> verify linkage -> reassess after change -> escalate on deterioration -> document uncertainty`.

Never:

`discharged -> safe`

or:

`refused help -> has capacity and accepts all risk`.

# II. Discharge is a transition, not a safety certificate

## 12. Discharge changes setting, not underlying uncertainty

A person may leave:

- emergency department;
- psychiatric inpatient unit;
- general hospital;
- crisis service

while still carrying unresolved risk factors, symptoms, or practical barriers.

## 13. Post-discharge period is elevated-risk

A 2026 case-crossover study of adults who died by suicide after mental-health discharge found markedly elevated short-term risk:

- IRR ≈ 5.48 within 14 days;
- first-week IRR ≈ 6.32 compared with within-person control periods.

This design strengthens, but does not make assumption-free, the inference that discharge transition itself can be a high-risk period.

## 14. Population elevation != exact individual prediction

The elevated post-discharge rate does not identify exactly who will die by suicide.

## 15. Clinical risk factors still discriminate imperfectly

A 2025 meta-analysis of 83 primary studies found post-discharge risk factors such as:

- prior self-harm;
- suicidal ideation;
- depressive symptoms

had mostly modest discrimination.

Therefore:

`high-risk profile -> precise individual outcome`

remains invalid.

# III. Discharge planning is part of safety management

## 16. NICE NG225 discharge requirements

Before discharge after self-harm from general hospital, NICE requires:

- psychosocial assessment;
- further-management plan;
- discharge planning with relevant agencies/people;
- specified aftercare;
- clear written communication with primary care.

## 17. A referral is not a handoff

A referral can fail because of:

- no appointment;
- long wait;
- transport;
- cost;
- communication failure;
- refusal;
- wrong service;
- service rejection.

## 18. Linkage should be operationally verified when possible

Useful indicators:

- receiving service identified;
- contact information available;
- appointment/time known;
- records transmitted appropriately;
- patient understands next step;
- barrier plan exists.

## 19. "Warm handoff" is a continuity strategy

It means active connection rather than passive instruction.

Evidence and implementation quality vary.

# IV. Early aftercare

## 20. NICE aftercare timing

If ongoing safety concerns remain after self-harm, NICE recommends initial aftercare within:

- 48 hours of the psychosocial assessment.

## 21. The rationale is transition vulnerability

NICE notes:

- repeat self-harm is common soon after an episode;
- prompt follow-up can improve engagement;
- continuity of personnel may help.

## 22. 48 hours is a guideline action point, not a universal biological threshold

Do not generalize one health-system recommendation into a universal law for every setting.

## 23. Follow-up content matters

Contact should not be reduced to:

> "Are you alive?"

It may need to address:

- current safety;
- barriers;
- treatment linkage;
- medication/access issues;
- support;
- safety plan;
- new stressors.

# V. Caring contacts

## 24. Caring contacts are brief non-demanding messages

Examples:

- postal notes;
- text messages;
- other brief supportive communications.

They are not psychotherapy by themselves.

## 25. VA/DoD 2024 recommendation

The guideline gives a:

- **weak-for** recommendation

for periodic caring communications for 12 months following hospitalization related to suicide risk, in addition to usual care, to reduce attempts.

## 26. Evidence is outcome-specific

VA/DoD discussion notes the supporting systematic review suggested:

- protective effect for attempts at one year;
- much less certain evidence for suicide deaths.

## 27. Weak recommendation != ineffective

It means evidence/benefit-harm certainty is limited enough that context and preferences matter.

## 28. Caring contact != completed follow-up

A message does not establish:

- receipt;
- engagement;
- treatment attendance;
- safety.

---

# VI. Brief interventions and contacts after suicide attempt

## 29. 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis

Included:

- 36 trials;
- 9,552 adults;
- 33 trials meta-analyzed.

## 30. Re-attempt outcome

Brief interventions and contacts reduced suicide re-attempts:

- OR = 0.72;
- 95% CI 0.54–0.95;
- I² = 56.8%;
- moderate-certainty evidence.

## 31. Suicidal ideation

Small reduction:

- SMD = -0.20;
- low-certainty evidence;
- substantial heterogeneity.

## 32. Self-harm recurrence

No clear evidence of reduction:

- OR = 0.66;
- very wide CI 0.22–1.97;
- very low certainty;
- high heterogeneity.

## 33. Linkage to mental-health services

No clear pooled evidence of improvement:

- OR = 2.25;
- CI 0.71–7.17;
- very low certainty;
- high heterogeneity.

## 34. Therefore

`brief contact works`

must always name:

- population;
- intervention type;
- outcome;
- certainty.

---

# VII. Evidence across acute-phase interventions is heterogeneous

## 35. 2026 acute-phase meta-analysis

Sixty randomized trials:

- 22,654 participants.

## 36. Repeat self-harm

No intervention category showed a statistically significant pooled reduction in repeat self-harm across the analyzed follow-up intervals.

## 37. Mortality signals

Some intervention categories showed associations with reduced suicide mortality at selected follow-ups.

## 38. The result is not contradictory to the BIC meta-analysis

Different:

- inclusion rules;
- outcomes;
- intervention grouping;
- follow-up;
- estimators

can yield different pooled conclusions.

## 39. Safety science therefore needs outcome-specific synthesis

Avoid:

`intervention prevents suicide`

without specifying exactly what was reduced.

# VIII. Safety planning

## 40. Safety planning is a structured process

Common elements include:

- warning signs;
- internal coping;
- social contacts;
- support people;
- professional contacts;
- emergency routes;
- lethal-means safety.

## 41. NICE recommends collaborative safety planning after self-harm

The plan should be:

- person-held;
- accessible;
- shared as agreed;
- usable during crisis.

## 42. Safety plan != treatment package

It organizes crisis response.

It may coexist with:

- psychotherapy;
- medication;
- social intervention;
- crisis service.

## 43. Adult evidence is methodologically mixed

A 2026 systematic review of adult SPI research found substantial heterogeneity in:

- design;
- setting;
- intervention composition;
- outcomes;
- follow-up.

## 44. Many studies combine safety planning with follow-up

Therefore:

`SPI alone caused the outcome`

is often not identifiable.

## 45. Youth evidence cannot be silently imported to adults

A 2025 youth meta-analysis found limited evidence for safety planning as a standalone intervention on suicide-related outcomes.

Population-specific evidence matters.

## 46. Safety plan

Answers:

> What will I/we do if risk rises?

## 47. Caring contact

Answers:

> Does the system maintain a low-demand connection after crisis?

## 48. Follow-up appointment

Answers:

> Is active ongoing care connected?

## 49. These should not be collapsed

A complete transition strategy may need multiple components.

# X. Lethal-means safety versus population means restriction

## 50. Individual lethal-means safety

Usually involves collaborative reduction of access during periods of risk.

## 51. Population means restriction

Examples:

- barriers at jumping sites;
- pesticide regulation;
- medication pack-size limits;
- firearm-policy interventions.

## 52. These evidence levels differ

Population policy effects do not directly prove the effect size of one counseling session.

## 53. 2025 umbrella review

Included:

- 20 systematic reviews;
- 179 unique primary studies.

It found evidence for effectiveness across several means-restriction strategies.

## 54. Evidence quality varied strongly

Twelve of twenty included systematic reviews were rated critically low quality.

## 55. Strong effects at specific sites can be based on overlapping studies

For jumping barriers, large effects should be interpreted with primary-study overlap in mind.

## 56. Medication/pesticide/firearm evidence differs by method and context

No one universal means-restriction effect size exists.

---

# XI. Lethal-means safety counseling

## 57. Counseling aims to alter access/storage behavior

It is an individualized clinical/public-health process.

## 58. 2024 systematic review

The lethal-means safety counseling review evaluated interventions across:

- settings;
- providers;
- populations;
- storage behaviors;
- self-directed violence outcomes.

## 59. Behavior change evidence is stronger than hard-outcome evidence in many counseling studies

This distinction matters.

## 60. Means-safety counseling should therefore be described by target

Allowed:

- improves safe-storage/access behavior in some settings;
- is recommended as a risk-mitigation strategy in major guidelines.

Avoid:

- "one counseling session prevents suicide."

# XII. Method substitution is an empirical question

## 61. Restricting one method can lead to concern about substitution

## 62. Substitution is not automatically complete

The 2025 umbrella review found some interventions reduced site/method-specific mortality without clear evidence of equivalent displacement in the studied contexts.

## 63. But substitution can vary by method/context

Do not assume:

- zero substitution;
- complete substitution.

## 64. Means restriction is strongest when highly lethal/impulsive opportunities are altered

Mechanism plausibly involves:

- time;
- distance;
- friction;
- alternative action opportunity.

- poor service continuity;

## 66. NICE recommendation for frequent self-harm

If episodes are frequent or treatment has not worked, NICE recommends multidisciplinary review.

## 67. Review includes

- named coordinating professional;

`repeated self-harm -> manipulation`.

## 69. Repetition does not justify therapeutic nihilism

Repeated presentations still require assessment and care.

# XVI. Capacity is decision-specific

## 78. Capacity is not a global personality trait

A person may have capacity for one decision and not another.

## 79. Capacity depends on the specific decision/time/context

NICE NG108 requires a structured, proportionate assessment under its legal framework.

## 80. Risky or "unwise" choice != incapacity

NICE explicitly states not to assume lack of capacity because the person makes a decision the practitioner sees as risky or unwise.

## 81. "Insight" != capacity

NICE also separates:

- insight;
- capacity.

A person may retain decision-making capacity despite poor/limited insight as colloquially described.

## 82. Diagnosis != incapacity

Psychosis, depression, mania, neurodevelopmental condition, or substance use do not automatically determine capacity.

## 83. Acute state can affect capacity

Possible contributors:

- delirium;
- intoxication;
- severe thought disorder;
- cognitive impairment.

But formal assessment must remain decision-specific.

# XVII. Refusal

## 84. Refusal is not proof of capacity or incapacity

A person can refuse while:

- capable;
- incapable;
- ambivalent;
- frightened;
- mistrustful;
- overwhelmed.

## 85. First task is to clarify reasons/barriers

NICE capacity guidance recommends efforts to support participation before concluding incapacity.

## 86. Safety concern can persist despite refusal

Refusal does not erase:

- immediate danger;
- safeguarding concern;
- medical emergency.

## 87. Legal authority for overriding refusal is jurisdiction-specific

Do not invent it.

---

# XVIII. Supported decision-making precedes coercion where feasible

## 88. Support communication

May include:

- clear language;
- more time;
- interpreter;
- communication aid;
- trusted support person;
- quieter environment.

## 89. Least restrictive principle

NICE self-harm guidance emphasizes balancing autonomy and safety and using least restrictive measures.

## 90. Least restrictive != no restriction

If safety cannot be maintained, a more restrictive intervention may be warranted under applicable law.

## 91. SAF does not determine legal threshold

That remains local clinical/legal work.

# XIX. Confidentiality is strong but not unlimited

## 92. Default

Safety care should respect confidentiality and seek consent to involve others when possible.

## 93. NICE self-harm guidance

It emphasizes:

- seek consent early;
- obtain specialist/legal advice when capacity/consent issues arise;
- include safeguarding procedures where relevant.

## 94. Information from family/carers can still be received

Even without consent to involve them, family/carers may provide information.

Receiving information differs from disclosing information.

## 95. Serious/imminent threat exceptions exist in some jurisdictions

Example:

U.S. HIPAA permits necessary disclosure to people able to lessen a serious and imminent threat under specified conditions.

## 96. This rule is not universal law

Other jurisdictions use different:

- legal tests;
- reporting duties;
- definitions;
- procedural safeguards.

## 97. Disclosure should be necessary and proportionate

Even when permitted/required, avoid unnecessary disclosure.

## 98. Jurisdiction-specific confidentiality belongs to ETH

SAF only preserves the safety principle and legal uncertainty.

# XXV. Remote crisis management

## 121. HHS telebehavioral guidance

Remote services should have an emergency plan including:

- current location;
- local emergency contacts/services;
- support person;
- disconnection plan.

## 122. Remote handoff needs local reachability

A remote clinician cannot safely rely on emergency infrastructure in a different jurisdiction without knowing local routing.

## 123. AI has even less operational control

AI may not know or verify:

- identity;
- exact location;
- available local services;
- whether the person followed advice;
- whether emergency help arrived.

## 124. Therefore AI cannot declare a handoff completed

At most it can:

- provide/locate options;
- encourage immediate real-world contact;
- continue supportive communication while appropriate;
- avoid claiming control it does not have.

---

# XXVI. Remote/AI information gathering must be proportional

## 125. Safety can justify asking for operational details

But only when they materially change crisis response.

## 126. Do not collect unnecessary sensitive details

Privacy remains active during crisis.

## 127. Exact location is especially sensitive

In formal telehealth it can be operationally necessary.

In general AI interaction, use available location mechanisms/current resources only when required for safety and avoid exposing location unnecessarily.

## 128. Data minimization and safety are not enemies

Collect what is needed for the immediate safety goal, not everything available.

---

# XXVII. Lethal-means language should remain non-instructional

## 129. Safety reasoning does not require procedural descriptions of methods

The useful level is:

- access;
- lethality;
- delay;
- secure storage/removal;
- trusted support;
- professional guidance.

## 130. Avoid giving method optimization details

The goal is risk reduction.

Requires prior attempt and later suicidal attempt.

## 132. Repeat self-harm

Broader outcome under NICE-style definition and can include non-suicidal episodes.

## 133. BIC evidence differs by outcome

2026 meta-analysis:

- clearer reduction in re-attempts;
- no clear pooled reduction in broader self-harm recurrence.

## 134. Therefore endpoints must not be merged

# XXIX. Suicide death is a different endpoint again

## 135. Attempts and deaths differ statistically and causally

A reduction in attempts does not guarantee detectable reduction in death.

## 136. Caring contacts evidence illustrates this

Evidence was more supportive for attempts than suicide mortality.

## 137. Low event rates create imprecision

Mortality trials often require very large samples.

## 138. Do not overread null mortality results

They may reflect:

- no effect;
- low power;

# XL. Evidence-strength map

## High-confidence practical boundaries

1. post-discharge transition is a safety-critical period;
2. discharge does not establish safety;
3. NICE requires explicit discharge/aftercare planning after self-harm;
4. NICE recommends initial aftercare within 48 hours when ongoing safety concerns remain;
5. continuity of care/personnel is a relevant safety process;
6. caring contacts have modest supportive evidence, with VA/DoD giving a weak-for recommendation after suicide-related hospitalization;
7. 2026 adult BIC meta-analysis supports reduced suicide re-attempts on average but not all outcomes;
8. attempts, self-harm recurrence, ideation, linkage, and death are distinct endpoints;
9. safety planning and caring contacts are distinct intervention layers;
10. lethal-means counseling and population means restriction are distinct evidence levels;
11. several population means-restriction strategies show effectiveness, with heterogeneous evidence quality;
12. repeat self-harm warrants reassessment/multidisciplinary coordination rather than moral labeling;
13. capacity is decision-specific and risky/unwise choice does not by itself prove incapacity;
14. diagnosis and capacity are noninterchangeable;
15. confidentiality exceptions are jurisdiction-specific and should be necessary/proportionate;
16. safeguarding can require multi-agency action;
17. remote handoff requires local emergency infrastructure;
18. AI cannot verify crisis resolution or declare a handoff completed.

# XLIV. Canonical SAF control formula

**Safety work is a dynamic continuity problem: recognize acute danger, choose the least-delaying proportionate action, and maintain a verified path of care across the next vulnerable interval without pretending to predict the future exactly.**

`current evidence -> action threshold -> stabilization -> transition -> follow-up -> safety/means plan -> linkage -> reassessment -> escalation trigger -> documentation/uncertainty`.

`referral -> connected`

## Source-boundary note

NICE NG225 is the main current guideline source for discharge planning, 48-hour aftercare when ongoing safety concerns remain, repeat self-harm review, continuity, safety planning, safeguarding, least-restrictive safety, and care-plan access. The current PDF was opened and relevant aftercare/support pages were visually inspected with the PDF renderer.

VA/DoD 2024 is used for the weak-for caring-communications recommendation and post-acute-care framing. The PDF text and recommendation discussion were inspected; a screenshot attempt of the relevant page failed with a cache-miss error, so no claim is made that the VA/DoD page was visually inspected.

The 2026 Homan meta-analysis is used specifically for adults following suicide attempt. Its moderate-certainty reduction in re-attempts is not generalized to all self-harm outcomes or populations.

The 2026 acute-phase meta-analysis reaches more mixed conclusions for repeat self-harm across intervention categories; this is retained rather than reconciled away.

Population-level means restriction evidence is not treated as equivalent to individual lethal-means counseling evidence.

NICE NG108 capacity guidance is UK-law-specific. Its generic reasoning boundary — capacity is decision-specific and an unwise/risky decision alone does not establish incapacity — is transported; legal tests are not universalized.

HHS confidentiality guidance is U.S.-specific and used only as an example showing that serious/imminent-threat exceptions can exist. No U.S. legal rule is generalized globally.

No source is used to infer any individual's suicide risk, self-harm intent, capacity, dangerousness, safeguarding status, legal rights, or need for emergency/involuntary intervention.
