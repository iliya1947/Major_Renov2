# INT-01 — Cross-domain contradiction audit: construct identity, levels of explanation, epistemic/action thresholds, priority rules, and override logic

Status: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v1.0 — 20/20 PASS**

Evidence cutoff: **2026-11-19**

Scope: internal coherence audit of the current canonical qualification stack. No individualized psychological material is included.

## Purpose

Phase A, Phase B, and Phase C were developed as domain-specific qualification sequences. Each sequence already performed internal audits, but reliable individualized reasoning requires an additional question:

When rules from different domains are applied to the same case, do they remain compatible, or can terminology, level-of-analysis differences, action thresholds, safety overrides, or measurement assumptions produce contradictory conclusions?

This block audits five failure classes:

1. jingle collision — the same word is used for different constructs;
2. level collision — a rule valid at one explanatory level is imported into another;
3. epistemic/action collision — evidence strength is confused with permission or urgency to act;
4. override collision — safety, ethics, privacy, autonomy, or legal constraints are allowed to overwrite scientific truth claims rather than decision policy;
5. empirical-tension erasure — competing live theories are falsely harmonized into one mechanism.

Canonical principle:

**Cross-domain integration must preserve distinctions before it creates synthesis.**

Never:

- same word -> same construct;
- same behavior -> same mechanism;
- higher stakes -> claim becomes truer;
- safety action -> diagnosis proved;
- user disagreement -> automatically true or automatically pathological;
- neural evidence -> psychological ontology;
- population association -> person mechanism;
- domain disagreement -> average the domains.

For each apparent collision:

1. identify the exact proposition from each domain;
2. identify construct;

8. determine whether both propositions can be true simultaneously;
9. if yes, specify the scope boundary;
10. if no, identify whether one rule has domain-specific priority or whether empirical uncertainty remains unresolved.

# II. Cross-domain type system

## 6. Observation

A directly available datum under a specified channel.

Examples:

- self-report;
- observed behavior;
- task performance;
- physiological signal;
- score;
- event;
- collateral report.

Observation does not identify mechanism.

## 7. Description

A structured summary of observations.

Description is not automatically a trait or diagnosis.

## 8. Construct

A theoretically defined target such as working memory, attachment anxiety, cognitive effort, resilience, emotion regulation, or reward valuation.

A construct is not identical to its measure.

## 9. State

A relatively time/context-local condition or expression.

State does not imply stable trait.

## 10. Trait or enduring tendency

A relatively stable probabilistic tendency estimated across time/context.

Trait does not imply fixed essence or invariant behavior.

## 11. Process

A temporally unfolding operation or sequence.

Process can vary within a person and is not automatically an enduring individual difference.

## 12. Mechanism

A process through which a cause produces an effect.

Mechanism requires stronger evidence than association, prediction, mediation coefficient, diagnostic membership, or model fit.

## 13. Computation

A formal or information-processing operation.

A computation is not automatically its neural implementation.

## 14. Neural implementation

A biological realization at a specified scale.

Neural implementation is not automatically the psychological construct itself.

## 15. Classification

A category or dimensional placement under a defined system.

Diagnosis is one form of classification.

Classification is not mechanism or identity.

## 16. Prediction

Forecasting an outcome under a validation regime.

Prediction is not causation.

## 17. Decision or action threshold

A rule for what action is warranted under uncertainty and consequence.

Decision threshold is not a truth threshold.

## 18. Ethical permission

Whether an otherwise possible inference/use is proportionate, private, fair, contestable, and role-appropriate.

Ethical acceptability is not accuracy.

## 19. Safety escalation

A decision to increase real-world assessment, support, or protection because expected harm and uncertainty justify action.

Safety escalation does not prove diagnosis, mechanism, or future outcome.

# III. Blocking contradiction test

A blocking contradiction exists only if:

1. two canonical rules address the same construct;
2. at the same level of analysis;
3. under materially overlapping conditions;
4. and require mutually incompatible inferences or actions;
5. without a principled priority rule or evidence-based uncertainty representation.

Terminological difference alone is not a contradiction.

Different outcome targets are not a contradiction.

Different timescales are not a contradiction.

Different action thresholds are not a contradiction if epistemic claims remain unchanged.

**Audit result: no blocking cross-domain scientific contradiction was identified.**

## 24. Resolution

# V. Construct collision — effort

## 25. EXE effort

EXE separates objective demand, control allocation, exerted control, subjective effort, control efficacy, and transition cost.

## 26. MOT effort

MOT treats effort as one component of current valuation/action selection alongside expected success, delay, reward value, opportunity cost, competing goals, and ability.

## 27. STR effort/fatigue

STR can involve effort under stress, subjective fatigue, physiological activation, and resource constraints.

## 28. Resolution

EXE and MOT are complementary:

- EXE asks how control is allocated and implemented;
- MOT asks whether the action or control policy is currently worth selecting.

Canonical rule:

**capacity != allocation != subjective effort != willingness != performance.**

Status: **COMPATIBLE.**

# XI. Self-report: privileged and limited at the same time

## 56. Qualitative/methodology rule

First-person report has privileged access to subjective experience, perceived goals, felt emotion, and perceived meaning.

## 57. PER/PSYCHOMETRICS rule

Self-report is affected by memory, response style, self-concept, reference group, and interpretation.

It is one measurement channel, not universal ground truth.

## 58. ETH correction rule

User correction is evidence that must trigger re-evaluation.

It must not be automatically pathologized.

## 59. Resolution

Privilege is target-specific.

For subjective current experience or stated intent, direct report is often the most proximal evidence available.

For hidden causal mechanism, external effect on another person, diagnosis, objective performance, or future behavior, self-report is informative but not decisive.

Canonical rule:

**first-person authority is strongest for first-person experience and stated intent, not for every causal or external-world proposition.**

Status: **COMPATIBLE.**

# XII. User correction versus independent evidence

## 60. ETH

Correction must reopen the proposition.

## 61. PSY-RES

Contradictory evidence must be compared by source quality, dependence, construct fit, and alternative explanations.

## 62. Resolution

Three cases:

1. factual premise correction — recalculate the dependent inference when the corrected fact is the best available evidence;
2. subjective state or meaning — treat first-person report as primary evidence for that subjective target;
3. mechanism/interpretation dispute — retain competing explanations and compare evidence.

Canonical rule:

**correction has procedural priority, not universal epistemic supremacy.**

Status: **COMPATIBLE.**

# XV. Population evidence versus individual evidence

## 70. Cross-domain rule

PSY-RES, SOC, PER, DEV, BHC, and SAF all reject direct transfer from population average to person-specific fact.

## 71. Resolution

Working hierarchy for person claims:

1. valid direct person-specific evidence;
2. repeated/contextual individual evidence;
3. applicable subgroup evidence;
4. applicable population evidence;
5. broad generic pattern.

This is not a mechanical ranking when measurement quality differs.

Canonical rule:

**population evidence sets priors and constraints; individualized conclusions require individualized evidence.**

Status: **COMPATIBLE.**

# LI. Genuine empirical tensions that must remain open

The following are not logical contradictions and should not be administratively resolved.

## 187. Cognitive effort cost mechanisms

Live accounts include opportunity cost, error aversion, control-state transition cost, information-theoretic/valuation accounts, and biological/network constraints.

Current evidence does not justify one universal mechanism.

## 188. Emotion structure

Dimensional, categorical, appraisal, and constructionist approaches capture partially overlapping targets.

No single framework is declared the sole ontology.

## 189. Extinction/reconsolidation

Durable behavioral updating can be compatible with inhibitory learning, state/context learning, value updating, or memory destabilization/reconsolidation under specific conditions.

Outcome durability alone does not identify mechanism.

## 190. Trait change mechanism

Repeated state change, role/socialization, intervention components, selection, and developmental processes can contribute.

No universal state-to-trait mechanism is established.

## 191. Stress/resilience process

Protective factors, support, coping, resources, exposure timing, and physiological adaptation interact.

Resilience is not one agreed trait/process/outcome.

## 192. Psychotherapy mechanism architecture

Common and specific factors can coexist.

Alliance/process correlates do not yield causal variance decomposition.

## 193. Social influence

Observed conformity can reflect information, sanctions, identity, strategic behavior, or private belief change.

Behavior alone does not identify the pathway.

## 194. Neurobiological implementation

Many-to-many mappings remain common between psychological constructs and neural systems.

Cross-level models should compete rather than be collapsed.

Canonical rule:

**Live empirical alternatives remain explicit until discriminating evidence resolves them.**

# LII. Cross-domain precedence architecture

These are precedence rules for reasoning operations, not an ontology ranking of disciplines.

## 195. Priority 1 — immediate safety/medical action gate

If acute medical or safety conditions are plausibly present:

- take appropriate safety/medical routing action first;
- preserve uncertainty;
- do not infer diagnosis solely from the action threshold.

## 196. Priority 2 — legal/jurisdiction gate

If an action depends on law:

- verify jurisdiction/current law;
- do not convert legal rule into psychological truth.

## 197. Priority 3 — privacy/ethical use gate

Before using sensitive individualized information:

- purpose;
- necessity;
- role;
- fairness;
- disclosure;
- contestability;
- downstream consequences.

This can prohibit a use even when the proposition is scientifically plausible.

## 198. Priority 4 — construct/measurement validity gate

If the construct is undefined or the measurement interpretation is invalid:

**downstream causal, trait, diagnostic, or treatment inference is blocked.**

Sophisticated modeling cannot rescue construct failure.

## 199. Priority 5 — claim/design gate

Association/prediction evidence cannot be upgraded to mechanism/causation without design support.

## 200. Priority 6 — applicability/transport gate

Population evidence must be relevant to population, language/culture, context, timescale, and use.

## 201. Priority 7 — individual evidence gate

Strong person-level claims require direct/repeated person-level evidence and alternative testing.

## 202. Priority 8 — narrow conclusion

State only the strongest claim that survives all previous gates.

## 204. Ethical override

Can prohibit use or disclosure.

Does not make a true claim false.

## 205. Privacy constraint

Can prevent storage or secondary use.

Does not reduce the epistemic support of the underlying proposition.

## 206. Legal override

Can require or prohibit an action.

Does not prove psychological mechanism.

## 207. User challenge

Can suspend consequential reliance and trigger re-evaluation.

Does not automatically make the proposition false.

**Process authority and epistemic truth must remain typed separately.**

# LIV. Uncertainty propagation rule

## 208. Critical-premise uncertainty propagates

If conclusion C requires premise P, and P is materially uncertain, C cannot be reported with greater confidence merely because the narrative is coherent.

## 209. Independent evidence can compensate

A conclusion can gain support from multiple independent paths.

Confidence is not mechanically the minimum confidence of every input.

## 210. Correlated evidence must not be counted repeatedly

Examples:

- multiple prompts from one model;
- multiple instruments sharing items;
- multiple studies using the same cohort;
- multimodal neuroscience with shared task confound.

## 211. Downstream consequence does not upgrade confidence

High stakes increase required evidence/action threshold.

They do not make evidence stronger.

Do not generalize the exception.

# LXI. New cross-domain priority rules

## Rule A — construct validity is upstream

If target construct or measurement interpretation is invalid or materially unsupported, do not rescue the conclusion with neural sophistication, statistical significance, model fit, high-stakes urgency, or diagnostic familiarity.

## Rule B — action priority does not create epistemic priority

Safety, ethics, law, and privacy can change what should be done.

They do not by themselves change what psychological mechanism is true.

## Rule C — direct individual evidence is necessary but not magical

Person-specific evidence can outweigh population stereotypes when valid and relevant.

It remains subject to measurement, context, source, and alternative explanations.

## Rule D — critical uncertainty propagates

A downstream claim cannot become stronger simply because several weak dependent steps form a coherent story.

## Rule E — unresolved empirical alternatives remain unresolved

Integration must not manufacture consensus.

## Rule F — safety/power can override ordinary symmetric framing for action

Credible coercion or acute safety evidence changes the action pathway without proving a global trait or diagnosis.

## Rule G — user correction changes the evidence state

Correction must trigger re-evaluation.

It does not automatically settle causal or mechanistic disputes.

## Rule H — high stakes raise use thresholds, not truth values

More severe consequences require stronger evidence, more independent review, more reversibility, clearer uncertainty, and more contestability.

# LXII. New reusable error classes identified

## 231. Cross-domain homonym collapse

Failure: same term across domains treated as same construct.

Guard: type construct before inference.

## 232. Action-threshold/truth-threshold conflation

Failure: urgent action interpreted as high certainty, or uncertainty used to justify inaction.

Guard: separate epistemic confidence from action threshold.

## 233. Override leakage

Failure: safety/ethical/legal override becomes diagnosis or mechanism claim.

Guard: overrides change process/action only unless independent evidence supports the epistemic claim.

## 234. Downstream confidence inflation

Failure: a coherent chain of uncertain premises is presented as a high-confidence conclusion.

Guard: propagate critical uncertainty and test independence.

## 235. Domain averaging

Failure: two incompatible constructs or theories are split down the middle instead of typed or left unresolved.

Guard: determine whether difference is scope/level or a genuine empirical alternative.

## 236. Evidence-hierarchy absolutism

Failure: one evidence type is declared globally superior.

Guard: evidence hierarchy follows the claim.
