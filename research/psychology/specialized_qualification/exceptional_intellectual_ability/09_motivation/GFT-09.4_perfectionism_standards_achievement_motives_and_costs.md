## Practical gate — heuristic, not diagnostic
Distinguish demanded quality, personally chosen standard, perfection requirement, error appraisal, self-worth contingency, concern over others' evaluation, actual performance and marginal cost. A high objective standard can be appropriate under safety/quality requirements. A person's choice to continue corrections can reflect measurable improvement, external constraints, uncertainty, fear of evaluation, habit, or enjoyment. Compare task acceptance criterion, consequences of error, independently assessed quality and time cost; assess whether revision stops when criterion met and whether standards adjust when conditions change. Separate concern from striving without reifying statistical residuals as separate people. Observe approach/avoidance reasons, goal value, feedback, completion and wellbeing separately; high achievement can coexist with distress, low distress with unfulfilled standards. Voluntary low-stakes comparison of predefined sufficient criterion vs further optional polishing can test marginal benefit and cost, not a causal diagnosis. Do not apply this to safety-critical tasks by relaxing required controls. No inference of IQ from standards, fear of mistakes or performance. Persistent functional impairment warrants appropriate professional assessment rather than a giftedness label.

## Internal assessment — three wholly synthetic cases
A. Fictional instrument repair workshop: two technicians insist on the same tight tolerance. One stops after calibrated tests meet acceptance criteria; the other keeps rechecking and reports fear of criticism. Rival explanations actual instrument drift, uncertain calibration, distinct role requirements and evaluation anxiety. New: independent calibration confirms stable accuracy; extra checks continue only under evaluative observation, with no measured improvement. Retain legitimate tolerance; revise general high-standard explanation to conditional evaluation concern hypothesis, not a diagnosis or proof of causality. No inferred IQ.
B. Fictional calligraphy class: a participant reports ambitious standards, submits on time, receives good scores, but loses sleep before deadlines. Analyst calls strivings wholly adaptive. Separate attainment from costs and independent concerns, workload and scheduling. New: sleep loss also occurs in non-evaluative weeks with extra shifts; concern ratings fall when anonymous feedback is used, quality unchanged. Revise single-mechanism interpretation; both workload and evaluation may contribute, no established causal mediation. Maintain chosen standards unless actual cost-benefit comparison warrants change.
C. Fictional community choir: selected high-performing group reports stronger achievement standards but no significant difference in concerns. Coordinator says high ability guarantees adaptive perfectionism. Separate performance selection and measured ability, group mean and individual, null and equivalence, domain and general trait. New: independently screened community cohort shows overlapping scores, while concern predicts postponing auditions among both groups. Withdraw universal ability claim; retain group/context association, consider evaluative setting and feasible preparation. No individual IQ or causal diagnosis.
