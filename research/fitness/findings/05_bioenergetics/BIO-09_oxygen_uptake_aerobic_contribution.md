# BIO-09 — Oxygen uptake / aerobic contribution

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-19**  
Область: 05 — Bioenergetics / Exercise Metabolism

## 0. Core decision

Exercise VO2 reflects the integrated oxygen-consumption response of the whole oxygen cascade:

- pulmonary gas exchange;
- cardiac output;
- arterial O2 content;
- muscle perfusion/diffusion;
- O2 extraction;
- mitochondrial utilization.

Project rule:

**VO2 is an integrated oxygen-consumption flux, not a direct cellular ATP meter.**

---

## 1. VO2

By the Fick principle:

`VO2 = cardiac output × arteriovenous O2 difference`.

Pulmonary VO2 is the practical whole-body signal.

Project rule:

**pulmonary VO2 ≠ direct local muscle mitochondrial ATP production.**

---

## 2. Pulmonary vs muscle VO2

Pulmonary and muscle oxygen-consumption signals are coupled but not identical during rapid transitions because of:

- circulation/transit delay;
- lung/body O2 stores;
- pulmonary blood-flow dynamics;
- non-exercising tissue contribution;
- breath-by-breath processing.

Project rule:

**pulmonary VO2 kinetics ≠ identical instantaneous muscle VO2 kinetics.**

---

## 3. Oxygen deficit

Oxygen deficit estimates the transient gap between:

- modeled oxidative O2/energy requirement for the workload;
- measured accumulated oxygen uptake during adjustment.

Project rule:

**oxygen deficit is an indirect energetic estimate, not literal whole-body oxygen shortage and not direct anaerobic ATP measurement.**

---

## 4. VO2 kinetics

After exercise onset, oxidative oxygen uptake rises toward the new demand.

Useful applied components:

- early cardiodynamic response;
- primary/phase-II response;
- slow component at sufficiently high constant workloads.

Project rule:

**VO2 kinetics are time-, intensity-, state- and analysis-dependent.**

Faster kinetics reduce the transient non-oxidative supplementation requirement, all else equal.

---

## 5. VO2 slow component

The slow component is a real delayed rise in VO2 during heavy/severe exercise.

Its exact mechanism remains unresolved.

Current evidence supports multiple interacting candidates:

- efficiency loss;
- fibre recruitment changes;
- fatigue-related changes;
- altered metabolic pathway contribution.

Project rule:

**slow component = established phenomenon; one universal causal mechanism is not retained.**

---

## 6. Steady state and intensity domains

### Moderate
VO2 steady state is normally attainable.

### Heavy
A slow component can develop; a delayed steady state may remain attainable.

### Severe
A true metabolic steady state is normally not sustained before exhaustion.

Project rule:

**constant workload ≠ guaranteed physiological steady state.**

Exact heavy–severe boundary identification remains method dependent; CP and MLSS are not treated as perfectly interchangeable individual markers.

---

## 7. VO2max

VO2max is the maximal attainable whole-body oxygen uptake under defined test conditions.

It reflects the integrated oxygen cascade.

Project rule:

**VO2max ≠ isolated cardiac output and VO2max ≠ isolated mitochondrial capacity.**

---

## 8. VO2peak

VO2peak is the highest VO2 observed in a particular test.

Project rule:

**VO2peak ≠ automatically confirmed VO2max.**

---

## 9. Plateau / verification

A VO2 plateau can support maximality.

Verification phases can also support interpretation.

But:

- plateau is not always observed;
- verification outcomes depend on workload/recovery/protocol;
- verification is not an infallible universal gold standard.

Project rule:

**absence of plateau alone does not prove submaximal effort, and one verification protocol does not universally prove VO2max.**

Detailed CPET protocol belongs TST.

---

## 10. Aerobic contribution to high-intensity work

Oxidative ATP production is active during short maximal work and its integrated contribution rises with duration.

Project rule:

**anaerobic dominance ≠ aerobic silence.**

No universal time threshold is retained.

---

## 11. EPOC

EPOC = oxygen consumption elevated above the relevant pre-exercise/resting baseline after exercise.

It reflects multiple recovery processes.

Project rule:

**EPOC ≠ one simple oxygen debt.**

---

## 12. EPOC vs PCr

PCr resynthesis contributes to recovery oxygen cost.

But EPOC also reflects other processes.

Project rule:

**EPOC ≠ PCr resynthesis.**

---

## 13. EPOC magnitude

EPOC magnitude/duration depend on:

- intensity;
- duration;
- exercise mode;
- recovery measurement window;
- baseline definition;
- population.

Project rule:

**EPOC is real but no universal large “afterburn” calorie effect is retained.**

---

## 14. Between-bout recovery

Recovery intervals remain oxidatively active.

Oxidative metabolism supports:

- PCr resynthesis;
- ATP-dependent homeostasis;
- continued substrate oxidation;
- restoration processes.

Project rule:

**recovery interval ≠ metabolically inactive period.**

---

## 15. Recovery VO2 vs recovery completeness

High recovery VO2 can coexist with:

- incomplete PCr recovery;
- altered pH/Pi;
- incomplete ionic restoration;
- neuromuscular fatigue.

Project rule:

**elevated recovery VO2 ≠ complete recovery readiness.**

---

## 16. O2 delivery, extraction, utilization

Separate:

### Delivery
- cardiac output;
- arterial O2 content;
- pulmonary gas exchange.

### Peripheral extraction/distribution
- muscle perfusion;
- capillary diffusion;
- arteriovenous extraction.

### Mitochondrial utilization
- respiratory capacity;
- oxidative enzymes;
- substrate/ADP control.

Project rule:

**O2 delivery ≠ O2 extraction ≠ mitochondrial utilization.**

---

## 17. VO2max limitation

The dominant rate-limiting step can change with:

- training status;
- population;
- exercise mode;
- environment;
- pathology.

Young/elite endurance athletes are often strongly delivery limited, while other populations/states can show greater peripheral/utilization limitation.

Project rule:

**VO2max is not universally heart-limited or mitochondria-limited.**

---

## 18. VO2 kinetics vs VO2max limitation

The determinants overlap but are not identical.

Training can speed VO2 kinetics without proportional changes in VO2max/performance.

Project rule:

**VO2-kinetic speed ≠ VO2max.**

---

## 19. Canonical non-equivalences

1. pulmonary VO2 ≠ direct local mitochondrial ATP production.
2. VO2 ≠ total ATP production.
3. pulmonary VO2 ≠ identical instantaneous muscle VO2.
4. oxygen deficit ≠ literal whole-body O2 shortage.
5. oxygen deficit ≠ direct anaerobic ATP measurement.
6. faster VO2 kinetics ≠ higher VO2max by definition.
7. constant workload ≠ guaranteed steady state.
8. VO2 steady state ≠ complete physiological stasis.
9. slow component ≠ one settled mechanism.
10. CP ≠ MLSS as perfectly interchangeable individual boundary.
11. VO2peak ≠ automatically VO2max.
12. no plateau ≠ proof of submaximal test.
13. verification trial ≠ universal gold standard.
14. high-intensity exercise ≠ zero aerobic contribution.
15. anaerobic dominance ≠ aerobic silence.
16. EPOC ≠ one simple oxygen debt.
17. EPOC ≠ PCr resynthesis.
18. EPOC ≠ guaranteed large calorie expenditure.
19. recovery VO2 ≠ complete recovery.
20. O2 delivery ≠ O2 extraction.
21. O2 extraction ≠ mitochondrial utilization.
22. VO2max ≠ cardiac output alone.
23. VO2max ≠ mitochondrial capacity alone.
24. VO2max limiter ≠ VO2-kinetics limiter.

---

## 20. Confidence

### HIGH

- VO2 is an integrated oxygen-consumption flux;
- pulmonary VO2 is not direct local ATP production;
- oxygen deficit is an indirect/model-derived construct;
- VO2 kinetics are state/intensity/method dependent;
- moderate/heavy/severe domains differ in steady-state behavior;
- VO2max is an integrated oxygen-cascade capacity;
- VO2peak is operationally distinct;
- high-intensity exercise has non-zero aerobic contribution;
- EPOC is a multi-process recovery response;
- EPOC is not PCr resynthesis;
- recovery intervals are oxidatively active;
- delivery/extraction/utilization are distinct components.

### MODERATE-HIGH

- VO2 plateau/verification strategies can support maximality but protocol matters;
- active/short recovery can increase subsequent aerobic contribution;
- systemic O2 delivery is often a major VO2max limiter in young fit/elite endurance populations;
- training can shift the dominant limitation across the oxygen cascade;
- VO2 kinetics and VO2max have overlapping but different determinants.

### MODERATE / CONTEXT DEPENDENT

- exact slow-component mechanism;
- exact heavy–severe boundary for an individual;
- exact VO2 kinetic time constant;
- exact aerobic contribution in a specific high-intensity task;
- exact EPOC magnitude/duration;
- exact central vs peripheral limitation in an individual;
- whether a specific verification protocol is useful.

### NOT RETAINED AS UNIVERSAL

- pulmonary VO2 directly equals muscle ATP production;
- oxygen deficit is literal body oxygen shortage;
- aerobic metabolism starts only after 1–2 min;
- constant exercise always reaches steady state;
- slow component has one settled cause;
- VO2peak always equals VO2max;
- plateau is mandatory;
- verification trial is a universal gold standard;
- high-intensity exercise is purely anaerobic;
- EPOC is repayment of one lactic oxygen debt;
- EPOC equals PCr resynthesis;
- EPOC always creates a large calorie afterburn;
- recovery between sets is metabolically inactive;
- VO2max is limited only by heart or only by mitochondria.
