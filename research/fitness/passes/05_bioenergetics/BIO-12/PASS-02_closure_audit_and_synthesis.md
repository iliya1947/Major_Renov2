## 3. Core resistance-training bioenergetics audit

PASS.

Resistance exercise remains:

- intermittent;
- non-steady-state;
- metabolically mixed;
- mechanically heterogeneous.

Phosphagen, glycolytic and oxidative ATP-resynthesis pathways can all contribute over a set/session.

But:

- contribution is not equal;
- contribution changes within a set;
- contribution changes across repeated sets;
- integrated percentages do not describe every instant or fibre.

Canonical rule:

**resistance training is a mixed-pathway task with continuously changing relative contribution, not an anaerobic-only category.**

No blocker.

## 4. Energy demand within a set audit

PASS AFTER MEASUREMENT NARROWING.

PASS-01 correctly listed relevant variables:

- load;
- reps;
- repetition duration;
- set duration;
- contraction type;
- active muscle mass;
- ROM/mechanics;
- proximity to failure;
- starting metabolic state;
- prior work;
- rest interval.

However these do not form a directly measurable universal “metabolic dose equation.”

Canonical wording:

**set energetic demand is jointly constrained by mechanical work, contraction pattern, duration, effort and starting metabolic state; no single field metric fully quantifies it.**

No blocker.

## 5. Energy-expenditure method audit

PASS.

PMID **38896201** included **166 studies**.

Methods included:

- indirect calorimetry: 136 studies;
- blood lactate: 25;
- wearables: 31;
- MET methods: 4.

The review explicitly identifies the substantial anaerobic contribution as a major challenge for quantifying resistance-exercise energy expenditure.

Therefore retain:

**indirect calorimetry is useful for oxidative energy cost but does not directly capture total non-oxidative ATP turnover.**

Do not infer:

- one “true” kcal value from one method;
- lactate correction perfectly reconstructs anaerobic energy;
- wearable estimates are interchangeable with laboratory calorimetry.

No blocker.

## 6. Integrated energy cost vs pathway flux audit

PASS.

A session-level or set-level energy estimate integrates across time.

It is non-equivalent to:

- instantaneous phosphagen ATP flux;
- instantaneous glycolytic ATP flux;
- local muscle ATP turnover in each fibre.

Canonical rule:

**total energy expenditure and pathway contribution are different constructs.**

No blocker.

## 7. Repetition-tempo audit

PASS WITH MATCHING-CONSTRAINT RULE.

PMID **35394146**:

- n=11 recreationally trained adults;
- belt squat;
- TUT and effort matched;
- TRAD: 20 reps at 2-s eccentric / 1-s concentric;
- SLOW: 10 reps at 4-s eccentric / 2-s concentric;
- faster condition produced higher volume load, blood lactate, HR and RPE;
- slower condition used greater external load and produced greater impulse;
- muscle oxygenation was not different.

PMID **42319447**:

- n=30 resistance-trained adults;
- barbell squat;
- both tempos trained to failure;
- traditional tempo produced greater volume load and HR;
- slow tempo produced greater TUT, nVCO2 and accumulated muscle-deoxygenation metric;
- measured total EE and RQ were not different.

Canonical rule:

**tempo effects depend on what is matched: TUT, effort, load, repetitions, work or failure endpoint.**

No blocker.

## 8. Slow-tempo proxy audit

PASS AFTER WORDING TIGHTENING.

The 2026 authors describe slow tempo as increasing “anaerobic metabolic pressure,” based partly on nVCO2 and muscle oxygenation measures.

BIO-12 does not elevate those proxies to direct ATP-flux measurements.

Retain:

- longer TUT;
- greater measured nVCO2;
- greater accumulated local deoxygenation in that squat protocol.

Reject:

- slow tempo universally increases anaerobic ATP production;
- SmO2 directly quantifies ATP production;
- nVCO2 is a direct glycolytic ATP meter.

Canonical rule:

**tempo changes measurable metabolic/oxygenation responses, but proxy signals must not be converted into exact pathway flux.**

No blocker.

## 10. Set-duration audit

PASS WITH NON-INDEPENDENCE BOUNDARY.

Longer continuous sets usually imply more cumulative contractions/ATP turnover, but set duration covaries with:

- repetitions;
- tempo;
- load;
- ROM;
- proximity to failure.

Therefore BIO-12 does not retain “set duration” as an independent causal dose.

Canonical rule:

**set duration changes the time over which ATP demand is sustained, but its effect must be interpreted jointly with load, reps, tempo and effort.**

No blocker.

## 11. Same repetitions / different set distribution audit

PASS WITH SMALL-STUDY BOUNDARY.

PMID **22808209**:

- n=6 trained men;
- bilateral leg press;
- same initial load;
- same total 50 repetitions;
- same inter-set rest;
- 5×10 to failure vs 10×5 not to failure;
- 5×10 produced greater power loss, larger PCr/ATP reduction and higher muscle lactate/IMP.

This supports:

**set distribution materially changes intramuscular energetic disturbance.**

It does not isolate “repetitions per set” from proximity to failure because the 5×10 condition reached failure whereas 10×5 did not.

No blocker after adding this confounding boundary.

## 12. Load audit

PASS WITH STRICT PROTOCOL BOUNDARY.

PMID **41841121** compared 10%, 30%, 50%, 70% and 90% 1RM conditions.

Retain only:

- in the studied exercise/protocol, 30–90% 1RM conditions that reached failure showed broadly similar local/whole-body metabolic perturbation;
- the 10% condition did not reach comparable failure/perturbation;
- muscle excitation differed with load.

Do not generalize:

**30–90% 1RM always has the same metabolism.**

Canonical rule:

**%1RM constrains the task but does not uniquely determine metabolic disturbance; effort/set duration/repetition number matter.**

No blocker.

## 13. Load × proximity audit

But direct evidence is confounded by:

- load;

- tempo;

**more repetitions per continuous set usually increase metabolic disturbance when other relevant constraints are sufficiently comparable.**

## 17. Acute proximity vs chronic adaptation audit

PASS AFTER SEPARATION.

PMID **42410632**:

ACSM 2026 states momentary muscle fatigue did not consistently affect primary training outcomes.

**greater acute metabolic disturbance from closer-to-failure work does not imply that literal failure is required for adaptation.**

## 19. Rest-interval acute audit

PASS.

Longer rest generally allows more:

- PCr restoration;
- metabolite/ion normalization;
- force/velocity recovery;
- capacity to maintain work.

Shorter rest generally creates:

- greater density;
- more residual disturbance;
- higher acute internal strain;
- greater risk of later-set output loss.

Canonical rule:

**rest interval changes the starting physiological state of the next set.**

No blocker.

## 23. PCr recovery vs performance audit

PCr restoration can support high-rate performance.

But repeated-set performance also depends on:

- Pi;
- ions/pH;
- E–C state;
- neural drive;
- substrate state;
- mechanics.

Canonical rule:

**PCr recovery is one contributor to later-set performance, not a readiness certificate.**

## 24. Glycolytic contribution audit

PASS AFTER LACTATE CAUSALITY NARROWING.

Evidence supports greater glycolytic disturbance in longer/higher-repetition continuous sets through:

- muscle lactate in PMID **22808209**;
- blood lactate patterns across protocols;
- known glycolytic ATP support during sustained high ATP demand.

But:

- blood lactate ≠ glycolytic ATP flux;
- muscle lactate ≠ complete glycolytic ATP accounting;
- no universal rep range defines glycolytic dominance.

Canonical rule:

**glycolytic contribution often rises in longer continuous resistance sets, but exact pathway partition requires method-specific estimation and cannot be inferred from lactate alone.**

No blocker.

## 26. Oxidative contribution audit

PASS.

Oxidative metabolism is retained as active:

- from set onset;
- increasingly relevant as work duration/repeated bouts accumulate;
- especially important during between-set recovery via PCr resynthesis and ATP-dependent homeostasis.

Evidence base includes:

- upstream BIO-06/08/09;
- oxygen-consumption studies in resistance exercise;
- circuit data showing large differences in VO2 by density.

Canonical rule:

**resistance exercise is not anaerobic-only; oxidative contribution is present during work and is central to recovery.**

Exact within-repetition oxidative percentages are not retained.

No blocker.

## 27. Circuit evidence audit

PASS WITH PROTOCOL/POPULATION BOUNDARY.

PMID **33171830**:

- high-intensity resistance circuit vs traditional strength structure;
- same blocks/exercises;
- shorter between-exercise transitions in circuit;
- ~75% higher VO2;
- ~39% higher HR;

## 28. Training-density audit

PASS.

Density changes when work is compressed into less time.

It is distinct from:

- volume load;
- set count;
- session duration alone;
- intensity.

Higher density can raise internal strain while reducing the ability to sustain:

- load;
- repetitions;
- velocity;
- power.

Canonical rule:

**density is a time-distribution variable with stimulus–fatigue trade-offs, not a synonym for volume or “better stimulus.”**

No blocker.

## 30. Rest redistribution audit

PASS WITH POPULATION/OUTCOME BOUNDARY.

PMID **37649783**:

- 13 young women;
- 5×10/90-s vs 10×5/30-s;
- same intensity;
- more-frequent shorter rests produced lower lactate, lower internal load and preserved MVIC better.

PMID **41369549**:

- 50 physically active postmenopausal women;
- volume, intensity and work:rest matched;
- shorter set configurations improved mechanical preservation and reduced lactatemia.

PMID **42032773**:

- 15 randomized studies;
- 346 participants;
- rest redistribution improved some outcomes (mean power, peak torque, RPE);
- no uniform advantage across all strength/power variables.

Canonical rule:

**redistributing the same or comparable rest can reduce continuous-set metabolic disturbance and sometimes preserve mechanical output, but effects are not uniform across populations/tasks/outcomes.**

No blocker.

## 31. Superset acute audit

PASS.

PMID **39903375**:

- 313 participants;

## 32. Superset chronic audit

PASS AFTER WORDING NARROWING.

PMID **39903375** pooled chronic outcomes and found no clear average difference in:

- maximal strength;
- strength endurance;
- hypertrophy.

However PMID **39072654**:

- 10-week heavy multi-joint RT;
- supersets nearly halved session duration;
- traditional training produced greater lat-pulldown strength gain;
- seated-row difference also numerically favored traditional;
- leg press/bench press were similar;
- body composition changes were similar.

Therefore reject:

**supersets are chronically equivalent for every outcome/exercise.**

Retain:

**average chronic outcomes can be broadly similar, but exercise-specific mechanical-performance trade-offs can occur.**

No blocker.

## 42. Measurement boundaries

### Indirect calorimetry
Useful for oxidative whole-body energy expenditure; incomplete for total anaerobic ATP turnover.

### Blood lactate
Systemic concentration influenced by production, transport, oxidation and timing.

### Muscle lactate
Local metabolite concentration; not complete glycolytic ATP accounting.

### 31P-MRS
Local PCr/Pi/pH; muscle/protocol/model dependent.

### Biopsy
Local sampled tissue; not every fibre/muscle.

### NIRS / SmO2
Local oxygenation/hemodynamic proxy; not direct ATP-production measurement.

### nVCO2
A metabolic proxy under specific modeling assumptions; not direct glycolytic ATP flux.

### Velocity loss
Mechanical/performance-fatigue proxy; not direct metabolite or ATP measurement.

### RPE
Perceptual response; not biochemical measurement.

Canonical rule:

**no routine single measure directly quantifies total resistance-set bioenergetics.**

## 46. Closure decision

**BIO-12 = CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0.**

Meaning:

1. resistance training is metabolically mixed, not anaerobic-only;
2. energy-system contribution changes within and across sets;
3. set energetic demand depends jointly on load, reps, tempo, duration, effort, exercise and starting metabolic state;
4. no single common field measure directly quantifies total set bioenergetics;
5. tempo effects depend on what is matched;
6. slow tempo can extend TUT/local deoxygenation without universally increasing total energy cost;
7. %1RM is not a standalone metabolic dose;
8. more repetitions in a continuous set generally increase disturbance when other constraints are comparable, but there is no fixed rep threshold;
9. closer proximity to failure generally increases acute internal/metabolic strain;
10. literal failure is not required for chronic adaptation;
11. rest changes the metabolic starting state of the next set;
12. PCr recovery between sets is oxidative and variable;
13. no fixed rest→PCr-recovery mapping is retained;
14. longer/moderate-high-rep sets can have substantial glycolytic contribution without a “glycolytic rep range”;
15. blood lactate is not direct glycolytic ATP flux;
16. oxidative metabolism contributes during work and is central to between-set recovery;
17. higher density can increase cardiorespiratory/metabolic strain while compromising mechanical quality;
18. rest redistribution can reduce continuous-set metabolic disturbance in some contexts;
19. supersets are time efficient and often raise acute internal/metabolic load;
20. superset type matters, and pooled chronic similarity does not mean every lift/outcome is identical;
21. circuit RT can substantially increase VO2/energy cost depending on density;
22. acute metabolic cost does not rank chronic adaptation;
23. metabolic stress/pump/cell swelling are not established as necessary or independently sufficient causal hypertrophy doses;
24. no exclusive energy-system rep-range zones are retained;
25. exact load/rep/rest/density prescription remains PRO.
