# WOM-02.1 — Women's motivation, needs, values and competing priorities

Date: 2026-11-25. Status: bounded research note complete; WOM-02 IN_PROGRESS.
Scope: women's goal systems, values, psychological needs and allocation of effort; nonclinical descriptive and conditional inference, not a universal female motivational hierarchy.

## 1. Question and prerequisites

How do women's values and goals vary across individuals, contexts and adulthood? Which evidence distinguishes enduring values, psychological need satisfaction, goal importance, actual priority, autonomous endorsement and constrained allocation? What evidence can distinguish a life transition changing goals from pre-existing selection or anticipation?

Rival explanations: genuinely changed value, changing relative importance, competing demands, changed opportunity/expectancy, selected role, anticipated transition, enacted norms or obligations, autonomous endorsement and combinations. Need strength, satisfaction, frustration and value priority are different constructs.

## 2. Targeted evidence review

Public searches, 2026-11-25: sex/gender differences basic psychological need satisfaction; Schwartz value priorities cross-cultural; women's life goals longitudinal; gender and values European Social Survey; goal change during transition to parenthood; daily goals women goal conflict; interpersonal need support meta-analysis. Included relevant primary/synthesis evidence and null or contrary findings. No private information in search. Selected review, not systematic or exhaustive; no data reanalysis.

E1. Schwartz & Rubel (2005), Sex differences in value priorities: Cross-cultural and multimethod studies. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.89.6.1010 ; https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/sex-differences-in-value-priorities-cross-cultural-and-multimetho/
Access: published abstract through author institution, read in full; direct PubMed blocked. 127 samples, 70 countries, N=77,528. Women averaged higher benevolence and universalism; men averaged higher power, achievement, self-direction, stimulation and hedonism. Security pattern less consistent; no difference in tradition or conformity reported. Median absolute difference d=.15, maximum .32; culture moderated the gaps. Do not treat this historical aggregate as a current individual's value hierarchy. Value scales are not SDT need-strength measures. Cross-cultural moderation is not causal identification.

E2. Buchinger, Richter & Heckhausen (2022 issue), The Development of Life Goals Across the Adult Life Span. https://doi.org/10.1093/geronb/gbab154 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34417801/
Access: indexed primary and author-institution abstracts; direct publisher and institutional opening failed, PubMed blocked. German Socio-Economic Panel longitudinal data, N=52,052, ages 18–84, nine life goals, multiple-group latent growth curves. Relationship/marriage, children and helping others rated highly across much of adulthood. Goal importance trajectories vary with age and social characteristics; parental status amplified gender differences in work/family goals. Do not infer every woman follows the average trajectory. Detailed coefficients, measurement invariance, attrition and model specifications not verified. Goal importance after attainment need not vanish.

E3. Sortheix, Ilmarinen, Mannerström & Salmela-Aro (2026 issue), Gender and values in 20 years of the European Social Survey: Are gender-typical values linked to parenthood? https://doi.org/10.1177/08902070251332098
Access: publisher HTML theory, methods, selected results (Tables 1–2), robustness and discussion/limitations inspected, not end-to-end. Repeated cross-sectional survey rounds 2002–2020, N=155,242 (82,913 women), 35 countries, ages 18–45. Outcome is currently or previously having a child in the household, not desire for children or biological fertility. Same-sex cohabiting respondents excluded. PVQ21 short scales (mean reliability .53); composite from penalized binary-gender classifier, training/test separated; composite gender discrimination limited (reported .62). Higher composite scores in direction of values more typical of men were associated with no children among women (adjusted OR 1.26 per standardized unit, 95% CI 1.22–1.31); smaller association in men (OR 1.07, CI 1.04–1.10), within authors' practical equivalence interval. Country-level conservatism and religiosity moderation not practically meaningful in their models. No significant change in association across survey rounds. This is NOT a panel tracking individual value change over 20 years. Composite labels denote a statistical profile, not masculinity/femininity as essential traits or identity. Reverse direction, selection, role adaptation, structural constraints and residual confounding remain possible; outcome does not identify voluntary choice, unmet desire or motive. OR is not probability or percentage-point change.

E4. Wehner, van Scheppingen & Bleidorn (2022 issue), Stability and change in major life goals during the transition to parenthood. https://doi.org/10.1177/0890207021996894
Access: publisher introduction, hypotheses, design, selected results/discussion and limitations/conclusion inspected. Preregistered Dutch longitudinal study, N=542 at baseline, including 132 expecting mothers and 151 women without children; goal assessments approximately six weeks before expected birth and twelve months after. Importance of achievement, power, variety, intimacy, affiliation and altruism measured separately. Expecting mothers already rated achievement and variety lower than comparison women; little evidence for mean-level change following birth. Goal profile stability generally high. Counterexample to an automatic post-transition reprioritization of all underlying goals. Baseline late in pregnancy cannot separate prior selection from earlier anticipation; comparison group approximately four years younger, subgroup power limited, selective attrition on power and poor model fit for that domain. No direct measure of actual time allocation; stability of importance does not imply stable action. This evidence qualifies E3 rather than proving causal direction. Detailed maternal development is deferred to WOM-05.

E5. Slemp, Field, Ryan, Forner, Van den Broeck & Lewis (2024), Interpersonal Supports for Basic Psychological Needs and Their Relations With Motivation, Well-Being, and Performance: A Meta-Analysis. https://doi.org/10.1037/pspi0000459 ; https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/2024_SlempFieldRyanEtAl_Interpersonal.pdf
Access: published PDF title page visually inspected; abstract, theory, selected results and limitations inspected, not entire article. 4,561 effects, 881 independent samples, N=443,556. Support for autonomy, competence and relatedness each associated with satisfaction of needs, motivation and wellbeing. Competence and relatedness support added explanatory variance beyond autonomy support in meta-analytic models. High heterogeneity (I-squared often above 90%); over 70% cross-sectional, strong correlations among support types, reverse and reciprocal effects possible. This is general human-motivation evidence, not a woman-specific need hierarchy or proof of unique causal pathways. Autonomy refers to volitional endorsement, not social isolation. Need satisfaction differs from how much a person values or desires an outcome. No sex-specific moderation established in the inspected results.

E6. Terrell, Martins, Delkoski & Torkelson (2022), What Do Women Want? Identifying Life Goals in a Community-Based Sample of Women Aged ≥60 Years. https://doi.org/10.3928/00989134-20221108-03 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36441062/
Access: complete published abstract directly inspected; full article not inspected. Cross-sectional convenience sample of 303 women, mean age 68. Open-text goals cover health, work/leisure, relationships and personal growth, demonstrating diverse goal domains within women rather than a single family-centered motivational type. Abstract states up to three goals per respondent yet reports 1,053 goals, exceeding 303×3=909. This numerical inconsistency may reflect coding or reporting, but cannot be resolved without methods; do not use goal totals, percentages or frequencies as verified quantitative evidence. No age-related causal inference from one older sample.

E7. Daily Goals and Psychological Well-Being in Midlife and Older Women: Physical Pain Interacts with Goal Conflict (2016). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28603467/ ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5464603/
Access: indexed primary abstract and article excerpts; direct opening blocked. N=200 women, up to seven daily diaries (reported 1,380 diaries), ages 50–75. Goal conflict showed context-dependent associations with fatigue and cognitive functioning according to pain status; not uniformly adverse across women. This illustrates why health-related constraints and outcome-specific moderation matter for women's daily motivational experience. Exact coefficients, adjustment and interpretation require full text; not a causal conclusion or broad claim about women's goal conflict.

Evidence limits: E3/E4/E5 substantive sections directly inspected, others abstract/excerpt level; E6 discrepancy unresolved. No article read end-to-end, no full supplement audit or independent validation. E1/E3 distinct historical datasets; E4 is directly discussed by E3, so distinguish cited earlier findings from new data. The present evidence supports conditional descriptive distinctions and motivates hypotheses, not comprehensive sex-specific psychological mechanisms. ## 3. Integrated motivational framework

Keep separate:
- Basic psychological needs (SDT: autonomy, competence, relatedness), their satisfaction/frustration, subjective valuation and desired experiences.
- Trans-situational values, broad life goals, domain-specific goals and immediate means.
- Agency (self-directed accomplishment) and communion (connection/care) as potentially coexisting orientations, not opposite female/male categories.
- Value hierarchy versus current goal salience and priority. A value can remain important while another goal temporarily takes precedence.
- Autonomous endorsement versus controlled regulation. Fulfilling a role can be freely valued, externally constrained, internally pressured or mixed. Enacted roles are not sufficient evidence of motives.
- Absolute importance, relative importance, attainable opportunity, expected efficacy, time/resource cost, actual effort, and observed progress. Strong goal importance does not guarantee enactment; behavior can be informative without identifying a unique motive.
- Development: selection into roles, anticipation, adaptation, changing constraints and actual within-person value changes. Do not assume a life event necessarily restructures a woman's motivation; preserve both continuity and possible change.

Do not infer that women need relatedness more than autonomy from value differences or that an observed choice exhausts all her goals. Relatedness need satisfaction differs from preference for a particular relationship, and neither is commitment or consent. No established universal female hierarchy of needs follows from this evidence.

## 4. Application and checks

Specify target goal, evidence channel and timescale. Compare reported value with observable effort only where opportunities and constraints are known. Identify competing goals from evidence rather than inventing a ranking. Distinguish actual behavior change, relative priority change and durable value change. Consider role selection, anticipated demands and changes in resources. Use comparable naturally occurring observations and voluntary reports to update hypotheses; do not covertly test or pressure another person. If several models predict the same outcome, preserve uncertainty. Population data are qualified priors under IND-03, not a person's motive.

Original synthetic scenarios, unrelated to private data:
A. A fictional adult woman values both creative mastery and civic participation, but reduces workshop attendance during a period of mandatory professional training. Value change, priority shift, constraints and changed expectancy compete. Compare her reported goals and later participation under comparable opportunities; do not infer a durable value loss.
B. A fictional woman fulfills an assigned community role while describing it as meaningful and tiring. Endorsement and strain may coexist; enacted obligation alone does not identify autonomous or controlled motivation.
C. A survey finds an association between value profile and entering a role. Selection, anticipatory change, adaptation and confounding compete. A repeated cross-section does not establish within-person change. E3/E4 provide external contrast for this distinction.
D. A group with higher relatedness satisfaction reports higher wellbeing. This does not establish a stronger relatedness need, unique cause or preference for a particular person. E5 supports construct separation; direction remains uncertain.

Internal reasoning check only; no independent examiner, held-out prospective task or personal predictive accuracy. No numerical competence score assigned.
