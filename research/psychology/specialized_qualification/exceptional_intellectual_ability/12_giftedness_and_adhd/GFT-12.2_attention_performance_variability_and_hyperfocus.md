# GFT-12.2 — Attention, performance variability and hyperfocus in ADHD and high intellectual ability

27.11.2026. COMPLETE_FOR_SPECIFIED_SCOPE (bounded general scientific-methodological level). Prospective question card. Starting HEAD 7d3b50c8726c5f40e42b9389dec20930fbf67c8e.
Purpose: distinguish attentional capacity, deployment, variability, task-dependent engagement and difficulties disengaging; avoid interpreting absorption or fluctuations as diagnostic or as a unique gifted phenotype.
1. Define sustained/selective attention, vigilance, lapses, reaction-time variability (RTV), mind wandering, hyperfocus, flow, perseveration, productivity and switching. Separate between-person and within-person associations, subjective states and objective performance.
2. Search primary adult ADHD/control experiments, meta-analyses, hyperfocus questionnaires and counterevidence; include youth and high-IQ comparative studies separately. Recheck 2026 pediatric candidate from 12.1; seek independently measured adult IQ150–160+.
3. Compare state regulation, motivation/reward, task demands, fatigue/sleep, strategy, motor and measurement factors; distinguish high engagement from impaired disengagement and actual functional cost. No universal mechanism or diagnosis from single metric.
4. Predict: interest, external structure or immediate feedback may improve performance without excluding ADHD; intense focus can occur outside ADHD. Examine objective and reported measures, error/speed trade-off, time-on-task, distributions, developmental history and ecological validity.
5. Closure: bounded general scientific-methodological synthesis, direct evidence and limitations, three new wholly invented cases with contradictory updates; six-category rubric all 2, no critical errors; internal same-author assessment only, not T1. No personal data or treatment recommendation.

## Evidence and contradictions
E1. Kofler et al. (2013), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23872284/ and https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0272735813000871, indexed primary author abstract, PubMed direct CAPTCHA. Meta-analysis 319 studies, corrected random effects group contrasts versus typically developing groups: children/adolescents g=.76, adults g=.46. Adult/adolescent comparison against clinical controls did not distinguish groups; childhood clinical-control effect g=.25. Not equivalence, sensitivity/specificity or individual diagnosis. Metric, task, age, speed and clinical selection matter. Karalunas et al. transdiagnostic review https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4267725/ indexed excerpt restates Kofler; not independent replication. Review https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22930417/ indexed abstract: incentive/event-rate literature mixed; attentional lapse and neural mechanisms hypotheses, not direct observation of thoughts. Mean RT, within-person SD, coefficient of variation, distribution tails and trial accuracy are different estimands; speed/variability dependence, outlier/exclusion rules, trial number, motor timing and fatigue complicate interpretation. No universal neural mechanism or inference of motivation from slow responses.
E2. Tucha et al. 2015 online, https://doi.org/10.1007/s00702-015-1426-0, Springer HTML abstract/introduction inspected. 29 adults ADHD and 30 comparison adults, four 20-min tasks of alertness/selective/divided attention/flexibility. Distinguish lower average performance from steeper time-on-task decrement relative to controls; general fatigue does not imply ADHD-specific deterioration. Prior studies with and without group×time interactions summarized. Gmehlin et al. 2016 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27193369/, indexed author abstract, 24+24 matched adults; steeper increase in slow-tail ex-Gaussian tau during basic sustained alertness. Tau describes a distribution parameter, not a unique count of consciously observed lapses. 2022 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9102294/ indexed results/discussion excerpt, direct CAPTCHA: no greater time-on-task decrement in tested vigilance measures, contrary to task-specific positive studies. No broad deficit/absence claim; compare measures, sample sizes, task design and performance criteria. Exact test-specific effects/intervals not verified for all studies.
E3. Marx et al. 2013, https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0067002, PLOS HTML introduction, selected methods/discussion inspected; indexed primary abstract https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23840573/. Adults aged18–40 and IQ/age/sex-matched controls, multiple cognitive tasks with financial reward contingencies. Reported ADHD contrasts in omissions, n-back accuracy, CPT RTV and time reproduction accuracy; reward normalized time reproduction accuracy in tested setting. Different speed/accuracy strategies and task-specific benefits matter; no evidence every reward normalizes attention or that improvement proves/excludes ADHD. Exact allocation, sample and complete interactions not checked. Fast task combining incentive and event rate https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32594941/ indexed methods N43 ADHD/46 NT; outcomes not independently rechecked; do not isolate reward causal effect from changed event rate. Motivation/impulsivity deeper analysis deferred 12.3. Mind-wandering/sleepiness EMA https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32155443/ and thought variability/constraints EMA https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40118952/ indexed snippets only; candidate measures, not verified effect estimates. Separate spontaneous thought, sleepiness, external distraction and deliberate thought. No claim of default-mode network cause.
E4. Ashinoff & Abu-Akel (2021 issue, online 2019), https://doi.org/10.1007/s00426-019-01245-8 Springer HTML review definition section inspected. Proposed operational construct includes engagement-triggered intense sustained/selective attention, reduced awareness of irrelevant stimuli and improved task performance. Proposal rather than validated necessary/sufficient diagnostic criteria; questionnaire studies often use different outcomes (time loss, absorption, disengagement difficulty), so do not assume performance improvement by definition and then infer it empirically. Separate flow (positive absorbed experience), narrow focus, perseveration (persistence despite changed task demand), and desired/undesired disengagement; overlapping constructs, not categorical opposites or proven common mechanism. Measured output and cost need independent criteria.

## Operational gate: distinguish attention from output and control
1. Specify timescale: trial-level variability, block time-on-task slope, day-to-day performance, task selection, initiation, maintenance, switching, stopping, recovery. A high average and high variability may coexist; low average and stable response may coexist. No conversion of task RT into daily productivity. Define actual quality, output per time, error rate, effort and opportunity cost; account for expertise, task difficulty, interruptions and context. A high-output episode need not be HF; a low-output episode need not be inattentive.
2. Analyze engagement and disengagement separately. Ask whether task was chosen, goal remained relevant, peripheral information was missed, stopping was desired/possible, actual output improved, important obligations or basic needs affected. Distinguish pleasure, perceived absorption, narrowed attention, compulsive persistence and actual impairment. No diagnostic cutoff for duration or AHQ score; do not equate focus with IQ or moral worth.
3. Competing accounts: local task mismatch, genuine interest/flow, practiced skill, workload, external cueing, sleep/fatigue, stress, mood/anxiety, other neurodevelopmental conditions, ADHD-associated regulation and combinations. If interest increases output, this does not establish ADHD or its absence. If disengagement fails despite a changed goal, test competing demands/awareness and voluntary supports; no covert testing, withdrawal of needed supports, or deliberate sleep deprivation. Consider developmental history and multiple settings per 12.1.
4. For research compare ADHD×IQ and within-person context contrasts, not just two unmatched means; separate between-person from within-person associations. Record base rate, task design, reliability, absolute versus relative performance, accuracy/RT trade-off, response distribution, trial exclusions, medication/context and selection. A significant group effect is not an individual test; null not equivalence. Avoid equating questionnaire endorsement with observed performance or objective neural mechanism. Clinical diagnosis needs qualified assessment; no treatment advice or individual diagnosis.
5. For self-understanding use ordinary, voluntary comparable observations of intended task, challenge/interest, starting conditions, interruptions, output/error, switching and recovery/cost. Compare competing predictions before interpretation; preserve counterexamples. If observed patterns change with context, update local explanation without declaring a unique causal process. Do not invent standardized probabilities or infer latent intelligence. General mechanisms remain hypotheses for measured adult IQ150–160+; no unmarked transfer.

## Internal assessment — new wholly invented cases
A. Fictional weather station trainee processes most readings quickly but misses a few late entries; mean output equals colleague's. Supervisor interprets equal mean as equal sustained attention, then slow trials as definitive ADHD. Compare within-task distribution, initial level vs slope, accuracy, equipment latency, interruptions and fatigue; repeated ordinary comparable blocks with equipment timing logs. New: delayed readings cluster at automated data refreshes shared by both trainees; residual late-block errors persist only on one unfamiliar procedure. Withdraw equipment-caused lapse inference and definitive diagnosis, keep procedure-specific performance gap; compare training/expertise and history, not global deficit. No inferred IQ. 
B. Fictional textile designer becomes absorbed for several hours, completes elaborate patterns, misses a planned meeting and says stopping was difficult. Analyst calls this productive gifted hyperfocus. Compare actual quality, time cost, planned priorities, perceived engagement and ability to disengage; predict different patterns under intended stops or alternate tasks. New: designer reports little enjoyment and repeated revision of already approved details, independent quality ratings unchanged after first draft, voluntarily uses reminder and switches as planned next session. Revise from assumed productive absorption to possible perseveration/goal monitoring issue with context support; neither proves diagnosis nor disproves ADHD, preserve observed initial meeting cost and later ability. No deliberate disruption of needed supports.
C. Fictional research team finds high-ability ADHD volunteers report more hyperfocus than unscreened controls, but task accuracy equal. Claims special IQ-driven attentional phenotype. Compare four groups, within-person/task contexts, sampling and questionnaire vs performance criteria, age/medication, score precision. New: matched high-ability non-ADHD controls endorse comparable absorption; high-ability ADHD volunteers report more difficulty stopping when goals change, but no detected task-group interaction. Withdraw broad gifted-HF claim, preserve reported disengagement difference and objective null with uncertainty; distinguish self-report and actual stopping, no causal mechanism or extreme-tail inference. Need representative and separately measured tail data.
