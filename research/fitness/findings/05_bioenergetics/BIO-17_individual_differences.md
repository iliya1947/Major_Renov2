# BIO-17 — Individual differences

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-20**  
Область: 05 — Bioenergetics / Exercise Metabolism

## 0. Core decision

Individual bioenergetic differences arise from several non-equivalent layers:

1. relatively stable traits;
2. trained phenotype;
3. current metabolic state;
4. current recovery/environmental context.

Project rule:

**trait, trained phenotype and current state must not be treated as interchangeable explanations.**

---

## 1. Response variability

Observed response spread after training can reflect:

- measurement error;
- within-person variability;
- adherence/execution;
- nutrition/sleep;
- baseline state;
- regression to the mean;
- true treatment-response heterogeneity.

Project rules:

- **observed variability ≠ proven true response heterogeneity**;
- **failure to prove heterogeneity ≠ proof that everyone responds identically**;
- **one training block ≠ stable responder/non-responder identity**.

Repeated reliable measurements are required before individual response labels.

---

## 2. Training status

Training status changes:

- relative intensity of a fixed external task;
- oxidative/glycolytic profile;
- substrate disturbance;
- recovery;
- adaptation potential.

But “trained” is domain specific.

Project rule:

**same external workload ≠ same relative metabolic demand across training states.**

Endurance-trained, resistance-trained and sprint-trained are not interchangeable categories.

---

## 3. Baseline fitness

Lower baseline capacity can sometimes permit larger relative improvement.

But:

- ceiling effects;
- scale;
- regression to the mean;
- dose/context

can influence the apparent relation.

Project rule:

**baseline value is context, not an individual response prediction by itself.**

---

## 4. Muscle fiber composition

Broadly:

- type I fibers tend to be slower/more oxidative;
- type II fibers tend to be faster/more glycolytic/high-power;
- type IIa/hybrid phenotypes are intermediate/plastic.

Project rules:

- **fiber type influences local metabolic phenotype**;
- **fiber type ≠ whole-person energy-system identity**;
- **fiber composition ≠ immutable destiny**;
- **local biopsy ≠ whole-body phenotype**.

---

## 5. Biological age

Age can modify:

- muscle phenotype;
- mitochondrial state;
- cardiovascular capacity;
- recovery;
- adaptation context.

Exercise remains effective in older adults.

Project rules:

- **older age ≠ inability to adapt**;
- **chronological age ≠ complete biological/metabolic phenotype**;
- **age alone does not determine training prescription**.

Health/training status must be considered separately.

---

## 6. Biological sex

Sex-linked physiology can alter average distributions of:

- hemoglobin;
- body composition;
- substrate use;
- thermoregulation;
- absolute VO2/power.

But observed differences depend on:

- body composition;
- scaling;
- fitness;
- mode;
- relative intensity;
- age/hormonal context.

Project rules:

- **biological sex is a contextual modifier, not a deterministic prescription**;
- **group sex difference ≠ individual prediction**;
- **women do not universally have higher relative fat oxidation**.

Detailed reproductive/hormonal physiology remains PHY/IND/MED.

---

## 7. Nutrition

Nutrition acutely modifies:

- glycogen;
- glucose;
- free fatty acids;
- insulin;
- substrate oxidation;
- hydration/electrolytes.

Project rule:

**nutritional state is a modifiable current-state variable, not a fixed trait.**

Exact prescription remains NUT.

---

## 8. Energy availability

Energy availability is conceptually:

`(energy intake − exercise energy expenditure) / fat-free mass`

It is an estimate with meaningful uncertainty because EI, EEE and FFM are measured imperfectly.

Project rules:

- **nutrition ≠ energy availability**;
- **LEA ≠ REDs diagnosis**;
- **30 kcal/kg FFM/day ≠ universal diagnostic threshold**;
- **problematic LEA can constrain health/performance/adaptation, but individual effect magnitude varies**.

Clinical REDs assessment remains MED/NUT.

---

## 9. Glycogen status

Muscle glycogen is a transient/local state affected by:

- prior exercise;
- carbohydrate availability;
- time since exercise;
- training status;
- muscle/fiber recruitment.

Project rules:

- **glycogen status ≠ carbohydrate intake alone**;
- **same CHO intake ≠ same glycogen concentration**;
- **glycogen storage capacity ≠ current glycogen state**;
- **no universal glycogen-performance threshold is retained**.

---

## 10. Creatine stores

Muscle creatine/PCr stores vary with:

- diet;
- endogenous synthesis;
- muscle mass;
- uptake;
- supplementation history.

Project rules:

- **creatine-store variability is real**;
- **store response ≠ performance response**;
- **low dietary creatine exposure ≠ guaranteed performance benefit from increasing stores**;
- **baseline store level is not retained as a deterministic performance-response predictor**.

Exact supplementation remains NUT.

---

## 11. Sleep and stress

Sleep loss, mental fatigue and psychological stress can reduce realized performance and recovery quality.

They can act through:

- perceived effort;
- attention/motivation;
- autonomic/endocrine state;
- recovery behavior;
- appetite/energy regulation.

Project rules:

- **sleep/stress are indirect bioenergetic context modifiers**;
- **sleep loss ≠ ATP/glycogen depletion diagnosis**;
- **mental fatigue ≠ metabolic substrate depletion**;
- **stress ≠ one metabolic deficit**.

Detailed management remains REC/PSY.

---

## 12. Genetic variability

Athletic/metabolic traits are highly polygenic and environment/training dependent.

Current evidence shows:

- heterogeneous candidate-gene associations;
- limited replicated genome-wide findings;
- weak external validation/ancestry transferability of many PGS/TGS approaches.

Project rules:

- **genetic contribution is real but probabilistic**;
- **ACTN3/ACE ≠ athlete type**;
- **polygenic score ≠ validated training prescription**;
- **current sports-genetic testing is insufficient for routine individual training prescription**.

When available, repeated measured phenotype should dominate speculative genotype-based prediction.

---

## 13. Environmental conditions

Environment can change:

- oxygen availability;
- thermoregulation;
- HR/ventilation;
- perceived effort;
- sustainable output;
- internal physiological strain.

Important contexts include:

- heat;
- cold;
- hypoxia/altitude;
- humidity;
- air quality.

Project rule:

**same nominal workout ≠ same internal load across environments.**

---

## 14. Acute environment vs chronic adaptation

Acute heat/hypoxia can impair performance.

Repeated exposure can produce selected acclimation/adaptation.

Project rules:

- **acute environmental stress ≠ chronic acclimation/adaptation**;
- **greater environmental stress ≠ better training by definition**;
- **heat acclimation benefit is context specific unless transfer is demonstrated**;
- **hypoxic training is not a universal VO2max/economy/performance upgrade**.

Detailed safety remains MED; exact protocols remain PRO.

---

## 15. Practical attribution hierarchy

When performance differs, use this coaching heuristic:

1. verify task equivalence;
2. verify measurement reliability;
3. inspect current state/context;
4. inspect training history/task familiarity;
5. only then consider slower-changing traits.

This is not a causal law.

Project rule:

**do not attribute today’s performance to immutable traits before excluding measurement and modifiable state factors.**

---

## 16. Individualization boundary

BIO-17 identifies plausible sources of variation.

IND owns:

- which factors matter for this person;
- what to measure;
- when to adjust;
- how to update the decision after repeated observations.

Project rule:

**BIO-17 explains variability; IND turns repeated individual data into action.**

## 18. Trainer-level implications

1. Compare athletes by relative as well as absolute demand.
2. Treat training status as task specific.
3. Do not program from fiber type/genetics as destiny.
4. Use age/sex as context, not prescriptions.
5. Separate nutrition, energy availability and glycogen.
6. Do not diagnose LEA/REDs from one universal EA number.
7. Treat glycogen and creatine as modifiable states.
8. Do not infer metabolic depletion from poor sleep/stress alone.
9. Do not assign responder labels from one block.
10. Account for environmental context when comparing sessions.
11. Prefer repeated measured phenotype over demographic/genetic stereotypes.
12. Exact intervention remains IND/PRO/NUT/REC/MED/TST.

## 19. Confidence

### HIGH

- observed variability must be separated from measurement/within-person variability;
- training status is task/domain specific;
- same external work can impose different relative metabolic demand;
- fiber phenotype is local/plastic and non-deterministic;
- age modifies physiology but does not abolish trainability;
- sex effects are context/scaling dependent;
- nutrition and EA are distinct;
- no universal 30 kcal/kg FFM/day diagnostic threshold is retained;
- glycogen is transient/state dependent;
- creatine store response and performance response are distinct;
- sleep/stress are indirect modifiers;
- sports genetics is not ready for routine individual prescription;
- environment changes internal load.

### MODERATE-HIGH

- training status can moderate substrate disturbance;
- selected fiber subtypes adapt differently;
- problematic LEA can impair performance/training response;
- lower dietary creatine exposure can influence stores;
- mental fatigue can reduce resistance-exercise volume;
- heat acclimation can improve heat-specific physiology/performance.

### MODERATE / CONTEXT DEPENDENT

- true treatment-response heterogeneity for one program/outcome;
- exact fiber contribution to whole-body performance;
- exact age/sex effect on one adaptation;
- exact glycogen threshold for one task;
- exact baseline creatine prediction of response;
- exact environmental transfer/adaptation for one individual.
