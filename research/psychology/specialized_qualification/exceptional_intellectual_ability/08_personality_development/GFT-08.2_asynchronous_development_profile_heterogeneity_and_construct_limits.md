## 2. Evidence cards
E01 — Concept history. Columbus Group definition (1991) reported by Morelock (1992), Giftedness: The view from within, author reprint https://www.davidsongifted.org/gifted-blog/giftedness-the-view-from-within/ indexed excerpts; Silverman (1997), The construct of asynchronous development, https://doi.org/10.1080/0161956X.1997.9681865 indexed publisher abstract. Links advanced cognition, intensity, qualitatively distinctive experience and vulnerability, proposes educational/counseling response. This is conceptual/clinical framing, not an experimentally established causal law, uniform diagnosis or necessary-and-sufficient psychometric criterion. Historical original group record not independently inspected. Worrell et al. Gifted Students (2019), https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-010418-102846 indexed author text describes competing definitions and talent development; a cited formulation of asynchrony is not empirical endorsement. 2026 Overexcitability Reconsidered: Domestication of a Developmental Theory, https://doi.org/10.1177/00169862261480141 indexed author argument challenges treating intensity as diagnostic; theoretical critique, not new prevalence estimate. Full article not inspected. Route OE and sensitivity evidence separately to GFT-10; no inference of emotional intensity from IQ.
E02 — Vaivre-Douret (2011), Developmental and Cognitive Characteristics of “High-Level Potentialities” (Highly Gifted) Children, https://doi.org/10.1155/2011/420297 indexed published text and table caption: review of early developmental observations, including reported sample of 60 followed children compared with French developmental norms. Author describes relatively synchronous early psychomotor/language/socioaffective/cognitive development, while some formal operational tasks are closer to chronological age than mental-age expectations. This is an important contrary conceptual/empirical observation to universal asynchrony, not a definitive longitudinal comparison: recruitment, underlying original study methods, contemporaneous controls, measurement and temporal trajectories not fully verified. Winisdorffer & Vaivre-Douret (2013), PMID23272468 indexed abstract, retrospective clinical-record follow-up 19 referred children with school/relational difficulties, cannot infer population prevalence or general developmental mechanism. Distinguish a developmental milestone reached earlier from rate of change, equal chronological-age stage from lag, history from retrospective attribution.
E03 — Lang, Matta, Parolin, Morrone & Pezzuti (2019 issue, online 2017), https://doi.org/10.1177/1073191117733547 ; author abstract opened PMID28948838 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28948838/. N130 (79 male), WAIS-IV; compared with matched group. Group scores higher in all assessed intellectual domains, perceptual reasoning highest (contrary to expectation that verbal would be highest); higher cross-domain discrepancy than comparison group. Selection cutoff described generally as 2 SD above mean; detailed inclusion (FSIQ versus any index), range, recruitment, precise matching, conditional discrepancy frequencies and reliability not extracted. Cross-sectional cognitive profiles only; no age slope, emotion/adaptive measurement or diagnosis. Relative weakness not absolute weakness. Contrast 2022 Intellectual giftedness in elderly people assessed by WAIS-IV, https://doi.org/10.1080/03601277.2022.2109890 indexed author abstract/results: N94 aged60–90, at least one primary index >=130, matched controls; higher performance in all indices, authors describe relatively stronger processing speed contribution. Different selection and age, no within-person speed preservation/increase established, no independent cohort overlap audit. Adult measured high ability evidence is relevant, not specifically adult FSIQ150–160+.
E04 — Two psychometric profiles of gifted children in referred samples (2025), https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intell.2025.101948 indexed publisher abstract/results/discussion; direct DOI redirect failed. N739, five testing locations France/Belgium, WISC-IV/V. Profiles of relatively homogeneous high performance and stronger crystallized/verbal knowledge, each roughly half referred sample. Standardization-sample comparison described by authors; no distinct profiles identified by their analysis there. Authors explicitly warn referred sample may differ from screen-identified population, including socio-cultural selection. Do not infer two natural kinds, universal frequencies, neurobiological pathways, stable developmental subtypes or intelligence deficit from an index difference. Clustering model, alternative continuous solutions, exact membership uncertainty, selection cutoffs, measurement linking and validation not independently inspected. Revisit GFT-02.2 distinction between statistically reliable difference, base rate and functional importance. Differentiation literature (Blum & Holling 2017 https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intell.2017.07.004 indexed author abstract, and methodological debate https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160289613000809): lower inter-test correlation/loadings at higher ability is a distinct *group covariance* question, not a longitudinal difference in slopes or necessary individual heterogeneity; no new effect estimate or resolution; route GFT-17.
E05 — Guénolé et al. (2013), https://doi.org/10.1155/2013/540153 ; author abstract and captions opened PMID23956988. N143 clinically referred gifted children aged8–12; 29 IQ>=145, 114 IQ130–<145; 51 with significant verbal-performance discrepancy, 92 without. More behavioral problems relative to normative sample; no higher problems for >=145 group; discrepancy group more externalizing and mixed syndromes. Authors operationalized asynchrony through cross-sectional verbal-performance discrepancy, not differential multi-domain developmental slopes. Clinic ascertainment, comparison choice, parent ratings, subgroup selection, small high-tail N and unmeasured alternatives limit inference. Association within referrals not a causal mechanism or prevalence estimate. No universal increase with IQ. Exact threshold is study/test-specific, not a general current WAIS/WISC decision rule. No adult transfer.
E06 — Lavrijsen & Verschueren (2023), https://doi.org/10.3390/jintelligence11020038 ; author abstract opened PMID36826936; indexed author methods/results. Nonselective community sample N3409, mean12.5 years, CoVaT-CHC; high scores >=120 N403 (indexed subgroup 120–130 N272 and >130 N127; subgroups total399, do not silently reconcile without actual analytic denominators). High cognitive ability group not at increased risk on studied adjustment indicators; previously identified/labeled group reported worse outcomes on some indicators. Identification pathway and confounding by referral/recognition or prior difficulty may explain difference; no causal label effect established, not proof no individual difficulty or equivalence in adult extreme range. Peyre et al. (2016), EDEN cohort, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0191886916300691 indexed author abstract/results: FSIQ>130 N23 vs 70–130 N1058, no significant SDQ differences except marginal emotional difficulty association age5–6. Small gifted subgroup, cannot infer equal risk or tail-specific rates. Different selection versus E05 is an important competing explanation, not a formally tested source of between-study differences. No full effect models reanalyzed.

Non-Cognitive Specificities of Intellectually Gifted Children and Adolescents (2023), https://doi.org/10.3390/jintelligence11070141 indexed author synthesis (direct MDPI 429): no stable noncognitive profile reliably distinguishing gifted from non-gifted youth across reviewed outcomes. Neuropsychological Profile of Intellectually Gifted Children: A Systematic Review (2021 online), PMID33998437 indexed abstract: 15/658 papers eligible; broad task domains and methodological difficulties. ## 3. Operational construct gate (PRACTICAL_HEURISTIC)
Do not conflate 1) level relative to age reference, 2) within-profile difference at one occasion, 3) change of each domain and of the difference across occasions, 4) absolute functional limitation, 5) mismatch between individual capacities/interests and context, 6) subjective experience, 7) causal mechanism. 
| Evidence | Legitimate statement | Not licensed |
|---|---|---|
| high reasoning, average speed | measured relative strength and relative weakness under conditions | impaired speed, developmental delay or ADHD |
| reliable cognitive discrepancy | score difference with reference and uncertainty | specific emotional age or cause |
| age-appropriate regulation despite advanced reasoning | observed differentiated strengths | emotional immaturity or obligatory lag |
| differing linked domain change across waves | observed nonparallel trajectories with uncertainty | universal path or disorder |
| different social outcomes across settings | observed context dependence | proof inability or cause of giftedness |
| reports of not fitting | subjective experience and concrete situations | population rate, diagnostic marker or immutable trait |
To substantiate a trajectory claim, define domains, instruments, reference, time interval, hypotheses and meaning of synchrony before assessment. Repeated domain-specific comparable measures needed (multiple waves preferable); distinguish absolute linked-score changes from changing age-standardized positions. Two different scale units cannot be subtracted as physical quantities; standardized differences depend on reference/norm, reliability and measurement invariance. Nonparallel norm-relative trajectories are not necessarily unequal absolute slopes. No literal mental age by multiplying IQ by chronological age or converting a score into emotional/social age. A specific developmental milestone age can be meaningful if validated; not a global age equivalent. 
Toy measurement illustration: on two standardized indices both mean100 SD15, A=145 and B=115 gives difference30; B is +1 SD, not below norm. If illustrative independent errors with SEM5 each, standard error of difference sqrt(25+25)=7.07. Actual correlated errors, age/ability-specific precision, selection, reliability and base rates can change interpretation; difference significance not rarity, impairment or cause. Use applicable manual (GFT-02); do not apply hypothetical threshold to real instruments. If A rises 10 linked units and B rises 10 on comparable defined scale, gap constant, not evidence of divergent change; if A rises15 and B5, gap changes10, still check reference, precision, practice and measurement equivalence. Unequal growth possible without disorder, different baseline not proof of differing rates. 
Selection example: if a program admits for either high A or high B, conditional correlation between scores may change and profile spread may increase even without two latent types; referral because of uneven performance or problems further distorts sample. Do not estimate person-specific causal probabilities from referral rates. Use separate counterfactual hypotheses: genuine uneven ability, testing artifact, context mismatch, difference in exposure/training, emotional/physical state, independent co-occurring condition. An independent diagnosis requires its own appropriate criteria and assessment (GFT-12/13), not discrepancy. Test predictions across relevant settings and over time; support concrete needs without requiring a giftedness label. Distinguish absence of evidence of universal vulnerability from evidence of no difficulties in any person. 
When comparing age- vs ability-matched groups: chronological age helps assess age-relative functioning, ability matching may select different ages/exposure, not a perfect causal control. Compare development, not expected similarity to older high-achieving children. An association of gifted status and social difficulty may be due to context/selection, not higher IQ causing difficulties. For claim of a distinct syndrome, require operational definition, reproducible reliable measurement, incremental validity beyond components and alternatives, longitudinal temporality and population/selection constraints; not established by retrieved evidence. Asynchrony usable as a descriptive hypothesis when concretely specified, not default latent cause.
