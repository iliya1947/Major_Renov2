# NEU-14 — Attention and focus

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-19**  
Область: 04 — Neurophysiology / Motor Learning

## 0. Core decision

Attentional focus should not be reduced to one universal rule.

A defensible project model is:

`task information need`
+
`learner expertise`
+
`current error/problem`
+
`performance vs learning vs recruitment goal`
+
`attentional load/context`
→
`focus strategy`.

Possible strategies include:

- internal focus;
- external focus;
- proximal/distal external focus;
- holistic/global focus;
- minimal/no prescribed focus;
- switching focus across preparation/execution.

Project rule:

**choose focus by the task-relevant information and outcome being optimized, not by a universal external-over-internal hierarchy.**

---

## 1. Internal focus

Internal focus directs attention toward:

- body part;
- joint/movement;
- muscle;
- bodily sensation.

It is not equivalent to:

- explicit learning;
- narrow attention;
- poor learning.

Internal focus can be useful when body/proprioceptive information is directly relevant.

Project rule:

**internal focus is not universally harmful.**

---

## 2. External focus

External focus directs attention toward:

- movement effect;
- implement;
- target;
- environment;
- task outcome.

Published motor-learning evidence often favors external focus.

However the strongest current reporting-bias reanalysis found:

- substantial publication/reporting bias;
- near-null adjusted average effects;
- high heterogeneity.

Project rule:

**external focus is often useful, but universal superiority is not established.**

---

## 3. Current evidence conflict

Older meta-analysis:

- external > internal for performance;
- external > internal for retention;
- external > internal for transfer.

2024 robust Bayesian reanalysis:

- strong reporting-bias signal;
- adjusted effects near zero;
- heterogeneity remains.

A new 2026 complex-manual-skill experiment again favored external focus for acquisition and next-day retention.

That study is:

- small;
- task specific;
- not resistance-exercise technique.

Project rule:

**positive individual trials do not restore a universal field-level rule.**

---

## 4. Cue classification matters

Attentional-focus labels are not always applied consistently.

A 2026 network meta-analysis reclassified most included studies under prespecified definitions and obtained changed rankings.

Therefore:

**inspect the actual cue, not only the study's internal/external label.**

---

## 5. External-focus distance

External focus can be:

- proximal;
- distal.

Recent meta-analysis found distal focus favorable on average.

But:

- novice subgroup did not show a clear distal-vs-proximal advantage;
- reporting-bias-adjusted analyses do not support a universal distance effect.

Project rule:

**“farther is better” is not a universal law.**

---

## 6. Expertise

Expertise can modify attentional-focus response.

Current evidence suggests possible differences across skill tiers, including greater value of holistic focus in more experienced groups.

But:

- findings are classification-sensitive;
- small-study/reporting-bias concerns remain.

Project rule:

**expertise is a modifier, not a fixed prescription table.**

---

## 7. Neutral / minimal cueing

No-focus/control means:

> no attentional direction was prescribed.

It does not mean:

> the learner had no focus.

Participants can spontaneously choose:

- internal;
- external;
- mixed focus.

Project rule:

**minimal cueing is a coaching condition, not a process-pure neutral cognitive state.**

It can be useful when additional instruction has no diagnosed information value.

---

## 8. Holistic focus

Holistic focus directs attention toward the overall/global movement feeling or effect.

It is different from:

- no focus;
- broad visual attention;
- generic external focus.

Evidence is promising but limited.

Project rule:

**holistic focus is a legitimate option, not an established universal best strategy.**

---

## 9. Narrow vs broad attention

Attentional breadth is separate from attentional direction.

A person can have:

- narrow internal focus;
- broad internal focus;
- narrow external focus;
- broad external focus.

The visual-attention literature supports a breadth construct.

But direct resistance-exercise evidence is sparse.

Project rule:

**do not equate external with broad or internal with narrow.**

---

## 10. Attentional load

Attentional/cognitive load can rise through:

- multiple simultaneous rules;
- secondary tasks;
- pressure;
- unfamiliar task demands;
- monitoring many variables.

Visuomotor evidence shows cognitive load can suppress explicit learning while sparing implicit adaptation.

But this does not establish a universal cue-count rule.

Project rule:

**remove unnecessary simultaneous cognitive demands, but do not invent a fixed maximum number of cues.**

---

## 11. Dual-task interference

Dual-task cost can indicate attentional competition.

It depends on:

- secondary task;
- task pairing;
- priority;
- practice history;
- task integration.

Project rule:

**dual-task cost is not a pure automaticity score.**

---

## 12. Automaticity

Automaticity is:

- graded;
- task specific;
- measure dependent.

Practice can improve speed/fluency without eliminating attentional demand.

Project rule:

**automaticity ≠ zero attention.**

---

## 13. Internal focus for correction

An internal cue can be useful when:

1. a specific information problem has been diagnosed;
2. body/proprioceptive information is relevant;
3. the cue improves the target outcome;
4. it is removed/faded when no longer useful.

Possible uses:

- body-position awareness;
- movement preparation;
- selective muscle recruitment;
- temporary technical correction.

Project rule:

**use internal cues to solve a diagnosed information problem, not as a universal correction method.**

Exact cue wording/fading belongs NEU-16.

---

## 14. Resistance-exercise performance

External focus can improve some acute outcomes:

- strength;
- muscular endurance;
- velocity/displacement.

But effects are:

- task dependent;
- load dependent;
- outcome specific.

Project rule:

**acute performance benefit ≠ technique learning.**

---

## 15. Target-muscle focus

Internal target-muscle focus can increase recruitment in some resistance exercises.

Evidence includes:

- selective EMG changes at moderate loads;
- changed squat muscle-excitation distribution;
- one 8-week RCT with greater elbow-flexor hypertrophy under internal focus.

Counterexamples also exist.

Project rule:

**target-muscle focus can be useful for recruitment/hypertrophy goals, but it is not a universal technique-learning strategy.**

---

## 16. Outcome dependence

The “best” focus depends on what is being optimized.

Possible outcomes:

- movement accuracy;
- technique retention;
- transfer;
- force;
- velocity;
- endurance;
- target-muscle recruitment;
- hypertrophy.

Project rule:

**do not infer one attentional strategy from a different outcome domain.**

---

## 17. Reinvestment / conscious control

Reinvestment means increased conscious engagement in movement control or decision making.

Meta-analytic evidence does not show a universal negative association with performance.

Negative associations become more evident under some conditions:

- psychological pressure;
- object manipulation;
- speed outcomes.

Project rule:

**conscious control is contextually risky, not universally harmful.**

---

## 18. When excess conscious control can disrupt skill

Risk is greater when:

- the skill is already well established;
- execution is fast/fluid;
- pressure increases self-monitoring;
- performer tries to control movement step by step;
- attention is simultaneously taxed.

But deliberate control can remain useful during:

- diagnosis;
- preparation;
- correction;
- learning a new relation.

Project rule:

**avoid unnecessary execution-phase micromanagement of an established skill, but do not remove task-relevant deliberate attention.**

---

## 19. Canonical non-equivalences

1. External focus ≠ universally superior.
2. Internal focus ≠ universally harmful.
3. External focus ≠ implicit learning.
4. Internal focus ≠ explicit learning.
5. External focus ≠ broad attention.
6. Internal focus ≠ narrow attention.
7. No prescribed focus ≠ no actual focus.
8. Control condition ≠ process-neutral state.
9. Distal external focus ≠ always better.
10. Holistic focus ≠ no focus.
11. Holistic focus ≠ broad visual attention.
12. Dual-task cost ≠ automaticity itself.
13. Smooth execution ≠ attention-free execution.
14. Acute force/velocity benefit ≠ motor learning.
15. EMG increase ≠ better technique.
16. Target-muscle focus ≠ universal correction method.
17. Conscious attention ≠ automatic performance failure.
18. Reinvestment association ≠ one proven choking mechanism.
19. Expert focus strategy ≠ novice strategy by default.

---

## 20. Measurement boundaries

### Prescribed focus
What instruction was given.

### Adopted focus
What learner actually attended to.

### Direction
Internal vs external.

### Distance
Proximal vs distal external.

### Breadth
Narrow vs broad.

### Holistic focus
Global movement feeling/effect.

### Acute performance
Current force, velocity, accuracy, endurance.

### Recruitment
EMG / activation pattern.

### Learning
Delayed retention / transfer.

### Automaticity
Task-specific control demand.

### Dual-task cost
Interference under a specified secondary-task condition.

Project rule:

**focus studies are interpretable only when instruction, adopted strategy and measured outcome remain separate.**

---

## 21. Practical coaching synthesis

For future resistance-exercise technique work:

1. do not impose a cue when current execution is already adequate;
2. prefer task-effect/external cues when they clearly simplify the movement goal;
3. use internal cues temporarily when body/proprioceptive/recruitment information is the diagnosed bottleneck;
4. consider holistic/global focus for integrated skilled execution;
5. avoid unnecessary simultaneous technical rules;
6. do not infer automaticity from smooth appearance;
7. avoid detailed step-by-step conscious control during established high-speed/pressure execution;
8. test learning with delayed target-specific outcomes.

Status:

**PRACTICAL_HEURISTIC**, not a universal attentional algorithm.

---

## 22. Direct compound-lift evidence boundary

Direct long-term attentional-focus evidence for squat/press/deadlift technique retention and transfer remains sparse.

Most resistance-training evidence concerns:

- acute strength;
- endurance;
- velocity;
- EMG;
- hypertrophy.

Therefore:

**general attentional principles constrain coaching logic, but exact compound-lift cue architecture remains downstream work.**

---

## 23. Confidence

### HIGH

- internal/external direction is distinct from explicit/implicit learning;
- no-focus condition is not necessarily no actual focus;
- dual-task cost is not a pure automaticity score;
- automaticity is graded;
- acute performance/EMG ≠ durable learning;
- focus labels/classification matter.

### MODERATE-HIGH

- external focus is often useful;
- universal external superiority is not established;
- expertise can modify focus effects;
- internal focus can be useful when task-relevant body information matters;
- conscious micromanagement can impair established skill in some demanding contexts.

### MODERATE

- distal external focus advantage in some expert/task contexts;
- holistic focus as a useful strategy;
- internal cueing for temporary correction;
- outcome-specific focus selection in RT.

### LOW / CONTEXT-DEPENDENT

- exact focus for a specific compound lift;
- exact attentional breadth;
- exact cue-load threshold;
- exact focus-switch timing;
- exact fading schedule;
- long-term technique-learning effects in resistance exercise.

### NOT ESTABLISHED AS UNIVERSAL

- external > internal;
- distal > proximal;
- internal = harmful;
- no cue = neutral;
- holistic = best;
- expert strategy = novice strategy;
- automaticity = no attention;
- conscious control = poor performance.
