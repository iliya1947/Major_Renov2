# RES-06 — Sampling, external validity и applicability

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v1.0**  
Дата: 2026-11-16  
Область: 13 — Методология исследований

## 0. Question Card

### Decision
Как определить, насколько internally valid evidence переносится на конкретную target population, setting и Илью, не требуя невозможного “идеального совпадения”.

### Question type
Methodological / applicability / indirectness.

### Critical outcomes
1. Distinguish internal validity from applicability.
2. Identify important PICO/context differences.
3. Avoid representativeness-as-binary thinking.
4. Separate relative-effect transport from absolute-effect differences.
5. Define project applicability assessment.

### Closure criterion
Есть systematic applicability checklist, sufficient for later evidence-to-Ilya translation.

---

## 1. Internal validity и external validity — разные вопросы

### Internal validity
Насколько result корректно отвечает вопросу **внутри изученной study population**.

Threats:
- bias;
- confounding;
- missing data;
- measurement;
- selective reporting.

### External validity / applicability / generalizability / transportability
Насколько result подходит для **другой target population/context**.

Study может быть:
- internally strong but poorly applicable;
- applicable in population but internally biased;
- both;
- neither.

Project rule:
**никогда не компенсировать poor internal validity аргументом “зато похоже на реальную жизнь”.**

## 2. Applicability — относительно конкретного target question

Нет “объективной external validity вообще”.

Один study может быть:
- highly applicable to trained young men;
- indirect for older adults;
- partly applicable to detrained adult home trainee.

Applicability всегда оценивается относительно pre-defined Question Card/PICO.

## 3. GRADE indirectness

Основные источники indirectness:

1. **Population**
2. **Intervention**
3. **Comparator**
4. **Outcome**
5. indirect comparison between interventions
6. context/setting where it plausibly modifies effect or implementation

Direct evidence:
исследует нужную population, intervention/comparator и relevant outcomes достаточно близко к target question.

Indirect evidence:
есть meaningful mismatch, способный изменить expected effect, harms, feasibility or interpretation.

## 4. Population indirectness

Проверять:

1. age;
2. biological sex when relevant;
3. training status;
4. health status;
5. baseline strength/fitness;
6. body composition;
7. prior training history;
8. relevant medication;
9. injury/pain status;
10. baseline risk;
11. recruitment source;
12. motivation/selectivity.

### Exercise example
Trial in resistance-trained competitive lifters:
может быть indirect для detrained home trainee по:
- adaptation rate;
- skill;
- tolerated volume;
- load availability;
- recovery expectations.

Но это не означает, что study “бесполезно”; нужно определить, **какая часть mechanism/effect plausibly transfers**.

## 5. Representativeness is not binary

“Sample not representative” слишком грубо.

Вопросы:
1. Какая target population?
2. Чем sample отличается?
3. Есть ли reason to expect effect modification?
4. Влияет ли difference только на baseline outcome, или на relative effect?
5. Насколько разница меняет practical decision?

Convenience sample не автоматически invalid.
Но recruitment may select:
- healthier;
- more motivated;
- more exercise-tolerant;
- university-aged;
- more available participants.

Это может ограничивать applicability.

## 6. Volunteer / healthy-participant selection

Exercise trials часто требуют:
- willingness to train repeatedly;
- schedule availability;
- no serious disease;
- completion of testing.

Поэтому participants могут быть более:
- motivated;
- healthy;
- adherent
чем real-world target users.

Consequences:
1. adherence may be overestimated;
2. dropout may be underestimated;
3. tolerability may be better;
4. physiological effect can still be valid if effect modification is absent.

Нужно отдельно оценивать efficacy и implementation/adherence applicability.

## 7. Intervention indirectness

Название intervention недостаточно.

Compare:
- supervised gym resistance training;
- unsupervised home dumbbells.

Даже при одинаковых sets/reps differences:
1. available load;
2. technique supervision;
3. encouragement;
4. equipment stability;
5. exercise menu;
6. progression;
7. adherence;
8. safety monitoring.

Question:
> Could these differences plausibly change the outcome relevant to decision?

## 8. Comparator indirectness

Effect is always relative to comparator.

Example:
“program A improves strength vs no training”
не отвечает напрямую:
“program A better than program B?”

Similarly:
- failure vs nonfailure with unmatched volume;
- high vs low frequency with different weekly volume
may produce comparison indirect to the variable we actually want.

## 9. Outcome indirectness

Surrogate/proxy outcomes can be indirect.

Examples:
- EMG for hypertrophy;
- acute MPS for chronic hypertrophy;
- muscle thickness at one site for total muscle growth;
- self-reported activity for actual training dose;
- soreness for effectiveness.

Outcome must match practical decision.

## 10. Time indirectness

Even if PICO matches, duration may not.

Examples:
- acute session response;
- 4-week adaptation;
- 12-week hypertrophy;
- multi-year health outcome.

Short studies may underrepresent:
- slow tissue adaptation;
- adherence decay;
- injury/tolerability;
- plateau;
- long-term sustainability.

Time remains explicit in project PICOT even if GRADE often treats it within intervention/outcome.

## 11. Setting/context

Cochrane highlights context-sensitive non-pharmacological interventions.

Potential modifiers:
1. supervision;
2. facility/equipment;
3. coach expertise;
4. culture;
5. resources;
6. time availability;
7. cost;
8. environmental conditions;
9. social support;
10. adherence incentives.

For Ilya:
**home + minimal equipment + ADHD-related behavioral constraints** are part of applicability, not an afterthought.

## 12. Adherence and implementation

High adherence in closely monitored trial may not transport to ordinary life.

Two distinct questions:
1. Does intervention work **when implemented**?
2. Will target person **actually implement** it?

Trial efficacy can be strong while practical effectiveness is poor due to:
- setup friction;
- session duration;
- boredom;
- complexity;
- equipment requirements.

This is why PSY/COA evidence is needed alongside physiological efficacy.

## 13. Relative effect vs absolute effect

Even if relative effect is similar across groups, absolute benefit/harm can vary with baseline risk/capacity.

Medical example:
same relative risk reduction → different absolute benefit at different baseline risk.

Fitness analogy:
same percentage strength increase:
- may correspond to very different absolute kg changes;
- may have different practical meaning depending on starting level.

Similarly:
same hypertrophy effect size can have different relevance depending on:
- baseline muscle size;
- training status;
- goal.

## 14. Effect modification

Difference in population matters most when characteristic plausibly modifies causal effect.

Potential moderators in exercise science:
- training status;
- age;
- baseline strength;
- sex for specific questions;
- exercise experience;
- energy availability;
- intervention dose;
- equipment/mode.

But:
subgroup claims require evidence.
Do not assume every demographic difference modifies response.

## 15. Inclusion/exclusion criteria

Strict exclusion criteria improve sample homogeneity but may reduce applicability.

Examples:
excluding:
- medication users;
- people with minor pain;
- irregular sleep;
- common comorbidities.

Study may then answer:
“does intervention work in selected healthy participants?”
not:
“does it work in ordinary population?”

This is indirectness, not necessarily internal bias.

## 16. Recruitment source

Potential samples:
1. university students;
2. gym members;
3. athletes;
4. clinic patients;
5. social-media volunteers;
6. population registry.

Recruitment source can affect:
- baseline characteristics;
- motivation;
- health;
- experience;
- adherence.

Need record source rather than merely sample size.

## 17. Attrition and applicability

Differential dropout is first an internal bias issue.

But even without strong bias:
if only highly tolerant/adherent participants finish, practical tolerability may be less applicable to target population.

Separate:
- causal result among intended study population;
- real-world feasibility.

## 18. Study diversity can improve applicability

Heterogeneous studies are not automatically bad.

If similar effect appears across:
- ages;
- settings;
- exercises;
- countries;
- training statuses,

this can support broader applicability.

But heterogeneity may also reveal real effect modification.

Detailed synthesis → RES-17.

## 19. “Exact match” is impossible and unnecessary

Cochrane explicitly notes that no individual will perfectly match research participants.

Correct question is not:
> “Is Ilya identical to study participants?”

Correct questions:
1. What differences exist?
2. Which differences plausibly change effect?
3. Is direction/magnitude likely important enough to change decision?
4. Can we start from evidence and monitor personal response?

## 20. Applicability hierarchy for Ilya

For each evidence body assess:

### A. Direct
Close enough on key effect-modifying dimensions:
- adult;
- similar training status;
- healthy enough for question;
- relevant resistance modality/dose;
- relevant outcome;
- useful timeframe.

### B. Moderately indirect
Mismatch exists, but no strong reason it reverses/invalidates effect.
Use with explicit caveat and monitoring.

### C. Highly indirect
Major mismatch in:
- pathology;
- training status;
- intervention;
- outcome proxy;
- duration;
- context
with credible reason for different effect.

Do not use as primary basis for prescription.

### D. Mechanistic/context-only
Not suitable for direct prescription but useful for explanation/hypothesis.

## 21. Applicability Card — project protocol

For each major claim:

1. **Target:** who/what decision?
2. **Population match:** age/training/health/baseline.
3. **Intervention match:** mode/dose/progression/supervision.
4. **Comparator match.**
5. **Outcome match:** direct vs proxy.
6. **Time match.**
7. **Setting match:** home/gym/clinical.
8. **Adherence/context match.**
9. **Known effect modifiers.**
10. **Expected direction of indirectness**, if justified.
11. **Applicability rating:** direct / moderate indirect / high indirect / mechanism-only.
12. **Personal monitoring needed?**

## 22. Exercise-science examples

### Example 1 — trained males, barbell study → Ilya with dumbbells
Potentially transferable:
- broad physiological dose-response.

Potentially indirect:
- absolute loads;
- specific exercise mechanics;
- skill;
- progression ceiling.

Result:
may inform principle, not exact exercise prescription.

### Example 2 — untrained participants
Likely more applicable after long inactivity than advanced-lifter evidence for:
- early strength gains;
- rapid learning;
- low initial dose response.

But if participants are complete novices and Ilya has prior training history, retraining dynamics may differ.

### Example 3 — supervised intervention
Physiological efficacy may transfer.
Adherence, technique and progression success may not fully transfer to unsupervised home context.

### Example 4 — elderly clinical population
Can inform general mechanism/safety boundaries.
Direct dosage transfer to younger healthy adult may be unnecessarily conservative or irrelevant.

## 23. Applicability vs personalization

External evidence defines a reasonable starting range.

Personalization then uses:
- baseline assessment;
- actual performance;
- recovery;
- adherence;
- pain;
- progression.

Do not require perfect external evidence before acting.
Do not ignore indirectness either.

Project strategy:
**best applicable evidence → conservative starting decision → monitoring → adaptation.**

## 24. Project rules

1. Never label evidence “not applicable” from one demographic mismatch alone.
2. Never assume same relative effect without considering credible modifiers.
3. Separate physiological efficacy from behavioral/implementation applicability.
4. Record supervision/equipment/setting.
5. Treat outcome proxies as indirect for target outcomes.
6. Treat training status as a high-priority applicability dimension in resistance training.
7. Use directness as a continuum with explicit reasons.
8. Apply to Ilya only after separate applicability assessment.

## 25. Confidence

### HIGH
- applicability is relative to target PICO/context;
- indirectness can arise from population/intervention/comparator/outcome mismatch;
- internal validity and applicability are distinct;
- exact participant match is neither possible nor necessary;
- context and adherence can affect applicability of non-pharmacological interventions.

### MODERATE
- exact weighting of each exercise-specific applicability dimension is topic-dependent.

### PRACTICAL_HEURISTIC
- Direct / moderate indirect / high indirect / mechanism-only project scale;
- Applicability Card.
