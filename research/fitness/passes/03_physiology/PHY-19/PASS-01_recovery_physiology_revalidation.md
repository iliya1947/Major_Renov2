## 1. Purpose / Question Card

### Decision

Build a bounded multi-system model of post-exercise recovery sufficient for later programming, monitoring, nutrition and individualization without:

- treating recovery as one binary state;
- assigning one universal recovery duration to a session;
- equating ATP-PCr restoration with full readiness;
- equating neural recovery with muscle contractile recovery;
- equating soreness or CK with performance recovery;
- assuming glycogen restoration guarantees neuromuscular recovery;
- treating elevated muscle protein synthesis as proof of completed repair;
- treating inflammation as something that should simply be suppressed;
- assuming muscle and connective tissue remodel on the same clock;
- treating sleep as a single isolated recovery mechanism;
- assuming subjective readiness and objective performance always move together;
- using one biomarker, wearable score or questionnaire as a universal readiness test.

### Question type

Time-course / systems physiology of recovery after exercise.

### Closure criterion for this PASS

All 10 exact Section 21 items must be covered at content level; ATP-PCr, neural, excitation-contraction, glycogen, protein turnover, inflammatory resolution, connective tissue and sleep must remain distinct; exercise-mode and damage/fatigue context must modify the time course; subjective and objective recovery must be allowed to dissociate; no universal “48/72 h” rule may be inferred.

## 3. Recovery is a vector, not a scalar

Different systems recover on different time scales.

A useful state vector is:

`R(t) = [PCr, neural drive, EC coupling/force, glycogen, protein turnover, inflammation, connective tissue, sleep/cognition, performance, perception]`.

At a given time point:

- one component can be near baseline;
- another can remain impaired;
- a third can remain actively remodeling/adapting.

Project rule:

**“recovered” must always specify what outcome/system is recovered.**

No universal whole-body recovery clock is accepted.

---

## 4. ATP-PCr recovery

PCr is depleted during high-intensity contractions and resynthesized predominantly through oxidative metabolism during recovery.

31P-MRS evidence shows that PCr recovery:

- is rapid relative to most structural processes;
- is approximately exponential under common measurement conditions;
- varies across muscle groups, health status, oxidative capacity, pH and methodology;
- is sufficiently method-sensitive that a universal “normal” constant is inappropriate.

Classical human studies show fast components on the order of tens of seconds with substantial restoration over the first few minutes.

Important boundary:

**PCr restoration within minutes does not mean neural, contractile, glycogen, structural or perceptual recovery is complete.**

Detailed energy-system kinetics remain BIO-owned (Area 05).

---

## 5. Neural-function recovery

Neural recovery is not one variable.

Relevant components include:

- central voluntary drive;
- corticospinal excitability/inhibition;
- motor-unit discharge behavior;
- recruitment/coordination;
- task-specific skill;
- afferent feedback.

Human eccentric-fatigue evidence shows motor-unit discharge rate can return toward baseline within ~10 min while voluntary torque and electrically evoked twitch torque remain depressed at 30 min.

Thus neural and peripheral contractile recovery can dissociate even within the same task.

Across strenuous sessions, field neuromuscular measures such as CMJ can remain impaired for 24–48 h or longer depending on protocol, participant and measurement.

Project rule:

**neural recovery ≠ peripheral muscle recovery, and neither has one universal time course.**

---

## 6. Excitation-contraction function recovery

Excitation-contraction coupling links membrane excitation to SR Ca2+ release and force production.

Fatigue can impair:

- SR Ca2+ handling;
- Ca2+ sensitivity;
- cross-bridge force;
- membrane/T-tubule function;
- low-frequency force production.

Human muscle-damaging eccentric exercise shows an extreme case:

- force-generating capacity was markedly reduced shortly after exercise;
- force remained incompletely recovered at 96 h;
- impaired Ca2+-activated force and altered SR Ca2+ uptake/redox state were detectable during recovery.

This does not imply that ordinary RT requires 96 h.

It demonstrates that severe damaging eccentric exposure can produce prolonged peripheral contractile impairment even after rapid metabolites such as PCr have recovered.

Project rule:

**fast energetic recovery and slow excitation-contraction/contractile recovery can coexist.**

---

## 7. Glycogen restoration

Resistance exercise can meaningfully deplete local muscle glycogen.

The 2025 meta-analysis found depletion depended on:

- number of sets;
- session duration;
- intensity pattern;
- training status.

Postexercise glycogen resynthesis is strongly influenced by:

- degree of depletion;
- carbohydrate availability;
- time available before the next demand;
- muscle damage;
- total energy intake.

Systematic evidence supports regular carbohydrate intake as the primary nutritional driver when rapid short-term restoration is needed.

With adequate carbohydrate, substantial restoration can occur over hours and commonly over ~24 h after major depletion, but the exact course is not universal.

Important boundary:

**glycogen restored ≠ all performance/neuromuscular systems recovered.**

---

## 8. Protein turnover

Resistance exercise increases muscle protein synthesis (MPS), but MPS is not a direct “recovery-complete” marker.

The 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis found:

- robust post-RT MPS elevation;
- measurable elevation can persist up to ~48 h;
- substantial heterogeneity by age, muscle/protein fraction, training status and loading parameters.

Protein ingestion can augment acute MPS, but:

- acute MPS does not equal net long-term hypertrophy;
- elevated MPS can reflect remodeling/repair as well as accretion;
- damaging exercise can increase repair-related turnover while performance remains impaired.

Project rule:

**ongoing protein turnover indicates active remodeling, not necessarily incomplete or complete readiness.**

Detailed protein/nutrition prescription remains NUT-owned.

---

## 9. Inflammatory resolution

Postexercise inflammation is not simply a harmful state that passively disappears.

Recovery involves:

1. initial inflammatory/immune signaling;
2. clearance of damaged material where present;
3. macrophage/immune-state transitions;
4. pro-resolving signaling;
5. tissue repair/remodeling.

Current review evidence emphasizes **active resolution**, including specialized pro-resolving lipid mediators and reparative immune transitions.

Important boundaries:

- acute inflammation is not automatically pathology;
- lower inflammatory biomarkers are not automatically better recovery;
- CK/IL-6/CRP trajectories are not interchangeable with performance;
- aggressively suppressing all inflammation is not assumed to improve adaptation.

Project rule:

**inflammatory resolution is an active phase of recovery, not equivalent to “inflammation = zero.”**

---

## 10. Connective-tissue recovery / remodeling

Connective tissue does not share the same clock as muscle energetics or subjective soreness.

Human tendon evidence shows:

- loading can rapidly activate ECM-related transcriptional programs;
- tendon collagen/protein synthesis and matrix turnover can remain altered over hours-to-days;
- older tracer studies observed tendon collagen synthesis peaking around ~24 h and remaining elevated toward 72 h after strenuous exercise;
- however, newer D2O work found no measurable short-term exercise effect on patellar-tendon protein synthesis over an 8-day integration window, while soluble matrix turnover greatly exceeded insoluble collagen turnover.

This apparent difference is important rather than contradictory:
- tissue fraction;
- tracer method;
- integration window;
- loading mode;
- baseline training status

change what “recovery/remodeling” means.

Project rule:

**there is no universal tendon/connective-tissue recovery timer, and absence of soreness or restored muscle performance does not prove matrix remodeling is complete.**

Detailed tissue loading belongs MEC/PRO/MED.

---

## 11. Sleep-dependent recovery

Sleep affects multiple recovery domains rather than acting as one isolated repair pathway.

Current evidence supports sleep effects on:

- perceived fatigue;
- aerobic/endurance performance;
- maximum force;
- speed/power;
- skill/cognitive performance;
- autonomic/metabolic and endocrine context.

The 2025 meta-analysis of sleep deprivation found impairments across several athletic performance domains and higher perceived exertion.

However sleep effects are outcome dependent.

A randomized sleep-extension study in elite youth hockey players improved perceived fatigue and demanding cognitive performance without changing CMJ or handgrip outcomes.

Project rule:

**sleep can improve one recovery domain while another objective physical measure is unchanged.**

Therefore “slept well” is relevant context, not proof of full physiological recovery.

## 12. Time course after different training stimuli

Recovery time depends on the stimulus.

Major modifiers include:

- exercise mode;
- eccentric/damaging component;
- volume;
- proximity to failure;
- active muscle mass;
- metabolic demand;
- novelty/repeated-bout status;
- training status;
- nutrition/energy availability;
- sleep;
- preceding training;
- measurement used to define recovery.

Examples:

### High-intensity / repeated-sprint / metabolic work

- PCr can recover substantially within minutes;
- glycogen may require hours to a day or more depending on depletion/refueling;
- repeated-performance capacity can remain limited despite PCr recovery if other constraints persist.

### Non-damaging resistance work

- neural/performance recovery may occur within hours to 1–2 days depending on dose;
- MPS/remodeling may remain elevated after performance has recovered.

### High-volume / failure / novel eccentric or muscle-damaging work

- force, EC coupling and soreness can remain altered for 48–96 h or longer;
- repeated-bout adaptation changes subsequent time courses.

### Endurance work

- substrate, fluid/thermal, autonomic and neuromuscular recovery can have different clocks;
- prolonged glycogen depletion or sleep loss can become the next-session bottleneck.

Project rule:

**recovery time must be indexed to both stimulus and target outcome.**

## 13. Subjective recovery vs objective performance

Subjective and objective recovery measures can correlate, but not perfectly.

Current readiness literature shows:

- readiness/recovery definitions are inconsistent;
- tools include fatigue, soreness, sleep, stress, HRV, CMJ and submaximal tests;
- multimodal approaches are common;
- ability of many “readiness” tools to predict actual performance is limited or untested.

The 2026 sport mHealth scoping review found only partial convergence in the small subset directly comparing subjective and objective internal-load measures.

Direct examples of dissociation:

- sleep extension improved perceived fatigue but did not change CMJ/handgrip in one RCT;
- presleep whey improved sleep metrics and soreness without improving next-day exercise performance;
- recovery interventions can change DOMS or biochemical markers without reliably changing performance.

Project rule:

**“I feel recovered” can be informative, but it is not identical to restored performance; equally, a normal wearable/biomarker value does not prove subjective or functional readiness.**

---

## 14. Why one recovery biomarker fails

Common recovery measures sample different constructs:

- soreness → pain/perception;
- CMJ → task-specific neuromuscular performance;
- HRV/resting HR → autonomic context;
- CK → muscle-membrane/damage-associated marker with high individual variability;
- glycogen → substrate availability;
- PCr recovery → oxidative energetic recovery;
- sleep metrics → sleep quantity/continuity/architecture proxies;
- MPS → protein remodeling;
- cytokines → immune/inflammatory signaling.

Therefore no single measure is a universal recovery truth.

Project rule:

**monitoring must triangulate the outcome that matters for the next task.**

Detailed measurement validity belongs TST.

## 16. Established project-level model

Recovery is multi-dimensional:

`exercise stimulus`
`→ energetic + neural + contractile + substrate + structural + inflammatory + perceptual perturbations`.

Then:

`each component R_i(t)`
returns/remodels on its own time course.

Therefore:

`PCr recovered`
does not imply
`glycogen + force + tendon + sleep + subjective readiness recovered`.

For the next task:

`readiness`
depends on
`which systems are limiting that task`,
not on whether every recovery variable has returned to baseline.

## 17. Training-relevant implications

1. **Do not use one universal 24/48/72 h recovery rule.**
2. **Specify what is being recovered: energy, force, glycogen, tissue, sleep, or perception.**
3. **Do not equate PCr recovery with session recovery.**
4. **Do not equate soreness with muscle damage or performance impairment.**
5. **Do not equate normal soreness with complete readiness.**
6. **Treat glycogen as one substrate constraint, not a whole-body recovery score.**
7. **Treat MPS/protein turnover as remodeling, not a readiness timer.**
8. **Do not attempt to suppress all postexercise inflammation by default.**
9. **Assume connective tissue can remain in remodeling states after muscle performance feels normal.**
10. **Treat sleep as a major cross-system modifier but not a single yes/no recovery switch.**
11. **Use subjective readiness and objective performance as complementary, not interchangeable, signals.**
12. **Detailed recovery interventions and monitoring algorithms belong REC/TST/PRO/NUT/IND.**

## 19. Confidence / unresolved issues

### HIGH

- recovery is multi-system and non-synchronous;
- PCr recovers much faster than structural remodeling;
- neural and peripheral contractile recovery can dissociate;
- glycogen restoration depends strongly on depletion and carbohydrate availability;
- resistance exercise elevates MPS for many hours and often up to ~48 h;
- sleep deprivation can impair multiple performance domains;
- subjective and objective recovery are non-identical constructs.

### MODERATE-HIGH

- severe eccentric/damaging exercise can prolong EC-coupling/force impairment for several days;
- inflammatory resolution is an active regulated process;
- connective-tissue remodeling follows a different time course from muscle energetics/performance;
- repeated-bout status materially modifies recovery after damaging exercise.

### MODERATE / CONTEXT-DEPENDENT

- exact recovery time for a given exercise/session/person;
- exact neural vs peripheral contribution at a given time point;
- universal tendon collagen-synthesis time course across loading modes and tracer methods;
- how much subjective readiness predicts next-session performance;
- which combination of monitoring signals best predicts readiness for a specific person/task.
