# RES-25 — Conflicting evidence: как разбирать противоречащие результаты

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v1.0**  
Дата: 2026-11-16  
Область: 13 — Методология исследований

## 0. Question Card

### Decision
Как действовать, когда studies/reviews дают разные выводы, не используя vote counting, авторитет или простое правило “новее = лучше”.

### Question type
Evidence synthesis / conflict resolution.

### Critical outcomes
1. Determine whether evidence truly conflicts.
2. Compare PICO/estimand/time.
3. Compare bias/directness/precision.
4. Use triangulation appropriately.
5. Preserve unresolved uncertainty.

### Closure criterion
Есть repeatable conflict-resolution workflow.

---

## 1. First question: is there really a contradiction?

Two studies may appear inconsistent but answer different questions.

Check:
1. population;
2. intervention dose;
3. comparator;
4. outcome;
5. timeframe;
6. estimand;
7. measurement method.

Example:
- acute EMG study;
- 12-week hypertrophy trial.

They do not “disagree” about the same outcome.

## 2. Vote counting is invalid

Wrong:
- 7 studies positive;
- 5 neutral;
→ majority wins.

This ignores:
- sample size;
- precision;
- bias;
- effect magnitude;
- outcome definitions.

Use estimates and evidence quality.

## 3. Search-date conflict

Two meta-analyses may differ because:
- one includes newer trials;
- search dates differ.

Newer review is not automatically better if:
- methods worse;
- PICO broader;
- bias ignored.

Compare methods + included studies.

## 4. PICO conflict

Common fitness case:
“failure training studies conflict.”

But trials may differ in:
- load;
- volume matching;
- exercise;
- definition of failure;
- training status.

Conflict can reflect real effect modification rather than random inconsistency.

## 5. Measurement conflict

Hypertrophy measured by:
- MRI;
- ultrasound;
- DXA.

Different tools can produce different effects because they estimate different constructs/errors.

Do not pool conflict away.

## 6. Bias-profile comparison

For each conflicting result:
1. randomization?
2. concealment?
3. missing data?
4. measurement bias?
5. selective reporting?
6. confounding?

A precise biased study does not necessarily outweigh a smaller low-bias study.

## 7. Precision

Study A:
large effect, CI −2 to +15.

Study B:
small effect, CI +1 to +3.

These are not equally informative.

Inspect compatibility ranges, not point estimates alone.

## 8. Directness

Direct longitudinal outcome generally outranks indirect proxy for target chronic decision.

Example:
acute lactate “supports” method A;
longitudinal hypertrophy finds no advantage.

This is not 1–1 conflicting evidence; chronic evidence is more direct.

## 9. Replication

Independent replication matters most when:
- labs/authors differ;
- methods differ but target same effect;
- biases are unlikely to be shared.

Repeated same-lab findings can still be valuable but less independent.

## 10. Triangulation

Evidence triangulation strengthens causal inference when:
1. approaches address same causal question;
2. key biases differ and are largely unrelated;
3. timing/exposure definitions align;
4. expected bias directions are considered.

Convergence across independent bias structures is stronger than mere repetition of same design. citeturn450474search1turn450474search9

## 11. Triangulation is not pooling

Different designs may be:
- RCT;
- cohort;
- mechanistic;
- natural experiment.

They often should not be statistically pooled.

Triangulation is frequently qualitative causal integration.

## 12. Conflicting triangulation

If methods with different biases disagree:
1. map expected bias direction;
2. identify which assumptions differ;
3. identify missing evidence needed.

Disagreement can be scientifically useful.

## 13. Mechanism vs outcome conflict

If mechanism predicts benefit but longitudinal outcome does not:
- mechanism may be incomplete;
- acute marker not valid surrogate;
- competing pathways exist;
- chronic study may be underpowered.

Do not let attractive mechanism override adequate direct outcomes.

## 14. Large old study vs small new study

New publication date is irrelevant by itself.

Compare:
- method;
- applicability;
- outcome;
- precision;
- bias.

## 15. Guideline vs new primary study

One new RCT rarely invalidates a guideline automatically.

Check:
1. was it within guideline uncertainty?
2. magnitude?
3. risk of bias?
4. does it change pooled body?
5. guideline search date?

Update decision based on cumulative evidence.

## 16. Meta-analysis conflicts

Two meta-analyses may differ due:
1. eligibility;
2. effect extraction;
3. study dependency;
4. fixed/random model;
5. dose classification;
6. subgroup choices;
7. search date.

Need compare primary study matrix.

## 17. Outcome priority

Evidence may conflict only because outcomes differ.

Method A:
- more hypertrophy;
- worse adherence.

There is no single scientific contradiction.
There is a trade-off.

Decision handled in RES-28.

## 18. Conflict-resolution workflow

1. State exact target question.
2. List evidence blocks.
3. Verify same PICO/estimand/time.
4. Separate direct vs indirect.
5. Compare RoB.
6. Compare effect estimates/CIs.
7. Compare measurement quality.
8. Compare applicability.
9. Inspect heterogeneity.
10. Check missing evidence.
11. Identify independent replication/triangulation.
12. Explain plausible reasons for discrepancy.
13. Re-grade certainty.
14. If unresolved, preserve uncertainty.

## 19. Project conflict labels

### C0 — apparent only
Different questions/outcomes; no true conflict.

### C1 — explainable
Differences plausibly explained by PICO/method.

### C2 — unresolved moderate
Comparable evidence differs; one source somewhat stronger.

### C3 — unresolved major
High-quality direct evidence genuinely disagrees.

These are project-specific.

## 20. What not to do

1. Vote-count.
2. Pick favorite author.
3. Pick newest automatically.
4. Pick largest effect.
5. Pick most “mechanistically plausible”.
6. Ignore null studies.
7. average incompatible outcomes.
8. hide uncertainty to produce clean recommendation.

## 21. Confidence

### HIGH
- apparent conflicts often reflect different PICO/measurement/time;
- vote counting is poor synthesis;
- evidence quality/directness/precision matter;
- triangulation is stronger when bias structures differ;
- unresolved high-quality disagreement should reduce certainty.

### PRACTICAL_HEURISTIC
- C0–C3 labels and conflict workflow.
