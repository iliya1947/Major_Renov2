## 3. Core closure decision

BIO-11 retains the following architecture:

`task demand > momentary sustainable capacity`

The metabolic contribution to sustainable capacity is shaped by interacting:

- PCr / adenine-nucleotide state;
- Pi;
- glycogen/glucose availability;
- H+/pH;
- Na+/K+/Cl−/Ca2+ regulation;
- E–C coupling / Ca2+ handling;
- O2 delivery/distribution/extraction;
- mitochondrial oxidative capacity/control;
- thermal and hydration state.

Canonical closure rule:

**metabolic fatigue is a task- and state-specific interaction among energetic, ionic, contractile, oxygen-transport and environmental constraints; no single metabolite, threshold or fixed hierarchy explains all fatigue.**

No blocker.

## 4. Limiter vs correlate vs consequence audit

PASS.

A variable may:

1. causally constrain the task;
2. correlate with task strain;
3. change as a consequence without being the decisive limiter.

Examples:

- blood lactate is not retained as a direct fatigue toxin;
- plasma K+ does not numerically identify muscle interstitial K+;
- PCr depletion does not by itself prove why task failure occurred;
- core temperature/body-mass loss does not identify a universal molecular failure mechanism.

Canonical rule:

**mechanism inference requires evidence at the relevant compartment and task, not merely a changing marker.**

No blocker.

## 5. PCr depletion audit

PASS.

PMID **40189235** confirms substantial methodological/biological heterogeneity in human 31P-MRS PCr recovery kinetics across 128 studies.

PMID **8964751** supports incomplete PCr restoration and altered contribution before a repeated severe sprint.

Retain:

- PCr can deplete rapidly;
- lower PCr reduces immediate high-rate phosphate-buffering reserve;
- short recovery can leave the next bout with incomplete restoration;
- this can matter for repeated high-power work.

Reject:

- PCr depletion = ATP depletion;
- PCr depletion = universal cause of task failure;
- one PCr percentage = fatigue threshold;
- PCr normalization = complete recovery.

Canonical rule:

**PCr state is an important high-power/repeated-bout constraint, not a complete fatigue diagnosis.**

No blocker.

---

## 6. PCr vs Pi coupling audit

PASS AFTER TIGHTENING.

PCr decline and Pi rise are coupled through high-energy-phosphate turnover but are non-equivalent state variables.

Do not infer:

- one-to-one PCr→Pi mapping;
- low PCr explains all Pi-related contractile effects;
- Pi can be estimated from PCr alone across states.

Canonical rule:

**PCr depletion describes loss of phosphagen reserve; Pi describes a separate fatigue-relevant metabolite state. Their relationship is task/metabolism dependent.**

No blocker.

## 7. Low-glycogen performance audit

PASS WITH STRONG GENERALIZATION BOUNDARY.

PMID **40895431**:

- n=10 healthy young men;
- within-person low-glycogen vs glycogen-loaded legs;
- intense one-legged knee-extension lasting ~5 min;
- low glycogen reduced time to task failure by ~40%;
- resting MVC, twitch force and RFD were not lower in the low-glycogen condition;
- MVC reductions at failure were similar.

This directly supports:

**low glycogen can reduce capacity in this high-intensity task without behaving like a simple resting contractile-force deficit.**

It does **not** authorize:

- a universal glycogen threshold;
- the same effect magnitude in women, other muscles, resistance sets or endurance tasks;
- “glycogen depletion causes all fatigue.”

Confidence for general trainer rule remains **MODERATE-HIGH**, with protocol/population limits explicit.

No blocker.

## 8. Glycogen–SR Ca2+ audit

PASS AFTER CAUSAL WORDING NARROWING.

PMID **24091991** in 14 highly trained male triathletes showed:

- reduced glycogen after prolonged glycogen-depleting cycling;
- reduced SR Ca2+ release rate;
- carbohydrate-supported glycogen recovery accompanied restoration of SR Ca2+ release;
- association between glycogen and SR Ca2+ release.

The intervention supports a plausible glycogen-linked E–C mechanism but does not isolate glycogen as the only mediator of recovery.

Canonical wording:

**glycogen state can be linked to SR Ca2+ handling and may contribute to E–C fatigue/recovery in some contexts; one universal glycogen→Ca2+ causal pathway is not retained.**

No blocker.

## 10. Inorganic phosphate audit

PASS.

Current evidence supports Pi as fatigue relevant through:

- cross-bridge effects;
- myofilament Ca2+ sensitivity;
- Ca2+-related processes;
- interaction with acid-base state.

PMID **40961462** in 16 young adults found intramuscular Pi more consistently related to fatigue than H+ in the studied intermittent maximal knee-extension model.

PMID **41348575** showed fatigue-like Pi + low pH markedly depressed Ca2+ sensitivity in isolated human fibres.

PMID **40686424** found a more consistent Pi-force relationship than H+ in rat in-vivo muscle.

Canonical rule:

**Pi is a major fatigue-relevant metabolite, but evidence from a specific human task, isolated fibres or animal models cannot establish one universal human Pi failure threshold or mechanism share.**

No blocker.

## 11. Pi ranking audit

PASS AFTER REMOVING UNIVERSAL RANKING.

Do not canonize:

`Pi > H+ > K+ > glycogen`

or any equivalent global hierarchy.

Retain only:

- in PMID **40961462**, Pi was more consistently related to fatigue than H+ in that human model;
- in PMID **40686424**, Pi-force association was more consistent than H+ in the studied rat protocols;
- severe acidosis remains independently fatigue-relevant;
- ions, Ca2+ handling, glycogen and oxygen constraints can dominate in other tasks/states.

Canonical rule:

**mechanism ranking is task/model specific; BIO-11 contains no universal fatigue ranking.**

No blocker.

## 12. Ionic disturbances audit

PASS.

PMIDs **34190344**, **37584745** and **38206444** support an interacting model involving:

- K+;
- Na+;
- Cl−;
- Ca2+;
- Na+/K+-ATPase;
- ion channels and membrane excitability;
- metabolic state.

PMID **37584745** explicitly supports context-dependent K+-related potentiation and force depression rather than a simple “K+ rise = fatigue” rule.

Canonical rule:

**ionic fatigue is a multi-ion membrane/excitability/homeostasis problem, not a one-ion switch.**

No blocker.

## 13. Digoxin / K+ audit

PASS WITH COMPARTMENT CAUTION.

PMID **39580613**:

- randomized crossover double-blind study;
- 10 healthy adults;
- acute digoxin shortened time to fatigue by ~7.7%;
- arterial plasma K+ rise was greater;
- measured skeletal-muscle NKA content/isoforms were preserved.

Retain:

**pharmacological perturbation of Na+/K+ handling can worsen intense-exercise performance and systemic K+ disturbance.**

Do not infer:

- plasma K+ = muscle interstitial K+;
- NKA content = NKA activity at every moment;
- K+ alone caused the entire performance change.

No blocker.

---

## 14. H+ / pH audit

PASS.

PMID **40088272** supports:

- lactate per se has little direct detrimental effect at physiologically relevant high concentrations;
- acidosis is not the sole cause of fatigue;
- moderate acidosis at physiological temperature can have limited direct force/power effect;
- severe intracellular acidosis can reduce maximal force, shortening velocity and power;
- H+ effects interact with Pi, ions and sensory pathways.

Canonical rule:

**severe intracellular acidosis can contribute materially to fatigue, but pH/H+ is neither necessary nor sufficient to explain all task failure.**

No blocker.

## 15. Severe vs moderate acidosis audit

PASS AFTER EXPLICIT SEVERITY BOUNDARY.

BIO-11 must not translate “acidosis can matter” into “every pH fall is performance limiting.”

Retain:

- severity matters;
- fibre type and compartment matter;
- physiological temperature matters;
- coexisting Pi/ion state matters.

No universal pHi threshold is retained.

No blocker.

## 16. Pi + H+ interaction audit

PASS WITH MODEL BOUNDARY.

PMID **41348575**:

- isolated human fibres;
- young and older adults;
- fatigue-like pH 6.2 + Pi 30 mM;
- marked depression of Ca2+ sensitivity.

PMID **42306919**:

- human fibres from older adults at 37°C;
- fibre-type-specific mechanical/kinetic responses to Pi/acidosis.

These support mechanistic interaction.

They do not provide a direct whole-body exercise percentage for Pi vs H+.

Canonical rule:

**Pi and H+ interact at contractile/Ca2+-sensitivity levels; quantitative whole-task attribution remains context dependent.**

No blocker.

## 18. Central/peripheral boundary audit

PASS.

A metabolic/E–C limitation can reduce force without primary central-drive failure.

But:

- central fatigue can coexist;
- peripheral evidence does not prove central contribution = 0;
- BIO-11 does not partition central/peripheral percentages.

Canonical rule:

**metabolic limitation is one component of neuromuscular fatigue, not a replacement taxonomy for central/peripheral fatigue.**

## 19. Oxygen-delivery audit

PASS WITH TASK/POPULATION BOUNDARY.

PMID **41845944** supports systemic O2 delivery as the primary VO2max limiter in elite endurance athletes while emphasizing peripheral extraction, capillarization and mitochondrial characteristics.

PMID **42028867** supports progressive reduction of VO2max with increasing altitude/hypoxic exposure.

Retain:

- O2 delivery can be a major or primary constraint in maximal aerobic contexts;
- local perfusion/diffusion and extraction remain distinct;
- elite VO2max evidence does not transfer as a universal explanation for resistance sets, short sprints or every endurance task.

Canonical rule:

**O2 limitation must specify the level of the cascade and the task/population.**

No blocker.

## 21. Mitochondrial limitation audit

PASS.

PMID **42730845** supports:

- better endurance performers often show greater mitochondrial content/function;
- mitochondrial characteristics correlate with several performance markers;
- training-induced changes do not always correlate one-to-one with performance;
- increasing O2 delivery can improve performance, showing that mitochondrial characteristics are not always the limiting step.

Canonical rule:

**mitochondrial capacity is an important peripheral determinant but neither necessary-and-sufficient proof of performance nor a universal sole limiter.**

No blocker.

## 22. Physiological limitation vs pathology audit

PASS.

In a healthy athlete:

- “mitochondrial limitation” can describe a relative rate constraint;
- it does not mean mitochondrial disease/dysfunction.

Likewise:

- “O2-delivery limitation” does not mean cardiovascular disease.

Medical diagnosis remains MED.

Canonical rule:

**physiological rate limitation ≠ pathology.**

No blocker.

## 23. Mitochondrial construct audit

- citrate synthase ≠ complete mitochondrial function;
- O2 consumption ≠ ATP production;
- mitochondrial characteristics ≠ VO2max.

**one mitochondrial marker cannot establish the limiter of a whole-body task.**

## 24. Heat audit

PASS.

PMID **41826976** across 23 studies / 414 healthy participants found impaired performance and greater physiological strain during heat, hypoxia and especially combined heat+hypoxia conditions.

PMID **40835807** found hot conditions increased carbohydrate oxidation/glycogen use during prolonged endurance exercise, with dehydration effects context dependent.

Canonical rule:

**heat changes cardiovascular, thermal, metabolic and perceptual strain; it is not reducible to dehydration alone.**

No blocker.

## 25. Dehydration / hypohydration audit

PASS WITH EVIDENCE-CERTAINTY BOUNDARY.

PMID **41572530** in 11 male soccer players showed hypohydration worsened several early RTD measures and other neuromuscular outcomes around intermittent exercise in 33°C heat.

PMID **43010950**:

- 24 studies retained;
- 225 study-reported participants;
- no clear time-trial/fixed-duration benefit from hydration vs no/minimal fluid in pooled estimate;
- time-to-exhaustion estimate favored hydration but prediction interval included no benefit;
- all critical/important estimates rated **very low certainty**;
- programmed/individualized drinking did not clearly outperform self-regulated drinking for performance.

Canonical rule:

**hypohydration can impair performance in some high-strain/heat contexts, but current evidence does not justify one universal performance effect or body-mass-loss threshold.**

No blocker.

## 26. Heat vs dehydration audit

PASS.

PMID **40835807** supports separate and interacting effects of heat and dehydration on carbohydrate use.

Retain:

- heat with maintained hydration is possible;
- hypohydration in temperate conditions is possible;
- combined heat+hypohydration can produce different strain.

Canonical rule:

**heat stress and hydration state must be measured and interpreted separately before being combined.**

No blocker.

## 27. Cooling intervention audit

PASS.

PMID **42987616**:

- 51 reports;
- 480 adult men after accounting for overlap;
- small average benefit for self-paced performance;
- longer time-to-exhaustion;
- small benefit for intermittent/repeated-sprint performance;
- heterogeneity and sparse subgroups limit protocol ranking.

This supports environmental heat as modifiable.

It does not prove:

- one core-temperature threshold;
- dehydration as the sole mechanism;
- one cooling method as universally best.

Canonical rule:

**successful cooling does not identify one molecular fatigue mechanism or guarantee individual benefit.**

No blocker.

## 29. Task-profile audit

PASS WITH PROFILE-NOT-LAW BOUNDARY.

### Brief maximal / near-maximal work
Likely contributors can include:

- rapid PCr use;
- Pi accumulation;
- ion/excitability disturbance;
- E–C/Ca2+ impairment.

Do not define this as a fixed mechanism sequence.

### Repeated high-power bouts
Likely contributors can shift toward:

- incomplete PCr restoration;
- evolving Pi/ion state;
- greater oxidative contribution;
- local glycogen use.

### High-repetition / high-density resistance work
Can show:

- high ATP turnover;
- PCr use;
- glycolytic contribution;
- Pi/H+/ion disturbance;
- E–C impairment;
- incomplete inter-set recovery.

### Prolonged endurance
Potential limiting families include:

- systemic O2 delivery;
- local distribution/extraction;
- mitochondrial capacity;
- carbohydrate availability;
- thermal/hydration stress;
- neuromuscular/perceptual factors.

Canonical rule:

**these are task profiles used to generate hypotheses, not deterministic laws.**

No blocker.

### Ions

### VO2

Content ≠ mass-specific function ≠ intrinsic function.

Core ≠ skin ≠ local muscle temperature.

Canonical rule:

**limiter inference cannot exceed the compartment and construct actually measured.**

**BIO-11 = CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0.**

Meaning:

1. metabolic fatigue is task specific and multi-mechanistic;
2. no one metabolite or fixed threshold explains all task failure;
3. PCr depletion can constrain repeated/high-power work but is not ATP depletion or a complete fatigue diagnosis;
4. glycogen availability can constrain some high-intensity/prolonged tasks without being the universal immediate cause of failure;
5. glycogen can interact with E–C/Ca2+ processes, but no universal single causal pathway is retained;
6. Pi is a major fatigue-relevant metabolite, but no universal human Pi threshold or global Pi-first hierarchy is retained;
7. severe intracellular acidosis can contribute to force/power loss, but H+/pH is not the universal sole cause;
8. ionic fatigue is a multi-ion excitability/homeostasis problem rather than a K+-only switch;
9. metabolic/ionic state can impair E–C/Ca2+ function, while detailed ownership remains PHY;
10. systemic O2 delivery, local extraction/distribution and mitochondrial utilization are distinct;
11. systemic O2 delivery can be primary for VO2max in elite endurance contexts without being universal across tasks/populations;
12. mitochondrial capacity is an important peripheral determinant, not a complete whole-body performance explanation or pathology label;
13. heat and hydration state are distinct interacting stressors;
14. current hydration-performance evidence does not justify one universal fluid/body-mass-loss threshold;
15. heat-illness diagnosis/treatment remains MED;
16. task profiles are hypothesis-generating, not deterministic laws;
17. the dominant limiter can change during the same task;
18. starting substrate, recovery, training and environment change the limiting architecture;
19. mechanism inference cannot exceed the compartment/construct actually measured;
20. BIO-11 contains no exact rest, fueling, hydration, cooling or readiness prescription.
