## 1. Evidence-to-Decision is the bridge

Evidence synthesis answers:
> what effects are likely and how certain are we?

Decision asks:
> given those effects and this person's context, what should we do?

These are separate stages.

GRADE EtD explicitly structures this bridge. Current 2026 framework considers:
- problem;
- desirable effects;
- undesirable effects;
- certainty;
- values;
- balance;
- resources;
- equity;
- acceptability;
- feasibility. citeturn469391search1turn469391search13

For one-person fitness, some criteria are adapted but not discarded silently.

## 2. Target person and perspective

Decision perspective:
**Ilya as an individual home trainee.**

Therefore relevant resources:
- money;
- time;
- equipment;
- attention;
- setup friction;
- recovery capacity.

Equity/public-system criteria are less central to a one-person decision, but affordability/access remain relevant.

## 3. Desirable effects

Potential outcomes:
1. hypertrophy;
2. strength;
3. health;
4. skill;
5. enjoyment;
6. adherence;
7. time efficiency.

Need magnitude + certainty.

Not:
“method works”.

## 4. Undesirable effects

Potential:
1. injury/medical risk;
2. pain;
3. excessive fatigue;
4. DOMS;
5. sleep disruption;
6. time burden;
7. boredom/aversion;
8. cost;
9. cognitive complexity;
10. opportunity cost.

## 5. Certainty

A large point estimate with very-low certainty may not justify complexity.

A small high-certainty advantage may also not justify complexity.

EtD combines:
- magnitude;
- certainty;
- consequences.

## 6. Values and preferences

For an individual, values can be directly elicited.

Known project-level preferences:
- practical utility;
- free solutions preferred;
- low unnecessary complexity;
- program must survive ADHD-related execution barriers.

These shape choice among otherwise valid options.

They do not change biological evidence; they change decision weights.

## 7. Feasibility

Ask:
1. equipment available?
2. space?
3. technique feasible?
4. session duration?
5. progression possible?
6. tracking burden?
7. medical constraints?

An efficacious intervention that cannot be implemented is not the correct practical choice.

## 8. Acceptability

Would person actually do it repeatedly?

Includes:
- enjoyment;
- discomfort;
- monotony;
- embarrassment/privacy;
- complexity.

Adherence is part of effectiveness.

## 9. Resources / cost

Cost is broader than money:
1. time;
2. equipment;
3. subscription;
4. travel;
5. setup;
6. cognitive effort.

User preference means free option has strong default advantage when expected outcomes similar.

## 10. Balance of effects

Decision is not:
benefits minus harms as one fake score.

Need explicit judgment:
- benefits clearly outweigh burden?
- close trade-off?
- harms dominate?
- uncertainty too high?

## 11. Reversibility

Project-specific high-value criterion.

### Highly reversible
- exercise swap;
- rest interval change;
- reminder method.

Can trial with lower certainty.

### Less reversible/high-cost
- expensive equipment;
- aggressive diet;
- risky supplement;
- medical decision.

Requires stronger evidence.

## 12. Complexity penalty

Every added component costs:
- decisions;
- tracking;
- attention;
- maintenance.

Project rule:
**complexity must earn its place through meaningful expected benefit.**

This is especially relevant to ADHD adherence.

## 13. Robustness / sensitivity of decision

Ask:
Would decision change if:
- effect at lower CI bound?
- adherence 20% lower?
- sleep worse?
- equipment unchanged?

If decision only works under optimistic assumptions, classify fragile.

## 14. Monitoring and reversibility plan

Every uncertain personal recommendation should include:
1. expected outcome;
2. measurement;
3. evaluation window;
4. stop criterion;
5. change criterion.

This converts uncertainty into managed experiment.

## 15. Multiple valid options

If evidence shows similar effects:
do not invent “best”.

Choose by:
- preference;
- feasibility;
- cost;
- adherence;
- safety.

Conditional recommendation is often more scientifically honest.

GRADE’s current recommendation framework explicitly distinguishes strong vs conditional recommendations. citeturn469391search0

## 17. Decision strength classes

Project-specific:

### D1 — Strong default
Evidence/directness and balance clearly favor option.

### D2 — Conditional preference
Several options reasonable; personal context decides.

### D3 — Experimental
Evidence uncertain but option safe/reversible; test and monitor.

### D4 — Do not use
Risk/burden or evidence balance unfavorable.

Not official GRADE labels.

## 18. Avoiding false optimization

If options differ trivially:
select simplest.

Do not spend research/behavioral resources optimizing 1% theoretical difference while adherence is unstable.

This is not anti-science; it is opportunity-cost aware decision-making.

## 19. Example — extra volume

Evidence:
more sets may increase hypertrophy with diminishing returns.

Decision:
add sets only if:
- marginal expected growth matters;
- recovery okay;
- session still tolerable;
- adherence unaffected.

Population curve does not automatically dictate maximal dose.

## 20. Example — equipment purchase

If 6 kg becomes clear strength ceiling:
- free mechanical progressions exhausted;
- target requires higher absolute force;
then equipment purchase may become decision-relevant.

Until then free solution preferred.

## 21. Example — supplement

Creatine may have evidence.
But recommendation still checks:
- expected magnitude;
- safety;
- cost;
- preference;
- whether fundamentals handled.

Evidence of effect ≠ automatic need.

## 22. Documentation

Every substantive personal decision later goes to:
- DECISIONS.md;
- or personal decision log.

Include:
1. evidence version/date;
2. rationale;
3. uncertainty;
4. test plan;
5. outcome.

## 23. Confidence

### HIGH
- evidence-to-decision requires more than certainty/effect estimate;
- current GRADE EtD explicitly considers benefits/harms, certainty, values, resources, acceptability and feasibility;
- recommendation strength may be conditional even with good evidence when trade-offs/preferences vary.

### PRACTICAL_HEURISTIC
- reversibility, complexity penalty, ADHD friction and D1–D4 classes are project-specific extensions.
