## 1. Purpose / Question Card

### Decision

Build a bounded mechanistic model of human resistance-training hypertrophy that is useful for later prescription without committing the central methodological error:

`acute molecular response → presumed superior chronic hypertrophy`

The model must separate:

1. protein accretion from protein-turnover snapshots;
2. acute MPS from chronic hypertrophy;
3. protein synthesis from protein breakdown;
4. mechanotransduction from any single molecular sensor;
5. mTORC1 necessity for acute translation from chronic prescription validity;
6. translational efficiency from translational capacity/ribosome abundance;
7. satellite-cell/myonuclear contribution from a universal “myonuclear threshold”;
8. ECM remodeling from myofiber protein accretion;
9. nutrient sufficiency from supplement-specific claims;
10. energy availability from “bulk required” dogma;
11. mechanical tension from external load percentage;
12. long-muscle-length effects from a universal sarcomerogenesis claim;
13. volume dose-response from one fixed optimal set number;
14. load/repetition range from “heavy = hypertrophy”;
15. proximity to failure from “failure required”;
16. frequency from total weekly volume;
17. tempo from time-under-tension mythology;

## 3. Integrated hypertrophy model

The most defensible project model is:

`repeated high-enough mechanical loading → mechanotransduction → increased translational efficiency + translational capacity → repeated periods of positive net protein accretion → retained myofiber growth`

This process is modified by:

- amino acid/protein availability;
- energy availability;
- training volume;
- effort/proximity to failure;
- muscle length/ROM;
- fiber/muscle/region;
- satellite-cell/myonuclear biology;
- ECM remodeling;
- individual biological responsiveness.

No single pathway, acute biomarker, or prescription variable completely explains hypertrophy.

---

## 4. Net muscle protein balance

Muscle size is ultimately constrained by cumulative protein accretion.

Conceptually:

`net muscle protein balance = muscle protein synthesis − muscle protein breakdown`

For hypertrophy over time:

`integrated synthesis > integrated degradation`

must occur sufficiently often to retain more muscle protein.

Important boundary:

**net balance is a time-integrated accounting concept, not a single post-workout blood/biopsy value.**

Protein turnover also serves remodeling/quality control; breakdown is not simply “bad.”

---

## 5. Muscle protein synthesis

A resistance-exercise bout robustly raises MPS.

The 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis found a pooled postabsorptive MPS increase and showed that the response can remain elevated up to ~48 h, with substantial heterogeneity by age and exercise parameters.

However:

- acute MPS can include repair/remodeling;
- early training MPS is especially confounded by muscle damage;
- one acute MPS measurement is not a validated hypertrophy surrogate.

Damas et al. showed integrated MyoPS related to hypertrophy after damage had attenuated, but not during the earliest high-damage phase.

Project rule:

**repeated productive MPS is part of hypertrophy biology; acute MPS magnitude alone is not a program ranking tool.**

---

## 6. Muscle protein breakdown

Resistance exercise also modifies proteolytic pathways.

Relevant systems include:

- ubiquitin–proteasome;
- autophagy–lysosome;
- calpain/remodeling processes.

Protein breakdown contributes to:

- removal of damaged proteins;
- remodeling;
- protein-quality control;
- turnover of structural components.

Current human evidence for acute MPB is less straightforward and less commonly quantified than MPS.

Project rule:

**hypertrophy is not achieved by “turning breakdown off”; productive remodeling requires regulated turnover.**

No practical RT prescription is derived from maximizing or minimizing one acute MPB marker.

---

## 7. Mechanotransduction

Mechanical loading is converted into biochemical/transcriptional responses through a network rather than one single sensor.

Candidate/established components include:

- integrin/focal-adhesion signaling;
- cytoskeletal/ECM force transmission;
- phosphatidic-acid and related lipid signaling;
- titin/costamere-associated sensing;
- YAP/TAZ-related mechanosensitive transcription;
- MAPK pathways;
- mTORC1-linked signaling.

A 2025 human phosphoproteomic study identified a resistance-exercise-associated MKK3b/6 → p38 → MK2 → mTORC1 pathway and found MKK3b activation strongly correlated with protein-synthesis induction.

Project rule:

**mechanotransduction is distributed and redundant; no one phosphorylation event is a complete “hypertrophy switch.”**

---

## 8. mTORC1

mTORC1 is a central regulator of translational initiation/efficiency.

Human rapamycin studies provide perturbation evidence:

- blocking mTORC1 suppresses the acute contraction-induced rise in MPS;
- similar inhibition occurs after low-load BFR exercise.

This supports **condition-specific necessity for the acute MPS response**.

But:

- acute phosphorylation magnitude varies;
- mTORC1 is not the only pathway;
- ribosome biogenesis includes rapamycin-sensitive and -insensitive components;
- acute signaling amplitude is not a validated predictor of chronic hypertrophy.

Project rule:

**mTORC1 matters mechanistically; do not prescribe training by chasing p70S6K/mTOR phosphorylation.**

---

## 9. Ribosomal biogenesis

Long-term growth requires not only greater translation per ribosome but enough translational machinery.

Human RT data show increases in:

- total RNA;
- ribosomal RNA;
- ribosome-related proteins/biogenesis markers

during early training.

A human early-phase study found ribosomal abundance/biogenesis rose rapidly over the first sessions and plateaued later while muscle thickness increased.

Current reviews treat ribosome biogenesis as a major candidate determinant of sustained translational capacity.

Boundary:

**“ribosome bottleneck” is a useful model, not a validated training variable that can currently be prescribed directly.**

---

## 10. Satellite cells

Satellite cells are muscle stem/progenitor cells capable of proliferating and fusing with existing fibers.

Human training studies show:

- satellite-cell responses can accompany hypertrophy;
- larger hypertrophic responders often show larger satellite-cell expansion;
- some hypertrophy can occur without clear expansion of the satellite-cell pool.

Therefore:

**satellite-cell participation is biologically relevant, but a universal requirement for every measurable amount of adult human hypertrophy is not established.**

They may become increasingly important as fiber growth/myonuclear demand rises.

---

## 11. Myonuclear accretion

Myonuclei support transcriptional capacity in large multinucleated fibers.

Human evidence supports:

- myonuclear addition during substantial RT-induced hypertrophy;
- association between greater myonuclear accretion and larger fiber growth;
- no fixed hypertrophy threshold at which nuclei must suddenly be added.

The meta-analysis found myonuclear content can increase even with <10% fiber hypertrophy, with more consistent addition at larger growth magnitudes.

Human detraining work also supports persistence of previously acquired myonuclei, though the practical retraining advantage remains uncertain.

Project rule:

**myonuclear accretion supports growth capacity but should not be reduced to a rigid “myonuclear domain ceiling.”**

---

## 12. ECM remodeling

Hypertrophy occurs inside a force-transmitting extracellular matrix.

ECM remodeling can involve:

- collagen turnover;
- matrix metalloproteinases/TIMPs;
- adhesion proteins;
- force-transmission architecture;
- satellite-cell niche regulation.

PHY-06 established that ECM is training-responsive.

PHY-07 boundary:

**ECM remodeling is part of the hypertrophic environment, but greater collagen turnover is not itself proof of greater myofiber hypertrophy.**

Detailed tendon/connective tissue adaptation remains PHY-12.

---

## 13. Amino acids / protein

Essential amino acids provide substrate and signaling support for postexercise MPS.

Current acute evidence shows protein ingestion after RT can augment MPS, although source comparisons have uncertainty and acute MPS differences do not automatically equal chronic hypertrophy differences.

Chronic RT trials/meta-analyses support an additional lean-mass benefit from protein supplementation in some populations, especially when baseline intake is not already sufficient.

Project rule:

**adequate protein/amino-acid availability supports hypertrophy; exact daily dose, distribution, source and energy-deficit targets belong to NUT.**

Do not turn acute MPS ranking of protein sources into a hypertrophy ranking.

---

## 14. Energy availability

Muscle accretion is energetically costly.

Energy deficit does not make hypertrophy biologically impossible in every circumstance, especially in:

- novices;
- higher-fat individuals;
- detrained persons;
- high-protein well-designed programs.

But sustained low energy availability / energy deficit can impair:

- lean-mass gain;
- anabolic response;
- training adaptation.

Meta-analytic evidence shows energy deficit impairs RT-induced lean-mass gains more consistently than strength gains.

Project rule:

**energy sufficiency facilitates hypertrophy; “caloric surplus is always mandatory” is too absolute, while chronic low energy availability is a real constraint.**

Exact calorie targets belong to NUT/IND.

---

## 15. Mechanical tension

Mechanical loading is the central external stimulus in the current hypertrophy model.

But “mechanical tension” should not be equated with:

- external kg alone;
- %1RM alone;
- perceived stretch/burn;
- time under tension alone;
- maximal EMG.

Relevant internal stimulus depends on:

- active fiber force;
- recruited fibers;
- muscle length;
- contraction history;
- fatigue state;
- architecture and moment arms.

Project rule:

**mechanical tension is an internal tissue-level loading concept, not one directly observable gym variable.**

Detailed force distribution belongs to MEC/NEU.

---

## 16. Training at longer muscle lengths

Training with meaningful loading at longer muscle lengths is a credible hypertrophy modifier.

Current evidence is nuanced:

1. long-length partials generally outperform short-length partials in the 2026 partial-ROM meta-analysis;
2. lengthened partials can produce similar hypertrophy to full ROM in trained adults;
3. broader longer-vs-shorter mean-muscle-length regional meta-analysis found average between-condition regional differences were mostly trivial;
4. evidence for true longitudinal sarcomere addition in humans remains indirect because fascicle length is a proxy and often estimated by ultrasound extrapolation.

Project rule:

**load at long muscle lengths is promising and often useful, but “stretch = guaranteed extra sarcomeres/hypertrophy” is not established.**

---

## 17. Volume

Weekly set volume has one of the clearest modifiable dose-response relationships with hypertrophy.

The 2026 volume/frequency meta-regression found:

- 100% posterior probability that the volume slope for hypertrophy exceeded zero;
- diminishing returns as volume rises.

The ACSM 2026 overview found higher-volume prescriptions (approximately ≥10 weekly sets in the synthesized review literature) enhanced hypertrophy.

Boundary:

**this does not establish one universal optimal or maximal weekly set number.**

Individual recoverability, exercise overlap, direct vs indirect sets, training status and effort matter.

---

## 18. Load / intensity

External load is not a unique hypertrophy determinant.

Network meta-analysis shows many load prescriptions produce hypertrophy.

Recent within-subject human data also found hypertrophy relatively conserved across different loads when sets were performed to high effort.

Higher loads have stronger specificity for maximal strength, but not a unique hypertrophy advantage.

Project rule:

**hypertrophy can occur across a broad loading spectrum when active fibers receive sufficient repeated stimulus; load selection also changes fatigue, repetitions, joint stress and strength specificity.**

Very light loading may require closer proximity to failure to recruit/stimulate enough fibers.

---

## 19. Proximity to failure

Current evidence requires two statements simultaneously.

### Supported
The 2024 continuous meta-regression found hypertrophy tended to increase as sets were terminated closer to failure.

### Also supported
The ACSM 2026 overview found training to **momentary muscle fatigue/failure itself** did not consistently improve outcomes.

Therefore:

**closer proximity to failure likely matters, but literal failure is not required.**

The exact optimal RIR is unresolved because:

- RIR was often estimated retrospectively in studies;
- effort/load/volume interact;
- fatigue cost rises near failure.

Project rule:

**do not use “failure required” or “failure irrelevant.”**

---

## 20. Repetition range

Repetition range is largely a consequence of load and set endpoint.

Current chronic evidence indicates hypertrophy can occur across low-to-high repetition ranges when sets provide sufficient effort/stimulus.

Therefore:

- 5 reps is not inherently “strength only”;
- 10–12 is not a unique hypertrophy zone;
- 20+ is not automatically “endurance only.”

Practical differences include:

- cardiovascular/metabolic discomfort;
- set duration;
- technique degradation;
- joint/loading constraints;
- ability to estimate proximity to failure.

Project rule:

**there is no uniquely anabolic repetition range.**

---

## 21. Frequency

Training frequency primarily distributes weekly stimulus.

The 2026 volume/frequency meta-regression found the frequency–hypertrophy slope compatible with negligible effects, while weekly volume had a clear positive relationship.

Earlier volume-equated meta-analysis similarly found little independent hypertrophy effect of frequency.

Project rule:

**frequency is mainly a tool to distribute tolerable weekly volume/quality, not a primary hypertrophy mechanism by itself.**

Do not infer that frequency never matters practically.

---

## 22. Tempo

The 2025 tempo systematic review/meta-analysis found broadly similar hypertrophy across faster and slower repetition tempos, with pairwise effects trivial to small overall.

The ACSM 2026 overview likewise found time under tension did not consistently influence outcomes.

Project rule:

**deliberately slow tempo is not inherently more hypertrophic.**

Tempo remains relevant to:

- control;
- technique;
- exercise constraints;
- fatigue;
- contraction-mode exposure.

Extreme tempo manipulation can alter load/repetition capacity and therefore total stimulus.

---

## 23. Full vs partial ROM

The historical evidence favored full ROM over short partial ROM for several lower-body hypertrophy outcomes.

Newer evidence refines this:

- long-length partials can perform similarly to full ROM in trained participants;
- a 2025 multi-site trial found small between-condition effects for lengthened partial vs full ROM;
- short-length partials are generally less favorable than long-length/full-ROM approaches in the current literature.

Project rule:

**ROM should be interpreted by what muscle lengths are loaded, not only by “full” versus “partial.”**

Full ROM remains a robust default; lengthened partials can be a viable hypertrophy tool.

Exercise-specific ROM selection remains MEC/TEC/PRO.

---

## 24. Regional hypertrophy

Hypertrophy is regionally nonuniform.

Different exercises/ROMs can produce different growth along a muscle or between heads.

But current 2025 regional meta-analysis found only trivial average differences between longer- and shorter-mean-muscle-length conditions at proximal/mid/distal sites, despite a trend favoring distal growth.

The 2026 long-vs-short partial-ROM meta-analysis found an overall advantage for long-length partials, including distal/central regions.

These are not necessarily contradictory because they use different comparison definitions and study sets.

Project rule:

**regional adaptation is real; large predictable “targeting” effects require direct evidence for the specific muscle/exercise.**

---

## 25. Individual variability

Hypertrophic response varies substantially between people.

Sources of observed variation include:

- true biological responsiveness;
- baseline muscle phenotype;
- ribosome/satellite-cell biology;
- nutrition/energy availability;
- training adherence/effort;
- program fit;
- measurement error.

Recent within-subject data suggest individual hypertrophic tendency can be more conserved within the same person than between people, even across different limbs/loads.

Another 2025 within-subject trial found some participants responded differently to load- vs repetition-progression strategies.

Project rule:

**individual variability is real, but one short block cannot establish a stable “nonresponder” or unique optimal protocol.**

Repeated standardized measurements are required before individualization claims.

---

## 26. Hyperplasia

Human hypertrophy should not be explained by fiber-number gain unless strong direct evidence exists.

The 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis included 11 studies and found no significant estimated increase in muscle fiber number following RT interventions up to ~6 months.

Limitations:

- human fiber number is difficult to measure directly;
- included estimates are indirect;
- longer durations remain less studied.

Project rule:

**meaningful human RT-induced hyperplasia is currently unproven and unlikely to be practically important for ordinary program design.**

Hypertrophy should be modeled primarily as enlargement/remodeling of existing fibers.

## 27. Mechanism → prescription hierarchy

### High practical value

1. progressive repeated mechanical loading;
2. sufficient weekly volume with diminishing returns;
3. sufficiently high effort / reasonably close to failure;
4. broad usable load/repetition ranges;
5. adequate protein/amino acids;
6. adequate energy availability;
7. meaningful loading through useful muscle lengths/ROM;
8. sufficient recovery to repeat quality stimulus.

### Important biological mechanisms, weak direct prescription variables

1. acute mTORC1 phosphorylation;
2. acute MPS peak;
3. ribosome-biogenesis markers;
4. satellite-cell count;
5. myonuclear count;
6. ECM biomarkers;
7. individual cytokine/metabolite responses.

Project rule:

**mechanistic importance does not imply that measuring/maximizing the marker improves prescription.**

## 29. Established project-level model

Hypertrophy is best represented as:

`repeated sufficiently challenging mechanical loading`
`+`
`adequate weekly exposure`
`+`
`nutrient/energy support`
`→ mechanotransduction / mTORC1-linked translation / ribosome capacity / cellular support`
`→ repeated positive net protein accretion`
`→ retained myofiber growth`

with modifiers:

- muscle length/ROM;
- regional loading;
- satellite-cell/myonuclear biology;
- ECM;
- baseline phenotype;
- individual responsiveness.

This model explicitly rejects:

- pump/metabolic stress/hormone spike as primary growth drivers;
- one acute signaling marker as prescription target;
- one fixed rep range;
- mandatory failure;
- one universal weekly set optimum;
- hyperplasia as ordinary human growth mechanism.

## 30. Training-relevant implications

1. **Mechanical loading is central, but external load alone does not define fiber stimulus.**
2. **MPS is necessary to accrete protein, but one acute MPS peak does not rank programs.**
3. **mTORC1 matters mechanistically, but phosphorylation is not a hypertrophy score.**
4. **Ribosome biogenesis supports sustained translational capacity.**
5. **Satellite cells/myonuclei matter more as growth demands increase, but no rigid human threshold is established.**
6. **Adequate protein and energy support growth; exact nutrition prescription belongs to NUT.**
7. **Weekly volume has a positive dose-response with diminishing returns.**
8. **Hypertrophy can occur across a broad load/rep spectrum.**
9. **Closer-to-failure work is generally more hypertrophic than stopping very far away, but momentary failure is not required.**
10. **Frequency mainly distributes weekly volume.**
11. **Slow tempo is not inherently superior.**
12. **Loading long muscle lengths is useful; lengthened partials can be comparable to full ROM.**
13. **Regional growth exists but cannot be inferred solely from “feeling” or anatomy.**
14. **Individual response requires repeated measurement, not one training block.**
15. **Human hyperplasia is not a practical programming mechanism.**

## 32. Confidence / unresolved issues

### HIGH

- repeated mechanical loading drives hypertrophic adaptation;
- RT increases MPS;
- cumulative positive protein accretion underlies retained protein growth;
- mTORC1 participates critically in acute exercise-induced MPS;
- hypertrophy can occur across broad load/repetition ranges;
- higher weekly volume generally increases hypertrophy with diminishing returns;
- momentary failure is not required;
- frequency has limited independent hypertrophy effect when volume is considered;
- tempo has minimal average hypertrophy effect;
- regional hypertrophy exists;
- human RT-induced hyperplasia is not currently established.

### MODERATE-HIGH

- ribosome biogenesis is important for sustained growth capacity;
- satellite cells/myonuclear accretion support substantial hypertrophy;
- long-length loading is often favorable vs short-length partial training;
- lengthened partials can approximate full-ROM hypertrophy in some contexts;
- energy deficit/LEA constrains lean-mass adaptation.

### MODERATE / CONTEXT-DEPENDENT

- exact optimal proximity to failure;
- exact volume optimum/ceiling;
- exact load × fiber interaction;
- exact satellite-cell necessity at different hypertrophy magnitudes;
- exact causal contribution of ECM remodeling;
- exact regional superiority of a given exercise/ROM;
- exact personal response stability;
- true serial sarcomere addition from long-length RT.
