### 1. Creativity: breakpoints are outcome-specific, not universal
E17.2a — Jauk et al. 2013 DOI 10.1016/j.intell.2013.03.003, author-uploaded article text https://www.researchgate.net/publication/245030070_The_relationship_between_intelligence_and_creativity_New_support_for_the_threshold_hypothesis_by_means_of_empirical_breakpoint_detection directly inspected §3.1. N297, mean age30.40 from indexed primary methods; age18–55 and score range59.27–147.37 inherited from GFT-07.1 source audit, not independently reverified here. Fluency change point86.09 CI75.57–96.61 (Davies p<.05, n21 below,275 above); Top-2 originality104.00 CI93.07–114.90 p<.05. Average originality119.60 CI107.5–131.7 but Davies p=.14, although split correlations differ. Achievement breakpoint p=.64. Thus the widely repeated 120 claim is not a confirmed slope change for average originality in this study; no proof of no effect in upper segment. Multiple outcome/model choices, small lower subgroup, broad intervals, no independent validation. Earlier abstract/illustration in 17.1 refined; 07.1 already records this distinction. Short report DOI 10.1016/j.paid.2013.07.128 same N/description, not replication.
E17.2b — Weiss 2020 DOI 10.3390/jintelligence8040038 original-text mirror https://www.academia.edu/56533910/A_Reappraisal_of_the_Threshold_Hypothesis_of_Creativity_and_Intelligence selected methods/results/discussion directly inspected. Reanalysis of existing datasets: N456 ages13–20 (6 multivariate outliers excluded), N438 ages18–49 (12 excluded). Different intelligence and DT batteries. No detected slope changes across examined breakpoints; heteroscedasticity and local SEM analyses not supportive of proposed threshold. Only 47/43 above sample-standardized z=1.33, not automatically normed IQ120. Not exact replication of Jauk's scoring; no proof of equivalence or all-domain linearity. No separately measured adult extreme stratum. Full supplements/raw data not checked; same datasets as earlier studies, not new participants. E17.2a/b previously covered by 07.1/17.1, not independent replication across qualification areas.
E17.2c — Chiang et al. 2026 DOI 10.3390/jintelligence14060097 indexed original abstract/results (institutional direct cache miss): 9358 Hong Kong students ages6–20, 12 education-program cohorts over five years. Fixed 120 breakpoint not significant; data-driven change around study intelligence score102 (bootstrap mean101.9, CI97–104); authors describe flattening AUT fluency. Indexed mean predictor119.65 SD13.92, reported range40–160 in original-text index. Not representative adult FSIQ or evidence of a universal 102/120 cutoff. Bootstrap on same data is not external validation. Cohort, culture, selection and scoring may matter; full analysis, range-specific n and adult-tail measurement not verified; no exact multibreakpoint slopes adopted. Prior GFT-07.4 records same study. This is substantive positive evidence for a conditional nonlinear association, compatible with differing task/measure results in Weiss/Jauk.
E17.2d — Karwowski 2016 DOI 10.1016/j.intell.2016.04.006 indexed original text and inherited GFT-07.1 author-abstract audit: necessary-condition boundary differs from change in conditional mean; empty scatterplot regions can reflect support/scale/sample, not causal necessity or a validated universal IQ threshold. Gerwig 2021 PMC8167550 indexed original text explicitly does not retest threshold in pooled DT correlation meta-analysis. No new pooled estimate; association meta-analysis does not resolve shape. Distinguish fluency, originality, product quality and achievement.

### 2. Ability differentiation: supported association, disputed interpretation
E17.2e — Blum/Holling 2017 ScienceDirect article S0160289616303609 indexed original abstract, direct403: 106 articles/408 studies over ~100 years, generally lower test intercorrelations/g saturation with increasing ability. Group-based and heterogeneous methods; full effect estimates and sample dependence not audited. Tucker-Drob 2009 DOI 10.1037/a0015864 indexed original text PMC2855504, direct CAPTCHA: Woodcock-Johnson III standardization across ages4–101; nonlinear factor models support ability differentiation, limited evidence for age modification; N6273 inherited 17.1, not independently verified this turn. Factor loading moderation not same as decline in absolute skill, predictive slope for occupational outcomes, cognitive architecture in an individual or threshold of qualitative thought.
E17.2f — Breit et al. 2022 DOI 10.1037/bul0000379 University of Amsterdam author abstract directly inspected https://dare.uva.nl/id/3103c9d0-c4db-42d1-b0e2-5a5125b09fd2. 33 reports,51 studies,264300 participants; predominant support for ability differentiation in children and adults and age dedifferentiation in older adults; child/adolescent age differentiation unsupported, some negligible adolescent age dedifferentiation; small subset supports interaction. Full inclusion/quality and effect estimates not inspected, no summing N across reviews/primary samples. Counterexample Breit 2021 PMID33539120 indexed original abstract N2619 German children ages6–12 THINK1–4 norming, no consistent ability/age differentiation or interaction. Breit 2020 DOI10.1037/dev0000876 indexed abstract, second adolescent sample little evidence for ability differentiation. Null results constrain universality; test family, age, precision and methods differ.
E17.2g — Breit et al. 2025 DOI 10.1037/pag0000875 indexed original author abstract PMID39804364: nonlinear factor analysis integrated across German Wechsler norming samples N4129, support ability differentiation across ages, largest reported effects in young adults, complex age pattern. Not a measured adult IQ150–160+ contrast or validation of adult test accuracy. Breit et al. 2026 DOI 10.1037/bul0000531 author publication page indexed abstract https://ulrich-schroeders.de/fixed/publications/ (direct page version did not locate exact entry; indexed published abstract and earlier preprint distinguished). 29 samples from20 German child/adolescent norming studies N42207; reported moderation parameter lambda2=-.046 CI[-.064,-.028], age lambda3=.003 CI[-.024,.030], interaction lambda4=-.005 CI[-.012,.002]; test-family differences. These are model parameters, not IQ points or prediction slopes for life outcomes. No equivalence for age/interaction. Potential overlap with 2025 Wechsler, prior German studies and systematic reviews, not audited; do not sum N or count as independent cohorts. Full published methods not retrieved. Child/adolescent data not adult tail.
E17.2h — Murray/Dixon/Johnson 2013 ScienceDirect S0160289613000809 indexed original abstract/results (direct403): simulations with positive manifest skew generated SLODR, negative skew generated reverse pattern; selection on subtests can also distort covariance. Sorjonen/Madison/Melin 2021 PMC8121961 indexed original text: statistical dependence of within-person mean and variance can produce related patterns. Methodological counterexamples do not prove all observed differentiation spurious; newer continuous nonlinear models address some limitations but remain assumption-dependent. Compare latent structure, residual variance and measurement scale/skew, reliability, range restriction and independent anchor. A group loading pattern does not identify an individual mechanism. Dimensional alternative Toffalini et al. 2025 article S0191886925002363 indexed original text N111 gifted drawn from2173 ages16–90; authors broadly support dimensional account with age/profile variation. Same normative data as GFT-16.1 evidence, not independent replication; direct institutional403, specific fitted contrasts/tail precision not independently adopted here.

### 3. Life outcomes: conditional form, not global optimum
E17.2i — Brown/Wai/Chabris 2021 DOI10.1177/1745691620964122 APS author abstract directly re-read https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/perspectives/1745691620964122/; four representative longitudinal US/UK cohorts total48558, youth ability and adult outcomes, mean R² across measures .002–.256; mean added nonlinear R² .001. Most associations linear positive or practically null, nonlinear additions usually small/nonreplicated. Publisher/eprint robots denied, exact models/range counts/weights and tail precision not verified. Limits universal harmful threshold, does not prove none at every outcome/age/scale. Same source GFT-16.2g/17.1e. Coyle 2015 S0160289615001051 indexed author abstract/methods: NLSY N1950 and College Board Validity Study N160670, linear terms dominate aptitude/g/GPA, quadratic added variance <1% on average. NLSY data potentially overlap Brown; college admissions tests not adult validated FSIQ; sample sizes not evidence of tail support.
E17.2j — Ganzach et al. 2013 DOI10.1016/j.intell.2013.07.015 Tel Aviv University author abstract directly read https://cris.tau.ac.il/en/publications/general-mental-ability-and-pay-nonlinear-effects/; reports concave association of general ability with pay and stronger association at higher occupational complexity. Full sample/age, model conditioning, overlap and exact effect estimates unverified. Concavity not reversal or zero slope; pay not pure productivity, occupation can be selection/mediator. Antonakis/House/Simonton 2017 DOI10.1037/apl0000221 author-site abstract directly read https://simonton.faculty.ucdavis.edu/research/abstracts/abs400/; adult midlevel multinational leaders, Wonderlic predictor, perceived leadership ratings and fixed effects/covariates, fitted inverted-U peak near IQ120. Not independently measured WAIS adult tail, objective performance or causal optimum; employee ratings/context/selection, quadratic curve and tail support require verification. No universal social or leadership maximum; proposed relative group ability mechanism not established merely by fit. These examples refute blanket linearity assumption in all outcomes, not Brown's cross-outcome predominance.
E17.2k — Czerwiński/Atroszko/Konarski 2025 DOI10.5114/hpr/187337 publisher original HTML https://hpr.termedia.pl/Mental-health-of-intellectually-gifted-individuals-Investigating-the-nonlinearity,187337,0,2.html selected methods/Table1/2/3/discussion directly inspected. BCS70, 11212 complete intelligence measures age10, outcome-specific N2870–7984, mental health measures ages16–50. Quadratic and spline models at +2SD/+1.28SD, several significant curvature tests but displayed total R² typically .00–.02 (rounded); several null results, matrices subtest mostly null. Authors interpret high-end reversal yet explicitly note high scorers need not be worse than population mean. Do not infer slope reversal from F-change alone, exact local slopes/CI and multiple-testing/attrition handling not fully audited. Same BCS70 as Brown with different predictors, waves and outcomes; not independent replication. No causal claim about high intelligence harming mental health; GFT-15 handles detailed wellbeing interpretation. Not current adult FSIQ and no 150–160+ calibration. This limits simplistic universal beneficial/linear interpretation while preserving absolute-outcome distinction.

## Comparison and transfer
| Hypothesis | Evidence within studied support | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Universal IQ120/other creativity threshold | contradictory, measure-specific positive and negative tests | no universal cutoff, no causal necessity |
| Ability differentiation in cognitive covariance/loadings | majority support in method-conscious review and newer analyses, counterexamples | effect varies by instrument/age/model; no discrete kind or mechanism |
| Universal loss of ability benefits at high IQ | large cohort comparisons generally not supportive | youth predictors, not validated adult extreme tail |
| Local concavity/reversal/interaction | pay, leader ratings, mental-health measures provide conditional evidence | outcomes and samples differ; not proof of global optimum |
| Adult normed IQ150–160+ qualitative transition | suitable separately analyzed and calibrated evidence not retrieved | uncertainty, not proof of no transition |

Keep the estimands separate: conditional mean slopes, conditional variance/upper boundary, latent loadings, predictive error and causal effect. Compare linear, smooth nonlinear and segmented alternatives with meaningful contrasts and support per 17.1; sample-derived thresholds, significance differences and point estimates are not replicated changes. Absolute outcomes can remain higher even with negative local slope. Overlapping cohorts, review/primary reports and alternate analyses must not be pooled as independent. Direct adult tail search did not establish valid separately analyzed current FSIQ150–160+ functional form for these outcomes; developmental or selected samples and score maxima do not establish that range. No blanket statement that high intelligence causes problems, or guarantees wellbeing/performance. For individual analysis, use observed context/behavior/effort, domain-specific skills, time-course and independent competing evidence; do not infer cognitive architecture from IQ or group curves.
