## 3. Additional scientific checks and corrections
R1 — Dependency of observer evidence. Primary article PMC10921981 explicitly acknowledges prior publications on the same data. Fleeson & Law 2015 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4673017/ also reports 97 targets/183 observers. A separate article on a different estimand is not a new sample. Root cause: E6 provenance omitted shared-data warning, not a false result. Scope: IND-01.1 E6, inherited PER evidence if same cohort cited, SOC-X01 observer agreement, SYS-03 integration. Correction: record shared-data lineage and never count as independent cohort confirmation. Retest: methods statement and counts agree; state variability and observer agreement remain distinct claims, not invalidated.

R2 — Scope of personality change and measurement invariance. Olaru et al. https://doi.org/10.1177/08902070221145088, complete author abstract directly inspected at https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/personality-change-through-a-digital-coaching-intervention-using-/ ; publisher direct access 403. Secondary analysis of PEACH trial data N=552, not independent replication of E6/E7. Self-reported gains concentrated in sociability and in productivity/organization facets, not uniformly across the corresponding broad domains; observer estimates generally small and nonsignificant. Measurement invariance tested; full models, exact coefficients and supplementary tables not inspected. Not contradiction of all original Stieger results: samples, estimands and analyses differ. Root cause: prior E6 summary insufficiently explicit about hierarchy/facet concentration. Correction: do not equate broad score change with coherent change across all facets, or observer confirmation of selected results with all outcomes. Distinguish construct change from measurement shift; lack of scalar invariance may reflect differential facet/item change rather than simply measurement error. Retest: consistent with IND-01.2 context and measurement gate, extends it with explicit hierarchy.

R3 — Structural change versus level change. Beck & Jackson, Detecting Idiographic Personality Change, https://doi.org/10.1080/00223891.2021.1984246 (indexed original abstract/method extracts, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9727837/). N388, 21790 reports, weekly over 60 weeks, preregistered kernel change-point approach examining covariance structure. This provides a distinct operational target, not validated universal diagnostic tool. Full results and sensitivity analysis not read, no numerical success claims. Haehner et al. https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000525 indexed original abstract: 229 longitudinal studies, N230492, explicitly estimates between-person variation in change; PubMed direct open CAPTCHA, full effect/moderator estimates unverified. Add an explicit seventh estimand to IND-01.2: structural change in associations among within-person variables, distinguished from trait-level change, rank stability, population variance and frequency of states. Estimate only with sufficient longitudinal observations and a suitable design; sparse descriptions may suggest a hypothesis, cannot fit such a model. Population change-score heterogeneity not proof of a specific person's structural change. Root cause: structural target not separately named in six-estimand list. Scope IND-01, IND-03, SYS-01/03. Retest: identical means/variances can coexist with different covariance; for a two-variable covariance matrix with unit diagonal and correlation +.5 versus -.5, eigenvalues .5,1.5 in both, valid positive definite matrices, identical marginal variances, different covariance structure. This is a mathematical counterexample, not a practical exam or empirical validation. 

R4 — Stronger causal claim check. Bauditz authors favor construal as primary driver; simultaneous self-ratings and partial contact control do not identify its causal share. Correct inference is conditional association and differential contribution under operationalized designs. Existing SPQ guardrail retained; explicit reliability limitation added. The 2026 longitudinal life-event paper https://doi.org/10.1111/jopy.70055 indexed original source excerpts suggests moderation by event perception, age, gender, timing and measurement; direct Wiley access 403, no effect estimates or universal moderation asserted. It is additional evidence against a single universal trajectory, not causal person-level evidence. Retest: no contradiction between small mean changes and potentially heterogeneous individual responses.

## 4. Methodological adequacy and revised rule
For adult personality inference distinguish: (1) observed act and opportunity; (2) momentary state/report; (3) repeated conditional pattern; (4) facet vs broad trait; (5) change in level or distribution; (6) change in relations among variables; (7) causal mechanism. Check comparable contexts, informant/method, sampling, longitudinal measurement equivalence, regression to mean, pre-event trend, other transitions and dependent observations. A pattern may support a bounded conditional forecast without identifying cause. No universal count/time threshold, unsupported diagnosis or personalized numeric estimate. A global label should not erase specificity of observed change. Separate proposed structural change from data sufficient to estimate it. Confirm independent outcome/provenance before forecast scoring. These rules are compatible with IND-03 and SYS-01/03; not a validated individual diagnostic test.

Quantitative control: 3094/186=16.6344 (matches rounded 16.63), 157/1523≈0.103086; the latter is descriptive sample retention ratio. Published Table 2 of Roemer confirms the distinction between 22% annual patterns with >=1 point and 22% people with >=1.5 point extraversion change at least once in a different analysis; no interchange of these quantities. No synthetic practical tests. No evidence that any verified correction invalidates core IND-01 descriptive inference.
