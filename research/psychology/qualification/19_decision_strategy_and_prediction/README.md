# Decision-making, strategy, and prediction

## Purpose

This directory is the canonical storage location for the **DEC** Phase C qualification domain.

DEC covers generic psychological reasoning about:

- judgment and choice;
- heuristics and biases;
- risk and uncertainty;
- framing;
- probability/forecasting;
- calibration;
- value/preference construction;
- multi-attribute decisions;
- intertemporal choice where not already owned by MOT;
- social/affective influences on judgment;
- decision quality versus outcome quality;
- limits of person-specific prediction.

### DEC-01 — Judgment and decision foundations

Status: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v1.0 — 18/18 PASS**

Canonical block:

`DEC-01_judgment_decision_foundations_normative_descriptive_risk_uncertainty_heuristics_framing_calibration_choice_architecture.md`

Established:

1. normative and descriptive decision questions are distinct;
2. the relevant normative benchmark must be identified before labeling a response biased/irrational;
3. decision quality is distinct from realized outcome quality;
4. risk and ambiguity/uncertainty are distinct;
5. expected utility is a major benchmark/model rather than a complete descriptive psychological law;
6. prospect-theory patterns are robust but model fit does not establish one mechanism or stable trait;
7. loss aversion is not treated as a universal fixed ~2x coefficient;
8. framing effects are robust/heterogeneous and do not identify one mechanism;
9. heuristics are strategies rather than errors by definition;
10. bias labels describe response patterns before they explain mechanisms;
11. base-rate use varies across individuals and probability representations;
12. probability format, denominator, absolute/relative risk, and visual representation change decision interpretation;
13. calibration, discrimination, confidence, and different forms of overconfidence are distinct;
14. dual-process language is not reified into two literal modules or fast=biased/slow=rational;
15. cross-task risk-preference convergence is low enough to block one-task global trait inference;
16. defaults/choice architecture influence behavior without automatically improving welfare;
17. a 104-question Judgment / Decision Foundations Inference Gate and 18/18 closure audit are canonical.

### DEC-02 — Applied decision quality and forecasting and sufficiency audit

Status: **COMPLETE_FOR_CURRENT_SCOPE — 20/20 PASS**

Canonical block:

`DEC-02_applied_decision_quality_forecasting_information_value_aggregation_aids_debiasing_real_life_sufficiency_audit.md`

Established:

1. probabilistic forecasts require repeated evaluation and cannot be judged from one realized event;
2. calibration, discrimination, sharpness/resolution, proper score, and decision utility are distinct;
3. Brier score is not a pure calibration measure and cross-population comparisons can mislead;
4. collective forecasting can be strongly calibrated without implying individual or universal-domain calibration;
5. aggregation benefits depend on independence, diversity, expertise, and aggregation rule;
6. advice weight should not be driven by persuasiveness/confidence alone;
7. information value depends on decision impact minus search/delay/emotional costs;
8. sampling quality and stopping/termination quality are distinct;
9. multi-attribute decisions require explicit attributes, weights/thresholds, uncertainty, and protection against quantification fixation;
10. reversibility changes optimal commitment policy and can make early action plus correction rational;
11. major real-life decisions can involve self-change, irreversibility, and uncertain experiential value beyond small-lottery models;
12. decision aids can improve knowledge, risk understanding, participation, and values-choice congruence without guaranteeing downstream welfare improvement;
13. debiasing interventions show small average targeted-task effects while durability/far transfer remain uncertain;
14. person-level decision competence is multidimensional and requires repeated multimethod psychometric evidence;
15. a 112-question Applied Decision Quality Inference Gate and 20/20 DEC sufficiency audit are canonical;
16. no scientifically necessary DEC-03 dependency is identified.
