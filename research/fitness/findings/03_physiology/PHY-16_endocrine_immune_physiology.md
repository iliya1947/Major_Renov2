# PHY-16 — Endocrine / immune physiology

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-18**  
Область: 03 — Physiology

## 0. Core decision

Exercise produces coordinated endocrine and immune regulation.

Canonical model:

`exercise demand`
`→ autonomic/endocrine + pancreatic regulation`
`→ substrate availability + cardiovascular/stress support`
`+ immune-cell trafficking + myokine/cytokine signaling`
`→ transient systemic regulation`.

Chronic adaptation is not the sum of acute circulating hormone/cytokine spikes.

It depends on repeated tissue-specific loading, local signaling, metabolic adaptation and recovery.

## 1. Resting endocrine regulation

Resting endocrine state is multi-axis and context dependent.

Relevant context includes:

- circadian timing;
- feeding/fasting;
- sleep;
- energy availability;
- recent exercise;
- psychological stress;
- age/sex/reproductive context;
- assay/sample method.

Project rule:

**one hormone concentration does not define the endocrine system.**

## 2. Catecholamines

Norepinephrine and epinephrine rise with exercise stress and support:

- cardiovascular responses;
- substrate mobilization;
- hepatic glucose output;
- lipolysis;
- pancreatic regulation;
- immune-cell mobilization.

Project rule:

**catecholamine response is acute regulation, not chronic training quality.**

## 3. Cortisol

Cortisol supports stress regulation and substrate availability.

Its exercise response is affected by:

- time of day;
- intensity/duration;
- energy status;
- recent training;
- psychological stress;
- sampling.

Project rule:

**cortisol alone is not a readiness or overtraining score.**

## 4. Insulin

Insulin regulates glucose uptake/storage and hepatic glucose output.

Contracting muscle can increase glucose uptake through mechanisms partly independent of insulin.

Project rule:

**exercise glucose uptake is not reducible to circulating insulin.**

## 5. Glucagon

Glucagon supports hepatic glucose production.

Its exercise role interacts with:

- insulin;
- catecholamines;
- liver glycogen;
- exercise demand;
- nutrition.

Project rule:

**glucagon is part of integrated counter-regulation.**

## 6. Testosterone

Acute exercise can transiently raise circulating testosterone.

Response varies by mode, intensity, sex and sampling.

Project rule:

**acute endogenous testosterone response ≠ hypertrophy dose.**

Physiological androgen signaling remains biologically relevant; acute endogenous fluctuations are not equivalent to pharmacological exposure.

## 7. Growth hormone

GH can rise strongly after metabolically demanding exercise.

Project rule:

**large acute GH response is not a validated surrogate for muscle hypertrophy.**

## 8. IGF-related signaling

IGF biology is compartment specific.

Distinguish:

- circulating IGF-1;
- interstitial IGF-1;
- local intramuscular IGF-1/receptor signaling.

Project rule:

**circulating IGF-1 ≠ local muscle IGF signaling.**

## 9. Acute systemic hormones vs local adaptation

Exercise can acutely change multiple circulating hormones.

These changes support whole-body exercise regulation.

Local tissue adaptation depends more directly on:

- mechanical loading;
- tissue-specific signaling;
- protein turnover;
- remodeling;
- repeated exposure/recovery.

Project rule:

**acute systemic hormone spike ≠ chronic local adaptation.**

## 10. Chronic endocrine adaptation

Training does not create one universal “good hormone profile.”

Reliable chronic adaptations can occur without changes in resting testosterone, GH, cortisol or IGF-1.

Improved insulin sensitivity/glucose regulation is more reproducible in metabolically impaired populations.

Project rule:

**no resting hormone change ≠ no training adaptation.**

## 11. Sleep / energy / stress modifiers

Endocrine and inflammatory background is modified by:

- sleep restriction;
- low energy availability;
- carbohydrate availability;
- psychological stress;
- recent training load.

Project rule:

**training biomarkers require recovery/nutrition/stress context.**

## 12. Acute inflammatory response

Exercise can transiently change cytokines and immune-cell distribution.

Project rule:

**acute exercise inflammation is a regulated stress/repair response, not automatically pathology.**

## 13. Cytokines / myokines

Contracting muscle releases signaling factors.

IL-6 commonly rises acutely after endurance and resistance exercise.

Exercise-derived IL-6 can have metabolic/immunoregulatory roles.

Project rule:

**an acute cytokine rise does not by itself mean harmful chronic inflammation.**

## 14. Immune-cell trafficking

Acute exercise mobilizes immune-cell subsets into blood and redistributes them during recovery.

Some circulating lymphocyte populations can temporarily fall below baseline after exercise.

Project rule:

**postexercise lymphopenia ≠ automatic immune suppression.**

## 15. Habitual exercise and immunity

Regular exercise can support immune regulation and reduce selected inflammatory markers.

However:

- biomarker effects are heterogeneous;
- certainty for many outcomes is low;
- IL-6 chronic effects are inconsistent;
- biomarker change is not equivalent to proven infection protection.

Project rule:

**“exercise boosts immunity” is too nonspecific.**

## 16. Training stress and illness risk

Illness risk depends on interacting factors:

- training/competition load;
- sleep;
- energy availability;
- psychological stress;
- travel/crowding/pathogen exposure;
- recovery;
- individual susceptibility.

Project rule:

**one hard session or one immune marker does not define an “open window.”**

## 17. Under-recovery / overtraining

No single endocrine or immune biomarker is a universal diagnostic of:

- readiness;
- under-recovery;
- overreaching;
- overtraining syndrome.

Project rule:

**recovery is a longitudinal multi-signal problem.**

## 18. Clinical boundary

PHY-16 does not diagnose:

- diabetes;
- thyroid disease;
- hypogonadism;
- adrenal disease;
- REDs;
- autoimmune disease;
- immunodeficiency;
- infection;
- chronic inflammatory disease;
- overtraining syndrome.

Clinical interpretation belongs to MED.

## 19. Canonical project rules

1. One resting hormone value ≠ total endocrine state.
2. Catecholamines are acute regulators, not training scores.
3. Cortisol alone ≠ readiness.
4. Contraction-mediated glucose uptake is distinct from insulin action.
5. Glucagon is integrated counter-regulation.
6. Acute testosterone spike ≠ hypertrophy dose.
7. Acute GH spike ≠ hypertrophy surrogate.
8. Circulating IGF-1 ≠ local muscle IGF signaling.
9. Acute systemic hormones ≠ local chronic adaptation.
10. No universal trained resting-hormone profile exists.
11. Sleep/energy/stress modify endocrine/immune state.
12. Acute exercise cytokine rise ≠ pathological inflammation.
13. Postexercise lymphopenia ≠ automatic immunosuppression.
14. Habitual exercise can support immune regulation, but “boosts immunity” is too broad.
15. Illness risk is load + recovery + exposure + person.
16. No single endocrine/immune biomarker diagnoses under-recovery or overtraining.
17. Clinical endocrine/immune disease belongs to MED.

## 20. Training-relevant implications

1. **Do not design workouts to maximize acute testosterone/GH spikes.**
2. **Do not infer muscle adaptation from serum IGF-1 alone.**
3. **Do not use cortisol or T:C ratio as a stand-alone readiness score.**
4. **Interpret glucose regulation through both insulin and contraction-mediated mechanisms.**
5. **Do not label acute exercise cytokines as automatically harmful inflammation.**
6. **Do not interpret temporary postexercise lymphocyte decline as immune shutdown.**
7. **Use sleep, energy availability, training load and illness exposure as context for biomarker interpretation.**
8. **Treat recovery as longitudinal and multi-signal.**
9. **Route persistent endocrine/immune abnormalities to MED.**

## 21. What PHY-16 does not prescribe

Not owned here:

- hormone replacement;
- endocrine diagnostic thresholds;
- glucose/diabetes treatment;
- REDs diagnosis/treatment;
- infection workup;
- immune disease management;
- exact biomarker panels;
- recovery scoring algorithm;
- nutrition dose;
- training-load prescription.

These remain MED, NUT, REC, TST, PRO and IND.

## 22. Confidence

### HIGH
- acute catecholamine response to exercise;
- insulin/contraction pathway distinction;
- glucagon counter-regulatory role;
- acute systemic hormone spikes are poor hypertrophy surrogates;
- systemic/local IGF compartment distinction;
- acute cytokine/myokine responses;
- exercise-induced immune-cell trafficking;
- clinical endocrine/immune boundary.

### MODERATE-HIGH
- selected chronic insulin-sensitivity improvements;
- selected chronic inflammatory-marker improvements;
- LEA/sleep restriction as endocrine/inflammatory modifiers;
- illness risk as multi-factorial.

### MODERATE / CONTEXT-DEPENDENT
- exact cortisol/testosterone/GH response to a session;
- exact chronic resting hormone changes;
- exact long-term role of individual myokines/exerkines;
- exact causal meaning of transient immune-cell redistribution;
- infection-risk contribution of training stress independent of exposure;
- biomarker signatures of nonfunctional overreaching/overtraining.
