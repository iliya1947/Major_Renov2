# PHY-07 — Hypertrophy mechanisms

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-18**  
Область: 03 — Physiology

## 0. Core decision

Human resistance-training hypertrophy is modeled as:

`repeated sufficiently challenging mechanical loading`
`+`
`sufficient weekly exposure`
`+`
`protein/energy support`
`→ mechanotransduction + translational efficiency/capacity + cellular/ECM support`
`→ repeated positive net protein accretion`
`→ retained myofiber growth`.

No single acute molecular marker, pathway, exercise sensation or prescription variable fully explains hypertrophy.

## 1. Net protein balance

Long-term growth requires cumulative net protein accretion:

`MPS − MPB > 0` over sufficient repeated periods.

MPB is part of normal remodeling and quality control.

Project rule:

**protein turnover is a time-integrated growth/remodeling process, not one acute score.**

## 2. MPS

Resistance exercise robustly raises MPS.

But:

- early MPS can reflect repair/remodeling;
- one acute MPS peak does not rank programs;
- integrated MPS becomes more meaningfully related to growth after early damage attenuates.

Project rule:

**MPS is necessary biology, not a stand-alone hypertrophy surrogate.**

## 3. Mechanotransduction

Mechanical loading is converted to cellular responses through a distributed network involving force transmission, cytoskeleton/ECM, focal-adhesion/integrin-related signaling, lipid signaling, MAPK/YAP/TAZ-related pathways and mTORC1-linked translation.

Project rule:

**there is no single universal “hypertrophy sensor.”**

## 4. mTORC1

Human rapamycin perturbation supports mTORC1 as critical for the acute contraction-induced MPS response.

Project rule:

**mTORC1 matters mechanistically; acute phosphorylation amplitude is not a validated chronic hypertrophy score or prescription target.**

## 5. Ribosome biogenesis

Resistance training increases ribosomal/translational machinery in human muscle, especially early in training.

Project rule:

**translational capacity supports sustained growth, but ribosome markers are not a practical training target.**

## 6. Satellite cells / myonuclei

Substantial hypertrophy often involves:

- satellite-cell expansion;
- myonuclear accretion.

However:

- no rigid universal hypertrophy threshold for myonuclear addition is supported;
- every small human hypertrophic response does not require a detectable satellite-cell expansion.

Project rule:

**myonuclear biology supports growth capacity without a fixed “domain ceiling” prescription rule.**

## 7. ECM

Hypertrophy occurs inside a training-responsive extracellular matrix.

Project rule:

**ECM remodeling is part of the adaptive environment but ECM biomarkers are not direct myofiber-growth surrogates.**

## 8. Protein / amino acids

Adequate protein/amino-acid availability supports postexercise MPS and can augment chronic lean-mass adaptation.

Project rule:

**protein sufficiency matters; exact intake/source/distribution belongs to NUT.**

Acute protein-source MPS ranking does not automatically equal chronic hypertrophy ranking.

## 9. Energy availability

Sustained energy deficit/low energy availability can impair lean-mass adaptation.

But hypertrophy can still occur in selected deficit contexts.

Project rule:

**energy sufficiency facilitates growth; surplus is not universally mandatory and energy deficit is not irrelevant.**

## 10. Mechanical tension

Mechanical loading is central, but internal tissue tension is not equal to:

- external kg;
- %1RM;
- EMG;
- burn;
- pump;
- time under tension.

Project rule:

**external training variables are proxies/constraints on internal fiber loading, not the mechanism itself.**

## 11. Long-muscle-length loading

Current evidence supports useful loading at longer muscle lengths.

- long-length partials generally outperform short-length partials;
- lengthened partials can produce growth similar to full ROM in some trained populations;
- universal superiority is not established;
- direct human serial sarcomerogenesis evidence remains limited.

Project rule:

**long-length exposure is useful but should not be mythologized as guaranteed extra sarcomeres/growth.**

## 12. Weekly volume

Weekly set volume has a positive dose-response relationship with hypertrophy with diminishing returns.

Project rule:

**more volume can produce more growth up to diminishing returns, but no universal optimal/maximal weekly set number exists.**

## 13. Load / repetition range

Hypertrophy can occur across a broad load/rep spectrum when effort/stimulus is sufficient.

Heavier loads have clearer maximal-strength specificity.

Project rule:

**there is no uniquely anabolic rep range.**

## 14. Proximity to failure

Closer-to-failure training tends to produce more hypertrophy than systematically stopping far from failure.

But momentary failure itself is not consistently superior.

Project rule:

**sufficient effort matters; literal failure is not required.**

Exact optimal RIR remains unresolved.

## 15. Frequency

Frequency mainly distributes weekly training dose and session quality.

Project rule:

**frequency is not a primary independent hypertrophy mechanism when weekly volume is considered.**

## 16. Tempo

Commonly studied faster vs slower repetition tempos produce similar average hypertrophy.

Project rule:

**slow tempo/time under tension is not inherently more hypertrophic.**

Tempo remains relevant to control, technique, fatigue and exercise constraints.

## 17. ROM

ROM should be interpreted partly by loaded muscle length.

Current synthesis:

- full ROM = robust default;
- lengthened partials = viable alternative/tool in some contexts;
- short-length partials generally less favorable.

Project rule:

**“partial ROM” is not one intervention class.**

## 18. Regional hypertrophy

Growth can be nonuniform along a muscle and between heads.

Project rule:

**regional targeting must be demonstrated for the specific muscle/exercise rather than inferred from sensation/anatomy alone.**

## 19. Individual variability

Hypertrophic response varies between people.

Observed variation includes:

- real biology;
- adherence/effort/program fit;
- nutrition/energy;
- measurement error.

Project rule:

**one short block cannot establish a stable nonresponder or unique optimal protocol.**

## 20. Hyperplasia

Current human evidence does not show a significant increase in estimated muscle-fiber number after typical RT interventions up to ~6 months.

Project rule:

**ordinary human training hypertrophy should be modeled primarily as enlargement/remodeling of existing fibers.**

Hyperplasia is not a practical programming target.

## 21. Mechanism vs prescription hierarchy

### Stronger prescription relevance
- repeated challenging loading;
- weekly volume with diminishing returns;
- sufficiently high effort;
- broad usable load/rep ranges;
- adequate protein/energy;
- useful loaded muscle lengths/ROM;
- recoverability/adherence.

### Mechanistically important, weak direct prescription target
- acute MPS peak;
- mTORC1 phosphorylation;
- ribosome marker;
- satellite-cell count;
- myonuclear count;
- ECM marker.

Project rule:

**mechanistic importance does not imply practical controllability or prescription validity.**

## 22. Canonical rules

1. Hypertrophy = retained myofiber growth, not acute swelling.
2. Cumulative net protein accretion matters.
3. Acute MPS ≠ chronic hypertrophy score.
4. mTORC1 matters but phosphorylation ≠ prescription.
5. Ribosome biogenesis supports translational capacity.
6. Satellite cells/myonuclei support substantial growth without a rigid threshold rule.
7. Mechanical tension ≠ external load alone.
8. Volume shows positive dose-response with diminishing returns.
9. Broad load/rep ranges can hypertrophy muscle.
10. Closer to failure generally matters; failure is not mandatory.
11. Frequency mainly distributes dose.
12. Slow tempo is not inherently superior.
13. Loaded muscle length matters when interpreting ROM.
14. Regional growth exists.
15. Individual response requires repeated standardized measurement.
16. Human RT hyperplasia is not practically established.
17. Acute mechanistic markers do not replace chronic outcome evidence.

## 23. Confidence

### HIGH
- mechanical loading as central stimulus;
- MPS response to RT;
- acute MPS surrogate boundary;
- mTORC1 importance for acute exercise-induced MPS;
- volume dose-response/diminishing returns;
- broad load/rep-range hypertrophy;
- failure not required;
- frequency's limited independent hypertrophy effect;
- minimal average tempo effect;
- regional hypertrophy existence;
- lack of established practical human hyperplasia.

### MODERATE-HIGH
- ribosome biogenesis as sustained-growth mechanism;
- satellite-cell/myonuclear support for substantial hypertrophy;
- long-length loading advantage vs short-length partials;
- lengthened partial/full-ROM equivalence in some contexts;
- energy-deficit constraint.

### MODERATE / CONTEXT-DEPENDENT
- exact optimal RIR;
- exact volume ceiling;
- exact satellite-cell necessity;
- exact ECM causal contribution;
- exact long-length advantage by muscle/exercise;
- human serial sarcomere addition;
- stable individual responder phenotype.
