# PHY-20 — Environmental physiology

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-18**  
Область: 03 — Physiology

## 0. Core decision

Environmental conditions change the internal physiological cost of the same external workload.

Canonical model:

`external workload + environment`
`→ thermal / cardiovascular / fluid / perceptual strain`
`→ altered sustainable performance`.

## 1. Thermoregulation

Exercise heat balance depends on:

- metabolic heat production;
- external work;
- radiation;
- convection;
- conduction;
- evaporation.

Project rule:

**air temperature alone is not enough to define heat stress.**

## 2. Sweating vs evaporation

Sweating is only an effective cooling mechanism when sweat evaporates.

Evaporation depends on:

- humidity;
- airflow;
- clothing;
- exposed skin;
- water-vapor pressure gradient;
- dripping/runoff.

Project rule:

**more sweat does not necessarily mean more cooling.**

## 3. Heat and humidity

At high humidity, evaporative capacity falls.

This can produce:

- higher skin/core temperature;
- greater thermal/cardiovascular strain;
- lower sustainable self-paced power/performance.

Project rule:

**humidity changes physiology by constraining evaporation, not merely comfort.**

## 4. Heat acclimation

Repeated heat exposure can produce:

- lower exercise HR;
- lower exercise core temperature;
- plasma-volume expansion;
- higher sweat rate;
- improved exercise capacity/performance in heat.

Magnitude depends on:

- number of exposures;
- duration;
- temperature/humidity;
- acclimation approach;
- prior training/climate exposure.

Project rule:

**heat acclimation is dose/protocol dependent; no fixed session count proves complete acclimation.**

## 5. Cold response

Cold exposure can trigger:

- cutaneous vasoconstriction;
- reduced peripheral blood flow;
- thermogenesis/shivering;
- sympathetic activation.

Project rule:

**core protection can coexist with impaired peripheral dexterity/strength/power.**

Cold air and cold water are not interchangeable exposures.

## 6. Fluid balance

Exercise fluid state depends on:

- sweat loss;
- drinking;
- respiratory water loss;
- urine;
- environment;
- exercise duration/intensity.

Project rule:

**hydration is one modifier of strain, not a guarantee of performance or thermal safety.**

Current randomized evidence does not establish one universal dehydration threshold or universally superior drinking strategy.

## 7. Cardiovascular drift

During prolonged exercise:

- HR can rise;
- stroke volume can fall;
- relative physiological intensity can rise despite constant external work.

Heat and dehydration can amplify drift.

Project rule:

**cardiovascular drift ≠ direct dehydration meter.**

## 8. Heat-illness boundary

Normal heat strain is distinct from clinical heat illness.

Exertional heat stroke is a medical emergency.

Project rule:

**suspected exertional heat illness belongs immediately to MED/emergency management.**

## 9. Canonical project rules

1. Same workload in a different environment can produce a different internal load.
2. Temperature alone does not define heat stress.
3. Sweat production ≠ evaporation.
4. Humidity reduces evaporative capacity.
5. Heat acclimation is dose/protocol dependent.
6. Hydration modifies strain but cannot abolish environmental heat load.
7. No universal dehydration-performance threshold is retained.
8. Cardiovascular drift is integrated, not a dehydration-specific signal.
9. Cold can protect core temperature while impairing peripheral performance.
10. Cold-air and cold-water exposure are not interchangeable.
11. Exertional heat illness is MED/emergency territory.

## 10. Training-relevant implications

1. **Interpret heat with humidity, airflow, radiant load and clothing—not temperature alone.**
2. **Do not use sweat rate as a cooling score.**
3. **Use heat acclimation as an adaptation process rather than a fixed protocol.**
4. **Do not assume hydration alone prevents heat strain.**
5. **Do not prescribe overdrinking from fear of any body-mass loss.**
6. **Interpret prolonged hot-exercise HR partly through cardiovascular drift.**
7. **Expect cold to affect peripheral skill/force before core failure is obvious.**
8. **Escalate suspected heat illness to medical/emergency protocols.**

## 11. What PHY-20 does not prescribe

Not owned here:

- individual fluid/sodium dose;
- specific heat-acclimation plan;
- environmental cutoffs for a specific person;
- cooling strategy;
- cold-weather clothing prescription;
- heat-illness treatment;
- medication-specific heat-risk advice.

These remain NUT, GPP, PRO, TST, REC, MED and IND.
