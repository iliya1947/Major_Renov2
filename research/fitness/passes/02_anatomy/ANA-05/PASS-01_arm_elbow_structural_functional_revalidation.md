## 2. Canonical elbow-complex model

The elbow region is not one simple hinge.

Core articulations inside the elbow capsular region:

1. **humeroulnar / ulnohumeral**;
2. **humeroradial / radiocapitellar**;
3. **proximal radioulnar joint (PRUJ)**.

The first two primarily contribute to elbow flexion/extension.

PRUJ participates in forearm pronation/supination together with the **distal radioulnar joint (DRUJ)**.

Project rule:

**elbow flexion/extension and forearm rotation are separate motion systems that can occur simultaneously.**

---

## 3. Distal humerus

Key landmarks:

1. trochlea;
2. capitulum;
3. medial epicondyle;
4. lateral epicondyle;
5. olecranon fossa;
6. coronoid fossa;
7. radial fossa;
8. medial/lateral supracondylar ridges.

Articular relationships:

- trochlea ↔ trochlear notch of ulna;
- capitulum ↔ radial head.

Epicondyles are major ligament/muscle attachment regions rather than principal articular surfaces.

---

## 4. Proximal ulna

Key structures:

1. olecranon;
2. coronoid process;
3. trochlear notch;
4. radial notch;
5. ulnar tuberosity;
6. supinator crest.

Functional structural relevance:

- trochlear notch gives high humeroulnar congruence;
- olecranon receives triceps insertion;
- coronoid/ulnar tuberosity region receives brachialis insertion;
- radial notch participates in PRUJ.

---

## 5. Proximal radius

Key structures:

1. radial head;
2. superior articular fovea;
3. circumferential articular surface;
4. radial neck;
5. radial tuberosity.

Relationships:

- superior radial head ↔ capitulum;
- circumferential radial head ↔ radial notch + annular ligament;
- radial tuberosity receives distal biceps tendon.

Because radius rotates relative to ulna during pronation/supination, radial insertion is central to biceps supination function.

---

## 6. Humeroulnar articulation

Trochlea ↔ trochlear notch.

Broad functional role:
- dominant flexion/extension articulation;
- high osseous congruence contributes intrinsic stability.

Project boundary:

It is hinge-like, but **not an ideal mathematical one-axis hinge throughout ROM**.

Actual axis migration/translation → MEC.

---

## 7. Humeroradial articulation

Capitulum ↔ radial head.

Contributes to:

1. elbow flexion/extension;
2. compressive load transfer;
3. radial-head rotation during pronation/supination.

Project rule:

The radius remains mechanically active in the elbow complex; it is not merely a distal forearm bone.

---

## 8. Proximal radioulnar joint

PRUJ = radial head circumference ↔ radial notch of ulna within the annular-ligament ring.

Type:
- synovial pivot articulation.

It works with DRUJ to permit forearm rotation.

Current 2026 review also emphasizes the proximal stabilizing system including:

- annular ligament;
- quadrate ligament;
- oblique cord.

Detailed pathology/reconstruction is not needed here.

---

## 9. PRUJ anatomical variability

Current 2026 CT study in 104 elbows without instability/deformity found substantial interindividual variability in radial-notch/radial-head coverage geometry.

Project conclusion is limited:

**PRUJ morphology varies normally; one atlas shape is not a universal personal geometry.**

Do not infer instability or pathology from morphology alone.

Biomechanical consequence of exact shape → MEC/MED.

---

## 10. Annular ligament

Annular ligament:

- attaches to anterior/posterior margins of the radial notch;
- encircles radial head;
- retains radial head while permitting rotation.

It integrates with the lateral ligamentous system.

Project rule:

**annular ligament is both a stabilizing and motion-permitting structure.**

---

## 11. Elbow capsule

The three elbow articulations share a common capsular/synovial region.

Capsule:

- surrounds distal humerus/proximal radius-ulna;
- permits required ROM;
- is reinforced by collateral ligament complexes.

Detailed capsular mechanics → MEC.

---

## 12. Medial / ulnar collateral ligament complex

UCL/MCL complex is a principal medial elbow stabilizer.

Classical bundles:

1. anterior;
2. posterior;
3. transverse.

Current 2025–2026 reviews continue to identify the UCL as a primary soft-tissue restraint to valgus stress.

Project rule:

- preserve bundle anatomy;
- do not convert this into pitching-injury management;
- angle-specific restraint magnitudes belong to MEC/MED.

---

## 13. Lateral collateral ligament complex

The lateral system includes interconnected structures commonly described as:

- radial collateral ligament;
- lateral ulnar collateral ligament;
- annular-ligament continuity;
- related capsular components.

It contributes to:
- varus restraint;
- posterolateral rotatory stability.

Project rule:

**lateral elbow stability is a complex, not one isolated ligament line.**

---

## 14. Multi-source elbow stability

Structural stability comes from combined:

1. humeroulnar congruence;
2. radiocapitellar articulation;
3. medial collateral complex;
4. lateral collateral complex;
5. annular/PRUJ structures;
6. capsule;
7. dynamic muscle groups.

Therefore no one ligament alone represents “elbow stability”.

---

## 15. Primary elbow flexors

Main elbow flexors for project use:

1. **brachialis**;
2. **biceps brachii**;
3. **brachioradialis**.

Project rule:

**elbow flexion ≠ biceps alone.**

Relative contribution varies with geometry and task.

---

## 16. Biceps brachii — two heads

### Long head

Proximal attachment:
- supraglenoid/superior-labral region.

### Short head

Proximal attachment:
- coracoid process.

Both cross the shoulder region and elbow.

Innervation:
- musculocutaneous nerve, predominantly C5–C6 contribution in standard descriptions.

---

## 17. Distal biceps architecture

Distal biceps has two important continuities:

1. distal tendon → radial tuberosity;
2. bicipital aponeurosis / lacertus fibrosus → antebrachial fascia.

Current 2025 work treats the bicipital aponeurosis as a mechanically meaningful structure rather than an irrelevant fascial appendage.

Project rule:

**distal biceps force transfer is not modeled as one isolated tendon-to-bone point only.**

Exact load sharing between tendon/aponeurosis → MEC.

---

## 18. Biceps functions

Broad functions:

1. elbow flexion;
2. forearm supination;
3. shoulder-related contribution because both heads cross GH region.

The radial insertion explains its strong anatomical leverage for supination.

Contribution changes with:
- elbow angle;
- forearm rotation;
- shoulder position;
- external task.

Exact torque/moment arms → MEC.

---

## 19. Biceps as multi-joint muscle

Biceps spans:

- shoulder;
- elbow;
- and functionally the radioulnar rotation system through radial insertion.

Therefore shoulder/elbow/forearm position changes its muscle-tendon geometry.

This is directly relevant later to:
- incline curls;
- preacher-like curls;
- supinated/neutral/pronated grips.

But anatomy alone does not establish which variant is superior for hypertrophy.

---

## 20. Brachialis

Location:
- deep to biceps in anterior distal arm.

Attachments:
- distal anterior humerus;
- coronoid process / ulnar tuberosity region.

Primary role:
- elbow flexion.

Because it inserts on ulna rather than radius, it does not act as a pronator/supinator.

Current reference also documents:
- possible superficial/deep architectural subdivision;
- predominantly musculocutaneous innervation;
- possible radial-nerve contribution to lateral portions.

Project rule:

**brachialis remains an elbow flexor across forearm orientations; grip orientation does not “turn it on/off”.**

---

## 21. Brachioradialis

Origin:
- proximal two-thirds lateral supracondylar ridge.

Insertion:
- distal lateral radius near radial styloid.

Innervation:
- radial nerve.

Broad roles:

1. elbow flexion;
2. forearm stabilization during flexion;
3. tends to return the forearm toward mid-prone/neutral from more extreme pronation/supination.

It is located in the lateral/mobile-wad/extensor-side anatomy despite being an elbow flexor.

Project rule:

**hammer/neutral grip does not isolate brachioradialis.**

Biceps and brachialis still contribute.

---

## 22. Coracobrachialis

Origin:
- coracoid.

Insertion:
- medial mid-humerus.

Innervation:
- musculocutaneous nerve.

Acts at shoulder:
- flexion;
- adduction.

It does **not** cross elbow and is not an elbow flexor.

This is important because anatomical compartment membership does not equal joint action.

---

## 23. Triceps brachii

Three heads:

1. long;
2. lateral;
3. medial.

All contribute to elbow extension and converge on a distal tendon complex inserting on olecranon/ulna.

Innervation:
- radial nerve in standard anatomy.

---

## 24. Triceps long head

Origin:
- infraglenoid tubercle of scapula.

Therefore long head crosses:

1. shoulder;
2. elbow.

Broad roles:
- elbow extension;
- shoulder extension/adduction contribution depending position;
- shoulder stabilization contribution in some contexts.

Shoulder position changes long-head muscle-tendon length.

This is the structural basis for later overhead-vs-arm-at-side triceps analysis.

---

## 25. Triceps lateral head

Origin:
- posterior humerus superior to radial-groove region.

Crosses elbow only.

Contributes to elbow extension.

---

## 26. Triceps medial head

Origin:
- posterior humerus inferior to radial-groove region.

Crosses elbow only.

Contributes to elbow extension and is largely deep to the other heads over much of its course.

---

## 27. Triceps distal insertion

The heads converge into a distal tendon/aponeurotic complex inserting on olecranon, with fascial expansions.

Because insertion is on ulna:

- triceps is not a forearm pronator/supinator;
- forearm rotation does not move its distal insertion around radius as in biceps.

Exact extension torque still varies with elbow angle → MEC.

---

## 28. Triceps heads are not three independent muscles

Different heads have different proximal origins.

All share elbow extension function.

The long head alone crosses the shoulder.

Project rule:

**head-specific geometry can vary, but complete “head isolation” is not anatomically supported.**

Training selectivity requires biomechanics + longitudinal evidence.

---

## 29. Anconeus

Attachments:

- lateral epicondyle;
- lateral olecranon / proximal posterior ulna.

Innervation:
- radial nerve.

Broad functions:
- assists elbow extension;
- contributes stabilization;
- participates in ulna/joint control during forearm rotation.

### 2026 update

A 3D anatomical/innervation study of 8 embalmed specimens described proximal, distal and deep partitions with different fiber orientation and proposed different functional roles.

Project conclusion:

**anconeus is structurally more complex than a homogeneous single-vector mini-triceps.**

But:
- this is a small cadaveric study;
- proposed functional specialization remains lower-certainty than the gross anatomical facts.

No training prescription follows from it.

---

## 30. Elbow flexion vs forearm rotation

A movement may simultaneously include:

1. elbow flexion/extension;
2. forearm pronation/supination;
3. shoulder motion/stabilization.

Project descriptions must separate these variables.

Example:
**elbow flexion with forearm supination** is more precise than “curl the wrist/hand”.

---

## 31. Forearm pronation / supination system

### Supination
Radius and ulna become relatively parallel.

### Pronation
Radius crosses relative to ulna.

Proximally:
- radial head rotates at PRUJ.

Distally:
- radius moves around ulnar head at DRUJ.

Therefore:

**forearm rotation = coordinated PRUJ + DRUJ motion, not wrist rotation.**

Detailed DRUJ/wrist anatomy → ANA-06.

---

## 32. Grip orientation — anatomical interpretation only

### Supinated
Biceps has direct supination role and participates in elbow flexion.

### Neutral / mid-prone
Brachioradialis geometry is particularly relevant, but biceps and brachialis remain contributors.

### Pronated
Biceps remains an elbow flexor/supinator structure; brachialis remains an elbow flexor; brachioradialis contributes depending geometry.

Project prohibition:

**anatomy alone cannot rank curl variants for hypertrophy.**

That belongs to MEC/TEC/PHY.

---

## 33. Joint position and muscle length

### Biceps

Length/geometry changes with:
- shoulder angle;
- elbow angle;
- forearm rotation.

### Triceps long head

Length/geometry changes with:
- shoulder angle;
- elbow angle.

### Brachialis

Primarily affected by elbow angle.

### Brachioradialis

Affected by:
- elbow angle;
- forearm orientation.

This establishes why exercise positions are anatomically different.

It does not establish which is optimal.

---

## 34. Multiarticular insufficiency boundary

Biceps and triceps long head cross shoulder + elbow.

Therefore joint combinations can change available muscle length/shortening.

Formal:
- active insufficiency;
- passive insufficiency;
- length–tension consequences

belong to ANA functional-anatomy / PHY / MEC.

ANA-05 only establishes the structural prerequisite.

---

## 35. Carrying angle

Extended/supinated elbow normally has a valgus relationship between arm and forearm.

Magnitude varies with:
- individual anatomy;
- measurement position;
- population factors.

Project rule:

**do not encode one textbook carrying angle as a universal personal normal.**

Clinical deformity thresholds → MED/TST.

---

## 36. Antecubital fossa

Important anterior elbow region containing/relating to:

- distal biceps tendon;
- brachial artery;
- median nerve;
- radial nerve laterally/branching region.

Bicipital aponeurosis overlies part of the neurovascular bundle.

This is relevant to later palpation/safety, not diagnosis.

---

## 37. Ulnar nerve boundary

Ulnar nerve passes posterior to the medial epicondyle through the cubital-tunnel region.

This establishes a real neural-anatomical reason why medial/posteromedial elbow structures require separate neural consideration.

Compression/pathology → ANA-12/MED.

---

## 38. Radial nerve boundary

Radial nerve courses through posterior arm and lateral/anterior elbow region before branching.

Relevant muscular relations include:

- triceps;
- anconeus;
- brachioradialis;
- supinator;
- lateral brachialis contribution.

Detailed branch anatomy → ANA-12.

---

## 39. Musculocutaneous nerve boundary

Musculocutaneous nerve supplies:

- coracobrachialis;
- biceps;
- most of brachialis.

It continues distally as the lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve.

This cleanly links anterior-arm compartment anatomy to later neural mapping.

## 40. Normal anatomical variation

Relevant variation includes:

- accessory biceps heads;
- distal biceps footprint/aponeurosis variation;
- brachialis architecture/innervation variation;
- accessory triceps heads;
- anconeus morphology;
- PRUJ shape;
- carrying angle.

Current 2026 PRUJ CT data directly demonstrate substantial interindividual proximal radioulnar morphology variation.

Project rule:

**atlas geometry is a reference, not an individual requirement.**

## 41. Arm-shape boundary

Visible upper-arm shape depends on:

- biceps;
- brachialis;
- triceps;
- humerus;
- fascia;
- subcutaneous tissue;
- individual muscle-belly/tendon geometry.

Exercise cannot relocate anatomical origins/insertions.

Detailed architecture → ANA-11.

## 42. Arm-circumference boundary

Upper-arm circumference includes more than contractile muscle.

Therefore:
- circumference ≠ biceps size;
- circumference change ≠ direct single-muscle hypertrophy measure.

Measurement → TST.

---

## 43. “Stationary elbow” boundary

Anatomy can establish:
- joints crossed;
- muscle attachments;
- length changes.

It cannot by itself establish:
- whether elbow should remain stationary in a curl;
- whether shoulder should flex/extend during a curl;
- one best triceps shoulder position.

Those are MEC/TEC/outcome questions.

## 44. Project canonical arm/elbow model

1. Elbow region contains humeroulnar, humeroradial and PRUJ articulations.
2. Flexion/extension primarily uses humeroulnar + humeroradial motion.
3. Forearm rotation requires coordinated PRUJ + DRUJ motion.
4. Biceps = elbow flexor + supinator + shoulder-crossing biarticular muscle.
5. Brachialis = ulna-inserting elbow flexor across forearm orientations.
6. Brachioradialis = radial-nerve elbow flexor with neutral/mid-prone relevance, not an isolated “hammer-curl muscle”.
7. Triceps = principal elbow extensor; long head crosses shoulder.
8. Anconeus = small extensor/stabilizer with emerging evidence of internal compartmentalization.
9. Bone + capsule + ligament complexes + muscles jointly stabilize elbow.
10. Grip/shoulder/elbow position changes geometry but does not by anatomy alone identify the best exercise.
11. Normal PRUJ and soft-tissue anatomy is variable.

## 45. What changed versus legacy ANA-05

### Confirmed

Legacy core remains valid:

- three-articulation elbow complex;
- humeroulnar/humeroradial flexion-extension;
- PRUJ + DRUJ forearm rotation;
- collateral/annular ligament systems;
- biceps/brachialis/brachioradialis distinctions;
- triceps head distinctions;
- biarticular long heads;
- neural boundary map;
- grip orientation is not muscle isolation.

### Strengthened / updated

1. Current 2026 PRUJ literature confirms meaningful normal interindividual shape variation.
2. Current 2025 bicipital-aponeurosis work reinforces that distal biceps architecture includes a mechanically meaningful fascial continuation, not just one tendon insertion.
3. Current 2026 anconeus 3D study supports internal morphological/innervation partitioning, but functional claims remain provisional because the study is small/cadaveric.
4. Current 2025–2026 elbow references preserve the elbow as a multi-articulation, multi-stabilizer system.

### Tightened

1. Brachioradialis “neutral grip” relevance is retained without calling it isolated.
2. Brachialis is not promoted as a universal “strongest” flexor in every condition; its robust flexor role across forearm positions is sufficient.
3. No exact carrying-angle normal range is encoded.
4. No muscle-head or grip-specific hypertrophy conclusion is made from anatomy.

## 46. Established findings

### HIGH

1. Elbow complex includes humeroulnar, humeroradial and PRUJ articulations.
2. Forearm pronation/supination requires coordinated PRUJ + DRUJ motion.
3. Biceps has two heads, inserts distally on radius with bicipital-aponeurosis continuation, flexes elbow and supinates forearm, and crosses shoulder.
4. Brachialis inserts on ulna and acts as elbow flexor without direct pronation/supination role.
5. Brachioradialis is radial-nerve innervated, crosses elbow only, and acts primarily as an elbow flexor.
6. Triceps has long/lateral/medial heads; all contribute elbow extension and the long head crosses shoulder.
7. UCL/MCL, LCL complex, annular ligament, bone and dynamic muscle groups jointly stabilize elbow.
8. PRUJ morphology varies among individuals.

### MODERATE / CONTEXT-DEPENDENT

1. Exact relative contribution of elbow flexors/extensors varies with angle, forearm orientation and task.
2. Exact carrying angle and joint morphology vary across individuals.
3. Anconeus internal functional specialization is anatomically plausible but current 2026 evidence is based on a small cadaveric sample.
