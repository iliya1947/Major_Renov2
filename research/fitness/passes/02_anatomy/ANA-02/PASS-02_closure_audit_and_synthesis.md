### 2.7 Aponeurosis

**Covered sufficiently, with one clarification added at closure.**

PASS-01 correctly defined aponeurosis as broad sheet-like tendinous tissue.

Current 2024–2026 literature reinforces:
- aponeuroses are collagen-rich connective tissues linking muscle fascicles with tendon;
- they are integral to the muscle–tendon unit;
- their mechanics can differ from free tendon.

Therefore canonical synthesis must keep:
**aponeurosis ≠ simply "flat tendon" in every mechanical sense**, even though both are tendinous connective tissues.

Detailed mechanics → MEC.

No blocker.

### 2.8 Fascia

**Covered with explicit uncertainty.**

Contested:
- whether "fascia" should be formalized as one body-wide anatomical "system";
- how broad the fascia category should be.

Current 2026 critique explicitly argues against classifying fascia as a system under established nomenclature principles.

Closure rule:
**name the actual structure; do not derive whole-body causal fascial chains from generic continuity.**

No blocker because later claims can be reviewed structure-by-structure.

### 2.12 Muscle–tendon unit

**Minor wording gap identified and resolved in closure synthesis.**

PASS-01 covered:
- muscle;
- endomysium/perimysium/epimysium;
- aponeurosis;
- tendon;
- MTJ;
- force-transfer continuity;

but did not explicitly define the canonical term **muscle–tendon unit (MTU)** as its own project entry.

Current aponeurosis/MTU literature supports using MTU as the functional structural unit comprising contractile muscle and its tendinous/aponeurotic force-transmission structures.

Project definition:

**Muscle–tendon unit (MTU) = skeletal muscle plus its intramuscular connective-tissue/aponeurotic/tendinous structures forming the functional force-transmission unit between contractile tissue and skeletal attachment.**

This is a structural working definition.

It does not imply:
- one uniform series elastic element;
- identical mechanics of tendon and aponeurosis;
- one-dimensional force transmission.

Current 2026 aponeurosis mechanics literature specifically cautions against collapsing tendon and aponeurosis into one mechanically uniform element.

Gap resolved within this closure block.

## 3. Critical distinction audit

### Tendon vs ligament

PASS.

They are not interchangeable merely because both are collagen-rich connective tissues.

### MTJ vs enthesis

PASS.

- MTJ = muscle ↔ tendon;
- enthesis = tendon/ligament/connective tissue ↔ bone.

Current 2026 MTJ and enthesis reviews support this boundary.

### Tendon vs aponeurosis

PASS with refined wording.

Both are tendinous connective tissues, but geometry/loading/mechanics can differ materially.

### Bursa vs tendon sheath

PASS.

- bursa = localized sac-like gliding structure;
- sheath = tubular synovial structure around a tendon path.

### Meniscus/disc vs labrum

PASS.

Both may contain fibrocartilaginous tissue but differ in morphology/location/function.

### Capsule vs synovium

PASS.

Fibrous capsule and synovial lining are not treated as one tissue.

### Anatomy vs pathology

PASS.

No structural label is used diagnostically.
