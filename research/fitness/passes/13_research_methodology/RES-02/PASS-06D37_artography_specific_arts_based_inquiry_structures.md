# 3. Lineage

A/r/tography developed primarily in Canadian arts education, especially around the University of British Columbia.

Current UBC documentation identifies a/r/tography as an arts-based educational research form first published in **2004**.

The methodology was subsequently elaborated through:

- arts-based research;
- curriculum studies;
- teacher education;
- practice-based research;
- feminist theory;
- post-structuralism;
- hermeneutics;
- relational aesthetics;
- action research;
- rhizomatic theory.

The current 2025 Irwin/Springgay synthesis explicitly describes a/r/tography as informed by:

- feminist;
- post-structuralist;
- hermeneutic;
- postmodern

approaches to knowing.

### Project lineage representation

**arts-based educational research  
+ artist/researcher/teacher practice  
+ action/living-practice lineage  
+ relational/rhizomatic inquiry  
→ a/r/tography as practice-based living inquiry.**

# 4. Canonical scientific target

A/r/tography is not best represented as:

> use art to illustrate qualitative findings.

Its stronger scientific target is:

> inquire through the ongoing, relational entanglement of art-making, researching and pedagogical/teaching practices, allowing understanding to emerge through making, writing, embodiment, relation and becoming.

### Canonical project representation

artist / researcher / teacher-pedagogue identities/practices  
+  
living inquiry  
+  
art-making as inquiry  
+  
writing/text in relation to art  
+  
relational/embodied/material engagement  
+  
renderings as conceptual practices  
+  
rhizomatic / emergent becoming  
+  
audience participation in meaning  
+  
contextual / evocative / conceptual claims.

# 5. The name matters

The term **a/r/tography** encodes several relations.

## a/r/t

Commonly refers to:

- artist;
- researcher;
- teacher.

The slashes are not merely decorative punctuation.

They mark:

- separation;
- connection;
- tension;
- in-between space;
- movement among roles/practices.

## graphy

Points toward:

- writing;
- inscription;
- representation;
- text.

### Critical rule

The name itself signals that:

> artist + researcher + teacher are not intended as three independent boxes.

A/r/tography studies and enacts what happens **between and across** them.

---

# 6. Artist/researcher/teacher identities are constitutive — but not necessarily job titles

Current UBC language describes the identities broadly.

The 2017 sport/exercise pilot is especially important because a swimmer participant was guided to take up:

- artist;
- researcher;
- teacher

roles/perspectives despite not being an arts professional.

### Project rule

Do not require:

> professional artist credential + academic researcher job + formal teaching appointment.

Instead inspect whether the inquiry genuinely enacts the **practices/perspectives** of:

- making;
- researching/questioning;
- teaching/learning/pedagogy.

Confidence: **MODERATE-HIGH**.

---

# 7. Equality/contiguity of roles

The 2017 sport paper explicitly interprets the slash architecture as signaling co-existence among:

- artist;
- researcher;
- teacher.

The aim is not:

researcher  
→ collects participant art  
→ analyzes it from outside.

Stronger a/r/tography asks participants/researchers to move through the different practices of:

- making;
- knowing;
- doing;
- teaching/learning.

### Aristotle-related sport formulation

The sport pilot links these with:

- poiesis — making;
- theoria — knowing;
- praxis — doing.

This is a useful explanatory analogy but should not be treated as the universal foundational definition of all a/r/tography.

---

# 8. Living inquiry

**Living inquiry** is one of the most stable core concepts.

A/r/tography is not conceived as an isolated research procedure that starts when data collection begins and stops when coding ends.

Instead inquiry can live through:

- art practice;
- teaching;
- everyday relationships;
- writing;
- observation;
- making;
- reflection;
- embodied experience;
- collaborative encounter.

### Current 2025 formulation

A/r/tography enacts and embodies the practices of:

- artists;
- researchers;
- educators;
- learners

as living inquiry.

---

# 9. Process over fixed endpoint

Irwin's “Becoming A/r/tography” foregrounds:

- becoming;
- movement;
- process;
- relationality;
- invention.

The important question can shift from:

> What does this practice mean?

toward:

> What does this practice set in motion?

### Critical rule

A/r/tography does not assume that the research object and final meaning are fully predetermined before inquiry begins.

---

# 10. Rhizomatic orientation

A/r/tography frequently draws on rhizomatic language.

A rhizomatic inquiry does not necessarily proceed:

question  
→ data  
→ code  
→ theme  
→ conclusion

in one linear sequence.

Instead:

- one making event changes the next question;
- writing changes the artwork;
- an audience response changes interpretation;
- teaching changes the researcher's relation to the phenomenon;
- a material encounter opens a new path.

### Critical rule

“Rhizomatic” does not mean:

> no traceable process is needed.

Researchers should still make enough of the inquiry path inspectable for readers to understand how interpretations emerged.

---

# 11. Relationality

The 2025 Irwin/Springgay chapter explicitly places a/r/tographic research conditions in:

- relational inquiry;
- relational aesthetics;
- relational learning.

Knowledge is therefore not treated simply as:

> information extracted by an isolated individual researcher.

It emerges through relations among:

- people;
- artforms;
- text;
- material;
- place;
- pedagogy;
- audience;
- researcher identities.

---

# 12. Art-making is a way of knowing

A/r/tography treats artistic practice as more than:

- decoration;
- dissemination;
- participant engagement.

Art-making can be part of:

- questioning;
- noticing;
- analysis;
- interpretation;
- conceptual development;
- knowledge production.

### Project rule

If art appears only after conventional analysis to make the paper attractive:

→ that alone does not establish a/r/tography.

---

# 13. Artform and text must be relational

The current 2025 Irwin/Springgay synthesis makes a strong distinction:

- artform and text are both integral;
- one does not simply speak for or illustrate the other;
- they operate relationally.

Current UBC guidance similarly says art-making and writing are:

- interconnected;
- woven through one another;
- used to create additional/enhanced meanings.

### Critical rule

Avoid coding:

> interview themes + decorative painting = a/r/tography

unless the making/text relation actually participates in inquiry.

---

# 14. Visual art is not mandatory

Although early/foundational work is strongly visual, Springgay/Irwin/Kind explicitly state that a/r/tography can engage:

- music;
- dance;
- drama;
- film;
- poetry;
- fiction;
- visual art;
- mixed forms.

### Project rule

Do not define a/r/tography as:

> visual-art methodology only.

The constitutive issue is arts-based living inquiry, not one medium.

---

# 15. Embodiment and materiality

A/r/tography is grounded in the physicality of:

- making;
- creating;
- sensing;
- moving;
- working with material.

The Oxford synthesis explicitly emphasizes the physicality of making and creating.

### Sport/exercise relevance

This is one reason a/r/tography can be useful for:

- embodied sport culture;
- body norms;
- pain;
- movement;
- gendered embodiment;
- performance identity;
- exercise experience.

---

# 16. Data are not always the right vocabulary

The Oxford synthesis explicitly states that some a/r/tographers use conventional:

- interviews;
- transcripts;
- field notes;

while other a/r/tographic practices do not necessarily describe what they generate as “data.”

### Taxonomy consequence

Do not force every a/r/tography paper into:

> data collection → data analysis → findings

language if the authors explicitly work through:

- making;
- becoming;
- renderings;
- relational events;
- creative practice.

### Critical rule

This does **not** mean the study can be methodologically opaque.

The project should still extract:

- what was made/done;
- by whom;
- when;
- in relation to what;
- how interpretations changed;
- how representations were produced.

# 17. Six foundational renderings

Springgay, Irwin & Kind (2005) identify six renderings:

1. **contiguity**;
2. **living inquiry**;
3. **openings**;
4. **metaphor/metonymy**;
5. **reverberations**;
6. **excess**.

Current 2025 Irwin/Springgay writing retains these as conceptual practices.

### Critical rule

They are **not six standardized data-collection techniques**.

They are also **not a validity checklist that every study must mechanically “pass.”**

---

# 18. Rendering ≠ method

The 2025 practice-based chapter explicitly states:

> concepts rather than research methods are used to engage a/r/tographers with their inquiries.

Renderings can:

- guide attention;
- open relations;
- provoke new insights;
- orient the inquiry;
- create deeper encounters.

### Project rule

Store:

- renderings used?;
- how enacted?;

but do not code:

> method = contiguity.

---

# 19. Contiguity

Contiguity concerns adjacent/interconnected relations.

Important forms include:

- artist ↔ researcher ↔ teacher;
- art ↔ writing;
- process ↔ product;
- self ↔ other;
- teaching ↔ inquiry;
- image ↔ audience.

### Scientific function

Contiguity makes the researcher attend to what becomes possible when things are placed:

- beside;
- within;
- against;
- through

one another.

---

# 20. Living inquiry as rendering

As a rendering, living inquiry foregrounds:

- embodiment;
- continuous reflection;
- relational practice;
- ongoing questioning;
- lived engagement.

### Critical rule

The researcher's practice/life is not merely contextual background.

It can become part of the inquiry architecture.

---

# 21. Openings

Openings create:

- rupture;
- discontinuity;
- gaps;
- uncertainty;
- contradiction;
- possibility.

They resist the assumption that good analysis must always close ambiguity into one stable explanation.

### Project interpretation

An opening may be scientifically useful when it:

- exposes a hidden assumption;
- destabilizes a normalized sport practice;
- allows another interpretation;
- creates a new research question.

---

# 22. Metaphor / metonymy

A/r/tography uses metaphor/metonymy not simply as literary ornament but as ways to:

- connect experiences;
- move meaning;
- juxtapose concepts;
- reveal relations unavailable through literal explanation alone.

### Critical rule

A metaphor is not evidence of a population-level fact.

Its contribution is:

- conceptual;
- relational;
- interpretive;
- evocative.

---

# 23. Reverberations

Reverberations concern how:

- one event affects another;
- meanings echo;
- relations change;
- inquiry produces further inquiry.

### Example in sport

A swimmer's artwork about body discipline may:

- alter her interpretation of a story;
- alter the researcher's concept of “slim to win” culture;
- alter a later discussion;
- change what the audience notices.

The knowledge lies partly in these reverberating relations.

---

# 24. Excess

Excess points toward what exceeds:

- fixed categories;
- expected representation;
- easy language;
- predetermined meaning.

It can create:

- provocation;
- overflow;
- multiple readings;
- unresolved affect.

### Critical rule

Excess does not license unlimited interpretation.

Claims must remain anchored to the enacted inquiry and its materials/relations.

---

# 25. Renderings are possibilities, not universal compulsory criteria

Foundational and current writing warns against treating renderings as rigid criteria.

### Project decision

Use the six renderings as:

**methodology-coherence / conceptual-process metadata**

not as:

**mandatory six-item checklist.**

Confidence: **HIGH**.

---

# 26. A/r/tography can use conventional qualitative methods

Possible components include:

- interviews;
- transcripts;
- field notes;
- observation;
- journals;
- participant discussion;
- reflective writing.

### But

These methods do not define a/r/tography.

They must operate in relation to:

- creative practice;
- artist/researcher/teacher positionality;
- living inquiry;
- relational meaning-making.

---

# 27. Making, analysis and representation can overlap

In conventional research one might separate:

1. data generation;
2. analysis;
3. representation.

In a/r/tography these can be entangled.

Example:

painting  
→ reveals an unarticulated relation  
→ changes interpretation  
→ becomes part of the research presentation  
→ audience interaction produces further meaning.

### Taxonomy implication

Store separate process fields where possible, but permit:

> one creative act = generation + analysis + representation

when the paper explicitly demonstrates this.

---

# 28. “Findings” are not universally rejected

Some a/r/tographic traditions emphasize:

- renderings;
- encounters;
- becoming;
- provocations

rather than conventional “findings.”

However sport/leisure papers may still report:

- themes;
- overarching imaginings;
- interpretations.

### Project rule

Do not impose a prohibition:

> a/r/tography cannot have findings/themes.

Instead ask whether the final analytic form is coherent with the declared methodology.

---

# 29. Audience can participate in meaning-making

The Oxford synthesis explicitly describes interactive spaces where:

- reader;
- viewer;
- audience

can co-create meaning.

This changes the role of representation.

The final artwork/text is not always intended to deliver one closed proposition.

It may be designed to:

- evoke;
- provoke;
- invite interpretation;
- create new relations.

### Critical rule

Audience co-meaning does not mean:

> every audience interpretation is equally supported.

The work still has a situated inquiry context.

---

# 30. A/r/tography is inherently self-involving but can be social/collaborative

Current UBC guidance says a/r/tography is inherently concerned with self as:

- artist;
- researcher;
- teacher

but can also become social when communities of a/r/tographers:

- inquire together;
- act as critical friends;
- evolve research questions;
- present work collectively.

### Project rule

A/r/tography can be:

- individual;
- collaborative;
- community-based.

Collaboration is not required in every study.

- embodiment;

Ethics cannot be reduced to:

A/r/tographic work can produce:

The “t” in a/r/tography is not incidental.

Pedagogy can appear as:

- teaching others;

# 35. A/r/tography vs broad arts-based research

## Broad arts-based research (ABR)

D14 already established this as a heterogeneous umbrella including:

- visual art;
- poetry;
- drama;
- dance;
- photography;
- creative nonfiction;
- performance.

Arts can function as:

- data generation;
- analysis;
- representation;
- dissemination.

## A/r/tography

Adds a specific architecture:

- artist/researcher/teacher entanglement;
- living inquiry;
- relationality;
- renderings;
- art ↔ text integral relation;
- becoming/rhizomatic process.

### Critical rule

**all a/r/tography is arts-based; not all arts-based research is a/r/tography.**

# 36. A/r/tography vs arts-informed research

“Arts-informed” is used variably across literatures.

A common weaker use means:

> conventional research influenced by artistic forms or represented artistically.

A/r/tography is stronger when artistic practice is constitutive of inquiry itself.

### Project rule

Do not classify solely from label.

Ask:

- Does art make knowledge?
- Does artist/researcher/teacher positioning matter?
- Are renderings/living inquiry enacted?
- Is art relational to text rather than post-hoc illustration?

Confidence for a universal arts-informed boundary: **MODERATE** because terminology is heterogeneous.

---

# 37. A/r/tography vs performance ethnography / research-based theatre

Performance ethnography/research-based theatre may:

- transform ethnographic data into performance;
- analyze culture through performance;
- use theatre as representation/engagement.

### A/r/tography

does not require:

- ethnographic cultural immersion;
- theatre;
- performance as the final form.

Its parent architecture is:

artist/researcher/teacher living inquiry.

### Hybrid

A study can legitimately be:

**a/r/tography  
+ research-based theatre/performance mode.**

The 2011 literature explicitly demonstrates integration between a/r/tography and research-based theatre.

---

# 38. A/r/tography vs autoethnography

D17 established autoethnography around:

**self experience  
→ cultural/social analysis.**

A/r/tography is organized around:

**artist/researcher/teacher practice  
↔ art-making/writing  
↔ relational living inquiry.**

### Overlap

Both may include:

- researcher as participant;
- first-person writing;
- embodiment;
- creative representation;
- self-reflexivity.

### Critical distinction

If the primary epistemic engine is:

> self → culture

then autoethnography remains the stronger parent.

If the primary epistemic engine is:

> making/researching/teaching in relational living inquiry

then a/r/tography is the stronger parent.

Hybrid coding is allowed.

---

# 39. A/r/tography vs Self-Study of Practice / S-STEP

D32 established S-STEP around:

- practitioner self-in-practice;
- professional improvement;
- systematic evidence;
- interactive criticality;
- public professional knowledge.

A/r/tography can also study:

- teaching;
- practitioner self;
- reflection.

### Distinction

S-STEP is anchored in practitioner practice improvement and public professional knowledge.

A/r/tography is anchored in:

- arts practice;
- relational aesthetic inquiry;
- artist/researcher/teacher becoming;
- art-text meaning production.

### Hybrid

An educator could conduct:

**S-STEP + a/r/tographic methods/orientation**

if both architectures are explicit.

---

# 40. A/r/tography vs research-creation

Research-creation is a broader category, particularly developed in Canadian funding/arts scholarship.

SSHRC historically defines research-creation as research activity/approach that:

- forms an essential part of a creative process/artistic discipline;
- directly fosters creation of artistic/literary works;
- addresses research questions and theoretical context.

### A/r/tography

is one more specific arts-practice methodology with:

- artist/researcher/teacher identity architecture;
- living inquiry;
- renderings;
- relational art-text practice.

### Project routing rule

**research-creation = broader architecture/category**  
**a/r/tography = specific methodology that may operate within research-creation.**

Do not treat them as universal synonyms.

Confidence: **MODERATE-HIGH**.

---

# 41. A/r/tography vs Post-Qualitative Inquiry

The Oxford/current literature shows a/r/tography can engage:

- posthumanism;
- diffraction;
- new materialism;
- post-qualitative concepts.

But these are not constitutive of every a/r/tography study.

### Critical rule

Do not retroactively classify all a/r/tography as PQI.

- PQI can be an ontology/epistemology/inquiry-orientation layer.

Hybrid coding may be appropriate.

# 43. Direct sport/exercise pilot — 2017

McMahon, MacDonald & Owton explicitly introduced a/r/tography into sport/exercise research.

The pilot focused on a harmful/normalized Australian swimming body ideology often framed as:

> “slim to win.”

### Architecture

The study used:

- a swimmer participant;
- artistic creation;
- researcher facilitation;
- artist/researcher/teacher perspectives;
- reflective questioning;
- embodied interpretation;
- final reflection.

The participant could choose among media such as:

- poetry;
- photography;
- painting;
- drawing;

# 47. Non-Arts participants — methodological access problem

A common feasibility concern:

> participants may not identify as artists or teachers.

### Project implication

Formal artistic expertise is not required.

- how participants were supported;
- whether technical/artistic anxiety constrained participation;
- how much facilitation shaped the artwork;

# 52. Sport/exercise questions suited to a/r/tography

- How do athletes artistically/research-wise/pedagogically reconstruct normalized body ideals?

# 53. Bad-fit questions

A/r/tography is usually a poor parent design for:

- Does creatine increase lean mass?
- What is injury incidence?
- What predicts ACL reinjury?
- What is VO2max reliability?
- Which exercise causes more hypertrophy?
- What is diagnostic sensitivity?
- What is population prevalence?

These require other designs.

# 54. What a/r/tography can establish

Depending on implementation, it can support contextual claims about:

- embodied experience;
- identity;
- professional/pedagogical becoming;
- material relations;
- affect;
- normalized cultural assumptions;
- alternative conceptualizations;
- participant meaning;
- creative/relational transformation;
- how art-making changes understanding;
- how audiences encounter/extend meaning;
- speculative/social imaginaries.

---

# 55. What a/r/tography cannot establish by itself

It does not establish:

- prevalence;
- incidence;
- average treatment effect;
- comparative efficacy;
- diagnostic accuracy;
- prediction performance;
- biological mechanism;
- causal mediation;
- population frequency of an interpretation;
- objective future prediction.

### Critical rule

Evocative/embodied insight is epistemically valuable but is not a substitute for an epidemiologic/causal design.
