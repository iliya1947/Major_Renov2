## 1. Scoping review is a distinct evidence-synthesis purpose

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

JBI defines scoping reviews as evidence syntheses that systematically identify and map the breadth of evidence on:
- a topic;
- field;
- concept;
- issue;
often across heterogeneous source types and contexts.

They can also:
- clarify concepts/definitions;
- identify characteristics/factors;
- map methods;
- identify evidence gaps.

Project consequence:
“broad question” does not automatically mean “badly formulated systematic review”.
Sometimes the correct design is deliberately a scoping/mapping question.

---

## 2. Six JBI indications for scoping review

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

Current JBI guidance identifies six major indications:

1. identify types of available evidence;
2. clarify concepts/definitions;
3. examine how research has been conducted/reported;
4. identify key characteristics/factors related to a concept;
5. act as a precursor to a systematic review;
6. identify/analyse knowledge gaps.

### Project consequence

These are valid mapping goals.

But they are different from:
- estimating an intervention effect;
- answering a diagnostic-accuracy question;
- estimating prognosis;
- producing a trustworthy practice recommendation.

---

## 3. Scoping reviews are often hypothesis-generating

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

JBI describes scoping questions as generally broader and often hypothesis-generating, whereas focused systematic reviews are more commonly hypothesis-testing.

Project consequence:
a scoping pass can legitimately answer:
- what evidence exists?
- what definitions are used?
- what outcomes are measured?
- what research designs dominate?
- where are gaps?

It should not silently mutate into:
> which intervention is best?

without a separate focused review question.

---

## 4. PCC is the default JBI scoping framework

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

JBI recommends:

1. **P — Population**
2. **C — Concept**
3. **C — Context**

to construct scoping-review questions.

### Population

Specify relevant participant/population characteristics only as tightly as the mapping objective requires.

### Concept

The central idea being mapped.

It may contain:
- intervention;
- phenomenon;
- outcome;
- definition;
- method;
- model;
- construct.

### Context

The setting/environment/geography/cultural or service context if relevant.

Project consequence:
PCC is broader than PICO by design.

---

## 5. Not every PCC element must always be explicit

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

Current JBI 2026 guidance states that, depending on objective/question:
- one PCC element may not be required;
- another framework such as PICO/PECO may sometimes be more suitable, especially when a scoping review is deliberately structured as a precursor to a later focused systematic review.

Project rule:
PCC is the **default**, not an inflexible mandatory acronym.

---

## 6. Objective and question must align

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

JBI explicitly requires alignment:

**objective → question → PCC → eligibility**

If the objective says:
> map research methods,

but eligibility only includes:
> RCTs measuring hypertrophy,

the design is incoherent unless that restriction is justified.

Project rule:
every scoping/mapping question must state:
1. mapping objective;
2. primary question;
3. PCC;
4. source types;
5. what dimensions will be charted.

---

## 7. Eligibility comes directly from PCC

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

JBI recommends breaking each PCC component into explicit inclusion/exclusion criteria.

Project consequence:
PCC must not remain a vague title mnemonic.

For each component define:
- included;
- excluded;
- ambiguous cases;
- rationale.

---

## 8. Scoping review can include broad source types

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

Depending on objective, sources may include:
- primary studies;
- systematic reviews;
- guidelines;
- letters;
- reports;
- non-empirical evidence;
- grey literature.

### Project rule

Do not restrict source type automatically.

Instead ask:
> Which source types are capable of answering the mapping objective?

Example:
to map professional definitions/guidelines, excluding policy/guideline documents would be incoherent.

---

## 9. Broad source inclusion does not mean indiscriminate inclusion

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

JBI requires source-type eligibility to be explicit and justified.

Project consequence:
“scoping review = include everything” is false.

A broad evidence map still needs:
- defined boundaries;
- transparent exclusions;
- source-type rationale.

---

## 10. Subquestions can operationalize a broad scoping question

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

JBI permits one or more subquestions to explore:
- population attributes;
- concept dimensions;
- context;
- methodological characteristics.

Project rule:
use subquestions when the primary scoping question is necessarily broad but the map needs structured extraction.

Example:

Primary:
> What evidence exists on low-load resistance training for hypertrophy?

Subquestions:
1. which populations?
2. which load definitions?
3. which outcome measures?
4. which durations?
5. which settings?
6. which study designs?

These subquestions describe the map; they do not automatically become causal effect syntheses.

---

## 11. Scoping review is not the default way to answer “what works?”

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

JBI distinguishes scoping from focused systematic reviews.

If the real decision is:
- effectiveness;
- diagnostic accuracy;
- prognosis;
- causal risk;
- meaningfulness/feasibility with a focused synthesis,

use the appropriate systematic-review methodology rather than a scoping review merely because literature is complex.

Project rule:
choose scoping review for a **mapping objective**, not as an escape from having to formulate a focused question.

---

## 12. Evidence gaps require broad enough mapping

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

JBI notes that identifying knowledge gaps generally requires an exhaustive enough mapping of available evidence.

Project consequence:
“we found no studies in our narrow first search” is not sufficient to declare a knowledge gap.

A gap claim must be proportionate to:
- search breadth;
- source coverage;
- eligibility scope;
- mapping completeness.

---

## 13. Critical project limitation: formal JBI scoping review requires a team

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

Current JBI guidance states:
- scoping reviews are a team activity;
- at least two people should screen search results and extract included evidence;
- one-person published scoping reviews raise methodological concerns.

### Project consequence

Our current process is:
- one assistant/reviewer;
- one end user;
- no independent second screener/extractor.

Therefore internal use of:
- PCC;
- mapping logic;
- scoping-style evidence reconnaissance

is valid as a **project research method**.

But until independent duplicate review exists, we must **not label the result**:
> JBI-compliant formal scoping review

or imply publication-grade duplicate screening.

Preferred wording:
> **scoping-style evidence map / evidence reconnaissance using JBI PCC principles**

unless a true multi-reviewer process is introduced.

---

## 14. Scoping map does not automatically support practice recommendation

### Established finding
**Confidence: HIGH**

A scoping review primarily maps evidence and commonly does not perform the same formal appraisal/synthesis required for focused practice recommendations.

Project rule:
mapping evidence may:
- identify what to investigate next;
- clarify terminology;
- define research architecture;
- reveal gaps.

It cannot by itself justify:
> do exercise X because it is better.

A focused effect/safety/etc. question must follow where a recommendation is needed.

---

## 15. Where scoping logic fits this fitness project

### Appropriate uses

1. Stage 0/1 architecture mapping.
2. Mapping terminology when a field is inconsistent.
3. Mapping what outcomes/measures are used before selecting a measurement review.
4. Mapping intervention variants before a focused effectiveness question.
5. Mapping evidence gaps before deciding where deep research is worthwhile.
6. Mapping professional guidelines/policy documents by source type.

### Inappropriate substitution

Do not use scoping evidence to replace:
- effectiveness review;
- safety review;
- diagnostic/measurement validation;
- prognosis;
- causal etiology;
- decision synthesis.

## 16. Project Scoping Question Module

Use only when question type = **SCOPING / MAPPING**.

Fields:

1. Mapping objective
2. Why scoping rather than focused systematic review?
3. Population
4. Concept
5. Context
6. Is any PCC element intentionally omitted?
7. Primary scoping question
8. Subquestions
9. Source types eligible
10. Evidence dimensions to chart
11. Definitions to map
12. Methods/outcomes to map
13. Equity dimension if relevant
14. Intended downstream use:
   - terminology clarification;
   - focused review;
   - research-gap analysis;
   - framework development;
   - evidence landscape only
15. What the map **will not** be used to conclude
16. Review-team status:
   - single-reviewer reconnaissance;
   - duplicate formal review
17. Formal JBI-compliance claim permitted? YES/NO

---

## 17. Practical rules produced by PASS-09A

1. Treat scoping/mapping as a distinct question type.
2. Use PCC as default JBI structure.
3. PCC may be adapted when objective justifies it.
4. Align objective → question → PCC → eligibility → extraction.
5. Use subquestions to structure broad maps.
6. Match source types to mapping objective.
7. Broad scope does not mean undefined eligibility.
8. Do not use scoping review as default answer to “what works?”
9. Gap claims require sufficiently comprehensive mapping.
10. Keep mapping conclusions separate from practice recommendations.
11. In this project, internal one-reviewer work may use JBI PCC principles but must not be called a formal JBI-compliant scoping review.
12. Add a conditional Scoping Question Module to final modular QUESTION_CARD.
