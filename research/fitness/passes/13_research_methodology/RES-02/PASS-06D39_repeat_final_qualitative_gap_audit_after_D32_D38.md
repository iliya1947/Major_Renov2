# 4. Audit result — NEW BLOCKER 1: Video-Reflexive Ethnography

## Finding

**BLOCKING — dedicated VRE taxonomy pass required.**

VRE is not safely reducible to:

- generic ethnography;
- video ethnography;
- video elicitation;
- Participatory Video;
- reflective practice;
- generic quality-improvement workshop.

### Defining architecture

Current methodology literature repeatedly describes an iterative three-phase structure:

1. ethnographic/contextual familiarisation;
2. in-situ video recording / footage selection;
3. collaborative reflexive sessions around footage.

The process can loop back:

ethnography  
→ filming  
→ reflexive session  
→ renewed observation/filming/practice change.

### Four current core principles

The 2024 methodological feasibility paper explicitly organizes VRE around:

1. **collaboration**;
2. **care**;
3. **reflexivity**;
4. **exnovation**.

These are not captured adequately by D4 ethnography + “video used” metadata.

---

# 5. Why VRE is not just an ethnographic subtype for this taxonomy

Generic ethnography can already capture:

- field immersion;
- observation;
- culture/practice;
- fieldnotes;
- video.

But VRE adds a distinctive epistemic/practice mechanism:

> participants are shown selected recordings of their own in-situ practice and collectively work with researchers to make tacit/ordinary practice visible, question it and potentially change it.

The reflexive session is not merely:

- member checking;
- interview elicitation;
- dissemination.

It is a central knowledge-production and practice-change mechanism.

### Distinctive concept: exnovation

Exnovation focuses on:

- making ordinary/taken-for-granted competent practice visible;
- innovation-from-within;
- revealing what practitioners already accomplish but normally do not notice.

This is not generic ethnographic analysis.

---

# 6. VRE vs Participatory Video

D36 covers Participatory Video.

They overlap in:

- video;
- reflexivity;
- participation;
- collaborative viewing;
- possible action/change.

But their canonical architectures differ.

## Participatory Video

Participants become active makers/owners of video communication.

Core issues:

- participant filming;
- media literacy;
- rotating production roles;
- editing/final-cut control;
- community/external audience pathway.

## VRE

The camera captures **existing practice in situ** and footage becomes a reflexive mirror/tool.

Core issues:

- ethnographic contextualisation;
- naturalistic work/practice;
- careful clip selection;
- participant-practitioner reflexive sessions;
- exnovation;
- practice optimisation.

### Rule

PV cannot safely absorb VRE.

---

# 7. VRE vs video elicitation

Video elicitation can mean:

researcher shows any video  
→ asks participant to comment.

VRE is narrower and structurally stronger:

- in-situ ethnographic practice;
- collaborative footage selection/contextualisation;
- reflexive practitioner sessions;
- practice relation;
- iterative change/exnovation.

### Rule

A one-off stimulated-recall interview is not automatically VRE.

---

# 8. VRE vs reflective practice

Reflective practice can involve:

- practitioner reflection;
- video feedback;
- self-evaluation.

VRE adds:

- research architecture;
- ethnographic contextualisation;
- collaborative reflexivity;
- researcher-practitioner co-analysis;
- exnovation;
- explicit investigation/improvement target.

### Rule

Watching one's own performance ≠ VRE.

---

# 9. Direct rehabilitation relevance of VRE

The 2016 physiotherapy study used focused VRE with:

- final-year pre-registration physiotherapy students;

### Audit significance

VRE is not merely a hospital-management edge case.

It has direct:

---

# 10. VRE claim limits

A future pass must distinguish:

VRE can support claims about:

- tacit practice;
- interactions;
- participant/practitioner interpretation;
- reflexive learning;
- practice reconfiguration;
- local improvement processes.

VRE alone does not establish:

- treatment efficacy;
- prevalence;
- population causal effects;
- diagnostic accuracy.

# 11. NEW BLOCKER 2: Portraiture

## Finding

**BLOCKING — dedicated Portraiture taxonomy pass required.**

Portraiture is not safely reducible to:

- narrative inquiry;
- phenomenology;
- ethnography;
- case study;
- arts-based representation;
- creative nonfiction.

It deliberately blends several traditions but has its own stable architecture.

---

# 12. Foundational Portraiture architecture

Lawrence-Lightfoot & Davis identify five essential elements:

1. **context**;
2. **voice**;
3. **relationship**;
4. **emergent themes**;
5. **aesthetic whole**.

These operate across the inquiry, not only at final writing.

### Core target

Portraiture deliberately blends:

- empiricism;
- aesthetics;
- interpretation;
- narrative;
- researcher voice;
- participant perspective.

It aims to produce an authentic, credible, context-rich portrait that can speak simultaneously to:

- intellect;
- emotion;
- academic audiences;
- public audiences.

---

# 13. Why D7 Narrative Inquiry does not fully absorb Portraiture

Narrative Inquiry can capture:

- stories;
- plot;
- temporality;
- identity;
- narrative construction.

Portraiture adds a methodology-specific combination of:

- explicit context architecture;
- researcher/participant voice relation;
- relationship as constitutive;
- disciplined search for emergent themes;
- composition into an aesthetic whole;
- characteristic attention to “goodness” / strength / complexity rather than only deficit/problem.

### Rule

A narrative portrait is not automatically full Portraiture.

A dedicated pass is needed to distinguish:

1. **full Lawrence-Lightfoot Portraiture methodology**;
2. **portrait / creative-nonfiction representation inside another methodology**.

---

# 14. Why D5 Case Study does not fully absorb Portraiture

Portraiture can be:

- case-like;
- contextual;
- bounded around persons/settings.

But its final and analytic logic is not simply:

> understand a bounded case.

It explicitly integrates:

- aesthetic composition;
- researcher voice;
- relationship;
- emergent themes;
- narrative portrait.

### Rule

Case focus ≠ Portraiture.

---

# 15. Why D2 Phenomenology does not fully absorb Portraiture

Portraiture may examine:

- lived experience;
- meaning;
- perception.

But it does not require a specific phenomenological reduction or lived-experience philosophy.

The methodology can combine:

- observation;
- interview;
- documents;
- researcher impression;
- narrative construction.

### Rule

Lived experience ≠ phenomenology ≠ portraiture.

---

# 16. Why D14/D37 arts routes do not fully absorb Portraiture

D14 correctly classifies broad arts-based research as a heterogeneous non-atomic umbrella.

D37 gives a specific route for a/r/tography.

Portraiture differs from a/r/tography:

## A/r/tography

- artist/researcher/teacher practices;
- living inquiry;
- renderings;
- art↔text relation;
- becoming.

## Portraiture

- portraitist;
- context;
- voice;
- relationship;
- emergent themes;
- aesthetic whole;
- empiricism + aesthetic narrative.

### Rule

Portraiture therefore requires its own route.
