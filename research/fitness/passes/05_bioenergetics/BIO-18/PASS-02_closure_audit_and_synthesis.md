## 3. Heavy-vs-high-rep rest hierarchy audit

PASS AFTER REMOVING UNIVERSAL HIERARCHY.

Short heavy sets often create:

- high force/high-rate ATP demand;
- substantial PCr disturbance;
- high requirement for mechanical quality.

High-repetition sets often create:

- longer continuous work;
- greater cumulative glycolytic/oxidative disturbance;
- larger metabolite/ion load.

But rest requirement depends on the programming goal:

- preserve force/velocity;
- preserve repetitions/volume;
- deliberately increase density;
- deliberately challenge local endurance.

Canonical rule:

**rep range does not determine a fixed rest interval.**

No blocker.

---

## 4. Mechanical-quality vs metabolic-density goal audit

PASS.

Longer recovery may be useful when the target is:

- repeated heavy force;
- power/velocity;
- repeated quality work.

Shorter recovery may be used when the target tolerates or requires:

- higher density;
- incomplete recovery;
- cardiorespiratory/metabolic challenge.

Canonical rule:

**rest is chosen relative to the target quality, not because one rep range “belongs” to one energy system.**

No blocker.

## 23. TUT-matched vs failure-matched tempo reconciliation audit

PASS — NOT CONTRADICTORY.

PMID **35394146**:

- TUT matched;
- faster condition produced higher volume load, lactate, HR, RPE.

PMID **42319447**:

- effort/failure matched;
- faster condition produced higher volume load/HR;
- slower condition produced longer TUT, higher nVCO2 and greater deoxygenation exposure;
- total EE/RQ did not differ.

Canonical rule:

**different matching constraints can reverse which acute metric is larger; no single tempo is universally “more metabolic.”**

No blocker.

## 40. Closure decision

**BIO-18 = CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0.**

Meaning:

1. rest is part of the physiological dose of intermittent training;
2. rep range alone does not determine rest;
3. heavy and high-repetition sets differ in typical energetic/mechanical profile, but both can require substantial recovery depending on target output;
4. PCr restoration is important but not a complete recovery explanation;
5. same external load/reps after different rest can represent a different internal task;
6. rest placement can change mechanical quality even at similar total rest/work;
7. repeated sets change energetic mix because later bouts start from altered recovery state;
8. glycolytic contribution is not assumed to increase monotonically across later sets;
9. oxidative recovery support is important without implying oxidative dominance in every set;
10. exact pathway shares cannot be inferred from HR, VO2 or blood lactate alone;
11. dense circuits can become partly cardiorespiratory-limited while remaining mixed tasks;
12. circuit physiological profile depends on exercise selection, active muscle mass, load, duration and rest;
13. same rep count does not define set duration/metabolic cost;
14. tempo effects depend on matching constraints;
15. no tempo is universally “more metabolic” across all metrics;
16. acute tempo/rest/density responses do not establish chronic strength/hypertrophy superiority;
17. energy-system training is an integrated demand profile rather than pathway isolation;
18. intensity, duration and recovery are necessary but must be interpreted with mode, total exposure and current state;
19. work:rest ratio alone is incomplete;
20. BIO explains mechanisms/consequences; exact prescription remains PRO/IND;
21. mechanism→acute consequence→chronic evidence→individual decision is retained as a practical heuristic, not a validated biological algorithm.
