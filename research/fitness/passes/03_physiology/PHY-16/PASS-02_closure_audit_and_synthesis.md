## 3. Resting endocrine-state audit

PASS.

Resting endocrine regulation remains a network of interacting axes and feedback loops.

Interpretation depends on:

- circadian timing;
- fasting/feeding;
- recent exercise;
- sleep;
- energy availability;
- psychological stress;
- age/sex/reproductive context;
- assay/sample matrix.

Canonical rule:

**one resting hormone value does not define whole-body endocrine state.**

No blocker.

## 4. Catecholamine audit

PASS.

Norepinephrine/epinephrine remain acute integrative regulators supporting:

- cardiovascular response;
- substrate mobilization;
- hepatic glucose output;
- lipolysis;
- pancreatic/endocrine interaction;
- immune-cell mobilization.

Response scales with exercise stress/workload and is not a chronic adaptation score.

Canonical rule:

**catecholamine response describes acute regulation, not training quality.**

No blocker.

## 5. Cortisol audit

PASS.

Cortisol remains a context-dependent HPA-axis signal.

Interpretation requires:

- circadian phase;
- intensity/duration;
- energy availability;
- recent training;
- psychological stress;
- sampling timing.

A single cortisol value or testosterone:cortisol ratio is not retained as a universal readiness/overtraining diagnostic.

Canonical rule:

**cortisol is not a stand-alone recovery score.**

No blocker.

## 6. Insulin / contraction-mediated glucose uptake audit

PASS.

Current Physiol Rev evidence supports distinct but interacting insulin- and contraction-responsive GLUT4 pathways.

Exercise muscle glucose uptake can rise without requiring an increase in circulating insulin.

Canonical rule:

**exercise glucose uptake ≠ “more insulin.”**

No blocker.

## 7. Glucagon audit

PASS.

Glucagon remains a hepatic glucose-production/counter-regulatory hormone.

Its exercise role is interpreted with:

- insulin;
- catecholamines;
- liver glycogen;
- exercise intensity/duration;
- nutritional state.

Canonical rule:

**glucagon is part of integrated substrate regulation, not an isolated exercise signal.**

No blocker.

## 8. Testosterone audit

PASS.

The 2026 systematic review confirms that acute exercise can transiently alter testosterone and that response differs by mode, intensity, sex and sampling.

Resistance exercise generally produces larger transient increases than aerobic exercise in the included evidence.

This does not show that spike magnitude causes hypertrophy.

Current hypertrophy reviews explicitly reject acute systemic hormonal responses as meaningful hypertrophy drivers.

Canonical rule:

**acute endogenous testosterone response ≠ hypertrophy dose.**

No blocker.

## 9. Growth-hormone audit

PASS.

GH can rise strongly after metabolically demanding exercise.

Its acute response can track session metabolic/stress characteristics.

The current hypertrophy evidence does not support using the acute systemic GH spike as a surrogate for chronic muscle growth.

Canonical rule:

**large acute GH response ≠ superior hypertrophy stimulus.**

No blocker.

## 10. IGF-system audit

PASS.

Human biocompartment data demonstrate divergent regulation among:

- serum IGF-I;
- interstitial IGF-I;
- intramuscular IGF-I/signaling.

Resistance training can produce hypertrophy while these compartments behave differently.

Canonical rule:

**circulating IGF-1 cannot substitute for local muscle IGF-related signaling.**

No blocker.

## 11. Acute systemic hormones vs local adaptation audit

PASS.

Acute circulating hormone changes remain whole-body regulatory responses.

Chronic local adaptation depends on repeated tissue-specific loading and local molecular/remodeling processes.

Canonical rule:

**acute systemic endocrine response is not a validated proxy for local chronic adaptation.**

No blocker.

## 12. Chronic endocrine-adaptation audit

PASS.

There is no universal resting “trained endocrine profile.”

More reliable chronic effects include improved insulin action in appropriate populations and lower endocrine/metabolic stress at the same absolute workload as fitness improves.

Resting testosterone, GH, cortisol or IGF-1 may remain unchanged despite meaningful training adaptation.

Canonical rule:

**absence of a resting hormone change does not mean absence of adaptation.**

No blocker.

## 13. Sleep / energy / stress audit

PASS.

Low energy availability, repeated sleep restriction and non-training stress can independently alter endocrine/inflammatory background.

Current evidence supports:

- LEA-related changes in leptin, sex/thyroid/IGF-related and other metabolic signals, with heterogeneity;
- increased IL-6 and CRP after multiple nights of partial sleep restriction in pooled experimental evidence.

Canonical rule:

**training biomarkers require sleep/energy/stress context.**

No blocker.

## 14. Acute inflammatory-response audit

PASS.

Exercise can transiently increase cytokines and alter leukocyte distributions without representing pathological systemic inflammation.

Magnitude depends on:

- mode;
- intensity;
- duration;
- glycogen/energy status;
- tissue damage;
- training status;
- sampling window.

Canonical rule:

**acute exercise inflammation is a regulated response, not automatically pathology.**

No blocker.

## 15. Cytokine / myokine audit

PASS.

The 2026 endurance meta-analysis supports acute increases in multiple myokines, especially IL-6, with responses moderated by exercise and participant characteristics.

Exercise-induced IL-6 is retained as a context-dependent metabolic/immunoregulatory signal.

Canonical rule:

**a rise in IL-6 or another cytokine does not by itself establish harmful chronic inflammation.**

No blocker.

## 16. Immune-cell redistribution audit

PASS.

Current human HIIT evidence shows rapid mobilization of NK, T-cell and monocyte subsets immediately after exercise followed by return to or below baseline in blood within recovery.

The mobilized phenotypes are enriched for migratory/cytotoxic characteristics.

This supports trafficking/redistribution rather than a simplistic “immune cells disappeared” interpretation.

Canonical rule:

**postexercise lymphopenia ≠ automatic immunosuppression.**

No blocker.

## 17. Habitual exercise / immune adaptation audit

PASS WITH CERTAINTY BOUNDARY.

Exercise is a context-dependent immunomodulator.

Current umbrella evidence suggests small reductions in selected inflammatory biomarkers, most consistently some CRP/TNF-alpha effects, but:

- IL-6 findings are inconsistent;
- certainty is very low for many biomarker outcomes;
- immunosenescence-marker evidence remains incomplete.

Canonical rule:

**habitual exercise can support immune/inflammatory regulation, but “boosts immunity” is too broad and biomarker evidence should not be overclaimed.**

No blocker.

## 18. Training stress / illness-risk audit

PASS.

The athlete ARI meta-analysis found a higher pooled respiratory-illness rate in athletes than non-athlete controls, but only six eligible studies contributed and exact event counts often required estimation.

That evidence cannot isolate a single causal mechanism.

Illness susceptibility remains an integrated interaction of:

- cumulative training/competition load;
- sleep;
- energy availability;
- psychological stress;
- travel/crowding/pathogen exposure;
- recovery;
- individual susceptibility.

Canonical rule:

**one hard workout or one immune biomarker does not diagnose an “open window” or predict infection.**

No blocker.

## 19. Under-recovery / overtraining-marker audit

PASS.

No single endocrine or immune biomarker is retained as a universal diagnosis of:

- readiness;
- under-recovery;
- nonfunctional overreaching;
- overtraining syndrome.

Longitudinal interpretation belongs to REC/TST/IND and clinical diagnosis to MED.

Canonical rule:

**recovery is a multi-signal longitudinal state, not a cortisol/testosterone/cytokine number.**

No blocker.

## 20. Clinical-boundary audit

PHY-16 does not diagnose or manage:

- hypogonadism;

## 24. Closure criterion test

Normalized-tree question:

> какие systemic responses реально важны, а какие переинтерпретируются?

Result: **PASS.**

Canonical synthesis:

`exercise demand`
`→ autonomic/endocrine + pancreatic + tissue signaling`
`→ substrate availability + cardiovascular/stress regulation`
`+ immune-cell trafficking + myokine/cytokine response`
`→ transient systemic regulation`.

Chronic adaptation depends on:

`repeated tissue-specific loading + local signaling + metabolic adaptation + adequate recovery`

rather than the magnitude of one circulating hormone/cytokine spike.

The background state is modified by:

`sleep + energy availability + psychosocial stress + illness/exposure + training load`.
