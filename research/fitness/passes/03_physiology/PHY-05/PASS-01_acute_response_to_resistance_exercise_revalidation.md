## 1. Purpose / Question Card

### Decision

Build a bounded model of acute responses to a resistance-exercise bout sufficient to interpret:

- within-set fatigue;
- immediate performance decline;
- local metabolic disturbance;
- muscle blood flow / perfusion;
- transient swelling;
- systemic hormonal responses;
- acute inflammatory/myokine responses;
- neuromuscular function;
- early recovery;

without converting any acute marker into an unsupported surrogate for long-term hypertrophy or strength.

### Critical errors to prevent

1. lactate/metabolic stress = hypertrophy;
2. pump/swelling = growth mechanism;
3. GH/testosterone spike = future muscle growth;
4. cytokine increase = damage or chronic inflammation;
5. acute EMG = recruitment/adaptation proof;
6. velocity loss = a direct hypertrophy score;
7. soreness/CK = session quality;
8. stronger acute perturbation = better chronic adaptation;
9. recovery defined by one marker only;
10. postexercise return of one metric = total-system recovery.

### Question type

Acute physiology / fatigue / recovery / surrogate-boundary.

### Closure criterion for this PASS

All 10 exact Section 6 items must be covered at content level, each acute response must be assigned a valid interpretation and a non-valid inference boundary, and no project-relevant content blocker should remain before separate closure audit/synthesis.

### Source-quality note

PHY-05 is specifically about acute responses.

Therefore acute controlled studies and acute-response syntheses are relevant for describing what happens after a bout.

However, claims about chronic hypertrophy/strength require longitudinal intervention evidence. Acute markers are not promoted to surrogates merely because they are biologically plausible or statistically associated with later change in one small study.

## 3. Force production during a set

Force-producing capacity is not constant across a fatiguing set.

As repetitions accumulate, depending on the protocol:

- maximal available force can decline;
- the force achievable at a required velocity can decline;
- repetition duration can increase;
- task failure can eventually occur.

The 2025 systematic review of acute RT fatigue identifies:

- set duration;
- proximity to failure;
- total volume;
- training density

as major determinants of performance impairment.

Project rule:

**force decline within or after a set is an acute fatigue signal, not a direct measure of hypertrophic stimulus.**

---

## 4. Velocity and power decline

When maximal intended velocity is used, repetition velocity commonly declines as fatigue accumulates.

Power also declines because power depends on both force and velocity.

A 2026 leg-extension study showed progressive reductions in:

- mean/peak velocity;
- peak power;
- peak force

across increasing velocity-loss thresholds, with muscle-specific EMG-frequency changes.

Project interpretation:

**velocity loss is a useful operational marker of within-set performance fatigue when measurement conditions are standardized.**

But:

**a given velocity-loss percentage is not itself a universal biological dose or hypertrophy predictor.**

Detailed VBT programming remains PRO/TST.

## 5. Local metabolic disturbance

Resistance exercise can acutely alter the local metabolic environment through:

- phosphocreatine depletion;
- increased glycolytic flux;
- lactate accumulation;
- H⁺/acid-base perturbation;
- inorganic phosphate/ADP-related changes;
- substrate turnover.

Magnitude depends strongly on:

- load;
- repetitions;
- set duration;
- rest interval;
- volume;
- proximity to failure;
- muscle mass involved.

Dense or superset protocols often produce greater lactate and energy cost than traditional longer-rest structures.

Project rule:

**metabolic disturbance describes acute energetic demand/fatigue context; it is not a validated stand-alone surrogate for hypertrophy.**

---

## 6. Blood flow and perfusion

Active skeletal muscle requires rapid adjustment of perfusion.

Human data show:

- a single contraction can trigger rapid, intensity-dependent post-contraction hyperemia;
- flow changes are governed by interacting mechanical, metabolic, endothelial and neural mechanisms;
- contraction itself can mechanically compress vessels, so perfusion can differ during contraction vs relaxation;
- brief inter-repetition rest can increase leg blood flow and muscle oxygenation during resistance exercise.

Therefore acute resistance exercise creates a dynamic pattern:

`contraction/compression + metabolic vasodilation → between-repetition / post-contraction hyperemia → post-set recovery perfusion`

Project rule:

**blood flow is a real acute physiological response and may constrain oxygen/substrate delivery, but greater hyperemia alone does not establish greater chronic hypertrophy.**

---

## 7. Acute muscle swelling / “pump”

Muscle thickness/volume can increase immediately after resistance exercise due to acute fluid shifts and swelling.

This is a real measurable phenomenon.

But acute swelling must be separated from true tissue accretion.

A small longitudinal study found a correlation between first-session swelling and later hypertrophy, but this does not establish swelling as the causal mechanism.

A current 2025 hypertrophy review concludes that claims that cell swelling or “the pump” meaningfully drives hypertrophy are not supported by current evidence.

Project rule:

**acute swelling = acute volume/fluid phenomenon, not proof that new muscle tissue has been built.**

Do not use post-workout muscle thickness as chronic hypertrophy unless the measurement is taken after sufficient resolution of acute swelling and under standardized conditions.

---

## 8. Acute systemic hormonal response

Resistance exercise can transiently alter circulating hormones including:

- testosterone;
- growth hormone;
- cortisol;
- catecholamine-related responses;
- insulin/IGF-related milieu depending on protocol/nutrition.

A 2026 systematic review supports that resistance exercise can transiently increase testosterone, with response magnitude/time course depending on exercise and participant factors.

However, current evidence does not support using the size of transient post-exercise systemic GH/testosterone/IGF-1 spikes as a predictor of hypertrophy or strength gains.

Longitudinal work has repeatedly found no meaningful relationship between acute systemic anabolic-hormone elevations and subsequent muscle/strength gain.

Project rule:

**acute hormone response is an endocrine response to the bout, not a “muscle-growth score.”**

Important distinction:

**normal chronic endocrine status can matter physiologically; that does not validate transient post-session hormone spikes as a training surrogate.**

---

## 9. Acute inflammatory / myokine response

A resistance-exercise bout can transiently alter circulating myokines/cytokines.

The 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis found immediate increases in:

- IL-6;
- IL-1ra;
- smaller/moderate changes in IL-15 and TNF-α;

while IL-10 did not show a significant pooled immediate effect.

Interpretation must remain nuanced:

1. exercise-related IL-6 can function as a myokine/signaling molecule;
2. a transient cytokine rise is not equivalent to chronic pathological inflammation;
3. systemic blood markers do not fully describe local intramuscular inflammatory signaling;
4. greater acute cytokine response is not established as a hypertrophy surrogate.

Project rule:

**acute inflammatory signaling is part of exercise response/remodeling context, not automatically “damage” and not automatically “growth.”**

---

## 10. Acute neuromuscular-function change

After fatiguing resistance exercise, neuromuscular performance can decline through combinations of:

- contractile/peripheral impairment;
- altered excitation–contraction coupling;
- metabolite-related afferent feedback;
- changes in voluntary activation;
- altered motor-unit behavior;
- reduced force/velocity/power.

The 2025 systematic review indicates contractile function, afferent feedback and perceptual fatigue are often affected more consistently than muscle-activation measures.

Project rule:

**surface EMG amplitude alone is not a complete neuromuscular-fatigue measure.**

Detailed central/peripheral mechanisms remain NEU and PHY-10.

---

## 11. Fatigue is protocol-dependent

Acute fatigue increases especially with:

- closer proximity to failure;
- greater set duration;
- greater total volume;
- higher density / shorter rests;
- in many cases high-repetition light-load work performed near failure.

This means two sessions using the same exercise/load can produce different acute states if their:

- repetitions;
- effort;
- rest;
- set count;
- velocity-loss threshold

differ.

Canonical rule:

**acute response belongs to the complete session dose, not to the exercise name alone.**

---

## 12. Post-exercise recovery

Recovery is the time-dependent restoration of relevant functions after the bout.

Possible tracked domains include:

- maximal force;
- submaximal movement velocity;
- jump/power performance;
- soreness;
- CK;
- autonomic/perceptual state;
- metabolic/hormonal normalization.

These recover at different rates.

Direct human studies show that training to failure can produce larger acute neuromuscular impairment and slower 24–48 h recovery than matched or lower-effort protocols.

Project rule:

**there is no single universal post-workout recovery clock.**

Recovery must be defined relative to:

- the function/marker;
- the exercise/protocol;
- training status;
- time point.

---

## 13. One-marker recovery error

A session is not proven “fully recovered” because:

- lactate normalized;
- soreness is absent;
- testosterone returned to baseline;
- CK is low;
- bar velocity looks normal;
- perceived readiness is good.

Different systems can recover asynchronously.

Canonical rule:

**recovery is multidimensional and outcome-specific.**

Detailed readiness/testing rules remain TST/REC.

## 14. Acute response vs chronic adaptation

This is the central PHY-05 distinction.

### Acute response
Minutes to days after a bout:

- force decline;
- velocity/power loss;
- metabolites;
- hyperemia;
- swelling;
- transient hormones;
- cytokines/myokines;
- soreness;
- temporary performance impairment.

### Chronic adaptation
Retained change after repeated exposure:

- hypertrophy;
- strength;
- power;
- architecture;
- metabolic remodeling;
- connective-tissue adaptation.

A biologically plausible acute response can be:

- necessary but not sufficient;
- associated but noncausal;
- a fatigue cost;
- a downstream consequence;
- irrelevant to the target adaptation.

Project rule:

**acute response magnitude ≠ chronic adaptation magnitude unless predictive validity is independently established.**

## 15. Examples of surrogate failure

### 15.1 Lactate

Higher lactate can reflect:

- high glycolytic demand;
- short rests;
- dense work.

But higher lactate does not automatically mean more hypertrophy.

Superset meta-analysis shows higher lactate/energy cost with similar chronic strength/hypertrophy outcomes compared with traditional sets.

### 15.2 Growth hormone

AEL meta-analysis found greater acute GH in some comparisons without superior chronic strength or muscle CSA.

Therefore:

**higher acute GH did not translate into a clear chronic advantage.**

### 15.3 Muscle swelling

AEL meta-analysis found similar acute swelling and similar chronic CSA across conditions.

Current mechanistic review does not support swelling as an established hypertrophy driver.

### 15.4 Soreness / CK

Damage markers can rise after novel or eccentric-heavy work, while repeated-bout adaptation reduces them without eliminating training efficacy.

Therefore:

**more damage is not more gain.**

## 16. Useful acute markers — valid use cases

Acute markers can still be useful.

### Velocity / power decline
Useful for:
- quantifying within-set fatigue;
- comparing effort/density;
- VBT stopping rules.

Not valid as:
- universal hypertrophy surrogate.

### Lactate / metabolites
Useful for:
- describing glycolytic/metabolic disturbance.

Not valid as:
- stand-alone growth score.

### Blood flow / oxygenation
Useful for:
- understanding perfusion and local oxygen delivery.

Not valid as:
- direct hypertrophy predictor.

### Swelling
Useful for:
- documenting acute fluid response;
- avoiding contaminated postexercise morphology measurement.

Not valid as:
- proof of tissue growth.

### Hormones
Useful for:
- endocrine physiology of the bout.

Not valid as:
- acute “anabolic score.”

### Cytokines/myokines
Useful for:
- studying signaling/immune response.

Not valid as:
- automatic damage/growth proxy.

### Force/neuromuscular testing
Useful for:
- quantifying fatigue/recovery state.

Not valid as:
- chronic adaptation itself.

## 18. Established project-level model

A resistance-exercise bout produces a coupled but non-identical set of responses:

`mechanical work + neural drive → force/velocity decline + metabolic disturbance + perfusion changes + transient fluid/hormonal/immune responses + neuromuscular fatigue → recovery`

Repeated bouts may produce chronic adaptation.

But the inference path is not:

`bigger acute marker → bigger gain`

unless a marker has independently demonstrated predictive/causal validity for that chronic outcome.

Confidence:

- **HIGH** for acute fatigue/performance, hyperemia, metabolic and endocrine-response existence;
- **HIGH** for acute ≠ chronic distinction;
- **MODERATE-HIGH** for current conclusion that pump/systemic hormone/metabolite magnitude should not be used as stand-alone hypertrophy surrogates;
- **CONTEXT-DEPENDENT** for exact recovery time.

## 19. Training-relevant implications

1. **Within-set velocity decline can quantify fatigue, but it is not a hypertrophy score.**
2. **More lactate/metabolic stress is not automatically more growth.**
3. **Blood flow and hyperemia are real responses but not direct chronic-gain surrogates.**
4. **The pump is acute swelling, not immediate hypertrophy.**
5. **Transient GH/testosterone spikes should not drive program selection.**
6. **Acute cytokine changes are not equivalent to harmful chronic inflammation.**
7. **EMG alone does not define neuromuscular fatigue or recruitment quality.**
8. **Training closer to failure / with more volume/density usually increases acute fatigue and often slows recovery.**
9. **Recovery must be defined by the outcome being monitored.**
10. **Acute physiology can inform session-dose management without being mistaken for adaptation.**
