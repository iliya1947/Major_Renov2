## 3. Integrated model architecture
Maintain a typed, versioned set of atomic propositions, not a single immutable portrait. Use layers:
- P: relatively stable individual tendencies and developmental history, with evidence about stability and contextual range.
- S: contemporaneous reported/observed states, physiological or environmental context, distinguish affect from clinical explanation.
- G: goals, values, relative priorities, expectations, perceived and actual opportunities/constraints; preferences and intentions can change, and action may be infeasible.
- D: dyadic context and relationship-specific patterns, actor and partner associations, shared inputs, roles and independent choices. Do not infer one actor's private state from the other's report alone.
- O: observation, wording, timing, behavior and source provenance; measurement and selection are not transparent access to internal states.
- H: competing descriptive or causal hypotheses, explicitly marked as hypotheses with assumptions, scope, alternatives, revision conditions.
- F: prospective observable predictions with frozen target/horizon and adjudication (SYS-03.1).
- A: feasible decision alternatives, consequences and autonomy/safety constraints (SYS-02).
These labels are a proposed record schema, not empirically discovered latent factors. A possible sequence is person/context -> appraisal/goal accessibility -> affect/regulation -> feasible action -> later state/context. Each arrow is a candidate explanation; causal identification is a separate test (SYS-01). A prediction does not identify an intention. Distinguish goal value from effort and actual attainment; opportunity from preference; stable trait from conditional regularity; cooperation from mutual commitment; emotion from expressivity and choice. Retain alternatives such as context changes, shared stress, measurement effects and own-state persistence.

## 4. Transfer, contradiction and evidence dependence
For each proposition, record precise construct, claim type, unit/level (population, subgroup, person, within-person, dyad, episode), domain, time scope, evidence source and its limits, source-to-observation chain, applicable population/study design and measurement, relevant independent individual support, competing explanations, counterevidence, and uncertainty. A scientific group result can suggest priors or candidate mechanisms when applicable; it is not a verified individual fact. Do not borrow numerical estimates without appropriate transport assumptions. Within-person variability and between-person regularities can coexist. A null average, a trait average and a conditional individual pattern may address distinct estimands. Separate true heterogeneity from estimation noise, time-varying context and measurement error.

Classify apparent contradictions: different constructs; different actors/levels; different time windows, opportunities or contexts; different measures or reporters; sampling/estimation differences; or genuine incompatible propositions within the same scope. In the final case do not average contradictory claims into certainty: check original evidence, preserve both supported alternatives and revise the relevant hypothesis. Direct correction is a reason to reopen evidence, not automatic proof of every dependent inference. Track raw provenance separately from evidential independence. A copied record, screenshot transcription, paraphrase and later summary of one event do not create four independent events. Different measurements of one episode may provide complementary information, but conditional dependence must be considered; avoid automatic assumption of either total redundancy or independence.

Bayesian likelihood illustration (not a personal model): posterior odds(H1:H0|E1,E2) = prior odds * LR(E1) * conditional LR(E2|E1). The second multiplier uses probabilities conditional on E1 under each hypothesis. Multiplying marginal likelihood ratios requires the relevant conditional independence under both hypotheses. For a literal duplicate E2=E1, conditional LR is 1 for observed E1 in this model.

B. Version dependency exercise. Invented graph (node -> prerequisite list) {"E":[],"F":[],"P":["E"],"H":["P","F"],"Q":["H"],"D":["Q"],"R":["F"]}. E is an instrument record; F an independent observation; P a derived proposition; H a hypothesis; Q a forecast based on H; D a decision analysis using Q; R a separate proposition based on F. A correction to E requires reevaluation of E and all descendants, computed by iterative reachability: ["E","P","H","Q","D"]. F and R unaffected by this particular dependency graph. Reopening does not mean automatically reversing each claim; check remaining independent support, alternative paths and decisions under uncertainty. Previously issued forecasts remain frozen historical records; new versions can supersede them but cannot retroactively change scoring. ## 6. Reusable revision procedure
1. Define the purpose, exact proposition and appropriate scope; collect only necessary legitimate evidence.
2. Preserve source originals, observation/provenance chain, time and opportunity. Distinguish raw records, voluntary reports, interpretations, research evidence and inferred state.
3. Check construct identity, level, context and criterion. Compare stable trait, context-conditioned state, goals/preferences, constraints, shared inputs, relationship-specific process and measurement error where plausible.
4. Evaluate study relevance, design, heterogeneity, generalization targets, causal limits and independent individual support. Do not infer unobserved motives from a profile, subgroup or expressive behavior.
5. Identify dependent observations and claims; test alternatives and search for disconfirmation. Resolve genuine contradictions where evidence allows, otherwise retain uncertainty.
6. When corrected, locate earliest erroneous proposition/source, reopen its descendants, recompute affected results where possible, record revised and superseded versions, and verify that unaffected conclusions are not changed without grounds. Reevaluate decisions affected by corrections; preserve prior forecast record for honest scoring.
7. Use prospective, ordinary observable evidence and suitable baselines where feasible; no contrived interpersonal tests, covert monitoring or pressure. Keep autonomy and safety separate from predictions. Never override a person's expressed choice on a model's authority.

## 7. Internal synthetic assessment
Rubric C1 claim/level separation, C2 credible alternatives, C3 discriminating evidence or calculation, C4 inference boundary. Critical failures: unsupported motives/diagnosis, dependent evidence counted as independent, incompatible levels conflated, causal inflation, ignoring correction, forecast rewriting, privacy violation or coercion. Six self-authored neutral examples, not independent/held-out.
A. A group trait score predicts average behavior, while a participant acts differently during a particular task. C1 average versus episode; C2 situational demand or ordinary variability; C3 repeated comparable context and valid state measure; C4 no diagnosis or trait reversal from one act. 4/4.
B. An employee reports wanting to attend training but cannot access the facility. C1 goal versus action/opportunity; C2 constraint, competing obligations or preference change; C3 actual access and voluntary report over time; C4 no inferred loss of interest solely from absence. 4/4.
C. Two investigators summarize the same device log. C1 reports versus underlying event; C2 duplicate or complementary measurement; C3 trace source and conditional dependencies (toy calculation A); C4 no inflated posterior or assumed independent votes. 4/4.
D. A cross-sectional study and a within-person analysis show different signs. C1 different estimands; C2 stable heterogeneity or time/context/sampling effects; C3 matched construct and relevant longitudinal comparison; C4 no universal direction or automatic contradiction. 4/4.
E. A corrected timestamp changes a factual proposition supporting a forecast. C1 record versus derived hypothesis and issued prediction; C2 remaining independent evidence or invalidated premise; C3 dependency graph traversal and reevaluation; C4 preserve original forecast and score, no retrospective rewrite. 4/4.
F. A well-fitting model predicts that a collaborator will accept an optional role; they decline. C1 forecast vs actual autonomous choice; C2 changed preference, unavailable opportunity or erroneous model; C3 outcome under the predeclared criterion and legitimate context; C4 respect decision, no pressure or hidden assent inferred. 4/4.
