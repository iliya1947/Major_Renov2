## 3. External-vs-internal evidence conflict audit

PASS — FIELD-LEVEL CONFLICT RESOLVED CONSERVATIVELY.

### Older pooled evidence

The 2021 meta-analysis (PMID 34843301):

- performance: external > internal, Hedges g ≈ 0.264;
- retention: g ≈ 0.583;
- transfer: g ≈ 0.584.

This is substantial published evidence favoring external focus.

### Reporting-bias correction

The 2024 robust Bayesian reanalysis (PMID 39480294) used multiple plausible publication-bias models and found:

- moderate-to-strong evidence of publication bias across analyses;
- bias-adjusted mean effects near zero:
  - performance g ≈ 0.01;
  - retention g ≈ 0.15;
  - transfer g ≈ 0.09;
  - EMG g ≈ 0.06;
  - distance effect g ≈ -0.01;
- Bayes factors favored the null for each analysis;
- substantial heterogeneity remained.

Therefore the closure cannot retain:

`external focus > internal focus in general`.

Correct project rule:

**external focus is frequently useful and often supported in individual tasks, but average universal superiority is not established once reporting bias and heterogeneity are considered.**

No blocker.

## 4. Latest 2026 complex-skill countercheck

PASS — SUPPORTIVE BUT NOT FIELD-RESOLVING.

PMID 43023672 was published 2026-11-13, only days before this audit.

Design:

- n=16 healthy novice adults;
- randomized external vs internal focus;
- complex manual twirling task;
- 45 acquisition trials;
- next-day retention.

Result:

- external-focus group improved more during acquisition;
- external-focus group had higher next-day retention scores;
- technical-execution outcomes favored external focus.

This is current positive evidence for external focus.

However:

- n is very small;
- one unusual manual task;
- only two instructional groups;
- short retention horizon;
- cannot resolve publication bias across the broader literature;
- cannot establish compound-lift generalizability.

Canonical interpretation:

**current individual studies can show large task-specific external-focus benefits without proving a universal attentional law.**

No blocker.

## 5. Cue classification audit

PASS — CRITICAL METHODOLOGICAL BOUNDARY.

The 2026 network meta-analysis (PMID 42419609):

- included 87 studies;
- reclassified focus instructions under prespecified definitions;
- 78/87 studies required reclassification;
- rankings changed after recoding;
- distal external and holistic focus were favorable in the primary network;
- expertise-stratified rankings differed;
- conclusions remained sensitive to small-study effects/reporting bias.

This makes author-provided labels insufficient.

For example, cues referring to:

- implement;
- movement effect;
- distal target;
- global/holistic movement

must not be collapsed into one generic “external” category without inspecting wording and task relation.

Canonical rule:

**attentional-focus evidence must be interpreted from the actual instruction and target of attention, not the paper's label alone.**

No blocker.

## 6. External-focus distance audit

PASS — “FARTHER IS BETTER” REJECTED AS UNIVERSAL.

The 2025 distance meta-analysis (PMID 40949725):

- 20 randomized trials;
- 497 participants;
- distal external focus outperformed proximal external and internal focus on average;
- distal vs proximal difference was not significant in novices;
- expertise moderated effects.

But the 2024 robust Bayesian reanalysis estimated the average distance effect near zero after reporting-bias correction.

Therefore:

- distal external focus is a plausible option;
- expertise/task relation matter;
- universal monotonic “more distal = better” is not supported.

No blocker.

---

## 7. Expertise audit

PASS WITH MODERATOR UNCERTAINTY.

Evidence is mixed.

### 2021 meta-analysis
Skill level did not emerge as a significant moderator.

### 2025 distance meta-analysis
Expertise moderated distance effects.

### 2026 network meta-analysis
After cue reclassification:

- distal external focus ranked favorably in lower expertise tiers;
- holistic focus ranked highest in higher tiers;
- internal focus appeared detrimental in the highest tiers;
- results were sensitive to small-study/reporting-bias concerns.

Therefore expertise is retained as:

**a plausible and increasingly supported modifier, but not a fixed tier-by-tier prescription.**

No blocker.

---

## 8. Neutral/minimal cueing audit

PASS AS A CONDITION, NOT A PROCESS-PURE MECHANISM.

PMID 28286760 showed that in a nominal no-focus/control condition participants spontaneously adopted:

- internal;
- external;
- mixed focus.

Thus:

`no prescribed focus`
≠
`no focus`.

This matters because many external/internal comparisons use “control” as if it were cognitively neutral.

Project rule:

**neutral/minimal cueing means the coach does not impose an attentional target; the learner may still self-select one.**

Practical use of minimal cueing is retained when extra instruction has no diagnosed information value.

Status:

**PRACTICAL_HEURISTIC**, not universal superiority evidence.

No blocker.

## 9. Holistic focus audit

PASS AS PROMISING / UNDER-REPLICATED.

PMID 33495909:

- novice badminton short serve;
- holistic and external focus supported learning;
- holistic focus showed favorable transfer/retention comparisons.

Sport-specific network meta-analysis (PMID 39119107) also ranked holistic focus favorably in some learning outcomes.

The 2026 expertise NMA again ranked holistic focus highly in more experienced tiers.

However:

- comparatively few holistic-focus studies exist;
- task definitions differ;
- reporting-bias sensitivity remains.

Canonical rule:

**holistic focus is a legitimate third option beyond the internal/external binary, but not an established universal best strategy.**

No blocker.

## 10. Narrow-vs-broad attention audit

PASS WITH STRONG SCOPE LIMIT.

Attentional breadth is conceptually separate from attentional direction.

The attention-window review (PMID 28332919) concerns the spatial distribution of visual attention across multiple stimuli.

That literature supports a breadth construct, but it does **not** directly validate:

- broad-vs-narrow cueing for compound lifts;
- a universal “broad focus improves integrated coordination” prescription.

Therefore PASS-01's taxonomy is retained, but applied strength is limited.

Canonical rule:

**narrow/broad attention is a separate axis from internal/external focus; exact breadth prescriptions for resistance-exercise learning remain unestablished.**

No blocker because Section 14 requires the distinction, not an unsupported prescription.

---

## 11. Attentional-load audit

PASS WITH ADAPTATION-TO-SKILL BOUNDARY.

PMID 40924717 used a visual-working-memory dual task during visuomotor rotation.

Findings:

- cognitive load suppressed explicit learning;
- implicit adaptation was comparatively spared;
- motor and cognitive tasks competed for resources.

This supports a general resource-competition mechanism.

It does not establish:

- a universal “one cue only” rule;
- a maximum cue count;
- exact working-memory limits during lifting.

Canonical rule:

**avoid unnecessary simultaneous cognitive demands when deliberate task learning is required, but exact cue-load limits are not established.**

No blocker.

---

## 12. Dual-task audit

PASS — NOT A PURE AUTOMATICITY MEASURE.

PMID 38484757:

- single- vs dual-task practice did not change later attentional demands of movement-sequence representations;
- initiation demanded more attention than later movement portions.

PMID 35878952:

- both visual-spatial and motor sequence representations required attention;
- additional practice did not eliminate attentional demands.

PMID 41844636:

- trained sequence became faster;
- dual-task cost did not disappear;
- cost increased for the trained sequence under that paradigm.

Therefore dual-task cost reflects a combination of:

- attentional competition;
- sequence representation;
- task integration;
- priority;
- secondary-task characteristics.

Canonical rule:

**dual-task cost is evidence about attentional demand in that task pairing, not a universal automaticity score.**

No blocker.

## 13. Automaticity audit

PASS AND CONSISTENT WITH NEU-11.

Automaticity remains:

- graded;
- task specific;
- measure dependent.

It is not:

- zero attention;
- immunity to dual-task interference;
- an all-or-none “autonomous stage.”

A highly practiced skill may still recruit attention for:

- initiation;
- monitoring;
- adaptation to context;
- error handling.

No blocker.

## 14. Internal-focus utility audit

PASS — UNIVERSAL HARM REJECTED.

Several evidence streams support context-specific usefulness.

### Visuomotor adaptation

PMID 42261257:

- internal-focus group adapted more than external-focus group during rotated-cursor training;
- post-training explicit and implicit adaptation did not differ across groups;
- interpretation centered on task-relevant proprioceptive processing.

This is important because it demonstrates a task in which internal focus was useful.

But it remains adaptation evidence, not compound-lift technique evidence.

### Preparation vs execution

PMID 35164654:

- primary analysis found no significant group differences in acquisition, retention or transfer;
- after excluding low-adherence participants, internal-preparation/external-execution outperformed sustained internal focus at retention.

Therefore this study supports flexibility, but the benefit is adherence-sensitive and secondary-analysis dependent.

Canonical rule:

**internal focus can be useful when body/proprioceptive information is task relevant, but it is not a universal default and should be judged by the actual outcome.**

No blocker.

## 15. Resistance-training outcome audit

PASS — OUTCOME DEPENDENCE CONFIRMED.

### Acute strength

PMID 34822352:

- external focus had a small acute strength advantage over internal focus;
- long-term strength difference was not statistically significant.

### Muscular endurance

PMID 35010348:

- external > internal, d ≈ 0.58;
- external > control, d ≈ 0.42;
- internal vs control not significant.

### Bench-press throw

PMID 38281488:

- external focus increased velocity/displacement at some loads;
- effects were load dependent;
- not all mechanical outcomes changed.

Therefore external focus can be useful for acute external-performance goals.

But these are not direct delayed technique-learning outcomes.

No blocker.

## 16. Internal focus / target-muscle recruitment audit

PASS WITH EMG/ADAPTATION BOUNDARY.

### Long-term RT RCT

PMID 29533715:

- n=30 untrained men;
- 8 weeks, 3 sessions/week;
- internal target-muscle focus vs external outcome focus;
- elbow-flexor thickness: 12.4% vs 6.9%, favoring internal;
- quadriceps result did not show the same clear between-group pattern;
- strength differences were not statistically significant.

This supports outcome-specific use of internal focus for hypertrophy/recruitment.

It does not establish better technique learning.

### Acute bench press

PMID 26700744:

- selective target-muscle focus increased target-muscle EMG at 20–60% 1RM;
- effect disappeared at 80% 1RM.

PMID 33170116:

- internal focus during squat altered muscle-excitation distribution.

### Counterexamples / complexity

PMID 40372111:

- repeated internal-focus bench-press sessions did not increase pectoralis/triceps EMG or RPE.

PMID 30137029:

- both internal and external instructed focus increased some upper-body EMG compared with no instructed focus.

Thus:

**the physiological effect depends on cue, load, athlete/task and comparison condition.**

No blocker.

## 17. “Internal cue for correction” audit

PASS AS CONDITIONAL TOOL / PRACTICAL HEURISTIC.

No current evidence supports:

`use internal cues whenever technique is wrong`.

A defensible use case exists when:

1. the error diagnosis identifies missing body-position/proprioceptive information;
2. internal information is directly relevant to the target;
3. the cue produces a better task outcome;
4. it is removed/faded once no longer useful.

Internal cueing can also be appropriate when the goal itself is:

- selective muscle recruitment;
- body awareness;
- movement preparation.

But exact cue wording/frequency/fading belongs NEU-16.

Canonical rule:

**use an internal cue to solve a diagnosed information problem, not because internal focus is inherently corrective.**

No blocker.

## 18. Reinvestment / conscious-control audit

PASS — CONTEXTUAL RISK, NOT UNIVERSAL HARM.

The movement-specific reinvestment meta-analysis:

- 29 studies;
- 410 effect sizes;
- no significant overall association between reinvestment score and motor performance;
- negative associations emerged under:
  - psychological pressure;
  - object manipulation;
  - speed outcomes.

The 2025 psychophysiology systematic review (PMID 40222713):

- included 21 studies / 791 participants;
- found possible neural, autonomic and oculomotor correlates;
- emphasized methodological/task/population heterogeneity.

Therefore:

`conscious control → poor performance`

is not a universal law.

Canonical rule:

**unnecessary detailed conscious micromanagement is a contextual risk for established skills, especially under pressure/speed/object-manipulation demands, but deliberate attention can remain useful for diagnosis, preparation and correction.**

No blocker.

## 19. Excess conscious control / automatic skill audit

PASS.

The strongest project-level formulation is conditional:

Risk of disruption rises when:

- skill is already well established;
- performer shifts into detailed step-by-step control during execution;
- speed/fluidity matters;
- pressure increases self-monitoring;
- the task has strong object-control demands;
- additional cognitive demands compete with execution.

But:

- distraction is an alternative pressure mechanism;
- conscious control can also be adaptive;
- no single reinvestment measure proves causal choking mechanism in one person.

Canonical rule:

**avoid needless execution-phase micromanagement of a stable skill, but do not remove deliberate attention when the task still requires it.**

No blocker.

## 20. Direct compound-lift learning audit

PASS WITH STRONG INDIRECTNESS WARNING.

Current resistance-training attentional-focus evidence primarily measures:

- acute strength;
- endurance;
- velocity/power;
- EMG;
- hypertrophy.

The closure search did not identify a robust long-term squat/press/deadlift RCT comparing internal/external/holistic/minimal focus using:

- delayed technique retention;
- load-transfer;
- technique-error transfer;
- long-term automaticity.

Therefore:

**NEU-14 cannot prescribe one focus architecture for compound-lift learning from direct RT evidence.**

This is not a blocker because exact cue architecture belongs downstream NEU-16/TEC/COA/IND.

## 21. Measurement non-equivalence audit

PASS.

The following must remain separate:

### Prescribed focus
What cue/instruction the experimenter gives.

### Adopted focus
What participant actually attends to.

### Direction
Internal vs external.

### Distance
Proximal vs distal external.

### Breadth
Narrow vs broad/global.

### Holistic focus
Whole-movement feeling/effect; not simply “broad external.”

### Acute performance
Force, velocity, accuracy, endurance.

### Recruitment
EMG / activation pattern.

### Learning
Delayed retention / transfer.

### Automaticity
Task-specific control demand.

### Dual-task cost
Interference under a specified paired-task condition.

Project rule:

**attentional-focus claims must identify which level and which outcome were actually measured.**

## 26. Closure criterion test

Topic:

> NEU-14 — Attention and focus

Result: **PASS.**

Canonical synthesis:

Attentional focus is not one binary rule.

A defensible model is:

`task information need`
+
`learner expertise`
+
`current error/problem`
+
`performance vs learning vs recruitment goal`
+
`attentional load/context`
→
`focus strategy`.

Available strategies include:

- internal;
- external;
- proximal/distal external;
- holistic/global;
- minimal/no prescribed focus;
- switching focus across preparation/execution.

No universal law was established for:

- external > internal;
- distal > proximal;
- internal = harmful;
- no cue = neutral mind;
- holistic = always best;
- expert strategy = novice strategy;
- automatic skill = attention free;
- conscious control = always disruptive.
