## 3. Protein-turnover audit

PASS.

Canonical accounting model:

`retained hypertrophy → repeated net positive muscle-protein accretion over time`.

MPS is a necessary component of accretion, but:

- early postexercise MPS can include repair/remodeling;
- one biopsy time point does not measure cumulative growth;
- MPB is part of normal remodeling and quality control.

Canonical rule:

**acute MPS/MPB snapshots are mechanism evidence, not stand-alone hypertrophy surrogates.**

## 4. Mechanotransduction / mTORC1 audit

PASS.

Mechanotransduction remains a network involving force transmission and multiple signaling nodes.

Human rapamycin perturbation supports mTORC1 as critical for the acute exercise-induced MPS response.

But the project does not infer:

`more phosphorylation → more chronic hypertrophy`.

Canonical rule:

**mTORC1 is mechanistically important; acute pathway amplitude is not a validated program-selection metric.**

## 5. Ribosome-biogenesis audit

PASS.

Human resistance-training evidence shows rapid increases in total/ribosomal RNA and ribosome-biogenesis markers during early training, with associations to later hypertrophy.

This supports translational capacity as part of sustained growth biology.

Boundary:

**ribosome markers are not a practical prescription target or monitoring requirement.**

## 6. Satellite-cell / myonuclear audit

PASS WITH PRESERVED UNCERTAINTY.

Evidence supports:

- satellite-cell expansion and myonuclear addition with substantial hypertrophy;
- association between larger growth and greater myonuclear response;
- myonuclear addition can occur below previously proposed rigid hypertrophy thresholds.

Current evidence does not establish:

- one universal myonuclear-domain ceiling;
- satellite-cell expansion as a mandatory detectable event for every small human hypertrophic response.

No blocker.

## 7. ECM audit

PASS.

ECM remodeling remains part of the hypertrophic environment and force-transmission system.

Canonical rule:

**ECM adaptation can support remodeling but ECM turnover markers are not direct myofiber-growth surrogates.**

Detailed connective tissue → PHY-12.

## 8. Protein / amino-acid audit

PASS.

Adequate protein/amino-acid availability supports postexercise MPS and chronic lean-mass response.

Boundary:

- acute source-specific MPS differences do not automatically equal chronic hypertrophy differences;
- exact intake/source/distribution prescription remains NUT.

No blocker.

## 9. Energy-availability audit

PASS.

Evidence supports reduced lean-mass adaptation under sustained energy deficit/low energy availability.

But hypertrophy can still occur in selected deficit contexts.

Canonical rule:

**energy sufficiency facilitates hypertrophy; neither mandatory surplus nor energy-indifference is justified.**

No blocker.

## 10. Mechanical-tension audit

PASS.

Mechanical loading is central to hypertrophy, but internal fiber-level loading is not equivalent to:

- external kilograms;
- %1RM;
- EMG;
- pump/burn;
- time under tension.

Canonical rule:

**mechanical tension is a tissue-level loading concept shaped by recruitment, active force, length, fatigue and mechanics.**

No blocker.

## 11. Long-muscle-length audit

PASS WITH PRESERVED UNCERTAINTY.

Current evidence supports:

- long-length partials > short-length partials on average in the current evidence base;
- lengthened partials can produce hypertrophy similar to full ROM in trained adults;
- broader regional comparisons do not show a universal large long-length advantage;
- direct proof of serial sarcomere addition in humans remains limited.

Canonical rule:

**useful long-length loading is credible, but universal superiority/sarcomerogenesis claims are not justified.**

## 12. Volume audit

PASS.

Current 2026 meta-regression supports:

- a positive weekly set-volume relationship with hypertrophy;
- diminishing returns as volume increases.

ACSM 2026 also identifies higher volume as a hypertrophy-enhancing prescription variable.

Canonical rule:

**volume is dose-like with diminishing returns; no universal optimal/maximal weekly set number is established.**

## 13. Load / repetition-range audit

PASS.

Hypertrophy can occur across a broad load/rep spectrum when sets provide sufficient stimulus.

Heavier loading has clearer maximal-strength specificity.

Canonical rule:

**there is no uniquely anabolic repetition range.**

No blocker.

## 14. Proximity-to-failure audit

PASS.

Two closure-critical facts coexist:

1. 2024 meta-regression: muscle-size gains increased as estimated RIR decreased;
2. ACSM 2026: training to momentary muscular fatigue/failure itself did not consistently improve outcomes.

Canonical synthesis:

**sets should generally be sufficiently challenging; closer-to-failure work tends to favor hypertrophy, but literal failure is not required and exact optimal RIR is unresolved.**

No blocker.

## 15. Frequency audit

PASS.

2026 volume/frequency meta-regression found strong evidence for the hypertrophy-volume slope but frequency effects compatible with negligible independent effects.

Canonical rule:

**frequency mainly distributes weekly dose/quality/recovery; it is not a primary independent hypertrophy mechanism.**

## 16. Tempo audit

PASS.

2025 meta-analysis found similar hypertrophy for faster vs slower commonly studied repetition tempos; central pairwise differences were trivial.

ACSM 2026 similarly found time under tension did not consistently affect outcomes.

Canonical rule:

**deliberately slow tempo is not inherently more hypertrophic.**

No blocker.

## 17. ROM audit

PASS.

Current synthesis:

- full ROM remains a robust default;
- lengthened partials can produce similar hypertrophy in some trained populations;
- short-length partials are generally less favorable than full/long-length approaches;
- ROM interpretation should be based partly on loaded muscle length.

Canonical rule:

**“partial” is not one intervention class; where in the ROM the muscle is loaded matters.**

## 18. Regional hypertrophy audit

PASS.

Regional adaptation is real, but the magnitude/direction depends on muscle, exercise and ROM.

Current evidence does not justify generic “targeting” claims from anatomy or sensation alone.

No blocker.

## 19. Individual-variability audit

PASS.

Observed response variation contains:

- true biological variability;
- program/effort/adherence differences;
- nutrition/energy effects;
- measurement error.

Canonical rule:

**one short training block is insufficient to label a stable responder/nonresponder or identify a unique personal optimum.**

## 20. Hyperplasia audit

PASS.

The 2026 meta-analysis of 11 human studies found no significant increase in estimated muscle-fiber number after RT interventions up to ~6 months.

Limitations are retained:

- indirect estimation;
- difficult human measurement;
- limited longer-duration evidence.

Canonical rule:

**ordinary human RT hypertrophy should be modeled primarily as enlargement/remodeling of existing fibers, not clinically/practically meaningful hyperplasia.**

## 21. Mechanism → prescription audit

PASS.

### Mechanistically important but weak direct prescription targets
- acute mTORC1 phosphorylation;
- acute MPS peak;
- ribosome markers;
- satellite-cell count;
- myonuclear count;
- ECM biomarkers.

### Stronger practical prescription variables
- repeated sufficiently challenging loading;
- weekly volume with diminishing returns;
- sufficient effort/proximity to failure;
- usable load/rep ranges;
- protein/energy sufficiency;
- useful muscle lengths/ROM;
- recoverability/adherence.

Canonical rule:

**mechanistic importance ≠ practical controllability ≠ validated prescription target.**
