## 3. Clarifications, counterweights and inference rules

R1 — Feedback is a model hypothesis, not the default cause. Madhyastha et al. compare forms of partner influence in two samples by BIC and explicitly identify own initial state/persistence as important alternatives. A fit comparison does not prove a zero partner effect or establish a mechanism. Timmons et al. daily reports address a different temporal unit; common events, concurrent stress and conflict, reporting and selection may all affect estimates. Lagged associations cannot be extrapolated to different intervals. A stable state is not evidence of satisfaction or voluntary endorsement (Felmlee & Greenberg mathematical model); a threshold representation is not proof of a naturally occurring discontinuity (Hamaker et al.). Separate empirical evidence from assumptions.

R2 — Event-aligned dynamics and between-person well-being predictions are different questions. Dejonckheere et al.'s limited incremental value pertains to prediction/explanation of individual differences in well-being with tested summaries. It cannot rule out short-term within-person prediction or context-specific recovery dynamics. The Lapate/Heller critique and reply are theoretical interpretations of study scope, not additional empirical replications. The competing requirement to compare dynamic measures with mean/variance remains valid when incremental prediction is the target.

R3 — Individual transfer and competing panel models. The ergodicity debate distinguishes strict sufficient conditions from other conditional forms of transfer; do not infer that every group result is inapplicable, or that individual mechanisms follow from a group average. Hamaker et al. warn that stable person-level differences may distort conventional cross-lags. Lüdtke & Robitzsch indexed results give a counterexample in simulation condition A2 (RL3 estimate -.04 versus true .10); exact data-generating parameters and uncertainty not independently checked. Do not universalize model ranking from one simulation. Usami et al. directly confirm that different models can give different conclusions and encode different common factors and dynamic residuals. RI-CLPM separates certain stable components but does not automatically identify causal effects, eliminate time-varying confounders or address measurement/selection. Specify target estimand and model assumptions before evaluating coefficients.

R4 — Causal identification, selection and interference. Rohrer graphical reasoning, Robins et al. time-varying confounding and Hudgens/Halloran interference address different identification problems. An adjustment set must follow the assumed causal structure, not the significance of a correlation. Conditional exchangeability, positivity, well-defined exposure/consistency and appropriate interference and measurement conditions are necessary for the simple adjustment formula as used in SYS-01.2. Conditioning on a mediator can change the target; conditioning on a collider can induce association. Prior exposure may change a later covariate that confounds subsequent exposure; ordinary adjustment for every observed covariate need not recover a sustained-regime effect. IPW or other g-methods do not resolve unknown unmeasured confounding by default. Interference estimands require explicit joint assignments/allocation strategies and identification assumptions; partner regression coefficients are not automatically causal effects. A dyad's reciprocal behavior does not imply symmetric responsibility, and causal reasoning does not authorize experimental perturbations of people.

R5 — Timescale, sensitivity and system recovery. Kuiper/Ryan directly accessed abstract specifies bivariate stable nonoscillating first-order systems with uniform intervals as restricted conditions generally avoiding qualitative cross-lag conflicts; do not replace this with the claim that all lag coefficients reverse sign. Haslbeck/Ryan generate data from a specified toy system and assess recovery, so limitations are design- and generator-conditional. Cinelli/Hazlett's partial-R² framework is a conditional linear-regression sensitivity tool, not a measurement of true hidden confounding or a universal robustness statistic. No validated numerical robustness value for narrative evidence. Distinguish structural identification uncertainty, finite-sample uncertainty and predictive error.

R6 — Empirical incremental forecasting is task-dependent. Beck/Jackson report three target behaviors/experiences (loneliness, procrastination, studying), 104 college-age adults/5971 assessments; both person and situation features contribute for many participants with heterogeneous predictor importance. This is not proof of identified causal drivers or transfer to unrelated outcomes. Chronological implementation not fully verified from the abstract/method snippets. In the 2025 network study, three 40-day trial datasets total N=145; hyperparameters tuned on sample 1 and tested on samples 2/3 (48 and 51 participants). Direct Results show rolling next-day forecasts, with nonlinear PLRNN MAE .831/.795, improved relative to VAR in both samples and Kalman filter in sample 2. Some linear models underperformed previous-value or person-mean baselines; error increased with horizon. These are individual-history forecasts within separate evaluation cohorts, not predictions for new persons without prior measurements. Simulated node perturbations and comparisons of modeled versus empirical post-intervention changes do not identify actual psychological causal edges; the authors explicitly note direct validation difficulty. 
Hammelrath et al. 2026 indexed source confirms 242 outpatients in the project and 19,792 paired data records (not 19,792 independent people); final retained participant count not independently verified. Indexed methods clarify an outer 10% participant holdout and inner time-based final 20% split for remaining individuals. In the time-based results, mixed-effects random forest MAE .596 versus person-specific intercept .600, R² .520 vs .512, RMSE .795 vs .802. Difference .004 MAE, approximately 0.67% of the baseline; no significance, equivalence or practical superiority threshold inferred. Direct source access remains blocked. Contrasting outcomes, available predictors, data collection, clinical populations and evaluation targets prevent pooling or a universal ranking of complex versus simple approaches. Compare against relevant baseline and specify forecast horizon and actual information availability.

## 4. Mathematical consistency checks (generic, not empirical)
Recomputed original synthetic examples:
- Symmetric transition matrix diagonal .5 and off-diagonal .2: eigenvalues .7/.3; for off-diagonal .6, eigenvalues 1.1/-.1. Asymptotic stability of the fixed point under this specific linear transition requires both eigenvalue moduli below one. No welfare or mechanism inference.
- Independent fair binary A,Y and selection S=1 when either equals 1: selected cells (0,1),(1,0),(1,1), equally likely. P(Y=1|A=1,S=1)=.5 and P(Y=1|A=0,S=1)=1, versus .5/.5 unselected. Selection can induce association without an effect.
- Fixed-origin forecast for withheld values [8,8,8]: persistence [8,8,8] MAE 0; extrapolated [10,12,14] MAE 4. The example demonstrates that perfect training fit is insufficient, not that persistence always wins.
- For independent standardized Gaussian U,eA,eY, A=U+eA and Y=U+eY give Var(A)=Var(Y)=2 and Cov(A,Y)=1 with zero direct intervention effect. Independent A~N(0,2), error~N(0,1.5), Y=.5A+error yields the identical joint Gaussian covariance matrix but intervention slope .5. Same observations and predictions do not distinguish the causal models. This is an algebraic check, not reproduction of published results.

## 5. Revised operational procedure
Define precise claim, time unit, evidence and intended use; separate observable outcomes from hypothesized states. Compare persistence, contextual input, reciprocal predictive coupling and alternative causal graphs. Specify plausible confounding, mediation, selection, measurement, interference, relevant timescales and causal contrast. Assess assumptions and possible falsifiers, not model elegance alone. For prediction define horizon/criterion before outcomes and evaluate time-respecting validation with baseline, uncertainty and no information leakage. Distinguish temporal generalization within a person from new-person generalization and causal explanation. No invented individual parameters or numerical probabilities without adequate evidence. Use legitimate observations, respect choice and safety, and do not engineer perturbations, monitor people or pressure disclosure for purposes of estimating dynamics.
