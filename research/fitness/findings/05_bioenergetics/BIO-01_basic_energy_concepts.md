# BIO-01 — Basic energy concepts

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-19**  
Область: 05 — Bioenergetics / Exercise Metabolism

## 0. Core decision

Exercise bioenergetics should be built on a distinction between:

`energy transfer / transformation`
+
`thermodynamic driving force`
+
`reaction kinetics`
+
`ATP turnover`.

A practical project model is:

`chemical free energy from substrates`
→
`activated carriers and metabolic intermediates`
→
`ATP resynthesis`
→
`ATP-dependent cellular work`
→
`mechanical/transport/signaling work + heat`.

Project rule:

**ATP is a rapidly recycled activated carrier / immediate phosphoryl-transfer and free-energy intermediary, not a large long-term fuel store or a literal packet of energy.**

---

## 1. Energy transfer

Energy is conserved and converted among forms.

Metabolism:

- transfers energy;
- transforms energy;
- captures part of free-energy change in useful intermediates;
- dissipates part as heat.

Project rule:

**do not describe metabolism as creating energy.**

---

## 2. ATP as immediate intermediary

ATP is the most widely used activated carrier in cells and is a practical immediate intermediary between energy-producing metabolism and ATP-dependent cellular work.

“Energy currency” is retained as shorthand.

Preferred technical description:

**activated carrier / immediate phosphoryl-transfer and free-energy intermediary.**

ATP is not the only activated carrier in metabolism.

---

## 3. ATP is not a large fuel reserve

ATP should not be conceptualized like:

- muscle glycogen;
- liver glycogen;
- adipose triglyceride.

It is a small, rapidly recycled pool.

Project rule:

**ATP availability during exercise depends mainly on continual resynthesis, not on drawing down a large stored ATP tank.**

---

## 4. ATP pool vs ATP turnover

### ATP pool
Amount/concentration present at a given time.

### ATP turnover
Rate at which ATP is consumed and regenerated.

These are different variables.

Project rule:

**high ATP turnover can coexist with relatively stable ATP concentration.**

---

## 5. Dynamic ATP homeostasis

ATP concentration is often strongly defended during exercise.

This does not mean ATP use is low.

At the same time:

**ATP concentration is not physiologically invariant.**

Direct human evidence shows modest ATP decline under sufficiently demanding contractions.

Project rule:

**stable [ATP] does not imply low energetic demand; falling [ATP] is not required for high ATP turnover.**

---

## 6. ATP concentration vs ΔGATP

ATP concentration alone does not define the energetic state of the ATP system.

The free energy of ATP hydrolysis depends on:

- ATP;
- ADP;
- Pi;
- biochemical conditions.

Project rule:

**[ATP] and ΔGATP are related but not interchangeable.**

---

## 7. Gibbs free energy

Gibbs free-energy change (`ΔG`) describes thermodynamic driving force under the specified conditions.

Operationally:

- `ΔG < 0` → forward reaction is thermodynamically favorable;
- `ΔG > 0` → forward reaction is thermodynamically unfavorable in isolation;
- `ΔG = 0` → equilibrium.

Project rule:

**ΔG describes direction/favorability, not reaction speed.**

---

## 8. Actual ΔG vs standard ΔG°′

Actual cellular ΔG depends on:

- reaction chemistry;
- reactant/product activities or concentrations;
- temperature;
- biochemical environment.

`ΔG°′` is a biochemical standard transformed reference value.

Project rule:

**do not use ΔG°′ as though it were the exact in-vivo ΔG for every muscle state.**

No universal numeric ΔGATP value is retained.

---

## 9. Exergonic vs endergonic

### Exergonic
Negative ΔG in the stated direction/conditions.

### Endergonic
Positive ΔG in the stated direction/conditions.

Important boundaries:

- exergonic ≠ fast;
- endergonic ≠ impossible.

Endergonic processes can proceed through coupling.

---

## 10. Coupled reactions

For a coupled process, free-energy changes are additive.

If:

`ΔG_A + ΔG_B < 0`,

the overall coupled process can be thermodynamically favorable even if one component reaction is unfavorable.

Project rule:

**an unfavorable cellular process must be analyzed together with the favorable reaction/process to which it is physically coupled.**

---

## 11. ATP coupling

ATP participates in coupling through reaction pathways including phosphoryl transfer and ATPase cycles.

A more useful question than:

> “How does ATP give energy?”

is:

> “What reaction/process is coupled to ATP conversion, and what is the net free-energy change?”

Project rule:

**ATP does not donate a literal energy substance; coupling changes the thermodynamics of the overall reaction pathway.**

---

## 12. ATP hydrolysis and the bond-breaking misconception

The statement:

> “breaking ATP’s terminal phosphate bond releases energy”

is chemically misleading.

Breaking a bond requires energy.

ATP hydrolysis is favorable because the complete reaction:

`ATP + H₂O → ADP + Pi`

has a favorable net free-energy change under the stated conditions.

Project rule:

**attribute usable free energy to the net hydrolysis/coupled reaction, not to bond breaking alone.**

---

## 13. Enzyme-mediated reactions

Enzymes:

- lower activation barriers;
- accelerate reactions;
- provide specificity;
- organize metabolic pathways;
- enable reaction coupling.

They do not:

- change the equilibrium constant of an isolated reaction;
- change its standard free-energy difference;
- make an isolated positive-ΔG reaction favorable through catalysis alone.

Project rule:

**catalysis changes kinetics, not thermodynamic equilibrium.**

---

## 14. Activation energy vs ΔG

### Activation energy
Kinetic barrier that must be crossed.

### ΔG
Thermodynamic difference between states under specified conditions.

A reaction can be:

- strongly exergonic;
- yet very slow

if activation barriers are high.

Project rule:

**never infer reaction speed directly from ΔG sign.**

---

## 15. Small skeletal-muscle ATP pool

Skeletal-muscle ATP stores are small relative to exercise demand.

The project does not retain one universal numerical resting ATP concentration because direct values vary with:

- muscle;
- measurement method;
- participant;
- energetic state;
- protocol.

Project rule:

**retain the qualitative small-pool boundary rather than false numerical precision.**

---

## 16. Continuous ATP resynthesis

Sustained muscular work requires continuous ATP resynthesis because the stored ATP pool is far too small to match exercise demand by itself.

Resynthesis can involve multiple pathways concurrently.

Detailed pathway contributions belong:

- BIO-02 — phosphagen;
- BIO-03 — glycolysis;
- BIO-06 — oxidative metabolism;
- BIO-08 — energy-system interaction.

Project rule:

**energy systems are ATP-resynthesis pathways, not sequential tanks that wait for one another to empty.**

---

## 17. Canonical non-equivalences

1. Energy transfer ≠ energy creation.
2. ATP ≠ long-term fuel reserve.
3. ATP currency ≠ literal energy packet.
4. ATP pool ≠ ATP turnover.
5. Stable [ATP] ≠ low ATP use.
6. High ATP turnover ≠ required large ATP depletion.
7. [ATP] ≠ ΔGATP.
8. ΔG ≠ ΔG°′.
9. Exergonic ≠ fast.
10. Endergonic ≠ impossible.
11. Activation energy ≠ ΔG.
12. Enzyme catalysis ≠ changed equilibrium.
13. Enzyme catalysis ≠ changed standard free-energy difference.
14. Coupling ≠ violation of thermodynamics.
15. Breaking an ATP bond ≠ source of the net released free energy.
16. Small ATP pool ≠ exact universal ATP concentration.
17. Small ATP pool ≠ universal number of seconds.
18. ATP resynthesis pathways ≠ sequentially isolated energy systems.
