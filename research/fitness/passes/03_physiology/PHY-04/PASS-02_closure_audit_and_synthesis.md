## 3. Mode-definition audit

PASS.

Canonical task-level definitions:

- **concentric** → active shortening;
- **eccentric** → active lengthening;
- **isometric** → no intended gross external length/joint change.

Important nuance preserved:

**external joint behavior does not guarantee identical internal fascicle/tendon behavior.**

This prevents false equivalence between:

- joint kinematics;
- muscle-tendon-unit length;
- fascicle length.

No blocker.

## 4. Isokinetic audit

PASS.

Isokinetic exercise is retained as:

**apparatus-controlled approximately constant joint angular velocity with adaptive resistance/torque.**

It can contain:

- concentric actions;
- eccentric actions.

It is not:

- a fourth sarcomeric contraction mechanism;
- equivalent to ordinary free-weight/isoinertial movement;
- proof of constant fascicle velocity.

No blocker.

## 5. Force–velocity audit

PASS.

### Concentric
As shortening velocity increases, maximal force capability generally decreases.

### Isometric
Zero gross shortening/lengthening velocity corresponds to the isometric point of the force–velocity relationship.

### Eccentric
Active lengthening can support force above concentric and often above isometric levels, but the eccentric force–velocity curve is not a simple mirror image of the concentric branch.

Relevant modifiers retained:

- starting length;
- temperature;
- activation;
- cross-bridge state;
- passive/history-dependent contributions;
- apparatus/task.

Canonical rule:

**force–velocity is mode-dependent and context-dependent.**

No blocker.

## 6. Force–length audit

PASS.

Active/passive force depends on muscle/sarcomere length through:

- actin–myosin interaction geometry;
- passive elastic structures;
- titin-related contributions;
- muscle architecture;
- tendon compliance.

Canonical rule:

**contraction mode cannot be interpreted independently of muscle length.**

Detailed torque/moment-arm/external exercise geometry remains MEC.

No blocker.

## 7. Force-capacity audit

PASS.

Current bounded model:

`eccentric force capacity ≥ isometric force capacity > concentric force capacity`

under many matched maximal laboratory conditions.

But this is not a fixed universal ratio.

Magnitude depends on:

- velocity;
- length;
- task;
- familiarization;
- neural activation;
- training status;
- apparatus/measurement method.

Canonical rule:

**higher eccentric force capacity is a physiological property, not direct proof of superior chronic training adaptation.**

No blocker.

## 8. Metabolic-cost audit

PASS.

Eccentric work generally has:

- lower oxygen cost;
- lower ATP turnover;
- lower activation requirement per unit force;
- lower intrinsic energetic cost

than concentric work for comparable mechanical demand.

Critical boundary:

**low intrinsic eccentric cost does not guarantee low whole-session fatigue/recovery cost.**

Session cost also depends on:

- dose;
- volume;
- tempo;
- novelty;
- muscle length;
- exercise selection;
- damage.

No blocker.

## 9. Fatigue audit

PASS.

Current model separates:

1. metabolic/peripheral fatigue;
2. neural/neuromuscular fatigue;
3. immediate performance loss;
4. delayed damage-related loss.

Concentric work can be metabolically more demanding for matched output.

Eccentric work can maintain high force economically yet still produce substantial prolonged recovery cost if loading/strain/novelty are high.

Canonical rule:

**“less metabolically fatiguing” is not equivalent to “less fatiguing in every practical sense.”**

Detailed fatigue mechanisms remain PHY-10.

No blocker.

## 10. Damage / DOMS audit

PASS.

Novel/high-strain eccentric exercise can increase:

- soreness;
- strength loss;
- swelling;
- CK response;
- structural stress.

But EIMD magnitude is modified by:

- novelty;
- load;
- muscle length/strain;
- volume;
- tempo/velocity;
- training status;
- prior exposure.

The current 2026 repeated-bout meta-analysis confirms substantial protective effects on MVC, DOMS and CK after prior exposure.

Canonical rule:

**eccentric damage is exposure-dependent and trainable; DOMS is not required for effective training.**

No blocker.

## 11. Chronic strength audit

PASS.

Mode/task specificity is a robust conclusion.

Current evidence supports:

- eccentric-only training preferentially improves eccentric strength;
- isometric training preferentially improves isometric strength;
- dynamic training transfers to untrained isometric strength, but less than to dynamic strength;
- cross-mode transfer exists but is incomplete.

Canonical rule:

**“strength” must be defined by test/task/mode rather than treated as one context-free quantity.**

No blocker.

## 12. Eccentric-only vs concentric-only audit

PASS.

The 2025 meta-analysis of eccentric-only versus concentric-only isokinetic training supports greater eccentric-strength adaptation after eccentric training while concentric and isometric outcomes show substantial cross-mode transfer.

Canonical interpretation:

**mode specificity matters more than a universal eccentric-training superiority claim.**

No blocker.

## 13. Isometric-training audit

PASS.

The 2025 systematic review/meta-analysis found:

- greater isometric strength gains with isometric RT than dynamic RT;
- no clear difference in isokinetic strength between the modalities.

Canonical rule:

**isometric training is a legitimate strength method with strong test/mode specificity and partial dynamic transfer.**

No blocker.

## 14. Dynamic-to-isometric transfer audit

PASS.

The 2025 task-specificity meta-analysis found:

- large/moderate dynamic strength gains after dynamic RT;
- smaller but real transfer to untrained isometric strength.

Canonical rule:

**specificity is graded, not absolute: transfer exists, but task-matched adaptation is larger.**

No blocker.

## 15. Hypertrophy audit

PASS with preserved uncertainty.

The eccentric-versus-concentric hypertrophy meta-analysis found no statistically clear overall difference in apparently healthy adults, with high heterogeneity and very-low certainty.

The current project therefore rejects:

`eccentric > concentric`

as a universal hypertrophy law.

Canonical rule:

**eccentric and concentric training can both produce hypertrophy; mode interacts with load, length, volume, effort and protocol.**

No blocker.

## 16. Accentuated-eccentric-loading audit

PASS.

The 2026 AEL meta-analysis found:

- greater eccentric-phase stimulus and some acute responses;
- similar chronic maximal concentric/eccentric/isometric strength;
- similar muscle CSA;
- no clear superiority for fascicle length/angle.

Canonical rule:

**a larger eccentric stimulus does not automatically create superior chronic strength or muscle architecture.**

Acute metabolic stress/RPE differences are not used as chronic-efficacy surrogates.

No blocker.

## 17. Architectural-adaptation audit

PASS with preserved uncertainty.

Mode can influence:

- fascicle length;
- pennation;
- regional hypertrophy.

But available evidence is heterogeneous and loading strategy often confounds pure mode effects.

Canonical rule:

**architectural adaptation may be mode-sensitive, but no simple fixed eccentric/concentric architecture prescription rule is justified.**

No blocker.

## 18. Isometric hypertrophy / muscle-length audit

PASS.

Isometric training can produce hypertrophy.

Long-muscle-length isometric work is potentially relevant, but current evidence does not justify treating it as universally superior to full-ROM dynamic resistance training.

Canonical rule:

**muscle length matters, but contraction mode alone does not determine hypertrophy outcome.**

Exact long-length programming remains TEC/PRO after MEC.

No blocker.

## 19. SSC ownership audit

PASS.

SSC combines:

- preactivation;
- active lengthening;
- rapid transition;
- shortening;
- elastic energy storage/recovery;
- muscle-tendon interaction;
- neural/reflex contributions;
- history-dependent contractile properties.

Canonical rule:

**SSC is an integrated muscle–tendon–neural phenomenon, not merely “eccentric followed by concentric.”**

Detailed ownership:

- mechanics → MEC;
- neural control → NEU;
- exercise use → TEC/PRO.

No blocker.

## 20. Acute → chronic inference audit

PASS.

The project explicitly rejects using any single acute mode response as proof of chronic superiority:

- higher eccentric force;
- lower oxygen cost;
- higher lactate;
- higher RPE;
- greater EMG;
- greater soreness;
- higher CK;
- slower eccentric tempo

do not independently establish better hypertrophy or strength.

Canonical rule:

**acute contraction-mode physiology constrains mechanism interpretation but does not replace longitudinal training evidence.**

No blocker.

## 24. Closure criterion test

Normalized-tree question:

> чем modes различаются по force, cost, fatigue и adaptation?

Result: **PASS.**

Canonical project synthesis:

### Concentric
`active shortening → positive work → lower maximal force at higher shortening velocity → relatively higher metabolic cost per unit force/work`

### Isometric
`active force without intended gross length change → strong task/angle specificity → partial transfer`

### Eccentric
`active lengthening → negative work/energy absorption → high force capacity → low intrinsic metabolic cost per unit force → higher novelty-dependent EIMD potential`

### Isokinetic
`apparatus-controlled constant joint angular velocity` applied to dynamic concentric/eccentric actions.

Chronic adaptation rule:

**mode shapes adaptation specificity but does not create one universally superior mode for all strength, hypertrophy, fatigue and power outcomes.**

This is sufficient for downstream physiology and training-evidence interpretation.
