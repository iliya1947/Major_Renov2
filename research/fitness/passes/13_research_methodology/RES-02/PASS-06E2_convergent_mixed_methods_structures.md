# 3. Canonical definition

For RES-02:

> **Convergent mixed methods = qualitative and quantitative strands addressing related aspects of one overarching research problem, usually collected during approximately the same study phase, initially analyzed with strand-appropriate methods, then deliberately merged/compared/synthesized to generate an integrated meta-inference.**

Canonical representation:

`QUAL || QUAN`
→ separate strand analysis  
→ identify commensurable integration targets  
→ merge / compare / transform  
→ assess fit  
→ meta-inference.

---

# 4. “Concurrent” does not mean same minute/day

Methodological literature describes convergent designs as:

- concurrent;
- parallel;
- approximately simultaneous;
- occurring in a similar timeframe.

### Project rule

Exact synchrony is **not required**.

What matters is that:

- neither strand is methodologically dependent on completed results from the other in the way that defines explanatory/exploratory sequential designs.

### Critical boundary

If QUAN results are first analyzed and then used to:

- select QUAL participants;
- build interview questions;
- determine which cases to study;

the architecture may have shifted toward:

**EXPLANATORY_SEQUENTIAL**

rather than pure convergent.

---

# 5. Separate initial analysis is canonical

In classic convergent design:

1. analyze QUAN according to its quantitative parent design;
2. analyze QUAL according to its qualitative methodology;
3. merge selected findings.

### Why

Separate initial analysis preserves:

- methodological integrity;
- component-specific assumptions;
- component-specific uncertainty.

### Critical rule

Do not force qualitative material into numerical bins before it has been analyzed coherently merely to make integration easier.

---

# 6. Separate analysis is not an absolute prohibition on crossover analysis

Current methodology includes integrated/crossover procedures in which one form is transformed to facilitate direct comparison.

Examples:

- quantitizing qualitative themes;
- qualitizing quantitative patterns;
- integrated contingency tables;
- case-linked matrices.

### Project decision

Store:

- original strand analysis;
- transformation/integration operation;
- transformed representation.

Do not erase the original data type.

---

# 7. Convergent design needs an integration target

The two strands should address:

- the same construct;
- related constructs;
- complementary dimensions of one phenomenon;
- different stakeholder perspectives on one process.

### Strong examples

QUAN:

- acceptability score.

QUAL:

- participant accounts of why app features feel acceptable/unacceptable.

or:

QUAN:

- rehabilitation dose and duration.

QUAL:

- clinician accounts of organizational adaptations shaping dose/duration.

### Weak example

QUAN:

- muscle strength.

QUAL:

- unrelated views about national food policy.

No meaningful shared integration target.

---

# 8. Exact construct identity is not mandatory

Convergent design does **not** require QUAL and QUAN to measure the exact same thing.

It requires enough conceptual linkage that merging answers a meaningful integrated question.

### Example

QUAN:

- fidelity/dose/reach.

QUAL:

- implementation process/context.

These are not identical constructs.

But together they can answer:

> how and why the intervention was delivered as observed.

---

# 9. Same participants are not mandatory

Convergent designs can use:

1. same participants;
2. overlapping samples;
3. nested subset;
4. different participants from the same population;
5. different stakeholder groups / units of analysis.

### Critical rule

The relationship between samples determines **what kind of integration is defensible**.

---

# 10. Same-participant convergent design

When each participant contributes both QUAL and QUAN data, researchers can perform:

- case-by-case comparison;
- within-person joint displays;
- score–theme linkage;
- concordance/discordance analysis.

### Example structure

participant ID  
+ scale score  
+ interview theme/profile  
→ integrated case interpretation.

### Advantage

Strong individual-level commensurability.

### Risk

Do not interpret qualitative themes as statistically representative merely because every participant also completed the survey.

---

# 11. Overlapping / nested samples

A quantitative sample may be larger while a qualitative subsample is purposively selected.

This can still be convergent if:

- QUAL collection occurs in the same overall phase;
- selection is not based on completed QUAN outcomes in a way that makes the later phase explanatory-sequential;
- merging occurs at the intended level.

### Project rule

Store:

- full QUAN N;
- QUAL N;
- overlap count;
- selection logic;
- timing relative to QUAN analysis.

---

# 12. Different samples can still be convergent

Different samples are legitimate when the integration purpose is:

- compare perspectives;
- combine complementary system views;
- explain implementation at different levels.

### Example

QUAN:

- patient records.

QUAL:

- clinicians/managers.

### What is allowed

Integration at:

- domain;
- process;
- program;
- organization;
- population perspective level.

### What is not allowed

Person-level concordance such as:

> “patients with lower scores said X”

when the qualitative sample contains different people.

---

# 13. Different units of analysis

A convergent study may intentionally combine:

- patients;
- clinicians;
- managers;
- clinics;
- routine records;
- trial outcomes.

### Critical rule

Do not pretend different units are directly exchangeable.

Integration should specify the bridge:

- same implementation domain?;
- same time period?;
- same site?;
- same intervention component?;
- same conceptual construct?

---

# 14. Equal sample sizes are not required

Qualitative and quantitative strands have different adequacy logics.

Therefore:

- QUAN may have large N;
- QUAL may have smaller purposive N.

### Critical rule

Equal N is **not** a requirement of convergent design.

But large imbalance can constrain the type of integration.

### Project rule

Record:

- sample-size asymmetry;
- reason;
- whether integration occurs at case or aggregate level.

---

# 15. Equal priority is not required

“Parallel” does not mean equally weighted.

Convergent studies may be:

- QUAN + QUAL equal;
- QUAN-dominant;
- QUAL-dominant.

### Project rule

Priority is based on:

- research aims;
- conceptual role;
- analysis/inference;

not sample size alone.

---

# 16. Merging is the core convergent integration mechanism

Fetters/Curry/Creswell distinguish **merging** from:

- connecting;
- building;
- embedding.

Merging means:

> bringing QUAL and QUAN findings/data into direct analytic relation.

### Typical convergent flow

parallel strands  
→ strand-specific findings  
→ merge  
→ integrated interpretation.

---

# 17. Results-level merging

The most common practical form is:

QUAN result  
↔ QUAL finding/theme  
→ integrated interpretation.

Examples:

- high quantitative usability + qualitative praise of content;
- low engagement score + qualitative complaints about notification failures;
- shorter rehabilitation duration over time + clinician accounts of restructuring.

### Critical rule

Results-level merging must produce more than repetition.

---

# 18. Narrative integration

Convergent results can be integrated in prose.

A strong narrative integration:

- explicitly pairs related QUAL/QUAN findings;
- states relationship type;
- gives integrated interpretation.

A weak narrative integration:

- reports quantitative results;
- then reports qualitative findings;
- never explains their relationship.

### Project rule

Store:

**narrative integration = yes/no + structure.**

---

# 19. Weaving

Narrative **weaving** integrates by topic/domain.

Example:

### Engagement

- quantitative engagement score;
- qualitative feedback on reminders;
- integrated inference.

Then:

### Information

- quantitative information score;
- qualitative feedback on content;
- integrated inference.

### Critical rule

Weaving by domain is stronger integration than separate QUAN chapter + QUAL chapter with one concluding sentence.

---

# 20. Joint displays

Joint displays visually organize mixed findings in one analytic structure.

Common convergent structure:

| QUAN result | QUAL finding | fit | meta-inference |

### Scientific role

A joint display can support:

- analysis;
- comparison;
- divergence detection;
- meta-inference generation.

It is not merely presentation.

---

# 21. Joint displays can be formative and summative

Current 2026 work shows joint displays can be used:

- during iterative analysis;
- at final reporting.

### Formative display

Helps researchers discover:

- missing comparisons;
- contradictions;
- clusters;
- case patterns.

### Summative display

Communicates final integrated interpretation.

### Critical rule

Joint display use is **not mandatory** for convergent validity.

---

# 22. Meta-inference column

A useful joint-display structure adds an explicit:

**meta-inference**

column.

This forces the team to state:

> what new conclusion emerges from the combination?

### Critical rule

If the final column merely repeats both strands, integration is weak.

---

# 23. Case-by-case integration

When data can be linked by participant/case, one can compare:

- quantitative profile;
- qualitative profile;
- integrated case explanation.

### Suitable for

- same participants;
- repeated linked observations;
- case-study mixed methods.

### Current methodological example

Tripartite Analysis uses:

1. case-by-case integrated analysis;
2. separate whole-dataset analyses then merging;
3. comparison/integration of both levels.

---

# 24. Case-level and aggregate-level results can differ

Example:

aggregate QUAN:

- mean acceptability high.

case-level:

- subgroup with low scores describes accessibility barriers.

### Integrated value

Mixed methods can reveal heterogeneity hidden by group means.

### Critical rule

Do not let aggregate convergence erase important case-level discordance.

---

# 25. Data transformation

Convergent integration may transform one data type.

## Quantitizing

QUAL → numerical/categorical representation.

## Qualitizing

QUAN → textual/categories/narrative representation.

### Purpose

Create a more directly comparable analytic form.

---

# 26. Quantitizing qualitative material

Possible procedure:

1. conduct valid qualitative analysis;
2. define theme/category;
3. code presence/absence or another explicit rule;
4. create quantitative representation;
5. compare with QUAN variables.

### Risk

Quantitizing can erase:

- context;
- intensity;
- ambivalence;
- meaning;
- contradictory positions.

### Project rule

Retain both:

- original QUAL interpretation;
- transformed variable.

---

# 27. Direct heart-failure exercise example

Scheer et al. 2026 used:

- qualitative baseline exercise-outcome expectations;
- content analysis generating five themes;
- transformation for integration;
- chi-square tests relating themes to 6- and 12-month exercise adherence.

### Taxonomy lesson

This is a clear **convergent + data-transformation/crossover integration** example.

### Claim limit

A statistical association between transformed themes and adherence does not establish:

- causal effect of expectations;
- mediation;
- long-term mechanism.

The authors themselves concluded long-term influence was unclear.

---

# 28. Qualitizing quantitative results

Quantitative patterns can be translated into:

- profile categories;
- descriptive narratives;
- case types.

### Example

Instead of a raw score:

- “high usability / low engagement profile.”

This may then be compared with qualitative accounts.

### Risk

Thresholds can be arbitrary.

### Project rule

Record exact transformation rule.

---

# 29. Data transformation is not required

Convergent mixed methods can integrate without transformation through:

- narrative weaving;
- joint displays;
- case matching;
- domain-level comparison.

### Critical rule

Do not quantitize rich qualitative data only because numbers feel easier to merge.

---

# 30. Fit of integration

Terminology is not universally standardized.

For project routing, preserve author terminology but map it to four practical fit categories.

## 1. Confirmation / convergence

Strands support the same broad conclusion.

## 2. Complementarity

Strands give different but nonconflicting information.

## 3. Expansion

One strand extends the scope/meaning of the other.

## 4. Discordance

Strands conflict materially.

---

# 31. Confirmation/convergence

Example:

QUAN:

- users rate information quality high.

QUAL:

- users praise clear SCI-specific exercise instructions.

### Meta-inference

Both support:

> information content is a relative app strength.

### Critical rule

Convergence does not make a biased measurement true.

Two biased methods can agree.

---

# 32. Complementarity

Example:

QUAN:

- 67% rate app feasible.

QUAL:

- users explain which specific technical features support or impair feasibility.

### Meta-inference

QUAL adds mechanism/context not contained in the percentage.

---

# 33. Expansion

Example:

QUAN:

- overall usability appears acceptable.

QUAL:

- participants identify future features not measured by the scale.

### Meta-inference

The combined conclusion extends beyond the original quantitative construct.

---

# 34. Discordance

Example:

QUAN:

- high satisfaction.

QUAL:

- repeated accounts of frustration.

### Correct response

Investigate the discrepancy.

### Incorrect response

Choose whichever strand supports the preferred conclusion.

---

# 35. Discordance diagnosis

Potential sources include:

1. different constructs;
2. different samples;
3. different units of analysis;
4. different time windows;
5. measurement ceiling/floor;
6. low quantitative power;
7. qualitative selection bias;
8. social desirability;
9. missing data;
10. scale content not matching participant priorities;
11. subgroup heterogeneity;
12. different context during collection;
13. transformation artifacts;
14. analytic errors.

### Project rule

Discordance should trigger **diagnosis**, not averaging.

---

# 36. Discordance may narrow the claim

Example:

QUAN:

- average app acceptability high.

QUAL:

- wheelchair users with specific accessibility needs report severe barriers.

### Better claim

> overall acceptability was favorable, but important subgroup-specific barriers remain.

### Worse claim

> the app is acceptable.

---

# 37. Discordance can generate a new hypothesis

Mixed-method divergence can identify:

- missing variable;
- subgroup interaction;
- mechanism hypothesis;
- measurement problem;
- implementation failure.

### Critical rule

Hypothesis generation is not hypothesis confirmation.

---

# 38. Convergent design vs triangulation

Historically, convergent designs are closely linked to triangulation logic.

But “triangulation” is broader.

It can mean:

- multiple qualitative sources;
- multiple observers;
- multiple methods within one paradigm;
- theory triangulation.

### Project rule

Triangulation alone ≠ mixed methods.

For mixed-methods classification, both:

- QUAL;
- QUAN;

and intentional integration are required.

---

# 39. Convergent design vs parallel multimethod study

## Parallel multimethod

Multiple methods run alongside each other.

May have:

- no integrated research question;
- no common comparison structure;
- no meta-inference.

## Convergent mixed methods

Requires:

- linked research problem;
- explicit merging/comparison;
- integrated inference.

### Critical rule

Side-by-side reporting is not sufficient.

---

# 40. Convergent design vs explanatory sequential

If:

1. QUAN is completed/analyzed;
2. QUAN results determine QUAL sampling/questions;
3. QUAL explains QUAN;

→ explanatory sequential.

If:

1. QUAL and QUAN are planned/collected in approximately same phase;
2. neither depends on completed other-strand results;
3. findings later merge;

→ convergent.

---

# 41. Convergent design vs embedded mixed methods

Convergent:

- strands are brought together as complementary evidence streams.

Embedded:

- one strand is nested inside a dominant design/purpose.

### Overlap

A study can have:

- embedded component;
- convergent integration at one stage.

### Project rule

Store:

- strand structural relationship;
- integration pattern

separately.

---

# 42. Direct exercise exemplar — community HIMT RCT

The 2026 European Journal of Sport Science study used:

- two-arm parallel randomized controlled trial;
- convergent mixed-methods evaluation;
- recreationally active adults;
- quantitative outcomes + perceptual/qualitative data.

### Taxonomy representation

quantitative parent:
- parallel RCT.

mixed-method architecture:
- convergent.

### Critical rule

The RCT provides randomized group contrast.

Convergent integration provides:

- feasibility;
- acceptability;
- perceptual/contextual interpretation.

Do not collapse them.

---

# 43. Direct rehabilitation exemplar — spinal cord injury app

Nataletti et al. 2026:

- n=12 individuals with SCI completed the trial;
- standardized AIM/IAM/FIM/uMARS measures;
- focus-group qualitative feedback;
- convergent parallel design.

### Integrated findings

Quantitative results suggested:

- generally favorable acceptability/feasibility;
- comparatively lower engagement.

QUAL findings identified:

- customization/support strengths;
- notification/EMA/technical problems;
- requests for more engaging features.

### Taxonomy lesson

This is a strong same-cohort domain-level convergent example.

---

# 44. Same-cohort does not guarantee case-by-case integration

Even if all participants provide both strands, authors may integrate only at:

- theme/domain level.

### Project rule

Store separately:

1. sample overlap;
2. integration granularity.

Possible granularity:

- person-level;
- subgroup-level;
- construct/domain-level;
- whole-study level.

---

# 45. Direct rehabilitation exemplar — long COVID process evaluation

Nielsen et al. 2026:

QUAN:

- baseline questionnaires;
- routine rehabilitation records;
- patient-level reach/dose/duration.

QUAL:

- health-professional focus group;
- manager interview.

Analyses:

- descriptive quantitative;
- content analysis qualitative.

Integration:

- narrative merging.

### Key integrated inference

Temporal reductions in:

- rehabilitation length;
- number of sessions

were interpreted alongside qualitative evidence about:

- adaptation;
- increasing referrals/workload;
- role clarification;
- increasing knowledge;
- greater structure.

### Taxonomy lesson

This is a strong example of convergent integration across **different participant/unit types**.

---

# 46. Long COVID exemplar — claim boundary

Because patients supply most QUAN data while professionals/manager supply QUAL data:

the study can support:

- program-level implementation interpretation.

It cannot support person-level statements such as:

> a specific patient received fewer sessions because that patient personally experienced X

unless linked evidence exists.

---

# 47. Direct exercise adherence exemplar — heart failure

Current 2026 Heart & Lung study:

- parent RCT dataset;
- baseline open-ended expectation data;
- qualitative content analysis;
- themes transformed for integration;
- adherence measured at 6/12 months;
- chi-square integration.

### Result

Some baseline expectation categories were associated with 6-month adherence.

No significant relationships at 12 months.

### Taxonomy lesson

Convergent mixed methods can operate through:

**data transformation + statistical integration**

rather than only narrative merging.

# 48. Current PCORI quality implications

PCORI 2026 MM standards require:

1. justification for mixed methods;
2. justified sampling strategy;
3. awareness of biases unique to each data method;
4. explicit integration plan;
5. identification and interpretation of conflicting/divergent findings.

### Project implication

A convergent paper that never states:

- what is merged;
- when;
- how;
- what happened when strands disagreed

has a serious mixed-methods reporting/coherence gap.

---

# 49. Strand-specific quality remains mandatory

## QUAN strand

Assess according to its parent design:

- randomization/confounding;
- measurement validity;
- missing data;
- statistical model;
- sample-size adequacy.

## QUAL strand

Assess according to its methodology:

- sampling/adequacy;
- reflexivity;
- analysis coherence;
- trustworthiness;
- representation.

### Critical rule

Convergence cannot compensate for low-quality strands.

---

# 50. Integration quality

For convergent studies, ask:

1. Is the integration target explicit?
2. Are strands conceptually commensurable?
3. Is sample relationship explicit?
4. Is integration granularity appropriate?
5. Is merging procedure described?
6. Are original strand findings retained?
7. Is fit classified transparently?
8. Is discordance investigated?
9. Is a true meta-inference generated?
10. Is added value of mixing demonstrated?

---

# 51. Common failure mode — token qualitative quotes

Example:

- RCT result;
- one participant quote in Discussion.

### Problem

A quote can illustrate a quantitative result without constituting:

- qualitative methodology;
- systematic qualitative analysis;
- mixed-method integration.

### Rule

Do not classify mixed methods from decorative quotes.

---

# 52. Common failure mode — open-ended survey comment box

Open-text responses can support a qualitative strand if:

- systematically sampled/collected;
- analyzed qualitatively;
- integrated meaningfully.

But:

one optional comment field  
+ a few examples

is not automatically a qualitative strand.

---

# 53. Common failure mode — “two result sections”

Structure:

1. Quantitative Results.
2. Qualitative Results.
3. General Discussion.

If no explicit linkage is performed:

this may be **parallel multimethod**, not a strong convergent mixed-methods design.

---

# 54. Common failure mode — forcing consensus

A study may assume convergence is the goal and suppress:

- contradictions;
- minority cases;
- qualitative criticism of scale content.

### Critical rule

Convergent design must allow:

**discordance as legitimate output.**

---

# 55. Common failure mode — p-value as arbiter of qualitative truth

Example:

- qualitative theme says barrier X matters;
- quantitative association p>0.05;
- authors conclude theme is false.

### Problem

The two strands may differ in:

- construct;
- sample;
- power;
- timing.

### Rule

Non-significance does not automatically invalidate qualitative evidence.

---

# 56. Common failure mode — theme frequency = prevalence

Quantitized themes from a purposive qualitative sample do not automatically estimate population prevalence.

### Critical rule

If a theme is present in 8/12 interviews:

that is not a population prevalence estimate unless sampling/inference justify it.

---

# 57. Common failure mode — person-level claim from population-level merge

If QUAL and QUAN samples differ:

do not make:

- individual concordance;
- individual explanation;
- mediation-like claim.

Integration must stay at the common unit/domain level.

---

# 58. Common failure mode — transform then forget context

After quantizing a theme, authors may use only:

- 0/1 indicator

and discard:

- why;
- how;
- ambiguity;
- context.

### Project rule

Transformation should be traceable back to original qualitative meaning.

---

# 59. Common failure mode — sample mismatch hidden

A paper can state:

> findings converged

without noting:

- QUAN sample = patients;
- QUAL sample = clinicians.

### Better wording

> patient-level delivery metrics and clinician accounts produced complementary program-level interpretations.

### Project rule

Integration fit must be described at the correct unit.

---

# 60. Convergent claim limits

Convergent mixed methods can strengthen:

- contextual understanding;
- construct interpretation;
- implementation explanation;
- usability/acceptability interpretation;
- detection of measurement mismatch;
- integrated case understanding;
- hypothesis generation.

It does **not automatically strengthen**:

- causal identification;
- generalizability;
- statistical precision;
- prevalence estimation.

### Critical rule

Mixed-methods richness ≠ higher causal certainty.
