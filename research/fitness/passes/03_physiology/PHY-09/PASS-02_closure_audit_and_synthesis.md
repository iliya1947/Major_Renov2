## 3. Power-definition audit

PASS.

Mechanical power requires both force and velocity:

`P = F × v`

or rotationally:

`P = torque × angular velocity`.

Canonical rule:

**maximal force, maximal velocity and maximal power are separate constructs.**

No blocker.

## 4. Measurement-boundary audit

PASS.

Reported power depends on:

- measured system;
- force estimation/measurement;
- external-load vs body/system mass;
- movement phase;
- sampling/filtering;
- exercise technique;
- device/software.

The 2026 methodological review specifically questions the physiological specificity of mechanical-power estimates from complex multijoint lifts.

Canonical rule:

**a watt value is only interpretable with the exercise, measured system, phase and measurement method stated.**

No blocker.

## 5. RFD audit

PASS.

RFD remains:

`ΔF / Δt`

rather than mechanical power.

Current time-window model is retained:

- earlier RFD → greater contribution from rapid neural activation;
- later RFD → increasing contribution from maximal force/muscle/MTU properties.

Canonical rule:

**RFD, maximal force and power overlap physiologically but are not interchangeable outcomes.**

No blocker.

## 6. Peak-vs-mean-power audit

PASS.

Peak power captures the maximum instantaneous/short-window expression.

Mean power averages across a defined phase/time/repetition interval.

Canonical rule:

**peak and mean power must be reported separately and cannot be treated as equivalent.**

No blocker.

## 7. Velocity audit

PASS.

External bar/load velocity, joint angular velocity and fascicle shortening velocity are distinct.

Project boundary from PHY-04 remains intact.

Canonical rule:

**external movement velocity is a task measure, not a direct readout of every active muscle fascicle's shortening velocity.**

No blocker.

## 8. Load–power audit

PASS.

Current evidence supports:

- load changes the force–velocity balance;
- many tasks show high power across a broad low-to-moderate/intermediate load region;
- ACSM 2026 identifies 30–70% 1RM, power RT and fast concentric intent as power-enhancing characteristics in the synthesized chronic literature.

However:

- exercise-specific acute load–power curves differ;
- calculation methods differ;
- no single %1RM maximizes power across all tasks.

Canonical rule:

**load–power relation is task/exercise specific.**

No blocker.

## 9. Acute optimal-load audit

PASS.

The 2026 power-clean meta-analysis found:

- ≤40% 1RM produced lower acute peak power;
- >40–70%, >70–85% and >85% zones all produced high acute peak power;
- pairwise differences among the three higher zones were small/trivial and nonsignificant.

Therefore:

**acute “optimal power load” can be a broad plateau rather than a single load.**

More importantly, acute expression remains a different estimand from chronic adaptation.

Canonical rule:

**do not prescribe chronic power training solely from the acute watt-maximizing load.**

No blocker.

## 10. Neuromuscular / muscular determinant audit

PASS.

Power remains modeled as a system outcome involving:

- maximal force capacity;
- rapid neural activation;
- high intended acceleration;
- velocity capacity;
- muscle size/PCSA;
- architecture/fascicle behavior;
- tendon/elastic behavior;
- intermuscular coordination;

Canonical rule:

**power requires both force-side capacity and rapid/high-velocity expression.**

Detailed central mechanisms → NEU.  
Detailed mechanics → MEC.

No blocker.

## 11. Maximal-strength foundation audit

PASS.

The current review literature identifies maximal strength as a major predictor/foundation of power capacity.

But maximal strength alone does not guarantee high task power because rapid force expression and movement velocity remain necessary.

Canonical rule:

**strength expands the force side of power potential; velocity-specific/ballistic practice improves its rapid expression.**

No blocker.

## 12. Strength-vs-power training audit

PASS.

### Maximal-strength training
Emphasizes:
- high force;
- relatively heavy loads;
- maximal-strength specificity.

### Power training
Emphasizes:
- rapid force production;
- fast concentric/ballistic intent;
- maintaining useful movement velocity;
- exercises/loads that expose the target force–velocity region.

Current ACSM synthesis supports power RT, moderate loads, Olympic-style weightlifting and fast concentric intent for power outcomes.

Canonical rule:

**strength and power training overlap, but the primary performance target and force–velocity region differ.**

No blocker.

## 13. Task-specificity audit

PASS.

Power adaptation is not one scalar capacity.

Different tasks can show different changes in:

- jump power;
- barbell power;
- sprint acceleration;
- RFD;
- maximal force.

Canonical rule:

**power transfer is task/load/velocity specific.**

No blocker.

## 14. Force–velocity profiling audit

PASS WITH PRESERVED UNCERTAINTY.

The 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis found individualized F–V-profile training favored:

- F0;
- V0;
- FV imbalance;
- vertical jump height

but did not significantly improve:

- Pmax;
- sprint performance

relative to non-individualized training, with high heterogeneity.

A November 2026 RCT in velocity-deficient older adults found matched vs deliberately mismatched F–V loading produced different F0/V0 changes but no difference in power or functional performance.

Canonical rule:

**F–V profiling can describe task-specific deficits and guide hypotheses, but current evidence does not establish universal superiority for power/functional outcomes.**

No blocker.

## 15. Acute→chronic inference audit

PASS.

Closure preserves the key evidence rule:

`acute power output at load X ≠ proof that load X maximizes chronic power adaptation`.

Likewise:

`acute F–V profile deficit ≠ proof that correcting the profile maximizes the target performance outcome`.

Canonical rule:

**acute mechanical description and chronic training efficacy are separate evidence questions.**

No blocker.

## 19. Closure criterion test

Normalized-tree question:

> чем power adaptation отличается от maximal strength?

Result: **PASS.**

Canonical synthesis:

`maximal strength` primarily reflects high-force capacity.

`RFD` reflects how quickly force rises.

`power` reflects force expressed at velocity.

Therefore power development requires both force-side capacity and rapid/high-velocity expression, with task-specific practice.
