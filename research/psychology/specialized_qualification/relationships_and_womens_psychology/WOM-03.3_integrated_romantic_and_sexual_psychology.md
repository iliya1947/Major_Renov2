## 3. Integrated analytical procedure
The conceptual model distinguishes (a) general desire for partnership and generalized sexual motivation; (b) preferences, partner-specific attraction, affection, love and sexual desire; (c) attachment regulation, experienced intimacy, perceived compatibility and perceived/enacted responsiveness; (d) subjective arousal, physiological response, satisfaction and emotional appraisal; (e) relative priorities, intention, readiness, chosen commitment, opportunities and freely agreed mutual behavior. No necessary linear sequence or deterministic transition between these levels. Context, personal history, goals, emotions, resources and interactions may modify them over time; outcomes may change subsequent appraisal and motivation. Feedback arrows are hypotheses until separately supported.
1. Identify precise target, actor, time window, evidence channel and whether general or person-specific. Do not collapse relationship evaluations into love or love into commitment.
2. Separate own desire and perceived partner response from partner's actual report and dyadic outcomes. Compare stated ideals, experiences and choices without assuming any one reveals a hidden true preference.
3. Compare plausible alternatives: changed value/priority, actual opportunity, motivation quality, relationship appraisal, state/stress, resources, developmental or measured biological factors, reporting and selection. Distinct motives may coexist; no inference of hidden attraction from disinterest.
4. Check timescale, actor/partner/shared effects, measurement overlap, within-person vs between-person estimates, dependence and competing directions. Association, prediction and causation differ; significance in one subgroup but not another does not demonstrate a subgroup contrast.
5. State supported individual propositions with uncertainty, alternative predictions and naturally occurring revision conditions under IND-03/INT-02. No invented probabilities or covert tests. No inference from general group membership to an individual's motives.
6. Respect freely given, specific, revocable consent and stated choices regardless of hypothesized attraction, attachment, arousal or desire. No pressure, engineered distress or manipulation. Low desire by itself is not a clinical diagnosis.
