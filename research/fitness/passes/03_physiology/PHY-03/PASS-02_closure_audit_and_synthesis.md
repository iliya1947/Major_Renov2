## 3. Human nomenclature audit

PASS.

Current human project nomenclature:

- Type I → MyHC I → MYH7;
- Type IIA → MyHC IIa → MYH2;
- Type IIX → MyHC IIx → MYH1.

Canonical rule:

**normal adult human limb/torso fiber classification uses I / IIA / IIX; historical human “IIb” labels must not be interpreted as true rodent-like MyHC IIB/MYH4.**

No blocker.

## 4. MyHC vs full phenotype audit

PASS.

MyHC is retained as a major contractile classifier because it strongly relates to cross-bridge kinetics and maximum shortening velocity.

But full fiber phenotype also includes:

- hybrid MyHC state;
- metabolic machinery;
- mitochondrial content/function;
- Ca²⁺ handling;
- fiber size;
- capillary context;
- proteomic/transcriptional state;
- activity/innervation history.

Canonical rule:

**MyHC identity is a strong contractile anchor, not a complete metabolic or functional diagnosis.**

No blocker.

## 5. Contractile-speed audit

PASS.

The useful adult-human slow-to-fast sequence is:

`I → I/IIA → IIA → IIA/IIX → IIX`

with progressively faster contractile kinetics on average.

Boundary:

**fiber phenotype contributes to contraction speed but does not uniquely determine whole-muscle or whole-body movement velocity.**

Architecture, neural activation, tendon mechanics and task mechanics remain relevant.

No blocker.

## 6. Metabolic-profile audit

PASS.

Current model correctly preserves broad tendencies:

- Type I → more oxidative-biased;
- Type IIA → mixed oxidative-glycolytic;
- Type IIX → more glycolytic-biased.

Critical rule:

**contractile MyHC and metabolic phenotype are correlated but not identical.**

Training can change oxidative/metabolic machinery without requiring a major MyHC class change.

No blocker.

## 7. Fatigability audit

PASS.

Broad tendency:

`I = more fatigue resistant → IIA = intermediate → IIX = less fatigue resistant`

But observed fatigue depends on:

- activation frequency;
- contraction mode;
- duty cycle;
- Ca²⁺ handling;
- substrate/metabolic state;
- temperature;
- prior fatigue;
- chosen endpoint.

Canonical rule:

**fiber type biases fatigue behavior; task and physiological state determine the observed fatigue response.**

Detailed fatigue mechanisms remain PHY-10.

No blocker.

## 8. Hybrid-fiber audit

PASS.

Hybrid fibers such as:

- I/IIA;
- IIA/IIX;
- occasionally broader combinations

are retained as biologically meaningful states.

They can represent:

- stable intermediate phenotypes;
- transitional/remodeling states.

Single-fiber methods are better able to identify hybrids than bulk homogenate methods.

Canonical rule:

**human fiber phenotype is a continuum, not three perfectly isolated boxes.**

No blocker.

## 10. Training-plasticity audit

PASS.

Current project model correctly separates multiple plasticity dimensions:

1. MyHC isoform expression;
2. hybrid ↔ pure-fiber state;
3. oxidative/glycolytic machinery;
4. mitochondrial phenotype;
5. Ca²⁺-handling machinery;
6. fiber size;
7. fiber-specific proteome.

These features can change without moving in lockstep.

No blocker.

## 11. IIx → IIA audit

PASS.

A common exercise-associated pattern is:

- reduced IIx / IIA-IIx representation;
- greater IIA-dominant representation.

This can occur across resistance, endurance and concurrent training contexts depending on baseline/program.

Canonical rule:

**IIx → IIA is a common training-associated direction and does not mean the muscle has become “slow”; IIA remains a fast phenotype.**

No blocker.

## 12. Slow ↔ fast conversion audit

PASS.

The evidence supports plasticity across the slow-fast continuum, but the magnitude and reproducibility of large I ↔ II shifts are context dependent.

Important modifiers:

- modality;
- duration;
- baseline phenotype;
- muscle sampled;
- measurement method;
- hybrid detection.

Canonical rule:

**do not promise large predictable I ↔ II conversion from ordinary resistance training.**

No blocker.

## 13. Resistance-training adaptation audit

PASS.

Resistance training can produce:

- hypertrophy in both type I and type II fibers;
- fiber-specific proteomic remodeling;
- changes in MyHC distribution/hybrid state;
- metabolic remodeling without complete MyHC-class conversion.

Canonical rule:

**both slow and fast fibers are trainable; fiber-specific responses do not imply that only one fiber class matters for hypertrophy or strength.**

No blocker.

## 14. Load-specific hypertrophy audit

PASS with preserved uncertainty.

The 2026 systematic review/meta-regression included 8 studies / 195 participants.

The reported fiber-type-specific pairwise contrasts across load ranges all had 95% confidence intervals including zero.

Canonical rule:

**current evidence is insufficient to prescribe high- vs low-load resistance training solely to target Type I versus Type II hypertrophy.**

This does not imply training load is irrelevant to strength, fatigue or task specificity.

Those questions remain PHY-08 / PRO.

No blocker.

## 15. Training-status causal audit

PASS.

Cross-sectional athlete literature can show:

- endurance athletes → more Type I representation on average;
- sprint/power athletes → more fast-fiber representation on average.

But these differences combine:

- training history;
- genetics;
- sport selection/self-selection;
- muscle sampled;
- sex;
- age;
- prior activity.

Canonical rule:

**cross-sectional athlete composition is not a clean causal estimate of what training alone will convert in another person.**

Longitudinal evidence receives greater weight for training-plasticity claims.

No blocker.

## 16. Age audit

PASS.

Current 2024 systematic review/meta-analysis supports:

- higher relative slow MyHC I expression with age in pooled data;
- lower fast MyHC II/IIA relative expression;
- similar Type I fiber size on average;
- smaller Type II/IIA fiber size;
- no uniform shift in fiber-type distribution across studies.

Canonical rule:

**aging prominently affects fast-fiber size and MyHC expression, but “all Type II fibers convert to Type I” is an overstatement.**

No blocker.

## 17. Sex audit

PASS.

Current 2025 systematic review/meta-analysis of 6222 healthy adults / 156 studies found group-average differences in:

- Type I and II fiber CSA;
- Type I vs Type II distribution;
- proportional fiber area.

The effects were moderated by factors including age and muscle group.

Canonical rule:

**sex modifies group-average fiber phenotype but does not diagnose the phenotype of an individual.**

No sex-specific load prescription is derived from fiber type alone.

No blocker.

## 18. Measurement audit

PASS.

“Fiber-type percentage” can refer to different quantities:

- fiber-count percentage;
- proportional CSA;
- MyHC protein abundance;
- single-fiber classification;
- histochemical/IHC classes.

These are not interchangeable.

Sampling limitations include:

- biopsy region;
- muscle-specific heterogeneity;
- hybrid-fiber detection;
- loss of single-fiber information in homogenates.

Canonical rule:

**always interpret fiber composition relative to method and denominator.**

No blocker.

## 19. Noninvasive-personalization audit

PASS.

The project does not treat:

- jump performance;
- repetition tests;
- subjective “explosiveness”;
- practitioner impression;
- sport history

as biopsy-equivalent fiber typing.

Canonical rule:

**do not classify Ilya as “fast-twitch dominant” or “slow-twitch dominant” from indirect performance anecdotes and then prescribe load from that label.**

If direct fiber typing ever becomes decision-relevant, validity and decision utility must be handled in TST/IND.

No blocker.

## 23. Closure criterion test

Normalized-tree question:

> что в fiber phenotype реально меняется от training?

Result: **PASS.**

Canonical project synthesis:

Training can alter:

- MyHC distribution;
- hybrid-fiber state;
- oxidative/glycolytic machinery;
- mitochondrial/proteomic phenotype;
- fiber size

without requiring all properties to change together.

A common MyHC direction with repeated exercise is:

`IIX / IIA-IIX → more IIA-dominant phenotype`

while the magnitude of broad Type I ↔ Type II conversion remains more context dependent.

This is sufficient for downstream physiology and training-evidence interpretation.
