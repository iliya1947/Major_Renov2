## 3. Integrated inference model
Track distinct layers: exact utterance or observable action; literal proposition and communicative act; available shared context; candidate pragmatic meaning; recipient interpretation; appraisals and affect; next actions and recurring sequence; unresolved issue and feasible resolution; confirmed breach versus alleged breach or legitimate refusal; responsibility and impact; domain-specific trust and evidence of reliability; voluntary future contact and mutual relationship choice. The observable content is not a direct measure of hidden motivation. Acknowledgment does not imply agreement or consent.

The conceptual sequence includes feedback: circumstances, prior preferences, expectations and relationship history shape interpretation and action; each response changes the subsequent context; later experience can modify expectations. Shared stress and actual resource limitations affect both parties. This is a candidate explanatory map, not a fitted causal model. Reciprocal behavior does not imply equal responsibility for harm. Separate within-episode escalation, subsequent recovery, recurring patterns, substantive resolution and long-term outcomes.

A breach-specific account requires identifying the actual agreement or expectation, its legitimacy, the event and the affected domain. Disagreement, disappointment or a refused proposal is not necessarily a breach. Separate apology, acknowledgment of impact, responsibility, appropriate amends, internal and expressed forgiveness, reduction of tension, relevant sustained behavior and revised expectations. Trust can be felt without corresponding evidence of safety; reliable behavior in one domain does not establish global reliability. Renewed communication does not establish renewed romance, mutual commitment, forgiveness or restored trust.

## 4. Differential reasoning and revision
1. Define actor, relationship, question, time window, issue, trust domain and actual evidence channel. Preserve wording and observed sequences; distinguish quotations, reconstructions, interpretations and unavailable information.
2. Identify explicit choices, agreements and boundaries. Check whether an alleged breach is established, ambiguous or merely an incompatible preference. Do not infer a motive from a communication cue alone.
3. Generate competing explanations appropriate to context: pragmatic misunderstanding; different common ground; temporary stress or recovery; resource constraints; different goals; recurrent interaction pattern; genuine repair; unchanged risk; ordinary cooperation; changed relationship preference. Coexistence is possible.
4. Identify naturally occurring discriminating evidence: voluntary clarification, comparable repeated contexts, actual feasible change, observed follow-through on agreed commitments, recurrence or sustained nonrecurrence where there was real opportunity. If rival accounts predict the same observation, do not treat it as diagnostic. No engineered tests or covert monitoring.
5. Compare like estimands: own versus cross-partner prediction, between-person versus within-person changes, simultaneous versus lagged outcomes, future levels versus change, retrospective versus prospective baseline. A pooled correlation does not supply a personal probability or causal mechanism. Check selection, cohort overlap, reporter effects, uncertainty and corrected estimates.
6. Keep recovery, resolution, forgiveness, trustworthiness, autonomy and relationship choice separate. An apology does not obligate forgiveness; no pressure to resume contact or accept vulnerability. Safety takes precedence; do not treat coercion or threats as symmetrical negotiation problems.
7. Update only the supported claim; document what would overturn the interpretation, and keep unsupported outcomes unknown. Avoid uncalibrated numerical forecasts. No clinical expertise or intervention efficacy claimed.

## 5. Internal integrated transfer checks
Rubric: four criteria per case: construct separation, credible competing explanation, discriminating evidence and inference boundary. Critical failures: unsupported motive/diagnosis, causal inflation, deterministic individual transfer, disregard of counterevidence, coercion or privacy breach. Six original generic cases; self-authored, not independent or held-out.

A. A project collaborator acknowledges a proposal but declines it. Response: acknowledgment, comprehension and choice differ; constraints and preference compete. Voluntary clarification of actual options can discriminate, without overriding the choice. No hidden assent.
B. A group dispute becomes calm after a break but the scheduling issue is still unresolved. Response: recovery differs from resolution; regulation, incompatible goals and resource limits may coexist. Check feasible agreed plan and actual implementation. No inference of trust repair.
C. A participant apologizes for a confirmed error, and the recipient reports forgiveness but declines future responsibility-sharing. Response: apology, forgiveness and willingness to accept risk differ. Continued caution and changed preference are alternatives; subsequent relevant conduct and freely expressed choice clarify. No obligation to reconcile.
D. A meta-analysis predicts later quality weakly, while longitudinal within-person models show inconsistent lags. Response: predictive levels, within-person change and causation differ. Stable differences and shared context can explain this. Compare aligned estimands and precision; no universal mechanism or personal probability.
E. A survey measures trust now and remembered trust at an earlier incident, with several perceived repair predictors. Response: retrospective perceived change differs from prospective change and causal effects. Recall, selection and common-method variance compete. Repeated contemporaneous assessments and relevant behavior would strengthen evidence. No recommendation to monitor or engineer vulnerability.
F. A person leaves a conversation after a credible threat, despite a later apology. Response: safety boundary, conflict withdrawal, apology and actual risk differ. Recurrence, relevant conduct and voluntary choices matter; no need to infer hidden motives. No requirement to return or forgive.
