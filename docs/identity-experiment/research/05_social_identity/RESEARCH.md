# 05 — Социальная психология и идентичность: материалы A1

**Назначение:** основания карты. В A1 фиксируется, почему конкретная социальная ветвь необходима; глубокие ответы откладываются.

## 15.10.2026 — первый проход

### Group identity changes the logic of influence
Spears (2021) сопоставляет self-categorization/group-identity explanation с normative, interdependence, network and persuasion accounts.

**Для карты:** S3–S4. «Другие повлияли» недостаточно: механизм зависит от того, кем источник является относительно self/group.

### Social influence is motivated and may be public or private
Cialdini & Goldstein (2004) organizes influence around accuracy, relationships and self-concept, including subtle/indirect influence.

**Для карты:** S4/S15/S19; обязательно различать compliance и deeper internalization.

### Norms are dynamic multilevel phenomena
Gelfand, Gavrilets & Nunn (2024) integrates norm acquisition, transmission, network/group/ecological origins, persistence and erosion. Tankard & Paluck (2016) highlights perceived norms as constructed from selective information, not simple readout of actual behavior.

**Для карты:** S5 and S19. Norm perception itself can be wrong.

### Relational self is context-dependent but potentially patterned
Andersen & Chen (2002) links knowledge of self to significant-other representations; relational selves can vary by interpersonal cues while showing stable if–then patterns.

**Для карты:** S2/S7; social context may reveal structured individuality rather than erase it.

### Attachment creates relationship-specific/generalized expectations
Fraley (2019) reviews continuity/change, attachment hierarchies/networks and change over adulthood.

**Для карты:** S6; close relational history is distinct from generic conformity.

### Identity has both group and developmental process literatures
Branje et al. (2021) reviews exploration, commitment, reconsideration, within-person micro-processes, life transitions and close relationships.

**Для карты:** S13. Identity ≠ static label.

### Social roles may feed back on personality/identity
Lodi-Smith & Roberts (2007) distinguishes psychological commitment to social roles from mere demographic occupancy and documents social-investment literature.

**Для карты:** S12/S19; selection into a role and change caused by role experience must be separated.

### Rejection can produce divergent responses
Williams (2007) and acceptance/rejection reviews show both reconnection-oriented and antisocial/control-oriented responses after exclusion.

**Для карты:** S9. Social influence is not always accommodation; same social event can produce opposite trajectories.

### Self-related motives are socially embedded
Leary (2007) reviews self-enhancement, self-verification, self-expansion and self-conscious emotions in relation to social approval/interaction.

**Для карты:** S7/S9/S15; self-maintenance processes need social context.

### Culture changes the structure of self
Markus & Kitayama (1991) distinguishes independent/interdependent self-construal and their consequences; later relational-self literature differentiates relational and collective interdependence.

**Для карты:** S14; Western individual self cannot be assumed universal.

### Digital identity is a social-development context
Pérez-Torres (2024) reviews self-presentation, social comparison, role models and online audience in adolescent identity development.

**Для карта:** S17; digital setting may amplify feedback/audience/context collapse but must be tested for genuinely novel mechanisms.
