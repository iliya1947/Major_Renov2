## 3. Experience-dependent plasticity audit

PASS — BROAD CONSTRUCT, NOT BEHAVIORAL OUTCOME.

Kleim & Jones (PMID 18230848) remains useful as a conceptual synthesis of experience-dependent neural plasticity.

Important scope:

- the paper explicitly addresses rehabilitation after brain damage;
- it reviews principles relevant to relearning and recovery;
- it is not a healthy-resistance-training dosing guideline.

The 2026 rapid structural-plasticity systematic review (PMID 42105826):

- 30 studies;
- structural MRI changes reported within <2 h;
- task-relevant regions frequently implicated;
- substantial intervention/method heterogeneity;
- perfusion/fluid/metabolic effects can mimic apparent structural change.

Therefore:

**experience can produce rapid measurable neural change, but “brain changed” does not establish a durable learned capability or one cellular mechanism.**

No blocker.

## 4. Use-dependent plasticity audit

PASS — DISTINCT FROM COMPLETE MOTOR LEARNING.

Classic human use-dependent studies show repeated movement can bias later evoked movement/representation.

The 2026 lower-limb study PMID 42089992 gives a critical modern boundary:

- n = 14;
- repeated force-tracking practice improved force steadiness;
- MEP amplitude, SICI and spinal excitability did not significantly change;
- only delayed silent-period change emerged.

Thus:

**behavior can improve without the measured use-dependent/corticospinal markers changing.**

Possible interpretations include:

- marker insensitivity;
- task/muscle-specific thresholds;
- different neural loci;
- homeostatic effects.

It does **not** imply no neural adaptation occurred.

Canonical rule:

**use-dependent change is one mechanism class, not the definition of learning.**

No blocker.

## 5. Neural biomarker audit

PASS — MARKER HETEROGENEITY IS CLOSURE-CRITICAL.

Neuroplasticity research uses:

- MEP amplitude;
- SICI/ICF;
- cortical silent period;
- H-reflex;
- V-wave;
- StartReact;
- ICAR/iMEP;
- fMRI activation/connectivity;
- MRI structural measures;
- MRS metabolites;
- BDNF;
- EEG/other measures.

These reflect different biological properties and timescales.

The 2026 RT meta-analysis (PMID 42460308):

- 11 studies;
- 244 healthy adults;
- pooled corticospinal-excitability SMD ≈ 0.59;
- motor-performance SMD ≈ 0.40;
- heterogeneity ≈ 69% and 65%.

The authors' pooled positive effect does not make corticospinal excitability a universal fitness target.

Canonical rule:

**name the marker and its biological interpretation; never report “more neuroplasticity” as a generic scalar.**

No blocker.

## 6. Resistance-training neural adaptation audit

PASS — REAL, DISTRIBUTED AND PROTOCOL-SPECIFIC.

PMID 41845576:

- 30 healthy participants;
- 3 weeks;
- metronome-paced vs self-paced dumbbell RT vs control;
- both training conditions increased strength;
- self-paced training showed larger StartReact/ICAR-related changes;
- metronome-paced training showed larger corticospinal-excitability and SICI changes.

This is strong evidence against a single-pathway model.

Canonical rule:

**strength gain can coexist with different descending-pathway adaptation profiles; neural adaptation is not one mechanism and does not by itself equal technique learning.**

No blocker.

## 7. Specificity audit

PASS — SPECIFICITY WITH NONZERO TRANSFER.

Specificity is strongly supported at behavioral and neural levels.

However it must not be turned into:

> “only exact constant practice works.”

PMID 43003929:

- thousands of trials across weeks;
- near-complete compensation;
- increased retention;
- increased spontaneous recovery;
- interlimb transfer;
- narrowed angular generalization.

This shows:

**stronger/longer memory can coexist with narrower generalization and some transfer.**

PMID 41351896:

- n = 80;
- constant vs variable bimanual shuffleboard;
- no significant 24-h criterion-task retention difference;
- Bayesian evidence supported the null.

Therefore:

**specificity constrains adaptation; variability is not universally superior or inferior.**

No blocker.

---

## 8. Repetition audit

PASS — NO UNIVERSAL DOSE/THRESHOLD.

Repetition is necessary exposure for many forms of learning/plasticity.

But current evidence rejects a universal count.

PMID 41795357:

- larger blocked repetition facilitated early subsequent adaptation;
- strongest benefit in the eight-repetition condition;
- exploratory 24-h retention subgroup did not retain the advantage.

PMID 42089992:

- repeated lower-limb practice improved behavior without expected corticospinal marker changes.

PMID 43003929:

- very extensive practice continued changing memory properties over weeks.

Canonical rule:

**repetition dose must be defined behaviorally/task-specifically, not by a universal neuroplasticity threshold.**

No blocker.

---

## 9. Salience audit

PASS ONLY WITH OPERATIONALIZATION.

“Salience matters” is useful only if salience is defined.

Possible meanings:

- reward;
- motivational relevance;
- meaningful goal;
- novelty;
- attention capture;
- emotional significance.

These are not equivalent.

PMID 34345810:

- n = 90;
- reward improved motor learning beyond reinforcement feedback alone;
- benefit persisted next day.

PMID 40139803:

- n = 65;
- monetary reward accelerated skill learning;
- altered early corticospinal-output variability;
- most other excitability measures remained comparable.

PMID 41730946:

- moderate (~50%) success produced more reward-based learning than enforced ~80% success.

This falsifies a simplistic:

`more reward/success → more learning`.

Canonical rule:

**motivational relevance can modulate learning, but salience is not one dose variable and maximal positive outcomes are not always optimal.**

No blocker.

---

## 10. Consolidation audit

PASS — BROADER THAN SLEEP OR OFFLINE GAIN.

Consolidation is retained as post-acquisition stabilization/reorganization.

PMID 40800901:

- ALE meta-analysis;
- 38 motor-task imaging studies;
- both overlapping and distinct online/offline neural patterns.

PMID 41145718:

- replacing passive intertrial rest with engaging tasks did not significantly alter micro-offline gains;
- Bayesian results supported no group difference;
- this weakens the claim that micro-offline gains necessarily equal rest-dependent consolidation.

Canonical rule:

**offline performance change is neither necessary nor sufficient to identify consolidation.**

No blocker.

---

## 11. Sleep audit

PASS — IMPORTANT BUT NOT UNIVERSAL.

Sleep can support some motor memories.

But it is not mandatory for every motor-memory process.

PMID 42648788:

- 48 healthy adults;
- total sleep deprivation impaired acquisition speed;
- spatial accuracy spared;
- 24-h and 7-day delayed consolidation remained preserved;
- BDNF/corticospinal measures did not explain the behavioral difference.

Therefore:

**sleep loss can impair acquisition components without necessarily erasing delayed consolidation.**

Exact sleep/recovery prescription remains REC scope.

No blocker.

---

## 12. Retention audit

PASS — DELAYED BEHAVIOR, NOT MARKER OR RELEARNING.

PMID 40455501:

- 1,344 effect sizes;
- 457 reports;
- longer nonuse associated with greater decay;
- approximate half-loss of initial acquisition gains:
  - ~6.5 months accuracy;
  - ~13 months speed;
  - ~11 months mixed measures;
- task type, complexity, intermittent practice and instructions moderated decay.

Critical limitation:

These are broad procedural averages.

They are **not**:

- compound-lift-specific;
- expiry dates;
- evidence that all memory is erased at half-loss.

The review explicitly emphasizes separating retention performance from relearning.

Canonical rule:

**measure retention directly and avoid contaminating the test with substantial reacquisition.**

No blocker.

---

## 13. Long-term memory development audit

PASS — EARLY RETENTION IS NOT FINAL MEMORY STATE.

PMID 43003929 shows memory properties continue evolving over thousands of trials/weeks.

Therefore:

- early 24-h retention is informative but incomplete;
- training can strengthen retention;
- generalization can narrow;
- spontaneous recovery can increase.

This is especially relevant to fitness skills practiced for months/years.

No blocker.

---

## 14. Interference audit

PASS — SIMILARITY/TIMING DEPENDENT.

PMID 41270933:

- 45 participants;
- isometric ballistic wrist-flexion skill;
- later isometric accuracy task caused retrograde interference;
- dynamic accuracy task using same muscles/direction did not;
- contraction-type similarity mattered.

Therefore:

**interference depends on overlapping task/process features; temporal proximity alone is insufficient.**

Also:

**motor-memory interference ≠ contextual interference.**

No universal “never train two skills together” rule is justified.

No blocker.

---

## 15. Savings / relearning audit

PASS — LATENT MEMORY SIGNAL, NOT OVERT RETENTION.

PMID 41805913:

- two-day adaptation;
- behavioral savings on relearning;
- fMRI savings associated with reinstatement of previously formed large-scale cortical manifold structure.

This supports a latent/reinstatement account.

However:

- exact neural mechanism is not uniquely established;
- savings can involve explicit strategy, priors, structural learning, implicit memory or contextual retrieval.

Canonical rule:

**faster relearning proves prior-learning influence, not intact overt performance or broad transfer.**

No blocker.

## 16. Clinical neurorehabilitation transfer audit

PASS — STRICT BOUNDARY REQUIRED.

The classic principles were designed for brain-damage rehabilitation.

The 2026 Neuroplasticity-Based Model (PMID 42070115) is also explicitly a neurorehabilitation framework.

The 2026 digital-neurorehab transfer review (PMID 42996797) emphasizes that trained-task improvement does not guarantee real-world function.

The 2026 SCI task-specific training scoping review (PMID 43020864):

- 70 RCTs;
- major underreporting of key training principles;
- only 8 trials reported participant-centered goals;
- 20 reported feedback;
- implementation detail often insufficient for causal replication.

Most importantly, the 2026 phase-3 SCI RCT (PMID 41722590):

- n = 220 randomized;
- 12 h/week × 10 weeks additional intensive task-specific training + strength training;
- no significant benefit on Total Motor Score or secondary clinical outcomes beyond usual inpatient rehabilitation.

This does not mean task-specific training is useless.

It directly demonstrates that even in the population where neuroplasticity principles are most clinically invoked:

**more intensive practice is not automatically more effective.**

Canonical rule:

**clinical principles can generate hypotheses, but healthy-fitness prescriptions require healthy/task-specific evidence.**

No blocker.

## 17. “Use it or lose it / improve it” audit

PASS AS BROAD HEURISTIC ONLY.

Healthy procedural skills can decay with nonuse.

But:

- decay rates vary;
- partial latent memory can remain;
- relearning can be faster;
- training quality matters.

Therefore:

**“use it or lose it” and “use it and improve it” are qualitative summaries, not quantitative laws.**

No blocker.

---

## 18. “More neuroplasticity is better” audit

PASS — REJECTED.

Plasticity can support:

- useful skill;
- compensation;
- narrow specialization;
- maladaptive persistence;
- pain-related or compensatory reorganization in clinical contexts.

Therefore:

**neuroplasticity should not be maximized as a standalone goal.**

Project endpoint is:

- desired behavioral capability;
- retention;
- transfer;
- task-appropriate adaptation.

Neural markers are mechanism evidence unless directly validated as outcome targets.

No blocker.

## 19. Measurement non-equivalence audit

PASS.

Separate:

### Neuroplasticity
Broad experience-associated neural change.

### Use-dependent plasticity
Repeated-use-associated bias/change.

### Neural marker
Measurement of one neural property.

### Motor performance
Observed task output now.

### Motor learning
Relatively durable capability inferred from delayed testing.

### Consolidation
Post-acquisition stabilization/reorganization.

### Retention
Delayed persistence.

### Transfer/generalization
Expression outside exact trained condition.

### Interference
Interaction between memories/processes.

### Savings/relearning
Faster reacquisition after prior exposure.

Project rule:

**do not infer one layer from another without direct evidence.**

No blocker.

## 23. Closure criterion test

Topic:

> NEU-19 — Neuroplasticity

Result: **PASS.**

Canonical synthesis:

`experience/practice`
can induce

`behavioral change`
+
`task-specific neural adaptation`

but the mapping between them is not one-to-one.

A defensible interpretation chain is:

`training exposure`
→
`behavior during acquisition`
→
`delayed retention/transfer/relearning`
+
`specific neural markers when measured`
→
`mechanism inference with uncertainty`.

No universal law was established for:

- one biomarker = neuroplasticity;
- neural-marker increase = better learning;
- more repetitions = more useful plasticity;
- specificity = zero transfer;
- variable > constant universally;
- more salience/reward/success = more learning;
- consolidation = sleep;
- offline gain = consolidation;
- close practice = interference;
- savings = intact retention;
- clinical rehab dose = healthy fitness dose.
