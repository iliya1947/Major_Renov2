## 3. EIMD definition / marker audit

PASS.

EIMD remains a heterogeneous response to unaccustomed or unusually demanding loading.

Possible affected domains include:

- contractile function;
- cytoskeletal/sarcomeric organization;
- sarcolemmal/permeability changes;
- inflammatory/repair signaling;
- edema;
- soreness;
- biochemical markers.

No single indirect marker uniquely quantifies tissue damage.

Canonical rule:

**CK, DOMS, edema and strength loss are non-equivalent EIMD outcomes, not one interchangeable damage score.**

## 4. DOMS vs damage audit

PASS.

Current 2026 DOMS imaging review characterizes DOMS as a self-limited entity that typically develops roughly 24–72 h after unaccustomed/intense eccentric exercise and may show diffuse edema without macroscopic fiber disruption.

DOMS can coexist with functional impairment and edema, but its magnitude is not a direct quantitative measure of structural damage.

Canonical rule:

**DOMS is a perceptual/nociceptive outcome, not a tissue-damage meter.**

## 5. DOMS vs training-efficacy audit

PASS.

Current hypertrophy evidence does not establish soreness or damage magnitude as necessary for chronic muscle growth.

Relevant facts:

- hypertrophy can occur with low damage;
- early high-damage MPS can partly represent repair;
- edema can mimic early growth;
- repeated-bout protection reduces soreness/damage responses while continued training remains effective.

Canonical rule:

**a productive session does not require DOMS or substantial EIMD.**

No blocker.

## 6. Inflammation audit

PASS.

Inflammatory/immune signaling participates in response and repair after unfamiliar damaging loading.

But:

- local and systemic inflammatory signals are not identical;
- acute exercise inflammation is not equivalent to chronic pathological inflammation;
- larger inflammatory response is not a validated hypertrophy or workout-quality surrogate.

Canonical rule:

**inflammation can be part of repair/remodeling without being a training-quality score.**

No blocker.

## 7. Edema vs hypertrophy audit

PASS.

Human data show early RT-related CSA increases can coincide with edema and damage markers.

Canonical rule:

**post-damage swelling can inflate muscle-size measurements and must be separated from retained hypertrophy.**

This preserves PHY-06/PHY-07 measurement boundaries.

No blocker.

## 8. Temporary strength-loss audit

PASS.

Temporary force loss is retained as one of the most functionally meaningful EIMD outcomes.

Potential contributors include:

- contractile/E–C impairment;
- altered Ca²⁺ handling;
- pain-related inhibition;
- neural adjustments;
- swelling/mechanical effects.

Canonical rule:

**functional impairment is important, but one strength-loss value does not uniquely identify the molecular lesion.**

No blocker.

## 9. Repeated-bout-effect audit

PASS.

The 2026 three-level meta-analysis of 35 studies found clear protective effects for:

- MVC;
- DOMS;
- CK.

Protection differed by outcome and postexercise time point.

Canonical rule:

**RBE is real, but its magnitude and time course are outcome specific.**

No blocker.

## 10. RBE duration audit

PASS WITH PROTOCOL-SPECIFIC BOUNDARY.

The 2026 duration study found protection under its protocol:

- broadly present at 3–6 weeks;
- retained for selected outcomes at 9 weeks;
- largely absent for most assessed outcomes by 12 weeks.

This does **not** establish one universal expiration time.

Canonical rule:

**RBE is time dependent, but duration must be interpreted by muscle, protocol, outcome and person.**

No blocker.

## 11. RBE mechanism audit

PASS WITH PRESERVED UNCERTAINTY.

The 2026 neuromuscular systematic review/meta-analysis found:

- peripheral contractile outcomes showed the most consistent recovery-compatible pattern;
- voluntary activation findings were mixed;
- corticospinal/spinal/motor-unit/EMG findings were limited or inconsistent.

Therefore:

**RBE is multi-mechanistic; peripheral contractile protection is better supported than any single central/neural explanation.**

No blocker.

## 12. Eccentric-loading audit

PASS.

Eccentric/lengthening loading can provoke substantial EIMD when novel or unusually demanding.

But the 2026 AEL meta-analysis found pooled AEL vs constant-load RT produced:

- similar CK;
- similar acute swelling;
- similar soreness;
- similar chronic muscle CSA;
- similar chronic strength outcomes across the reported categories.

Canonical rule:

**eccentric loading is a training mode, not a damage requirement.**

Damage risk depends strongly on novelty, dose, muscle length, training status and protocol.

No blocker.

## 13. Damage / DOMS vs hypertrophy audit

PASS.

Current human hypertrophy synthesis does not support greater damage, soreness or swelling as a validated driver/surrogate of greater growth.

Canonical rule:

**greater DOMS/EIMD is not a validated predictor, mediator or required dose of hypertrophy.**

No blocker.

## 14. Excessive-damage cost audit

PASS.

Excessive EIMD can impose practical costs:

- larger temporary force loss;
- prolonged soreness;
- reduced subsequent training quality;
- movement compensation;
- reduced feasible volume/frequency;
- adherence disruption.

Canonical rule:

**training should tolerate incidental damage, not maximize it.**

No blocker.

## 15. Medical-pathology boundary audit

PASS.

The current DOMS differential review explicitly distinguishes DOMS from:

- muscle strain;
- chronic exertional compartment syndrome;
- rhabdomyolysis;
- inflammatory myopathy;
- denervation-related changes.

Rhabdomyolysis is a medical condition involving muscle injury with marked CK elevation and possible myoglobinuria/systemic complications.

PHY-11 does not diagnose these conditions.

Canonical rule:

**severe or atypical postexercise symptoms must not be normalized as ordinary DOMS; clinical differential belongs to MED.**

No blocker.

## 16. Marker-hierarchy audit

EIMD-related information should be organized across:

Canonical rule:

**interpret a pattern of outcomes rather than a single marker.**

No blocker.

## 20. Closure criterion test

Normalized-tree question:

> какое место damage занимает в adaptation и чего DOMS не показывает?

Result: **PASS.**

Canonical synthesis:

`unaccustomed/unusually demanding loading`
`→ tissue/contractile perturbation + inflammatory/repair response + edema + soreness/biochemical changes`
`→ recovery/remodeling`
`→ repeated-bout protection with later similar exposures`.

Damage can accompany adaptation, but is not the adaptation target and is not required for hypertrophy.

DOMS does not quantify structural damage, hypertrophy stimulus, or workout quality.
