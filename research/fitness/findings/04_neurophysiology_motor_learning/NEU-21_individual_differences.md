# NEU-21 — Individual differences

Статус: **CLOSED_FOR_PRACTICE v2.0 — revalidated 2026-11-19**  
Область: 04 — Neurophysiology / Motor Learning

## 0. Core decision

Individual differences are real, but fixed learner typologies are not a defensible default.

A practical model is:

`task-specific history`
+
`relatively stable person variables`
+
`current state/context`
+
`task demands`
→
`observed response`

then

`repeatability / retention / transfer`
→
`individual rule`.

Project rule:

**use group moderators to generate hypotheses and repeated task-specific response to individualize decisions.**

---

## 1. Training age

Training age is a crude exposure descriptor.

It can mean:

- years since beginning training;
- years in structured practice;
- years in one sport;
- years using one exercise;
- accumulated practice;
- performance caliber.

These are not equivalent.

Project rule:

**prefer exact-task exposure and current proficiency over generic years trained.**

---

## 2. Training age ≠ expertise

A person can have:

- many years of gym experience;
- little experience with a specific lift;
- high strength but low technical familiarity;
- extensive exposure with poor-quality practice.

Project rule:

**years trained does not define motor-learning level.**

---

## 3. Biological age

“Biological age” must be operationalized.

Possible constructs include:

- maturation status;
- brain-age estimate;
- physiological/frailty markers.

It is not interchangeable with chronological age.

Project rule:

**name the age construct and measurement before using it.**

---

## 4. Healthy aging

Ageing can alter different learning processes differently.

Current evidence shows:

- some explicit strategy processes may decline;
- implicit adaptation can remain preserved or even increase in some paradigms;
- procedural learning and retention can remain intact.

Project rule:

**older age is a modifier, not a global motor-learning incapacity.**

---

## 5. Previous motor experience

Prior experience can produce:

- positive transfer;
- negative transfer/interference;
- no meaningful transfer.

The direction depends on overlap between:

- task goals;
- action mappings;
- dynamics;
- sensory information;
- control policies.

Project rule:

**ask what was learned before assuming previous experience will help.**

---

## 6. Handedness / dominance

Dominance is strongly task/practice dependent.

A dominant limb is not universally:

- easier to train;
- more accurate;
- more adaptable;
- globally better controlled.

Project rule:

**test the actual task rather than assuming general dominant-limb superiority.**

---

## 7. Interlimb transfer

Transfer between limbs can be:

- present;
- absent;
- asymmetric;
- altered by additional exposure.

Project rule:

**no universal side-first training rule is retained.**

---

## 8. Anthropometry boundary

Anthropometry belongs Area 02 / MEC.

NEU-21 retains only the interface:

**morphology can alter the available motor solution and should not be misclassified as a cognitive/neural learning difference.**

---

## 9. Cognitive load tolerance

“Cognitive load tolerance” is not one fixed learner trait.

Relevant components include:

- working memory;
- motor working memory;
- attention control;
- processing speed;
- strategy use.

Project rule:

**adapt instruction when overload is observed rather than assigning a permanent learner type.**

---

## 10. Attention profile

Attention is multidimensional.

Individual differences can exist in:

- control;
- sustaining attention;
- distractibility;
- attention intensity/consistency;
- strategy allocation.

But attention profile does not directly prescribe:

- internal focus;
- external focus;
- analogy cue;
- no-cue strategy.

Project rule:

**general attention capacity and task-specific cue response are separate.**

---

## 11. ADHD boundary

ADHD-specific instructional design belongs Area 12 / PSY-COA.

NEU-21 does not prescribe ADHD-specific:

- cue count;
- feedback frequency;
- practice schedule;
- medication timing;
- attentional-focus rules.

Project rule:

**preserve the handoff; do not infer ADHD coaching from generic individual-difference evidence.**

---

## 12. Fatigue sensitivity

Fatigue effects on motor learning are heterogeneous.

They depend on:

- fatigue modality;
- task;
- testing state;
- protocol;
- participant characteristics.

Project rule:

**fatigue response is contextual until repeatability is demonstrated.**

---

## 13. Fatigue trait vs state

One session is insufficient to classify a person as:

- fatigue sensitive;
- fatigue resilient.

Project rule:

**repeat the relevant task/protocol under comparable conditions before assigning an individual fatigue rule.**

---

## 14. Resistance-training fatigability

Fatigability in resistance exercise varies with:

- exercise;
- rest interval;
- relative load;
- strength level;
- technical proficiency;
- sex in some contexts.

Project rule:

**individualize from repeated observed performance/recovery, not identity labels.**

---

## 15. Preferred / efficient cueing style

Cue preference may affect:

- interpretation;
- perceived autonomy;
- acute performance.

It does not establish:

- durable learning superiority;
- permanent cue identity.

Project rule:

**test candidate cues against task performance and delayed learning, then update as expertise/context changes.**

---

## 16. Preferred cue ≠ learner type

Reject permanent labels such as:

- “internal-focus learner”;
- “external-focus learner”;
- “analogy learner”;
- “visual learner”;
- “one-cue learner.”

Project rule:

**cue individualization is dynamic and empirical.**

---

## 17. Group moderator ≠ personal treatment effect

A subgroup interaction does not automatically prove:

- stable responder status;
- repeatability in one person;
- causal treatment-effect heterogeneity across tasks.

Project rule:

**group moderators generate hypotheses; repeated individual response validates personal rules.**

---

## 18. State vs trait

### More stable variables
- age;
- accumulated task history;
- hand preference.

### State/context variables
- fatigue;
- arousal;
- momentary attention;
- motivation.

### Mixed/task-specific variables
- cue preference;
- instruction tolerance;
- dominance expression;
- perceived effort.

Project rule:

**do not convert transient state responses into stable traits.**

---

## 19. Practical synthesis

For later resistance-training individualization:

1. document exact-task training history;
2. assess current proficiency/consistency;
3. treat age as a modifier, not a verdict;
4. examine whether prior skills share relevant task structure;
5. test both sides when dominance matters;
6. reduce/reshape instruction when overload appears;
7. separate broad attention capacity from cue response;
8. defer ADHD-specific design to PSY/COA;
9. measure fatigue response repeatedly in the actual protocol;
10. test cue variants against performance/retention;
11. update individual rules as expertise and context change.

Status:

**PRACTICAL_HEURISTIC**, not a learner-typing system.

---

## 20. Canonical non-equivalences

1. Training age ≠ expertise.
2. Years trained ≠ skill quality.
3. Chronological age ≠ biological age.
4. Older age ≠ globally poor motor learning.
5. Previous experience ≠ positive transfer.
6. Dominant limb ≠ universal superior motor capacity.
7. Hand preference ≠ task-general skill superiority.
8. Working memory ≠ one instructional style.
9. Cognitive-load tolerance ≠ fixed cue count.
10. Attention profile ≠ attentional-focus prescription.
11. ADHD ≠ generic low attention.
12. Fatigue effect ≠ stable fatigue sensitivity.
13. One-session response ≠ phenotype.
14. Cue preference ≠ cue superiority.
15. Preferred cue ≠ learner type.
16. Expertise moderator ≠ deterministic individual rule.
17. Group subgroup effect ≠ personal treatment effect.
18. Anthropometry ≠ NEU-21 ownership.

## 21. Measurement boundaries

### Training age
Exposure history; specify task/training type.

### Expertise
Task-specific proficiency/performance.

### Biological age
Explicitly measured age-related construct.

### Previous motor experience
Prior learned control/task structure relevant to a new task.

### Dominance
Preference/learned asymmetry with task dependence.

### Cognitive capacity
Named construct such as working memory or attention control.

### Attention profile
Pattern across attention dimensions.

### Fatigue sensitivity
Repeated task-specific learning/performance response under fatigue.

### Cue preference
Subjective/behavioral preference.

### Cue efficiency
Observed task performance and delayed learning under a cue.

---

## 22. Confidence

### HIGH

- training age ≠ task expertise;
- age effects are process specific;
- prior experience can help or interfere;
- dominance is task/practice dependent;
- cognitive measures do not prescribe one learner type;
- fatigue effects are heterogeneous;
- cue preference does not define permanent cue style;
- ADHD/anthropometry remain downstream scope.

### MODERATE-HIGH

- expertise can moderate cue/focus effects;
- working memory can relate to different learning components;
- attention control is a meaningful individual-difference construct;
- fatigue response should be measured repeatedly.

### MODERATE

- repeated N-of-1 response testing as the preferred coaching individualization method.

### LOW / CONTEXT-DEPENDENT

- one training-age cutoff;
- one biological-age learning threshold;
- one cue assignment algorithm;
- one stable fatigue-resilience phenotype;
- one dominance-based side-first rule.
