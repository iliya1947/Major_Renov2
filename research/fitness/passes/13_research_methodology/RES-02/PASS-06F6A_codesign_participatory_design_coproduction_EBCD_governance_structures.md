Статус: **COMPLETE — PARTICIPATORY-DESIGN / CO-DESIGN GOVERNANCE TAXONOMY ESTABLISHED FOR RES-02 STRUCTURAL USE**  
Дата: 2026-11-18  
Topic: RES-02 — Study designs / what they answer

## Purpose

Этот PASS закрывает blocker, оставленный PASS-06F6:

> как отличать реальное co-design / participatory design / co-production от consultation/PPI, как кодировать decision rights и power, где проходит граница с PAR/CBPR, и как отдельно маршрутизировать EBCD / accelerated EBCD / E2CD.

Главная цель — не навязать полю одну универсальную дефиницию, которой фактически нет, а построить воспроизводимый governance router по реально enacted participation.

# 1. Final project routing

Canonical project classes:

1. **PARTICIPATORY_DESIGN**
   - design-oriented participatory framework/process;
   - core values include genuine participation, mutual learning and democratization.

2. **CO_DESIGN**
   - collaborative design process in which professional designers/researchers and non-designers/lived-experience stakeholders materially shape design decisions together.

3. **CO_CREATION**
   - broader collaborative creation label with heterogeneous meanings across design, public health, service innovation and governance.

4. **CO_PRODUCTION**
   - broader shared-power/shared-responsibility mode that may span agenda setting, research, design, implementation, evaluation and knowledge generation.

5. **EBCD**
   - specific health-service participatory co-design lineage centred on patient/service-user experience and touchpoints.

6. **ACCELERATED_EBCD**
   - adaptation of EBCD using pre-existing/national experience narratives or other compressed procedures.

7. **E2CD**
   - evidence-informed experience-based co-design framework integrating research evidence and lived experience in priority-setting and co-design.

### Critical project rule

**AUTHOR_LABEL ≠ ENACTED_GOVERNANCE.**

Every study must preserve:

- author’s term;
- actual participation stages;
- actual decision rights;
- actual power-sharing arrangements;
- final authority;
- ownership/provenance.

---

# 2. Participatory Design lineage

Participatory Design (PD) historically developed from democratic/worker-participation design traditions and differs from classic expert-led user-centred design by treating affected people as active contributors/partners.

Current health-methodology discussion identifies three core PD values:

1. **having a say**;
2. **mutual learning**;
3. **democratization**.

### Project route

PD is best stored as:

**PARTICIPATORY_DESIGN_PROCESS / GOVERNANCE FRAMEWORK**

not as:

- one data-collection method;
- one empirical causal design;
- generic qualitative research.

### Critical rule

A project can use interviews, workshops or prototyping without being participatory design if users/stakeholders lack meaningful influence over design decisions.

---

# 3. Co-design

Sanders & Stappers distinguish user-centred design from participatory/co-design traditions by shifting the user role from primarily a subject/informant toward partner/collective creator.

Project definition:

> Co-design is a design process in which people with relevant lived/practice expertise participate materially in generating, selecting, shaping, refining and/or governing design decisions with professional researchers/designers.

### Minimal evidence for project classification as CO_DESIGN

At least one substantive design stage must contain:

- collaborative generation or selection of design directions;
- material participant influence on resulting design;
- traceable design consequences.

### Stronger co-design

Stronger classification when stakeholders also influence:

- problem definition;
- priority setting;
- prototype creation;
- evaluation criteria;
- final feature decisions;
- implementation planning.

# 4. Consultation vs involvement vs co-design vs co-production

The project uses a **governance-depth router** rather than a universal normative ladder.

## LEVEL 0 — PARTICIPATION AS RESEARCH SUBJECT

People provide research data only.

Examples:

- interview participant;
- survey respondent;
- trial participant.

Not PPI/co-design by itself.

## LEVEL 1 — CONSULTATION

People provide one-off or episodic feedback.

Research/design team retains decision authority.

Examples:

- review a leaflet;
- comment on prototype;
- attend one feedback workshop.

## LEVEL 2 — INVOLVEMENT / COLLABORATION

People shape work across multiple stages.

They may influence decisions but do not necessarily share final authority.

## LEVEL 3 — CO-DESIGN

People materially participate in design generation/refinement and have demonstrable influence over design decisions.

## LEVEL 4 — CO-PRODUCTION / SHARED GOVERNANCE

Researchers/practitioners/public share power and responsibility across substantial portions of the project lifecycle, including joint ownership of key decisions.

### Critical rule

This is a **project routing device**, not a claim that one universal published hierarchy is accepted across all traditions.

---

# 5. Patient and Public Involvement (PPI) boundary

Current NIHR framing:

PPI = research carried out **with or by** members of the public rather than **to, about or for** them.

PPI is broader than co-design.

A public contributor may:

- help set priorities;
- review study materials;
- advise recruitment;
- serve on steering groups;
- contribute to dissemination;
- help interpret findings.

These activities can be valuable PPI without being co-design.

### Project rule

**PPI_PRESENT = YES** does not imply **CO_DESIGN = YES**.

Store separately:

- PPI role;
- design role;
- decision authority;
- research-participant role.

### Critical distinction

A person can simultaneously be:

- public contributor;
- co-designer;
- study participant;

but these roles must be separately documented because they carry different ethics, consent, payment and data-governance implications.

---

# 6. Co-production boundary

NIHR co-production guidance defines co-producing research as researchers, practitioners and the public working together while sharing power and responsibility across the project.

Key principles include:

1. sharing power;
2. including all perspectives and skills;
3. respecting/valuing knowledge;
4. reciprocity;
5. building/maintaining relationships.

### Project definition

Co-production requires more than:

- consultation;
- repeated feedback;
- presence on advisory board.

It requires meaningful shared responsibility/authority.

### Critical nuance

Shared power does **not** require every person to decide every issue.

Roles/accountabilities may remain different.

Therefore project stores:

- DECISION_DOMAIN;
- WHO_HAS_VOICE;
- WHO_HAS_VOTE;
- WHO_HAS_VETO;
- WHO_HAS_FINAL_ACCOUNTABILITY.

---

# 7. Co-creation boundary

Public-health literature uses co-creation inconsistently.

Some authors treat it as:

- umbrella term;
- broader lifecycle collaboration;
- synonym for co-design;
- collaborative intervention development.

Current systematic/scoping reviews confirm major overlap among co-design, co-creation and co-production.

### Project rule

Do not force a universal semantic hierarchy.

Store:

- AUTHOR_TERM;
- PHASES;
- ACTIVITIES;
- DECISION_RIGHTS;
- GOVERNANCE_DEPTH;
- PRODUCT.

Then assign project route from enacted process.

# 8. Decision-rights taxonomy

For every major decision domain record:

## VOICE

Can participants express views?

## PROPOSAL

Can they generate options?

## PRIORITISATION

Can they rank/select priorities?

## JOINT_DECISION

Do they share the decision?

## VETO / BLOCK

Can they prevent a decision?

## FINAL_AUTHORITY

Who can overrule?

## ACCOUNTABILITY

Who bears legal/professional responsibility?

### Decision domains

1. research question;
2. problem definition;
3. design requirements;
4. feature selection;
5. prototype choice;
6. study outcomes;
7. recruitment;
8. implementation;
9. dissemination;
10. data interpretation;
11. authorship;
12. continuation/termination.

### Critical rule

“Participants were involved” is insufficient reporting.

---

# 9. Participant / stakeholder selection

Co-design sampling is not ordinary statistical sampling.

Relevant contributors may include:

- service users;
- patients;
- carers;
- clinicians;
- coaches;
- therapists;
- implementers;
- managers;
- community leaders;
- policy makers;
- underserved groups.

### Project fields

1. stakeholder categories;
2. inclusion rationale;
3. recruitment route;
4. excluded/missing voices;
5. lived-experience relevance;
6. professional power;
7. diversity;
8. accessibility;
9. compensation/support;
10. attrition.

### Critical rule

A “representative” co-design group should not be claimed without a defensible sampling frame.

Co-design usually seeks relevant perspective diversity, not prevalence estimation.

---

# 10. Lived-experience expertise

Participatory design/co-design treats lived experience as a form of expertise relevant to the design problem.

### Critical rule

Lived-experience expertise does not mean:

- every participant has the same needs;
- preferences override safety/evidence;
- participant views are population prevalence estimates.

Project requires explicit reconciliation among:

- lived experience;
- empirical evidence;
- professional constraints;
- safety;
- feasibility.

---

# 11. Power asymmetry

Power differences can arise from:

- professional status;
- academic status;
- age;
- disability;
- language;
- income;
- digital access;
- institutional authority;
- funding control;
- ownership of data/artifact;
- ability to attend meetings.

### Project rule

Power management must be treated as a methodology-level issue.

Record:

1. known asymmetries;
2. mitigation;
3. unresolved asymmetry;
4. effects on decisions.

---

# 12. Facilitation

Facilitation can determine whether co-design produces genuine joint work or professionally dominated consensus.

Facilitator tasks may include:

- creating psychological/interactional safety;
- balancing turn-taking;
- translating jargon;
- surfacing disagreement;
- preventing professional dominance;
- keeping design constraints explicit;
- supporting accessibility;
- documenting decisions.

Current accelerated-EBCD evidence identifies facilitation as both a major enabler and a potential source of bias/control.

### Critical rule

Facilitator is not methodologically neutral.

Store:

- facilitator role;
- affiliation;
- training;
- conflicts;
- intervention in decision process.

---

# 13. Accessibility and inclusion

Co-design can exclude the people most affected if participation requires:

- travel;
- long sessions;
- digital literacy;
- fluent academic language;
- unpaid labour;
- sensory/cognitive capacities not accommodated.

### Project requirements

Report where relevant:

- accessible venue;
- remote option;
- assistive technology;
- interpreter;
- easy-read material;
- breaks/session length;
- transport;
- childcare;
- compensation;
- digital support.

### Critical rule

Accessibility is part of co-design validity because who can participate changes which design knowledge enters the process.

---

# 14. Compensation and reciprocity

There is no single universal compensation rule across jurisdictions.

However co-production guidance explicitly emphasizes reciprocity and recognition of contributions.

### Project rule

Store:

- payment/compensation;
- reimbursement;
- nonfinancial benefit;
- training/support;
- authorship/credit;
- reason if unpaid.

Do not equate volunteering with absence of contribution value.

---

# 15. Conflict and dissent

Co-design does not require artificial consensus.

Potential disagreements:

- user preference vs clinical safety;
- accessibility vs cost;
- staff workflow vs patient autonomy;
- short-term usability vs long-term behavior goals.

### Project rule

Record:

1. disagreement;
2. affected groups;
3. resolution method;
4. unresolved dissent;
5. final authority;
6. minority position.

### Critical rule

“Consensus reached” should not erase structured disagreement.

---

# 16. Prototype / artifact ownership

Co-design participation does not automatically establish legal ownership of:

- intellectual property;
- software;
- prototype;
- data;
- recordings.

### Project rule

Store separately:

- DESIGN_DECISION_OWNERSHIP;
- LEGAL_IP_OWNERSHIP;
- DATA_OWNERSHIP;
- LICENSE;
- FUTURE_USE_RIGHTS;
- PUBLICATION/AUTHORSHIP_RIGHTS.

### Critical rule

Shared design influence ≠ shared legal IP unless explicitly agreed.

---

# 17. Final-decision authority

This is the central anti-tokenism field.

For each high-impact design decision store:

- who proposed;
- who discussed;
- who decided;
- who could overrule;
- reason;
- effect on final design.

### Project classifier

If researchers retain unilateral final authority on all material design decisions:

- project may still contain valuable involvement/consultation;
- but strong co-production classification is not justified.

---

# 18. Experience-Based Co-Design (EBCD)

EBCD is a specific healthcare/service-improvement co-design lineage developed from experience-based design.

Core scientific/design object:

**service-user and staff experience, especially key “touchpoints”, translated into jointly designed service improvements.**

Current E2CD paper summarises an eight-stage EBCD architecture:

1. observation;
2. service-user interviews;
3. trigger film development;
4. staff feedback;
5. service-user feedback;
6. joint service-user/provider feedback;
7. co-design groups;
8. celebration/event.

### Critical rule

Exact stage counts differ across descriptions/adaptations.

Project therefore stores enacted EBCD components rather than assuming full fidelity from the EBCD label.

---

# 19. EBCD touchpoints

Touchpoints are emotionally/significantly salient moments in the service journey.

They are not simply:

- satisfaction scores;
- general complaints.

They identify experience elements that become design targets.

### Canonical chain

experience narrative  
→ touchpoint  
→ shared priority  
→ co-design workgroup  
→ service change.

---

# 20. EBCD trigger film

Classic EBCD often uses a locally produced trigger film from service-user narratives.

Purpose:

- communicate lived experience;
- surface touchpoints;
- catalyse joint reflection.

### Critical rule

Trigger film is a design/elicitation component, not evidence of effectiveness.

Governance fields:

- consent;
- edit authority;
- representation;
- re-use rights;
- identifiability.

---

# 21. Accelerated EBCD (AEBCD)

AEBCD reduces time/resources by adapting parts of classic EBCD.

A well-established route uses:

- pre-existing/national patient-experience narratives/films;
- compressed local process;
- local co-design groups.

### Critical boundary

AEBCD ≠ automatically low-quality EBCD.

But adaptation changes:

- local narrative generation;
- representation;
- contextual specificity;
- participant ownership.

Therefore store:

- which EBCD components replaced;
- source of trigger material;
- local validation;
- effect on co-design process.

---

# 22. EBCD fidelity

The 2020 systematic review of EBCD studies found substantial variation in fidelity and reporting.

### Project rule

Do not classify only:

**EBCD = YES/NO**.

Store:

- EBCD components;
- omitted components;
- modified components;
- reason;
- outputs by phase.

---

# 23. E2CD

**Evidence-informed, Experience-Based Co-Design (E2CD)** was introduced in 2024 to explicitly integrate:

- research evidence;
- lived experience;
- priority setting;
- co-design.

### Project route

**E2CD_SUBTYPE**

Useful when evidence and lived-experience priorities need explicit reconciliation.

### Critical rule

E2CD improves transparency of evidence + experience integration but does not itself establish:

- intervention effectiveness;
- causal impact;
- long-term sustainability.

---

# 24. PPI vs co-design vs EBCD

## PPI

Broad public involvement across research.

## Co-design

Specific collaborative design activity with material influence.

## EBCD

Specific experience-centred co-design architecture.

### Example

Patient advisory panel reviews recruitment materials:

**PPI**, not necessarily co-design.

Patients and clinicians jointly redesign a rehabilitation pathway from experience touchpoints:

**EBCD / CO_DESIGN**.

---

# 25. CBPR boundary

Community-Based Participatory Research (CBPR) is broader than co-design.

Current 2026 Annual Review confirms CBPR as a long-standing partnership approach emphasizing:

- community–academic partnership;
- equitable involvement;
- co-learning;
- capacity;
- local relevance;
- translation/action;
- health equity.

### Project route

**CBPR_PARENT_PARTICIPATORY_RESEARCH_ARCHITECTURE**

Co-design may be nested inside CBPR, but CBPR can govern:

- research question;
- data collection;
- analysis;
- interpretation;
- dissemination;
- action;

well beyond design.

### Critical rule

Community involvement in one design workshop ≠ CBPR.

---

# 26. PAR boundary

Participatory Action Research (PAR) combines:

- collaborative inquiry;
- reflection;
- action;
- iterative change;
- negotiated/shared power.

Current 2026 methodological work stresses:

- lived-experience involvement;
- shared decision-making;
- power-sharing;
- collaborative interpretation;
- collectively agreed action.

### Project route

**PAR_PARENT_PARTICIPATORY_ACTION_RESEARCH_ARCHITECTURE**

Co-design can be a method/process within PAR.

### Critical rule

Co-design does not require an action-research cycle.

PAR does.

---

# 27. CBPR vs PAR vs co-design

## CO_DESIGN

Primary object:

**design decision / artifact / intervention / service.**

## PAR

Primary architecture:

**collaborative inquiry → reflection → action → further inquiry.**

## CBPR

Primary architecture:

**long-term community partnership governing research and action.**

### Project rule

They can overlap.

Do not flatten them into one “participatory research” label.

---

# 28. PPI vs PAR/CBPR

PPI can range from light consultation to deep partnership.

PAR/CBPR require a broader methodological/epistemic architecture.

### Critical rule

PPI presence does not imply PAR/CBPR.

Conversely, PAR/CBPR normally entail involvement, but their defining features exceed generic PPI.

# 29. Current co-design quality layer — 2026 minimum standards

A 2026 double-consensus process developed minimum standards for co-design in public-health research.

Consensus was reached on 31 items across:

- critical principles;
- required co-design steps;
- evaluation criteria;
- follow-up actions.

Examples reported in the abstract include:

- inclusivity;
- transparent communication;
- accessibility;
- relationship building;
- clear purpose;
- early and sustained stakeholder engagement;
- iterative design;
- equity;
- integration;
- clarity;
- ethical standards;
- outcome dissemination;
- sustainability planning.

### Project classification

**CO_DESIGN_MINIMUM_STANDARDS_2026 = CURRENT EMERGING CONSENSUS QUALITY LAYER**

### Critical caution

These are current consensus standards from public-health research.

Do not treat them as a universal legally binding standard across every participatory-design tradition.

# 33. Deliberative Dialogue boundary

Current 2026 review identifies Deliberative Dialogue as a structured participatory approach bringing together:

- research evidence;
- professional expertise;
- lived/living experience;

for informed discussion/decision-making.

### Project route

**DELIBERATIVE_METHOD / DECISION PROCESS**

not a new co-design parent family.

It may be used inside:

- co-design;
- co-production;
- consensus;
- implementation;
- knowledge translation.

Detailed deliberative-method taxonomy remains a later consensus/KT handoff, not an F6A blocker.

# 34. Exercise / sport / rehabilitation relevance

Direct relevance is current and strong.

## PRO-BT — German medical rehabilitation, 2026

Co-design project produced practice recommendations for physical-activity promotion in:

- exercise therapy;
- physical therapy;
- movement-based therapies.

### Project lesson

Co-design can generate implementation/practice recommendations but does not by itself estimate intervention effects.

## Exercise maintenance after falls prevention — 2026

A multi-stage stakeholder consultation/co-design process developed an exercise-maintenance intervention with:

- older adults;
- rehabilitation professionals;
- service providers.

### Project lesson

Development output still required later feasibility/acceptability/effectiveness evaluation.

## Stroke physical-activity maintenance — 2026

Co-design/co-production workshops were used with people after stroke and stakeholders.

### Project lesson

Co-production label must still be checked against actual decision rights.

## Spinal cord injury sedentary behaviour — 2026

Behaviour Change Wheel + co-design was used to develop an intervention.

### Project lesson

BCW and co-design are separate layers:

- BCW = behaviour-change intervention framework;
- co-design = participatory design/governance process.

# 38. Current unresolved issues

No taxonomy-level blocker remains for co-design/participatory design/co-production/EBCD.

Known non-blocking issues:

1. terminology remains heterogeneous across disciplines;
2. no single universal participation ladder is authoritative;
3. legal IP/payment requirements are jurisdiction-specific;
4. EBCD adaptations vary substantially;
5. current 2026 minimum co-design standards are emerging consensus, not a universal cross-disciplinary standard;
6. Deliberative Dialogue remains a later consensus/KT method handoff.

### Project response

Classify by:

**enacted stages + decision rights + power + traceability**

rather than author label alone.

**HIGH**
