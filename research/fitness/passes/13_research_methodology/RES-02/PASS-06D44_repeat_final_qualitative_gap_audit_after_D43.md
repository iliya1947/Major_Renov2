# 6. Candidate re-test — Ideal-Type Analysis

## Finding

**NON-BLOCKER — qualitative analysis method.**

Stapley et al. explicitly describe Ideal-Type Analysis as a method for:

- constructing typologies from qualitative data;
- comparing whole cases;
- identifying within-type similarity and between-type difference.

Their seven-step approach includes:

1. familiarisation;
2. case reconstructions;
3. constructing ideal types;
4. identifying optimal cases;
5. ideal-type descriptions;
6. credibility checking;
7. comparisons.

### Current sport relevance

The 2026 Sport Sciences pedagogy paper uses Ideal-Type Analysis to construct four reading inclinations.

### Why no parent blocker

The method can analyze data generated under different parent designs.

Its scientific contribution is an **analytic product**:

> typology / ideal types.

It does not define one universal:

- ontology;
- fieldwork architecture;
- participant relationship;
- data-generation design.

### Route

parent methodology  
+ Ideal-Type Analysis  
+ typology output  
+ case-reconstruction metadata.

# 7. Ideal-Type Analysis vs CQR / phenomenography / case study

## vs CQR

CQR:

- domains;
- core ideas;
- cross-analysis;
- team consensus;
- auditor;
- frequency labels.

Ideal-Type Analysis:

- whole-case reconstructions;
- clustering into qualitative types;
- optimal cases;
- type comparison.

No conflict.

## vs phenomenography

Phenomenography:

- variation in ways of understanding;
- categories of description;
- outcome space.

Ideal-Type Analysis:

- groups whole participant cases into typological configurations.

No conflict.

## vs Case Study

Case Study is a design around a bounded case.

Ideal-Type Analysis is cross-case analysis.

Therefore no new parent family is needed.

# 30. Stopping rule

D44 introduces an explicit stopping rule to prevent infinite blocker-chasing.

A new qualitative blocker should be opened only when current evidence establishes all of the following:

1. stable/recognizable methodology-level lineage;
2. distinct scientific object or inferential product;
3. distinctive enough process/role architecture;
4. current taxonomy cannot reconstruct it without material information loss;
5. relevant to RES-02 scope or sufficiently fundamental cross-domain methodology;
6. more than a single author’s one-off naming convention.

### Why this matters

Without this rule, every:

- analysis technique;
- theoretical framework;
- data-collection innovation;
- representational form;
- niche adaptation

could generate a false “new methodology” blocker.

That would make taxonomy less scientifically precise.
