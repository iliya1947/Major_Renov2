## 3. Remodeling audit

PASS.

Bone remodeling is retained as a coupled process:

`activation → osteoclast resorption → reversal/coupling → osteoblast formation → mineralization`.

Osteocytes coordinate local remodeling and mechanical adaptation.

Canonical rule:

**remodeling is a coupled renewal process, not simply “bone building.”**

No blocker.

## 4. Osteoblast / osteoclast / marker audit

PASS.

Osteoblasts, osteoclasts and osteocytes remain distinct cellular actors.

Serum markers such as:

- P1NP;
- osteocalcin/BALP;
- CTX

are retained as turnover proxies.

They do not directly measure:

- local cell number;
- local formation–resorption balance at one skeletal site;
- whole-bone strength change.

Canonical rule:

**turnover markers ≠ direct osteoblast/osteoclast balance and ≠ whole-bone adaptation.**

No blocker.

## 5. Osteocyte mechanotransduction audit

PASS.

Osteocytes remain the central mechanosensory cell network in bone.

Current mechanistic evidence supports involvement of:

- fluid shear/matrix deformation;
- integrin/cytoskeletal signaling;
- Piezo1;
- Wnt/β-catenin;
- sclerostin/SOST;
- RANKL/OPG;
- YAP/TAZ-related pathways.

Recent Piezo1 work supports causal mechanobiology in animal models.

Canonical boundary:

**molecular mechanotransduction identifies biological pathways but does not define an optimal human loading dose.**

No blocker.

## 6. BMD audit

PASS.

The 2026 meta-analysis of 162 RCTs found small-to-moderate site-specific BMD benefits across adulthood at multiple sites.

But:

- rankings differed by skeletal site;
- credible intervals were often wide;
- certainty was limited by heterogeneity, imprecision and sparse direct evidence;
- dose-response optima were exploratory/boundary sensitive.

Canonical rule:

**exercise can improve/maintain BMD, but modality rankings and dose-response optima are hypothesis-generating rather than universal prescriptions.**

No blocker.

## 7. BMD vs geometry / strength audit

PASS.

Bone mechanical competence depends on:

- density;
- cortical/trabecular geometry;
- microarchitecture;
- material quality;
- mineralization;
- matrix properties.

The 2026 6-month QCT/finite-element trial found exercise-related improvements in regional vertebral ultimate compressive strength despite mixed/regional BMD behavior.

Canonical rule:

**BMD is important but is not equivalent to total bone strength.**

No blocker.

## 8. Mechanical magnitude audit

PASS.

Greater external load/impact does not produce a guaranteed monotonic osteogenic response.

The 2026 drop-jump RCT found that greater prescribed jump height:

- did not map simply to greater skeletal adaptation;
- produced substantial inter-individual external-load variation;
- did not yield significant DXA effects;
- showed pQCT changes that should be interpreted cautiously because they did not exceed least significant change.

Canonical rule:

**external task magnitude is an imperfect proxy for local bone stimulus.**

No blocker.

## 9. Loading-rate audit

PASS.

Loading rate is retained as an osteogenic signal dimension, especially in impact/ballistic tasks.

However:

- exact human thresholds differ by site/population;
- rate cannot be separated cleanly from magnitude, direction, frequency and muscle-generated force in most exercise studies.

Canonical rule:

**loading rate matters, but no universal rate threshold is established for human training.**

No blocker.

## 10. Novelty / distribution / cycles audit

PASS WITH TRANSLATION BOUNDARY.

Mechanistic/animal loading literature supports effects of:

- novel strain distribution;
- directionality;
- cycle number;
- rest insertion;
- waveform.

But precise animal-derived strain/cycle rules are not transferred directly into human training prescriptions.

Canonical rule:

**loading pattern and novelty matter conceptually; exact human dose rules remain uncertain.**

No blocker.

## 11. Resistance-training audit

PASS.

Resistance exercise can support bone adaptation through both external load and muscle-generated skeletal forces.

Current 2026 evidence shows:

- resistance training can improve/maintain site-specific BMD;
- high-intensity RT is clearly superior for muscle strength in older adults;
- high-intensity RT was **not significantly superior** to low-to-moderate intensity for lumbar-spine or femoral-neck BMD in the direct meta-analysis.

Canonical rule:

**greater muscle-strength adaptation from heavier RT does not imply proportionally greater BMD adaptation.**

No blocker.

## 12. Impact / high-rate-loading audit

PASS.

Impact/high-rate exercise can be osteogenic.

But:

- it is not universally required;
- exact dose depends on skeletal site and population;
- more impact height is not guaranteed to create greater adaptation;
- medical appropriateness depends on bone status.

Canonical rule:

**impact is one osteogenic tool, not a universal “more is better” requirement.**

No blocker.

## 13. Bone-turnover acute→chronic audit

PASS.

Acute exercise can alter bone-turnover/signaling markers.

These responses can inform mechanism.

They do not establish:

- chronic BMD gain;
- geometry change;
- microarchitectural change;
- fracture-risk reduction.

Canonical rule:

**acute bone biomarker response ≠ chronic skeletal adaptation.**

No blocker.

## 14. Multi-clock time-course audit

PASS.

Bone has multiple adaptation clocks:

- signaling/turnover markers can change acutely;
- BMD usually requires months to detect meaningful change;
- geometry/microarchitecture/estimated strength can follow different trajectories;
- remodeling/mineralization and measurement sensitivity further separate timelines.

Human trials demonstrate measurable structural/strength changes over approximately 12–24 weeks or longer in selected sites/populations, but no universal onset week exists.

Canonical rule:

**marker, density, geometry and strength timelines are not interchangeable.**

## 15. Age audit

PASS.

Aging is retained as more than simple BMD loss.

Current evidence supports age-related changes in:

- matrix composition/crosslinking;
- microdamage;
- mineral properties;
- osteocyte environment;
- mechanosensitivity;
- remodeling balance;
- cortical/trabecular structure.

Canonical rule:

**aging changes both skeletal state and the mechanobiological response to loading.**

No blocker.

## 16. Energy-availability audit

PASS WITH SURROGATE BOUNDARY.

The 2026 randomized crossover study in 19 healthy active adults found that 5 days of low energy availability:

- reduced fasting P1NP;
- maintained lower P1NP during recovery in the protein-matched condition;
- increased postexercise CTX in the protein-matched LEA condition;
- was not corrected by preserving protein intake.

This supports a causal short-term disturbance in bone-turnover biology.

It does **not** quantify long-term BMD or fracture-risk change.

Canonical rule:

**low energy availability can rapidly impair bone-remodeling signals; short-term biomarkers are not a direct measure of chronic bone loss.**

No blocker.

## 17. Exercise vs low energy availability audit

PASS.

Mechanical loading remains beneficial but cannot be assumed to fully compensate for sustained energy deficiency.

Canonical rule:

**bone adaptation depends on both mechanical and systemic/nutritional context.**

Exact energy prescription remains NUT/IND.
