## 3. Model construction and testing gate (PRACTICAL_HEURISTIC)
Problem representation is an operation, not a score: define target, constraints, variables, observables, unknowns, goal and uncertainty. Distinguish well-defined puzzle with a known answer from discovery in an open system with incomplete measurements, moving goals and multiple acceptable solutions. Difficulty varies with prior knowledge, representation, feedback, search space, dynamics, stakes and access to tools; not a direct readout of IQ. 
| Function | Observable criterion | Invalid shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Problem framing/reframing | Changed representation exposes testable distinctions; preserves relevant constraints | More abstract or unusual = better |
| Analogical transfer | Explicit mapped relations and disanalogies, success on new case | Similar vocabulary establishes common mechanism |
| Hypothesis/model generation | Alternative plausible structure and distinct predictions | Explanatory coherence proves model |
| Exploration/knowledge acquisition | Interventions/observations reveal uncertain relations | More tests = better understanding |
| Evaluation/application | Independent criteria, predicted vs actual consequences, resource cost | Correct one result = correct causal model |
| Revision | Change challenged assumptions/dependencies, preserve unaffected evidence | Any change is improvement |
| Transfer | New tasks, conditions, outcome and delay | Repetition with cue = broad skill |
Insight experience may prompt exploration; it is neither proof of accuracy nor necessary indicator of restructuring/unconscious processing. A solution may be valid but unoriginal, novel but invalid, or correct for reasons other than a proposed model. Distinguish descriptive fit (including retrospectively selected evidence), prospective prediction and causal identification/intervention. A predictive model can work without correct mechanisms, a plausible explanation can fail predictive tests, and a causal model valid under specific interventions may not extrapolate. 
For two candidate models M0/M1 and feasible observation/intervention X, compare predicted outcomes under each, quality and independence of measurement, probability/uncertainty if known, cost and relevance. In a deterministic illustration H0: device activates whenever input A is present; H1: activates only if A AND B present. Trials A=1,B=1 compatible with both; A=1,B=0 distinguishes, provided activation and inputs are reliable and other conditions matched. Positive tests of H0 can be useful, not all evidence needs to be negative; test predicted differences. If both models fail, widen search space or revise observation model, not select the less false as established. In stochastic systems repeated samples and uncertainty required; avoid binary falsification of all models from one noisy observation. For actual psychology, distinguish causal intervention, observation, self-report and hypothetical counterfactual; safe voluntary observations, no intrusive experiments. 
Adequate complexity: start with smallest model sufficient for purpose, explicitly compare competing richer model. If M1 adds latent state, feedback or interaction, specify what independent observation/time course/contrast it predicts that M0 cannot, additional assumptions and measurement needed. Higher in-sample fit expected when freedom added; use held-out prospective data or appropriate validation/complexity penalty if enough quantitative data; not compute AIC/BIC from anecdotes. Falsifiability not full truth criterion; model class can be incomplete, equivalent under available observations. Record when models are underdetermined rather than assign numeric psychological probabilities without calibration. This integrates GFT-04 causality/representation, GFT-05 knowledge/automaticity, GFT-06 calibration/control/cost and GFT-07.1 novelty/appropriateness. 
Scientific reasoning includes problem and question formulation, hypothesis generation, design/measurement, evidence evaluation, inference/argumentation and revision. These are distinct targets of assessment and teaching; reading/evaluating report not same as producing a sound original experiment. No fixed universal sequence, distinct brain modules or generalized rationality trait asserted.

## 4. Operational protocol
1. Fix decision/problem and observable success criteria; map facts, goals, constraints, known vs inferred relations. Assess prior knowledge and task structure before attributing difficulty to ability.
2. Construct M0 (simple) and at least one plausible M1; use structural analogies only with explicit correspondence/boundary. Separate originality, elegance, perceived confidence, empirical adequacy and feasibility.
3. Write distinguishing predictions BEFORE seeing new evidence, compare information/cost; choose safe observations with independent provenance. If cannot distinguish, mark unknown; don't add unsupported mechanism. Compare possibility of changed measurement, context or hidden variable.
4. Check response to contradictions: specify which assumption fails, revise affected predictions, preserve confirmed observations; don't protect original model with untestable ad hoc explanation. If errors may be learning opportunities, use safe simulated errors with explicit explanation/correction, not actual harmful interventions.
5. Evaluate results separately: fit, prospective performance, novel transfer, time/effort, reliability, consequences. No one-off global trait or IQ estimate; record limitations. For 07.3 separately study sustained output, achievement, domain expertise, resources and opportunity.

## 5. New internal transfer assessment
A. Fictional textile-drying apparatus: model M0 predicts drying rate solely from air temperature; M1 adds airflow and an interaction. Both fit prior batches where airflow covaries with temperature. An appealing story about turbulence does not establish M1. Prediction before new data: at same temperature and load, varying airflow distinguishes models, if moisture measurement reliable. New observation: rate changes with airflow only in thicker material; thin material unchanged. Revise M0's scope, M1's interaction needs thickness-specific tests; alternative sensor lag/initial moisture. Preserve old fit. No conclusion about researcher intelligence or mechanism from one contrast. 
B. Fictional museum exhibit puzzle: one participant suddenly sees a novel linkage, another uses a systematic comparison; first reports high confidence but mechanism fails under a specified weight, second solution works. Compare constraint validity and creativity separately; rival explanations: knowledge, omitted constraint, misleading analogy. New: the first linkage works when intended counterweight restored, but only under lighter loads. Revise from invalid under original heavy-load condition to conditional applicability; not global inferiority/superiority, insight still not proof. Verify new loads independent of examples, time and cost. 
C. Fictional seed storage study: batches using container material X have higher germination, but humidity differs. Proposed novel mechanism about X; simple confounding alternative. Pre-state prediction of both under matched humidity and blinded scoring; use simulated factorial design, compare X at each humidity. New: within each humidity level germination same across materials in observed trials, humidity association remains. Retract direct X explanation as supported by earlier observation, not prove exact null; alternative precision and interaction in untested range; retain actual observed differences. More complex model justified only by novel discriminating results. Scientific interpretation distinguishes design, causal identification and uncertainty, not judgment of intelligence.
