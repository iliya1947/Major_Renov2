## Provisional practical gate
Separate baseline ability and knowledge from actual improvement, delayed retention, near/far transfer, quality of feedback, available time, opportunity and task choice. Test rival models: prior exposure, domain interest, strategy quality, motivation, support, measurement, selection. A high posttest score does not by itself establish high learning gain; a quick solution to familiar tasks does not establish transferable strategy. Repeated observations and matched unfamiliar tasks can discriminate hypotheses without identifying IQ or unique causal mechanism. No attribution of all outcomes to intelligence or practice.

## Revised analytic protocol (generic heuristic)
Define task-specific competence, baseline knowledge, accuracy, time, aid, training dose, delayed retention and novel transfer separately. Keep motivation/effort, measured ability and strategy as separate hypotheses. Compare gains on equivalent pre/post forms and avoid floor/ceiling and regression artifacts. A posttest advantage may coexist with smaller gains. A learning strategy is conditionally useful, not a trait or IQ marker. Test relevant prior knowledge, misconceptions, instruction redundancy and opportunity; compare voluntary matched practice conditions, with feedback and delayed novel tasks. State predicted differences before comparison. No claim of causal identification from uncontrolled single-person observations. Age-related change requires repeated comparable measures and separation of cohort, practice and period. Avoid imposing a learning regime from a high IQ label.
