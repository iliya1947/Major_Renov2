## 3. Construct and criterion map
Use an explicit working distinction: a false proposition concerns correspondence to facts; an honest error concerns belief or memory without established intent to mislead; deliberate deception concerns an attempt to produce a mistaken understanding. A statement can mislead pragmatically without being literally false, but evidence of misleading effect alone does not establish intended deception. Nondisclosure includes privacy, irrelevance, lack of opportunity, uncertainty, discretion and possible deliberate withholding. Whether omission breaches a duty requires evidence of the actual relevant obligation or agreement, not merely someone's preference for disclosure. A later changed intention does not establish that an earlier stated intention was false when expressed. A failed promise may establish nonfulfillment without establishing prior intent not to perform. Distinguish claims about conduct from claims about internal state; neither can be inferred reliably from discomfort, hesitation, expressive style or silence alone.

Separate actual veracity, perceived credibility, observer confidence, cue validity and classification performance. An observable discrepancy can warrant clarification, but memory, interpretation, wording, changing circumstances and incomplete records are plausible competing explanations. Where evidence is unavailable, do not convert uncertainty into certainty in either direction. A proven falsehood or confirmed breach is meaningful even when its psychological motivation remains unknown.

## 4. Diagnosticity and base rates
Let D denote an established deception event under a specified reference criterion and F a flag produced by a judgment procedure. Sensitivity = P(F|D); specificity = P(no F|no D); overall accuracy = P(D)*sensitivity + P(no D)*specificity. P(D|F) additionally depends on prevalence and false-positive rate. These formulas describe a defined binary task, not an estimated personal probability.
Illustrative invented arithmetic, not empirical data: among 100 messages with 10 independently established deception events, a hypothetical procedure with sensitivity 50% and specificity 90% flags 5 actual deceptions and 9 truthful messages. Only 5 of 14 flags are correct, despite 86 of 100 total classifications being correct. An always-truth response would achieve 90% overall accuracy in that hypothetical set while detecting no deception. This separates accuracy, positive predictive value, response bias and discrimination. Real-world base rate and relevant operating characteristics are unknown; do not insert meta-analytic averages as personal test values.
A cue is useful for comparing hypotheses only to the extent that its probability differs across those hypotheses in the relevant setting. Multiple cues from the same episode, channel or underlying state are dependent; do not multiply their evidential weights as if independent. Perceived certainty, familiarity and agreement among observers do not establish actual accuracy. No individualized probability or classification without relevant validated evidence.

## 5. Operational reasoning
1. Define the precise proposition, actor, time of expression, context and available evidence. Preserve literal statement, surrounding context, interpretation, reconstructed material and independent facts separately.
2. Establish whether a contradiction is real and material. Distinguish factual falsity, incompatible memories, genuine change, contextual omission, legitimate privacy and deliberate misleading. Do not infer intention solely from an inaccurate statement.
3. Identify actual agreed disclosure obligations and freely expressed choices. Do not turn a unilateral expectation into an established duty, or privacy into evidence of deception.
4. Compare plausible explanations using relevant, legitimately available information, ordinary follow-through and voluntary clarification when appropriate. Specify what would distinguish alternatives and what would disconfirm the preferred explanation. Do not manufacture tests, interrogate, monitor private accounts or pressure for disclosure.
5. Evaluate criterion validity, selection, context, base rates, false positives and false negatives. Separate group-level average effects from individual classification and actual veracity from credibility. Treat correlated cues as dependent. No invented numerical forecasts or suspicion scores.
6. Update the narrowest proposition supported by facts. If a material agreement is not met or a threat is documented, a protective decision can be justified without proving hidden motives. Respect autonomy and privacy; do not require proof of benign intent or assume guilt.
7. Keep nonverbal and paraverbal cue evidence for SOC-X03.2; no body-language dictionary, universal sign of deception or technique for bypassing resistance.

A. A collaborator states a delivery date that conflicts with an earlier estimate. Response: estimate, actual delivery and intentional falsehood differ (C1); schedule change or misunderstanding can account for the difference (C2); contemporaneous agreed records and ordinary progress updates would clarify (C3); no inference of deliberate deception from inconsistency alone (C4). B. A club member declines to discuss personal finances when no relevant disclosure obligation exists. Response: nondisclosure and deception differ (C1); privacy or irrelevance are plausible (C2); assess only the actual agreement and relevant voluntarily supplied information (C3); no presumption of guilt or entitlement to private details (C4). C. A person confidently interprets another's nervous behavior as proof of dishonesty. Response: perceived credibility and truth differ (C1); ordinary stress or situational discomfort compete (C2); independent facts concerning the exact claim are the relevant criterion (C3); no verdict based on demeanor (C4). Review 4/4.
D. A hypothetical classifier flags 5 actual falsehoods and 9 truthful messages among 100 messages. Response: sensitivity, specificity, predictive value and accuracy differ (C1); differing base rates or response tendencies could account for performance (C2); use a matched independently labeled test set (C3); no transfer to a particular person's statements (C4). Review 4/4.
E. Two people assume opposite disclosure rules for an optional joint activity. Response: unilateral expectation and mutually agreed obligation differ (C1); different norms or ambiguous prior language can explain divergence (C2); actual agreements and voluntary clarification matter (C3); no automatic dishonesty label (C4). Review 4/4.
F. Independent evidence establishes that a factual assertion was false, but the speaker's contemporary belief is unavailable. Response: falsity and knowing deception differ (C1); mistake or deliberate misrepresentation remain alternatives (C2); contemporaneous evidence of what was known could help distinguish them (C3); correct the false claim without asserting unknown motive (C4). Review 4/4.
