# GFT-11.4 — Social compatibility, closeness and adaptation

1. Question: what does intellectual ability, partner similarity and person-environment fit predict about social connection, intimacy and adaptation, and under what conditions?
2. Separate assortment/partner selection, opportunity, perceived similarity, objective cognitive similarity, relationship satisfaction, responsiveness, friendship, loneliness, desired solitude, network size, social functioning and well-being.
3. Populations: general adults, longitudinal cohorts, gifted youth, selected gifted adults, measured high-ability adults and separately measured IQ150–160+ adults. No unmarked transfer.
4. Seek primary studies, longitudinal and dyadic designs, meta-analyses, explicit null and opposing results. Search gifted adult social adjustment, partner IQ similarity and relationship quality, actual vs perceived similarity, loneliness and social support, extreme range.
5. Alternatives: age, education, socioeconomic status, shared settings, interests/values, attachment, personality, mental health, selection/recruitment, relationship duration and cultural context. Distinguish opportunity to meet from preference and consequences of similarity after formation.
6. Predictions: if cognitive similarity is sufficient, it should predict outcomes after considering other partner properties and sampling; if responsiveness/opportunity explains context-dependent changes, closeness can change without ability change. Neither universal benefit nor deficit assumed. No diagnosis, IQ inference, or deterministic relationship advice.
7. ## Evidence
E1. Gignac & Zajenkowski (2019), https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intell.2019.01.004, UWA author abstract inspected. N=218 couples; Advanced Progressive Matrices plus self/partner estimates. Measured and perceived IQ compatibility not significantly associated with relationship satisfaction for either sex. Partner IQ estimates correlated modestly with measured scores (women r=.30, men r=.19; difference nonsignificant). Estimated IQs systematically exceeded measured scores in this sample. Measurement/norm details, precise null intervals, selection and possible nonlinear effects not rechecked. Restricted range among established couples, relationship survival and measurement error matter. No evidence here of equivalence or of high-tail specific effects. Reused 11.3 candidate.

E2. Dijkstra, Barelds, Ronner & Nauta (2017), https://doi.org/10.1080/01494929.2016.1177630. Groningen institutional author abstract inspected; 196 adult Mensa members vs 145 community participants. Reported relationship quality similar overall; Mensa sample reported more fearful attachment and less constructive conflict handling; statistical mediation by conflict styles reported in both groups. Cross-sectional/self-report and selected membership; not independently verified partner behavior, attachment diagnosis or causal mediation. Effect sizes, comparison adjustment, relationship inclusion and partner IQ not verified. Group-specific conflict differences can coexist with nonsignificant overall satisfaction differences. No universal deficit.

E3. Wirthwein & Rost (2011), https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ917069; ERIC abstract opened, Elsevier indexed excerpt https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1041608011000069. Marburg longitudinal project; 101 gifted adults mean IQ136 and 91 comparison adults mean IQ103. No significant group difference reported for main subjective well-being components; somewhat lower leisure satisfaction d=-.28 in gifted group. Separate domains and overall satisfaction; friendship/partner domains included. Cohort selection, sample precision, attrition, age of measurement and exact instrument not independently rechecked. No equivalence or causal inference. Zettergren & Bergman (2014), https://doi.org/10.1080/15427609.2014.936182, publisher indexed abstract; full text 403, institutional page cache miss. Swedish birth cohort N1326 with IQ data, top decile measured in adolescence; midlife adjustment mostly nonsignificant differences but some lower outcomes including friendship and global life satisfaction. Not adult measured IQ130+ or 150+; comparison with Wirthwein cautions against a single harmony/disharmony profile. Data subsets and effects not rechecked.

E4. Montoya, Horton & Kirchner (2008), https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407508096700. Murdoch institutional abstract inspected; 460 effect sizes/313 lab and field studies. Across designs r=.47 actual similarity, r=.39 perceived similarity; actual similarity association attenuated by interaction and nonsignificant in established relationships; perceived similarity associated across stages. Broad similarity domains, not IQ-specific; attraction not satisfaction, subjective perceived similarity may reflect liking, shared-method variance, or projection. Do not compare pooled coefficients as proof of superiority. Horwitz et al. (2023), https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-023-01672-z, Nature subscription preview abstract: meta-analyses 22 traits, UKB 133 traits up to 79,074 opposite-sex couples; partner correlations generally positive and often heterogeneous; trait-specific IQ estimate not independently verified, do not quantify it here. Partner resemblance may reflect direct selection, shared settings, indirect assortment or convergence; see methodological alternatives in https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-022-01500-w (Nature HTML conceptual figure inspected). Resemblance is not evidence of causal benefit. Distinguish probability of meeting, preference conditional on meeting, reciprocal choice, formation and maintenance, and selection on surviving relationships.

E5. Gignac & Callis (2020), https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intell.2020.101465. UWA author abstract inspected: community N236 and student N220. Rated desirability fell between labeled 90th and 99th IQ percentiles, not for EI; among participants reducing IQ desirability, reported concerns about compatibility (~60%) or social skills (~40%). Raters' measured IQ and self-estimates associated with desirability at 90th percentile. Hypothetical evaluations not actual partner choice, observed social skill, successful relationships or clinical difficulty. 99th percentile ~135 on idealized normal mean100 SD15 scale, not 150–160+, and percentile labels not direct assessments of targets. No evidence for a fixed optimum or causal IQ threshold. Selection and measurement details not independently checked. Mechanism could include stereotype, perceived mismatch, status concerns or context.

## Integrated analytical rules (methodological, not established universal mechanism)
1. Define outcome and phase: social opportunity, interest, reciprocal choice, formed connection, semantic understanding, experienced intimacy, relationship quality, support, loneliness, functioning and adjustment. Do not substitute one for another.
2. Model separately individual abilities (A and B), signed differences, domain expertise, communication/repair (11.3), interests, values, disclosure/response, goals and constraints. Similarity can reduce explanation cost in a specific domain without guaranteeing shared goals or safety; dissimilarity can be irrelevant or beneficial in another domain; compensation is a case-specific hypothesis, not universal rule.
3. If rarity reduces encounter opportunities, test actual opportunity set and access to relevant communities, selection preferences and mutual interest. A shortage of available like-minded partners is not evidence of inability to bond. Compare realistic encounters conditional on setting; do not infer partner IQ from conversation or attribute all preferences to ability.
4. Distinguish chosen solitude vs distressing discrepancy between desired and actual connection; contact counts, network diversity, perceived support and actual available support. Consider time, accessibility, recent transition, culture, discrimination, prior relationship experience, mood and personality before invoking giftedness. Do not label lack of interest in a particular conversation as global social impairment.
5. In established relationships compare observable responsiveness, mutual repair, trust, cooperation, boundaries and distinct desired forms of closeness. Good cognitive interaction can coexist with poor relational safety; successful close relationship can coexist with large measured ability difference. Repeated corroborated episodes more diagnostic than global self/partner ratings. No universal relationship prescription, IQ matching cutoff, or deterministic attachment profile.
6. Prospective discriminators: compare perceived and actual comprehension, shared tasks and independent criteria; sample changed opportunity or shared context without altering ability; record how connection and satisfaction change with availability and response. If improved fit changes experience, revise invariant trait explanation, without claiming unique cause. If no change, consider alternative contexts and measurement; no forced positive narrative. Distinguish prediction and cause, do not condition naively on couples who formed and stayed together. Extreme-tail and 2e hypotheses remain separate.

## Internal application assessment — wholly invented cases
A. A fictional community choir participant with a high measured reasoning score reports enjoying only two close friendships, declines optional gatherings and is content. Another member has frequent gatherings but wants a confidant. Analyst claims first is maladjusted because of low contact count and second is socially well-adjusted. Separate desired contact, distress, network access/support and functioning; do not estimate either person's intelligence from their networks. New: first seeks more connection after relocation and reports unmet need despite unchanged score; update loneliness as context-dependent, not fixed IQ consequence, preserve previous preference for solitude. No diagnosis.

B. Fictional pairs of board-game club members: one similar-scoring pair enjoys complex puzzles but habitually dismisses each other's preferences; a different-scoring pair learns each other's strengths and reports reliable care. Initial model predicts the former closer solely from IQ similarity. Separate task compatibility, reported intimacy and behavior, compare feedback, reciprocal interest and values; new: first pair adopts repair and shared activity while perceived intimacy rises with unchanged scores. Update a response/context hypothesis; no proof of causality from temporal coincidence, no denial of conditional cognitive mismatch, no universal IQ threshold.

C. A fictional adult with high measured ability reports difficulty finding conversation partners at a rural arts workshop and dislikes hypothetical dating profiles that advertise exceptional IQ. Analyst concludes high ability necessarily produces rejection and poor relationships. Compare opportunity, topic/knowledge, stereotypes, self-rated vs actual interest, stage and local sampling; new: an online arts community yields several reciprocal friendships, one with different measured ability, and one with similar ability remains superficial. Revise deterministic prediction; context and specific fit plausible, no extrapolation to all partners, no inference of IQ from liking. Preserve possibility of domain-specific preference, test actual interaction not percentile labels.
