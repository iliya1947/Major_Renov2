# RES-12 — Exercise-science-specific measurement pitfalls

## 0. Question Card

### Decision
Какие измерения в exercise science можно использовать для research/personal decisions, какие требуют стандартизации/familiarization, и где common tools дают misleading confidence.

### Question type
Measurement / validation / exercise-specific methodology.

### Critical outcomes
1. 1RM/familiarization.
2. RIR accuracy/reliability.
3. Ultrasound hypertrophy measurement limitations.
4. DXA/BIA body-composition interpretation.
5. Velocity devices/load-velocity monitoring.
6. Circumference and wearable limitations.

### Closure criterion
Есть map of major measurement risks; each tool later receives topic-specific review before personal use.

---

## 1. General principle

Exercise science contains many convenient proxies.

A measure can be:
- reliable but not valid for target construct;
- valid cross-sectionally but poor for detecting longitudinal change;
- accurate at group level but too noisy for individual decisions;
- valid only under standardized protocol.

Project rule:
**measurement usefulness is claim-specific and decision-specific.**

---

## 2. 1RM testing

### What it measures
Task-specific maximal dynamic strength in a defined exercise under defined technique/ROM.

### Strengths
1. direct performance outcome;
2. highly relevant to maximal strength;
3. intuitive units;
4. can guide load prescription.

### Main threats
1. familiarization/learning;
2. technique changes;
3. ROM inconsistency;
4. motivation/encouragement;
5. fatigue/readiness;
6. exercise specificity;
7. different equipment.

### Practical implication
A pre-post 1RM improvement may contain:
- true force-capacity adaptation;
- improved skill;
- improved confidence;
- altered technique.

Especially early in novice/retraining phases.

### Project rule
For baseline/follow-up:
- use same exercise/setup;
- familiarize before treating small changes as physiology;
- record technique/ROM;
- use repeated data if uncertainty high.

---

## 3. Repetitions in Reserve (RIR)

### Construct
Estimated repetitions remaining before momentary muscular failure.

RIR is useful because it aims to quantify proximity to failure without requiring failure every set.

### Current evidence signals
Recent studies show:
- trained participants can estimate low RIR reasonably accurately in some exercises;
- accuracy often improves closer to failure;
- familiarization/practice can improve estimation;
- accuracy depends on exercise/load/context.

A 2026 repeated-measures study found RIR estimation trainable over six sessions in younger and older adults.

### Measurement threats
1. different definition of “failure”;
2. unfamiliar exercise;
3. unfamiliar participant;
4. high RIR / far from failure;
5. changing tempo;
6. inter-repetition rest;
7. machine vs free-weight differences;
8. motivation to actually reach failure during validation test.

### Project rule
RIR is **calibrated subjective measurement**, not truth.

Before using it for Ilya:
- define failure;
- calibrate against selected safe sets;
- use broad decision bands rather than pretend 1-rep precision when not justified.

## 4. Muscle ultrasound

Ultrasound can measure:
- muscle thickness;
- cross-sectional area at selected sites;
- architecture parameters.

### Strengths
1. non-invasive;
2. repeatable under skilled protocol;
3. useful in research/clinical contexts.

### Threats
1. operator pressure;
2. probe angle;
3. exact anatomical site;
4. image segmentation;
5. hydration/glycogen state;
6. regional hypertrophy;
7. thickness at one site ≠ whole muscle volume.

### Important 2026 evidence
A 2026 study comparing resistance-training-induced change in ultrasound muscle thickness to MRI criterion measures for quadriceps found poor individual agreement and substantial underestimation of growth for several comparisons.

This means:
**high static reliability of ultrasound does not guarantee longitudinal validity for hypertrophy change.**

### Project implication
Ultrasound literature must distinguish:
- cross-sectional validity;
- repeatability;
- longitudinal change validity.

Never use “ultrasound is reliable” as blanket proof that small measured hypertrophy change is true.

---

## 5. MRI

MRI can provide:
- cross-sectional area;
- muscle volume;
- detailed regional anatomy.

Strengths:
- strong anatomical detail;
- useful criterion method for muscle size.

Limitations:
- expensive;
- segmentation choices;
- protocol dependence;
- regional sampling;
- not practical for home monitoring.

For this project:
primarily research-reference context, not personal routine monitoring.

---

## 6. DXA

DXA estimates:
- fat mass;
- lean soft tissue;
- bone mineral content.

### Important distinction
DXA lean mass ≠ direct skeletal muscle mass.

Lean soft tissue includes water and other non-fat, non-bone tissue.

### Threats
1. hydration/fluid changes;
2. food/gut content;
3. machine/software differences;
4. positioning;
5. regional algorithms;
6. device calibration.

Historical controlled work demonstrates fluid intake can alter measured lean tissue mass.

Current 2026 expert methodological standards treat DXA as useful but protocol-dependent body-composition method, not perfect direct muscle measurement.

### Project implication
For hypertrophy:
DXA lean-mass change is informative but more indirect than direct muscle imaging and can be confounded by water/glycogen shifts.

---

## 7. Bioelectrical Impedance Analysis (BIA)

BIA estimates body composition using electrical impedance + prediction models.

### Strengths
1. cheap;
2. convenient;
3. repeatable under controlled conditions;
4. useful for some population/trend applications.

### Main threats
1. hydration;
2. device-specific equations;
3. population-specific prediction equations;
4. electrode configuration;
5. food/exercise timing;
6. manufacturer differences.

A 2026 systematic review against a four-compartment criterion model found poor individual-level equivalence for body-fat percentage and fat-free mass across included BIA devices, despite sometimes modest mean bias.

### Critical lesson
Small average bias ≠ good individual agreement.

Wide LoA can make a device unsuitable for detecting small individual body-composition changes.

### Project rule
Consumer BIA body-fat % will not be treated as direct evidence of small hypertrophy changes.

At most:
- trend/context metric;
- under strict repeated conditions;
- after device-specific reliability review.

---

## 8. Circumference

Simple tape circumference can be useful for arm progress.

### Threats
1. landmark drift;
2. tape tension;
3. posture;
4. flexed vs relaxed;
5. pump;
6. glycogen/water;
7. fat change;
8. measurer variation.

### Strengths
- cheap;
- practical;
- direct external dimension;
- repeatable with strict protocol.

### Limit
Circumference change cannot by itself distinguish:
- muscle;
- fat;
- fluid.

### Project implication
Likely useful as low-cost **trend-grade** measure, not standalone proof of muscle hypertrophy.

---

## 9. Photos

Photos can show gross visual change.

Threats:
1. lighting;
2. camera distance;
3. lens;
4. angle;
5. posture;
6. flexing;
7. pump;
8. body-fat/water changes.

Useful:
- long-term visual corroboration.

Not useful:
- small short-term quantified hypertrophy.

---

## 10. Velocity measurement devices

Velocity-based resistance monitoring includes:
- linear position transducers;
- inertial sensors;
- optical/camera systems;
- smartphone systems.

### 2026 systematic-review evidence
A 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis found commercial velocity sensors on average showed good-to-excellent pooled relative validity/reliability, but:
- substantial heterogeneity;
- device technology mattered;
- exercise complexity mattered;
- agreement/measurement-error evidence was often insufficient;
- 1RM prediction validity varied, especially in lower-body exercises.

### Critical lesson
High pooled ICC does not prove:
- every device is interchangeable;
- low absolute error;
- accurate 1RM prediction for every exercise.

### Project rule
Device- and exercise-specific validation required before velocity guides decisions.

---

## 11. Load–velocity profiles

Potential uses:
1. monitor readiness;
2. estimate 1RM;
3. prescribe load;
4. track velocity loss.

Threats:
1. exercise-specific curve;
2. individual vs generalized profile;
3. execution intent;
4. ROM;
5. device error;
6. day-to-day variation;
7. lower-body heterogeneity.

For current dumbbell/home context:
low priority unless accessible validated tool appears.

---

## 12. EMG

Surface EMG measures electrical activity at/near muscle under electrode.

It does **not** directly measure:
- muscle force;
- hypertrophy;
- mechanical tension;
- recruitment of entire muscle in simple one-number form.

Threats:
1. electrode location;
2. adipose tissue;
3. cross-talk;
4. normalization;
5. muscle geometry;
6. dynamic movement;
7. signal processing.

Detailed acute proxy interpretation → RES-13.

Project rule:
EMG cannot rank exercises as a direct hypertrophy scoreboard.

---

## 13. Force plates / dynamometers

Can measure force-related outputs reliably if:
- equipment calibrated;
- protocol standardized;
- task defined.

Threats:
- technique;
- positioning;
- sampling/filter settings;
- familiarization;
- operator setup.

High precision does not automatically make test relevant to target performance.

---

## 14. Wearables: HR, sleep, HRV, activity

Device quality is outcome-specific.

One wearable may be:
- acceptable for HR during steady exercise;
- poor for sleep stages;
- variable for energy expenditure;
- proprietary for recovery score.

### Key rule
Validate each **metric**, not brand globally.

Proprietary composite score:
- may change algorithm;
- may lack transparent validation;
- may mix many signals.

Project rule:
never infer “readiness” merely because app reports a score.

---

## 15. Heart rate

HR can be measured well by:
- ECG;
- validated chest straps;
- some optical devices under specific conditions.

Optical wrist sensors can degrade with:
- resistance movement;
- wrist flexion;
- motion artifact;
- skin/device fit.

For strength training, HR is not a direct measure of local muscular stimulus.

---

## 16. Sleep tracking

Consumer wearables can estimate:
- sleep/wake reasonably in some contexts;
but sleep stages and derived recovery metrics can be less reliable/device-dependent.

Project use:
if used, prioritize:
- sleep opportunity/duration trend;
- subjective sleep;
over overinterpreting detailed stage percentages.

Specific wearable review later only if device selected.

---

## 17. Self-reported training logs

Often best source for:
- exercises;
- sets;
- reps;
- load;
- RIR;
- session completion.

Threats:
- forgetting;
- retrospective reconstruction;
- inconsistent definitions.

But for home training:
a simple immediate log can outperform sophisticated sensor data that does not capture actual intended dose.

---

## 18. Measurement hierarchy for this project

For a candidate metric ask:

1. What construct?
2. Is measure direct or proxy?
3. Reliability?
4. Absolute agreement/error?
5. Longitudinal responsiveness?
6. Familiarization?
7. Standardization burden?
8. Device/operator dependence?
9. Decision threshold?
10. Cost/friction?

Then classify per RES-11:
- A Decision-grade;
- B Trend-grade;
- C Context-only;
- D Reject.

## 20. Project rules

1. Never equate reliability with longitudinal validity.
2. Never equate correlation with agreement.
3. Never infer whole-muscle hypertrophy from one ultrasound site without caveat.
4. DXA lean mass is not identical to muscle mass.
5. BIA small mean bias can hide huge individual error.
6. RIR requires calibration/familiarization.
7. Velocity devices require device + exercise validation.
8. EMG is mechanistic electrical signal, not hypertrophy score.
9. Wearable algorithms are metric-specific, not globally “validated”.
10. Simple standardized measures can beat complex noisy technology.

## 21. Confidence

### HIGH
- exercise measurements require purpose/protocol-specific validation;
- RIR accuracy depends on context and can improve with practice;
- ultrasound reliability does not automatically prove longitudinal hypertrophy validity;
- BIA individual agreement can be poor even with small average bias;
- velocity-device performance varies by technology/exercise and agreement data matter.

### MODERATE
- exact usefulness of each method for Ilya awaits tool-specific review and personal measurement error.

### PRACTICAL_HEURISTIC
- provisional personal-monitoring hierarchy.
