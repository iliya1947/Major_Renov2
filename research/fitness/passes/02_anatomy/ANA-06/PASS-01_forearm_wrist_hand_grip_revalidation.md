## 2. Canonical forearm rotational system

Forearm rotation is a coordinated system containing:

1. PRUJ;
2. radius;
3. ulna;
4. interosseous membrane (IOM);
5. DRUJ.

Project model:

`PRUJ ↔ radius/ulna + IOM ↔ DRUJ`

During pronation:
- radius crosses relative to ulna.

During supination:
- radius and ulna become relatively parallel.

Project rule:

**pronation/supination = forearm rotation, not wrist rotation.**

---

## 3. Radius and ulna

### Radius

- lateral/thumb side in anatomical position;
- articulates proximally with capitulum/ulna;
- articulates distally with ulna and carpal complex;
- moves substantially around ulna during pronation/supination.

### Ulna

- medial/little-finger side in anatomical position;
- major proximal elbow articulation;
- distal ulnar head participates in DRUJ;
- does not directly form the primary radiocarpal articulation with the proximal carpal row in the same way as radius.

---

## 4. Interosseous membrane

IOM = fibrous syndesmotic structure between radius and ulna.

Roles:

1. binds/organizes radius and ulna;
2. separates/organizes forearm compartments;
3. provides muscle attachment;
4. participates in longitudinal/transverse force transfer;
5. contributes to rotational forearm stability.

Project rule:

**IOM is not merely a passive septum.**

Detailed fiber-specific load transfer → MEC.

---

## 5. Distal radioulnar joint

DRUJ:

- sigmoid notch of distal radius ↔ ulnar head;
- synovial pivot articulation;
- essential for pronation/supination;
- contributes translational stability of distal radius–ulna relationship.

Current NCBI reference explicitly treats DRUJ as part of the forearm ring with:
- radius;
- ulna;
- PRUJ;
- IOM;
- TFCC stabilization.

Project rule:

**DRUJ is a forearm-rotation joint, not the radiocarpal wrist joint.**

---

## 6. TFCC — current structural model

TFCC is a complex ulnar-sided stabilizing structure spanning DRUJ/ulnocarpal relationships.

The 2026 updated review describes a 3D/tripartite conceptualization including:

1. articular disc;
2. distal radioulnar ligaments as primary stabilizing components;
3. continuous peripheral capsular “wall” incorporating associated ulnocarpal/peripheral structures.

Associated structures can include:
- meniscus homologue;
- ulnocarpal ligaments;
- ECU tendon-sheath relationship;
- peripheral insertions.

Project rule:

**TFCC ≠ one triangular ligament/disc.**

Functional role:
- rotational/translational DRUJ stability;
- ulnar-sided load transmission/support.

Detailed pathology/imaging → MED/TST.

## 7. Forearm compartment organization

Useful project organization:

### Anterior
flexor-pronator dominant.

Layers:
- superficial;
- intermediate;
- deep.

### Posterior
extensor-supinator dominant.

Layers:
- superficial;
- deep.

Some anatomy references separately describe a lateral/mobile-wad group:
- brachioradialis;
- ECRL;
- ECRB.

Project rule:

Compartment labels organize anatomy; they do not imply one action per compartment.

---

## 8. Anterior superficial/intermediate flexor-pronator system

Core structures:

1. pronator teres;
2. flexor carpi radialis (FCR);
3. palmaris longus;
4. flexor carpi ulnaris (FCU);
5. flexor digitorum superficialis (FDS) as intermediate layer in common descriptions.

### Pronator teres

- medial-epicondylar/coronoid origins;
- inserts on lateral mid-radius;
- median nerve;
- pronation;
- secondary elbow-flexion contribution.

### FCR

- wrist flexion;
- radial deviation contribution.

### FCU

- wrist flexion;
- ulnar deviation;
- ulnar nerve;
- distal continuity through pisiform/pisohamate/pisometacarpal structures.

### Palmaris longus

- weak wrist flexion;
- tenses palmar aponeurosis;
- highly variable/commonly absent.

Project rule:

**palmaris-longus absence is normal variation, not loss of normal hand function.**

---

## 9. Deep anterior compartment

Core:

1. flexor digitorum profundus (FDP);
2. flexor pollicis longus (FPL);
3. pronator quadratus.

### FDP

- ulna/IOM origin;
- distal phalanges digits 2–5;
- DIP flexion;
- major grip contribution.

Innervation is split:
- lateral part largely anterior interosseous/median;
- medial part ulnar.

### FPL

- radius/IOM;
- distal thumb phalanx;
- thumb IP flexion;
- anterior interosseous nerve.

### Pronator quadratus

- distal ulna → distal radius;
- anterior interosseous nerve;
- pronation;
- contributes distal radioulnar dynamic stabilization.

---

## 10. Pronator system

Major pronators:

1. pronator teres;
2. pronator quadratus.

Both rotate radius relative to ulna.

Project boundary:

Exact load/angle-specific contribution is mechanics.

No one pronator is declared “the” pronation muscle for all tasks.

---

## 11. Posterior extensor-supinator system

Current 2026 wrist-extensor reference organizes superficial and deep posterior structures.

### Superficial / lateral structures relevant here

- brachioradialis;
- ECRL;
- ECRB;
- extensor digitorum;
- extensor digiti minimi;
- ECU;
- anconeus.

### Deep

- supinator;
- APL;
- EPB;
- EPL;
- extensor indicis.

---

## 12. Supinator

Attachments span:
- lateral epicondylar region;
- radial collateral/annular-ligament region;
- ulna/supinator crest;
- proximal radius.

Innervation:
- deep radial / posterior interosseous pathway.

Role:
- forearm supination.

Works with biceps.

Project rule:

**biceps + supinator form the main supination system; their relative contribution is task/angle dependent.**

---

## 13. Wrist extensors

### ECRL / ECRB

Broad functions:
- wrist extension;
- radial deviation contribution.

### ECU

Broad functions:
- wrist extension;
- ulnar deviation.

Current 2026 NCBI reference explicitly emphasizes coordinated wrist-extensor stabilization during grip.

Project consequence:

**wrist extensors are part of grip architecture even though they do not flex the fingers.**

Exact force contribution → MEC.

---

## 14. Extrinsic finger extensors

### Extensor digitorum

- digits 2–5;
- major MCP extension;
- acts through extensor expansions for IP extension;
- contributes wrist extension.

### Extensor indicis

- more independent index extension.

### Extensor digiti minimi

- more independent little-finger extension.

These are not merely antagonists; they contribute positioning and release/opening control.

---

## 15. Extrinsic thumb extensors / abductor

1. APL;
2. EPB;
3. EPL.

They act at different thumb/wrist relationships and travel through defined dorsal extensor compartments.

Detailed moment arms → MEC.

---

## 16. Wrist is a joint complex

“Wrist motion” is not one articulation.

Relevant system:

1. radiocarpal joint;
2. midcarpal/intercarpal joints;
3. ulnocarpal/TFCC interface.

DRUJ is adjacent but belongs primarily to forearm rotation.

Project rule:

**whole-wrist flexion/extension/deviation ≠ pure radiocarpal motion.**

---

## 17. Carpal bones

Eight carpal bones.

### Proximal row

1. scaphoid;
2. lunate;
3. triquetrum;
4. pisiform.

### Distal row

5. trapezium;
6. trapezoid;
7. capitate;
8. hamate.

Pisiform is a sesamoid bone within FCU tendon architecture.

---

## 18. Radiocarpal joint

Radiocarpal joint is a condyloid/ellipsoid synovial articulation.

Proximally:
- distal radius;
- ulnar-sided articular-disc/TFCC interface.

Distally:
- primarily scaphoid/lunate and triquetrum depending position.

Permits:
- flexion;
- extension;
- radial deviation;
- ulnar deviation;
- circumduction.

Ulna is separated from direct primary carpal articulation by the TFCC/articular-disc system.

---

## 19. Midcarpal / intercarpal contribution

Midcarpal articulation lies between proximal/distal carpal rows.

It contributes substantially to:
- flexion/extension;
- radial/ulnar deviation.

Therefore:
**radiocarpal ROM is not synonymous with total wrist ROM.**

---

## 20. Flexor retinaculum / carpal tunnel

Flexor retinaculum forms the roof of the carpal tunnel.

Canonical tunnel contents:

- median nerve;
- 4 FDS tendons;
- 4 FDP tendons;
- 1 FPL tendon.

Total:
**9 long flexor tendons + median nerve.**

Important distinctions:

- FCR passes through its own fibro-osseous compartment rather than ordinary tunnel contents;
- palmaris longus is superficial;
- ulnar nerve/artery travel through Guyon-region structures, not carpal tunnel.

Pathology → ANA-12/MED.

---

## 21. Extensor retinaculum

Extensor retinaculum:

- holds extensor tendons close to the wrist;
- limits bowstringing;
- organizes dorsal tendon paths.

Current 2026 reference describes six typical osteofibrous compartments:

1. APL + EPB;
2. ECRL + ECRB;
3. EPL;
4. extensor digitorum + extensor indicis;
5. EDM;
6. ECU.

Project rule:

Exact septation/tendon-slip patterns vary; the six-compartment model is the reference architecture, not identical individual anatomy.

---

## 22. Tendon sheaths

Long flexor/extensor tendons traverse synovial sheaths in constrained high-friction regions.

Functions:
- reduce friction;
- permit tendon gliding.

Presence of sheath is normal anatomy; tenosynovitis is pathology.

---

## 23. Extrinsic vs intrinsic hand muscles

### Extrinsic

Muscle bellies in forearm, tendons enter hand.

Examples:
- FDS/FDP/FPL;
- extensor digitorum;
- thumb extensors.

### Intrinsic

Muscle origin/insertion located within hand.

Core groups:
1. thenar;
2. hypothenar;
3. lumbricals;
4. interossei;
5. adductor pollicis / related deep thumb muscles.

---

## 24. Thenar / thumb intrinsic system

Main thenar region:
- abductor pollicis brevis;
- flexor pollicis brevis;
- opponens pollicis.

Adductor pollicis is a deep intrinsic thumb muscle often treated separately from the superficial thenar triad.

Innervation is not simply “thenar = median”:
- major thenar motor supply is recurrent median;
- adductor pollicis is ulnar;
- FPB can have mixed/deep-head ulnar contribution.

Project rule:

**thumb grip function crosses median + ulnar territories.**

---

## 25. Hypothenar system

Core:
- abductor digiti minimi;
- flexor digiti minimi brevis;
- opponens digiti minimi;
- palmaris brevis associated with ulnar palm.

Primarily ulnar-nerve system.

Roles:
- fifth-digit positioning;
- palm cupping/contour;
- grip support.

---

## 26. Lumbricals

Four lumbricals.

Origin:
- FDP tendons.

Insertion:
- extensor expansions.

Broad action:
- MCP flexion;
- IP extension through extensor mechanism.

Typical innervation:
- lateral two median;
- medial two ulnar.

Variation exists.

---

## 27. Interossei — current count nuance

### Dorsal interossei

Classically:
- four;
- finger abduction;
- MCP flexion + IP extension contribution;
- deep ulnar nerve.

### Palmar interossei

Classic teaching often counts **three** for digits 2, 4 and 5.

Current 2025 NCBI anatomy also recognizes a variable/rudimentary **first/pollical palmar interosseous**, so some sources describe four.

Project rule:

**do not use a rigid “always three” or “always four” count without defining the convention.**

Functional core remains:
- palmar interossei → adduction;
- dorsal interossei → abduction;
- both contribute MCP flexion/IP extension via extensor mechanism.

---

## 28. Extensor expansion / hood

Digital extensor mechanism integrates:

1. extensor digitorum;
2. lumbricals;
3. interossei.

This allows force distribution across MCP/PIP/DIP relationships.

Project rule:

**finger extension is not one tendon pulling one distal phalanx.**

---

## 29. Thumb structural specialisation

Thumb includes:

1. first CMC at trapezium;
2. MCP;
3. IP joint.

CMC allows combined:
- flexion/extension;
- abduction/adduction;
- axial rotation component;
- opposition/reposition.

Opposition is a composite 3D movement central to grip/pinch.

---

## 30. Finger joints

Digits 2–5:

1. MCP;
2. PIP;
3. DIP.

MCP:
- flexion/extension;
- abduction/adduction depending position.

IP:
- primarily hinge flexion/extension.

---

## 31. Power grip architecture

Power grip is a distributed system requiring:

1. FDP/FDS and other extrinsic finger flexors;
2. thumb flexion/adduction/opposition;
3. intrinsic hand stabilization;
4. wrist positioning/stabilization;
5. forearm rotational orientation matched to object/task.

Project rule:

**strong grip ≠ finger flexors alone.**

---

## 32. Why wrist extensors matter for grip

Extrinsic finger flexors cross the wrist.

If the wrist is excessively flexed, the finger-flexor muscle-tendon system is shortened across multiple joints.

Wrist extensors counterbalance flexor wrist moments and stabilize the wrist during forceful grip.

Current 2026 wrist-extensor reference explicitly identifies this grip-stabilization role.

Anatomy supports the coupling; exact optimal wrist angle → MEC/TST.

---

## 33. Tenodesis / wrist–finger coupling

Because long finger flexor/extensor tendons cross wrist and finger joints:

- wrist extension tends to produce passive finger flexion;
- wrist flexion tends to produce passive finger extension.

This demonstrates mechanical coupling between wrist position and finger posture.

Project boundary:

Exact grip force–angle relation belongs to biomechanics, not ANA-06.

---

## 34. Grip categories

Useful functional categories:

1. power/cylindrical grip;
2. precision grip;
3. pinch variants;
4. hook grip.

Different tasks redistribute demand among:
- extrinsic flexors/extensors;
- thumb muscles;
- interossei/lumbricals;
- wrist stabilizers.

For dumbbells/bars, power/cylindrical grip is usually the most directly relevant category.

No hypertrophy ranking follows from this taxonomy.

## 35. Wrist flexion/extension and deviation

Broad coordination:

### Wrist flexion
FCR + FCU can combine to flex with reduced net radial/ulnar deviation.

### Wrist extension
ECRL/ECRB + ECU can combine to extend with reduced net deviation.

### Radial deviation
radial-side flexor/extensor contributors combine.

### Ulnar deviation
FCU + ECU contribute.

Project rule:

These are coordinated force-couple descriptions; exact torque → MEC.

## 41. Project canonical forearm/wrist/hand model

1. Radius + ulna + IOM + PRUJ + DRUJ form the forearm rotational system.
2. TFCC is a multi-component ulnar-sided stabilizing/load-sharing complex, not one triangular ligament.
3. Anterior forearm is flexor-pronator dominant; posterior is extensor-supinator dominant.
4. Pronator teres/quadratus and biceps/supinator create the principal rotation system.
5. Wrist motion is distributed across radiocarpal + midcarpal/intercarpal structures.
6. Flexor/extensor retinacula constrain tendon paths and limit bowstringing.
7. Extrinsic finger muscles and intrinsic hand muscles cooperate rather than acting as separate “grip vs fine-motor” systems.
8. Strong grip requires finger flexors + thumb/intrinsics + wrist stabilization.
9. Wrist position mechanically couples to finger posture/force through multi-joint tendons.
10. Extensor expansion integrates extrinsic extensors + lumbricals + interossei.
11. “Forearm” contains multiple independent functions; wrist curls alone do not represent complete forearm training.
12. Normal hand/forearm anatomy is variable.

## 43. Established findings

### HIGH

1. Forearm pronation/supination requires coordinated PRUJ + radius/ulna/IOM + DRUJ function.
2. DRUJ is a pivot articulation distinct from radiocarpal wrist motion.
3. TFCC is a multi-component ulnar-sided stabilizing/load-sharing complex.
4. Anterior/posterior forearm compartments contain distinct flexor-pronator and extensor-supinator systems.
5. Wrist motion is distributed across radiocarpal + midcarpal/intercarpal structures.
6. Carpal tunnel contains median nerve + 9 flexor tendons; FCR/ulnar neurovascular structures follow separate routes.
7. Wrist extensors materially stabilize wrist during grip.
8. Power grip depends on extrinsic flexors, thumb/intrinsics and wrist stabilization.
9. Extensor hood integrates extrinsic extensor + lumbrical + interosseous forces.
10. Wrist position couples mechanically to finger posture through multi-joint tendon paths.
11. Normal intrinsic-hand anatomy shows meaningful variation.

### MODERATE / convention-dependent

1. Exact count/classification of palmar interossei depends on whether pollical palmar interosseous is treated as a distinct muscle.
2. Exact individual grip contribution of each muscle depends on wrist/forearm/finger configuration and task.
3. Exact mechanical effect of specific TFCC variants belongs to MEC/MED/TST.
