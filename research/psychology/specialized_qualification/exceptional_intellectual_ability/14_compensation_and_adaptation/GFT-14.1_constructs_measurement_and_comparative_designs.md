## Concepts: explicit working distinctions
- Compensation: alternative route or strategy overcoming a specified difficulty in a specified outcome despite persisting difficulty in the target process; terminology varies (Livingston & Happé 2017). A reported strategy is not demonstrated compensation. Distinguish repair/remediation of the target process, learning, alternative route, practice, and external accommodation.
- Masking: reducing observable signs; may or may not improve activity, communication or participation. Camouflaging umbrella includes overlapping masking, compensation and assimilation in some instruments; other frameworks reverse the nesting. Do not force a universal taxonomy. Concealment is not necessarily voluntary, deceptive or autistic.
- Adaptation: dynamic fit of person's strategies/resources and environment; not simply looking typical. Accommodation modifies the demands/context/support; a person can select accommodation as a strategy. Adaptive behavior as a measured construct differs from broad adaptation.
- Functional capacity and performance: activity ability under specified standardized circumstances vs actual daily activity/participation in context (ICF). No global unassisted ability inferred from IQ. Preserve supported achievement as real achievement. Cost separately measured in time, effort, errors, recovery, competing activities and wellbeing, not assumed from visible success. Compensation and high cost can coexist, but neither necessarily entails the other.

## Operational gate
1. Specify target task, difficulty, actual outcome, quality/accuracy and context; identify independent evidence of difficulty before inferring compensation. Keep observer report, person report, test, actual action and outcome separate.
2. Specify hypothesized strategy and comparator. Alternative explanations include skill, learning, less demanding context, accessible environment, selective tasks, familiarity, measurement error, anxiety/effort, hidden assistance and genuine improvement in target operation. Do not assume every discrepancy is a deficit. Compare matched task demands, repeated observations, reliability, competing predictions, ordinary safe variations. No forced withdrawal of necessary support, distress or covert tests.
3. Distinguish evidence tiers: reported strategy -> observed use -> association with output -> controlled benefit -> independent process evidence; none automatically proves all subsequent tiers. Counterfactual benefit is improvement relative to same person in comparable alternative condition, not relative IQ or another person's performance. Randomized/counterbalanced tasks or longitudinal designs may support causal claims with assumptions; measure baseline, practice/order, skill and costs. In ordinary life, preserve supports and use naturally occurring contrasts/consented alternatives only.
4. Separate appearance (observer impressions), actual activity/participation, support burden, subjective cost, measured time/recovery and person-defined goal. A behavior can conceal a difficulty without solving it; support can improve actual performance without changing underlying process; cost may fall with practice or accessible context. No mandatory link to distress, burnout or IQ. Distinguish internal/external strategies from willingness and opportunity.
5. Group comparisons require comparable measures, appropriate norms, actual IQ distribution, age/clinical matching, interaction tests, uncertainty and selection audit. Avoid part-whole coupling, arbitrary difference thresholds and comparing significance. Measurement invariance is necessary for some score comparisons, not sufficient for construct validity. No clinical diagnosis or extreme-tail extrapolation. Route efficacy/strategy to 14.2, longitudinal cost to14.3, dependencies to14.4.
