## 3. Fatigue-definition audit

PASS.

Fatigue is retained as:

**exercise/task-induced reduction in the capacity to produce or sustain required force, power or performance.**

Task failure is the point at which task requirements can no longer be met.

Perceived fatigue is subjective and related but not equivalent.

Canonical rule:

**fatigue can exist before task failure.**

## 4. Central / peripheral / neuromuscular taxonomy audit

PASS.

### Central fatigue
Reduced voluntary neural drive/activation to muscle.

### Peripheral fatigue
Impairment distal to central motor drive, including membrane, E–C coupling, Ca²⁺ handling, myofilament and cross-bridge processes.

### Neuromuscular fatigue
Umbrella reduction in neuromuscular force/power capacity that can include both central and peripheral components.

Canonical rule:

**central and peripheral fatigue coexist in variable proportions; no universal percentage split is valid.**

## 5. RT-specific acute-fatigue audit

PASS.

The current resistance-training systematic review supports:

- contractile function;
- afferent/perceptual responses;
- voluntary performance

as commonly affected domains.

Main protocol determinants include:

- set duration;
- proximity to failure;
- total volume;
- density.

Muscle-activation measures were less consistently altered.

Canonical rule:

**surface EMG change is not a complete fatigue partitioning tool.**

## 6. Metabolic-fatigue audit

PASS.

Metabolic fatigue remains a mechanism family involving:

- PCr depletion;
- Pi accumulation;
- ADP-related changes;
- H⁺/pH shifts;
- substrate use;
- interactions with ion transport and Ca²⁺ handling.

Canonical rule:

**metabolic fatigue is not equivalent to lactate accumulation.**

## 7. Pi / H⁺ / lactate audit

PASS.

### Pi
Retains strong mechanistic relevance to impaired force/Ca²⁺ sensitivity/SR Ca²⁺ handling.

### H⁺ / acidosis
Current 2025 review supports a meaningful contribution of severe intracellular acidosis to reduced force, shortening velocity and power in intense exercise, but not as the sole mechanism.

### Lactate
Lactate itself is not supported as a simple toxic fatigue agent.

Canonical rule:

**no single metabolite universally explains fatigue.**

## 8. E–C coupling / Ca²⁺ audit

PASS WITH MODEL-TRANSFER BOUNDARY.

E–C coupling failure remains a legitimate peripheral fatigue family:

- altered membrane/T-tubule excitability;
- impaired SR Ca²⁺ release;
- altered myofilament Ca²⁺ sensitivity.

The 2026 task-dependent Ca²⁺ study provides mechanistic evidence from isolated mouse muscle fibers, not direct human RT outcome evidence.

Canonical rule:

**Ca²⁺ handling is mechanistically fatigue-relevant, but animal/single-fiber estimates are not used to quantify human RT fatigue shares.**

## 9. Substrate-depletion audit

PASS.

The 2025 RT meta-analysis shows acute vastus-lateralis glycogen depletion after RT, with larger depletion associated with:

- more sets;
- longer sessions;
- protocol/training-status factors.

But the prediction interval is broad and this evidence does not establish glycogen depletion as the sole immediate mechanism of short-set task failure.

Canonical rule:

**glycogen is a repeated-work/recovery constraint, not a universal short-set fatigue explanation.**

## 10. Ionic-disturbance audit

PASS.

K⁺/Na⁺/Cl⁻/Ca²⁺ disturbances and Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase activity influence excitability.

Canonical rule:

**ionic fatigue is an interacting membrane-excitability problem, not a one-ion threshold model.**

Exact human RT shares remain context dependent.

## 11. Neural-drive audit

PASS.

Voluntary neural drive can fall, but:

- peripheral impairment can dominate while drive remains relatively high;
- EMG amplitude does not uniquely represent neural drive;
- task/intensity/test method alter apparent central fatigue.

Canonical rule:

**reduced neural drive is one possible limiter, not the universal final cause of fatigue.**

## 12. Local vs systemic audit

PASS.

### Local vs systemic
Describes distribution/scale of impairment.

### Central vs peripheral
Describes physiological site/mechanism.

Canonical rule:

**these are separate classification axes.**

A local muscle task can contain central fatigue; whole-body exercise can contain strong peripheral fatigue.

## 13. Acute vs accumulated fatigue audit

PASS WITH PRESERVED UNCERTAINTY.

Acute fatigue follows a bout and resolves according to outcome-specific kinetics.

“Accumulated fatigue” is retained as a practical longitudinal label for persistent/recurrent performance impairment under incomplete recovery.

The review evidence remains equivocal for a unique physiological entity or one accumulating fatigue substance.

Canonical rule:

**accumulated fatigue must be demonstrated by repeated outcome impairment, not assumed from metabolite accumulation.**

## 14. Task-dependency audit

PASS.

Fatigue mechanism weighting changes with:

- required force;
- duration;
- contraction pattern;
- velocity;
- muscle mass;
- duty cycle/rest;
- perfusion;
- environment;
- task skill.

Canonical question:

**what limits this task under these conditions?**

not:

**what single thing causes fatigue?**

## 15. Force / velocity / technique audit

PASS.

RT fatigue reliably reduces:

- force capacity;
- repetition velocity;
- power;
- RFD/performance.

Movement strategy and technique can also change, but current evidence does not support one universal fatigued movement pattern.

Canonical rule:

**performance decline is robust; compensatory technique is task/individual specific.**

## 16. Recovery-kinetics audit

PASS.

Recovery is outcome-specific and asynchronous.

The 2017 trained-male study showed failure-oriented RT produced larger acute mechanical impairment and slower recovery over roughly 24–48 h than non-failure protocols, including at matched total volume.

This is protocol-specific evidence, not a universal recovery duration.

Canonical rule:

**there is no universal recovery clock.**

## 17. One-marker recovery audit

PASS.

Normalization of any single marker does not prove complete recovery:

- lactate;
- glycogen;
- soreness;
- CK;
- bar velocity;
- CMJ;
- perceived readiness;
- voluntary activation;
- twitch force.

Canonical rule:

**recovery must be defined against the target function and time point.**

## 18. Integrated limiter model audit

PASS.

Repeated force production is modeled as competition between:

`required task output`

and

`momentary sustainable capacity`

where sustainable capacity emerges from:

- voluntary neural drive;
- membrane excitability;
- E–C coupling/Ca²⁺ handling;
- cross-bridge/contractile state;
- metabolic/ionic state;
- substrate availability;
- mechanics;
- perceptual/systemic context.

The dominant limiter can shift during the same task.

Canonical rule:

**fatigue is an emergent task-specific limitation, not a single molecule, organ or marker.**
